The proposition

A neoclassical civic frame, containing storybook narrative.

The game is not a genre, so the chrome cannot be one. What it is, across every genre it contains, is the founding of a republic — so the frame is Rome and Washington: ink, marble, brass, engraved capitals, seals, rules, and the plain typography of an official record.

That frame is also the answer to the second problem. Storybook warmth appears only inside the narrative panes — when you are actually reading the world. Chat, the CMS, the forums and the legislature stay civic and plain, which is what differentiates them, and what keeps a dense screen legible.

Old Glory Blue is a canton, not the whole cloth. At full saturation it is unreadable under dense text, so it holds mastheads, seals and panels while the reading ground is a deepened navy drawn from it. Brass and verdigris are the Rome half — bronze and patina, the materials of a republic — kept subordinate so the flag reads first. Paper appears only in narrative panes.

Where the red goes

The flag already answers this: red and blue never touch. The stripes are held off the canton by white, always. So the rule is one line — every red element carries white between it and the blue — and red gets the jobs that suit a stamp rather than a UI accent.

Type
Spectral Chrome display and engraved capitals. The inscriptional letterform, without licensing Trajan.
Public Sans All UI and dense text. Literally the US federal design system's face — civic by provenance, and neutral enough to disappear at density.
Newsreader World prose only. The storybook voice, quarantined to narrative panes.
