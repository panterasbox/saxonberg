Turn 3 · mobile catches up

One column, everything desktop learned.

Desktop spends horizontal space on parallelism — two feeds, a pane rail, several routed terminals at once. A phone has none of it, so every parallel thing has to become either sequential or switchable.

The rule that decides which: interleave what is causally related, switch what is independent. Panes are caused by what you just did, so they belong inline in the feed. Routed feeds are independent streams, so they get a switcher. Prompts are demands on you, so they keep a slot and are never hidden.

RULE 3 IS DIFFERENT HERE

On desktop the copy-to-Attention rule is a convenience — the frame is visible in World anyway. On a phone it is the safety net, because World may not be the feed you are looking at.

So it ships on by default, and turning it off should say what it costs.
