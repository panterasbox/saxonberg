# The teachability boundary — what the game can teach, and how

> **Status: design constraint, aspirational.** The test for what
> "live the lesson" can and can't reach, the **three teaching modes**
> (direct enactment · allegory · reference), the out-of-bounds categories,
> and the routing rule that tells the planning agents *what not to try to
> model*. Pairs with the fidelity audit
> ([study-com-vertical-fidelity-audit.md](./study-com-vertical-fidelity-audit.md)):
> that one says "add the reasoning where the discipline is livable"; this
> one says "here's what isn't livable, and how to teach it anyway."

---

## 0. The test

The game teaches by **enactment within a diegesis**: you act on the
world's own state and the world adjudicates truthfully. So a subject is
teachable in-game to the extent its competence can be **enacted, or
faithfully allegorized, inside the fiction without breaking it — and
living it beats reading it.** Where neither holds, the honest move is to
route it out, not to fake it.

## 1. Three teaching modes (not two)

The earlier framing had a false binary ("live it" vs. "read it"). There
are **three**, and the middle one does most of the work for conceptual
subjects:

1. **Direct enactment** — live the **real mechanism** the sim models
   honestly: thermodynamics, Ohm's law, metabolism, materials, ballistics,
   a conserved economy. Full fidelity; gradable by consequence. *"The
   game's own systems ARE curriculum."*
2. **Allegory** — live an **analogous invented situation** that teaches a
   real **pattern, dynamic, or normative lesson** *without importing the
   real referent*. This is the game's **primary mode for social /
   conceptual subjects**, and it's what the world is already built on:
   [arcane-science.md](./arcane-science.md) is an invented science teaching
   real thermodynamics; [compact-political-science.md](./compact-political-science.md)
   is an invented polity teaching real political science. **Allegory is not
   the uncanny thing — literal import is** (§4).
3. **Reference** — the **specific real facts / works / referents**
   (the dates, this novel, the real nerve, the actual statute). study.com's
   88k-lesson library, not the game.

**Allegory is the bridge.** It recovers the *lessons* of referent-bound
subjects that direct enactment can't reach; the *specific facts* stay with
reference.

## 2. What direct enactment can't reach — and which mode rescues it

| Out-of-enactment category | Example | Allegory rescues…? | Facts route to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Referent-bound real content** | history; **real human anatomy/pharmacology** (under nursing/medical); this-novel literature; real law/institutions | ✅ the **pattern / lesson** (how revolutions form, how institutions fail, tragedy/hubris, how a commons collapses) — **not** the particulars | reference (dates, names, the real drug, the real statute) |
| **B. Pure recall / nomenclature** | vocabulary, dates, terminology, citations | ❌ (a pattern isn't a vocab word) | reference + spaced repetition |
| **C. Sensorimotor / embodied** | surgical hands, phlebotomy, violin, welding form | ❌ the motor/haptic layer (the *decision* layer is enactable) | the real regulated practicum |

So the boundary isn't "in vs. out" — it's **which of the three modes each
layer of a subject belongs to.**

## 3. The method / pattern / content split (the escape hatch, refined)

Almost every referent-bound subject peels into three layers, one per mode:

- **Method / skill → direct enactment** (on the fiction's own material):
  source criticism, close reading, clinical judgment, experimental method.
- **Pattern / normative lesson → allegory**: the dynamic, the recurring
  structure, the loaded question — staged as an in-world analog.
- **Specific content → reference**: the facts, the works, the real cases.

*History, fully routed:* the **facts** of the Revolution → reference; the
**historical method** (weigh sources, detect bias, reconstruct events) →
direct enactment on the game's own chronicle / press / rumor / forensics;
the **lessons** (how power shifts, how propaganda works, how a polity
fractures) → allegory, lived through the Fall or a Compact crisis that
*rhymes* with real history. The lived analog + the method make the
reference reading **land harder** — the game motivates what it can't
itself teach.

## 4. Allegory's discipline (where the earlier caution was actually right)

Allegory is first-class, but it fails in three specific ways — and the
guardrails are the same the invented science already holds:

1. **It must be faithful to the real pattern.** A sloppy allegory teaches a
   *wrong* lesson (a caricature of how revolutions or markets work). The
   Compact 200 rails apply verbatim: *never misrepresent a real
   institution; never present a contested question as settled; put the
   strongest case against every design decision.* Allegory needs the same
   fidelity bar as "nothing contradicts real science."
2. **It carries patterns, not particulars.** You cannot smuggle real dates,
   names, or specific facts through an allegory — that's the reference
   layer's job. Trying to makes it brittle and, worse, subtly false.
3. **Avoid the one-to-one costume.** A named real event wearing a fictional
   hat is *literal import in disguise* — **that** is the uncanny the whole
   boundary is protecting against. Good allegory teaches the *dynamic* and
   lets the player draw the parallel; it doesn't map 1:1 onto a specific
   real event.

**Assessment note:** allegory's **lived half** is consequence-gradable
(did you navigate the situation well?); its **transfer half** (did you map
it back to the real world?) needs the reflection / viva layer
([classroom-model](./study-com-classroom-model.md) §5, §7) — an essay or
oral defense, not a computed key.

## 5. The routing rule (for the planning agents)

Per learning objective, pick the mode:

> **enactable mechanism → direct enactment · transferable pattern or
> normative lesson → allegory (faithfully) · specific real facts/works →
> reference (study.com) · sensorimotor → the real practicum.**

And three prohibitions that keep it honest:

- **Don't fake living what can't be lived** (history-as-events, the scalpel).
- **Don't costume a specific real event** as fiction (the uncanny).
- **Don't carry facts by allegory** (patterns only).

## 6. Why this is a feature, not a fence

The boundary is the **clean division of labor** with study.com and the
thing that keeps the honest-simulation thesis credible:

- **Game:** enactable mechanism + faithful allegory + livable method +
  immersive *use* (a language is livable — you use it) + the *motivation*
  to go learn what it can't teach.
- **study.com:** the referent-bound content, the recall, the reading — its
  library is *heavy on exactly these* (history, literature, K-12 academic).

Naming the boundary is what lets the game say, credibly, *"we don't fake
the parts we can't live"* — which is what makes the parts it *does* live
believable. Allegory is how it reaches further than "live the mechanism"
without ever crossing into "costume the real world."
