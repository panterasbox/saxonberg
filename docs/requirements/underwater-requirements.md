# Underwater — the water place — requirements

**Kind:** feature
**Leads from:** kernel (first consumers, in this build: the Wharfside
diving works on the Kestrel, the Duncan Hall cistern, the Hollow Mere
under its ice, the estuary's tide)

Today you can swim on top of water and nothing else: no one is ever below
the surface, so a pool, a mere and the sea are floors you float on. This
build makes water **a place you can be in** — any place may be water, and
deeper than you can stand there is a column of rooms down to the bed — and
makes the body, the medium and the work below the waterline true to the
physics. Its essence, from the slate: ⭐⭐⭐ *holding your breath and
reaching for something, while somebody waits above.* It is build 1 of the
water sequence (the river as a line and river craft follow). Seeded by
[underwater-slate](../slates/builds/underwater-slate.md) (decided
2026-10-18; § 17 fixes this build's contents) with
[river-slate](../slates/builds/river-slate.md) as companion.

## What already exists

- **Swimming at the surface** — `swim` in two pools (Hollow Mere, the
  estuary cove), across swimmable ways, into open water from ice; `stand`
  hauls out. A pool is a place whose floor is the water's surface or ice.
- **Breath** — `inhale` / `exhale` hold and release a fixed grace; a body in
  an unbreathable place blacks out and enters a short rescuable dying window
  (90 seconds, the same in any water, nothing before it but the blackout).
- **A drowning room** — the flooded cistern below Duncan Hall's steps,
  "water" done as the room's air, with a worn **air tank** and a
  **compressor** beside it that keep you alive down there.
- **Cold water** — immersion reads the water's temperature and soaks you.
- **The sea's numbers** — sea state, a band's depth and bottom, `measure
  depth` (a sounding). Nobody is ever at that depth.
- **Fish with habitats** (temperature, current, salinity, oxygen); species
  that breathe water; **a rescuable dying clock** that drowning does not
  use; **glowlight**, a held magical light.
- **Wharfside** — the bank at the confluence, a quay on the Kestrel with the
  city's intake and outfall; the *Hesper* lying off the estuary bar with
  her dinghy.
- **Nothing** called dive, descend, ascend, surface, wade, tide, lifeline,
  bell, helmet or equalize; **no** swimming or diving Discipline; no diving
  trade, gear, or gigs.

**Therefore what is genuinely new here is:** being *below* the surface —
water as a state of any place, the column and its bed; the body's real
breath budget, buoyancy and pressure; water as a medium every other system
respects; the diving gear ladder and the diving trade with its work; the
tide; and aquatic life breathing, sensing and living by the water's
numbers.

## Goals

- **Any place can be water**, by citing a water and a depth; whether it is
  dry, wadeable, swimmable or frozen *now* is derived, and changes with the
  tide and the ice without anything moving.
- **You can go down.** Deeper than standing there is a column of rooms —
  surface, the lit water, the dark, the bed — and a party is together by
  sharing one. You can walk the bed, surface somewhere else, and find
  authored rooms (a sunken works, a wreck) beside the column.
- **Breath is a budget you manage**: how long you last depends on your
  lungs, your conditioning, how hard you work and the cold. You feel the
  urge to breathe; you do **not** feel the oxygen running out, so you can
  black out without warning (hyperventilating makes it worse).
- **Drowning is the most rescuable death there is**: a blackout drifts or
  sinks by depth, then enters the dying clock, which cold lengthens; a
  buddy or a tender who gets you to air saves you; water in the lungs can
  still harm you hours later.
- **Buoyancy and pressure are felt**: what you carry drags you down or
  fights you up; ballast speeds you down and dropping it always works (and
  costs it); descending faster than you can equalize hurts and can burst an
  eardrum.
- **Water is a medium**: no fire or lamp burns in it, nobody's voice
  carries (a merfolk's does), sight fades to the water's clarity and red
  goes first, things get wet, objects cool as in water, the water is a
  source to wash and fill from, you cannot rest mid-water, and weapons
  that swing are useless where thrusts still work. Every refusal is in the
  words of the thing that failed.
- **Fighting underwater is about air**: grappling and holding someone under
  dominate, morale breaks for the surface, a drowning person grabs the
  rescuer, and pulling off a mask or a hose is an attack.
- **Diving pays from three city jobs and a market** — inspect the works,
  recover a body, lift what was lost — and the fishmonger buys a speared
  fish.
- **Diving is a trade** with gear at every rung — stone and belt, the
  lifeline (haul, line signals), the diving bell (trapped air that fouls),
  the hard-hat suit on a crewed pump (the bends, narcosis, dive tables,
  recompression) — a Discipline, and **paid work**: tender and pump crew,
  and the diver for hire on the city's submerged works.
- **Magic works underwater by its own canon**: you cast before you go in
  (a voice is needed), a wand works below, and held workings — breathing
  water, warmth, light — cost a standing bill and lapse when they lapse.
- **The tide comes and goes** at the estuary on the moon's clock, about
  fifty minutes later each day, springs and neaps; the waterline moves,
  tidepools are cut off at the ebb, the strandline brings in what the sea
  carried, and the stream at the mouth runs with the tide and slacks at the
  turn.
- **Swimming is a skill with a cost, and not swimming is how most people
  drown.** Staying up at the surface costs effort, and technique sets the
  cost: a non-swimmer in water over their head thrashes, cannot rest, and
  goes under in about a minute — silently (the instinctive drowning
  response: no voice, no wave) — unless they grab something within reach.
  The swimming Discipline lowers the cost, lets you float on your back to
  rest, and buys distance. Wading where you can stand is for everyone, and
  is where you learn.
- **Getting in and out is real**: you go in from a quay by jumping or by
  its steps; you cannot climb a quay wall from the water — steps, a ladder
  or a slipway are the ways out; an unconscious body can be carried ashore.
- **Cold shock**: the first minute in cold water takes your breath (the
  gasp), which is why cold water drowns people who can swim.
- **Things go where their weight sends them**: something dropped from the
  quay sinks to the bed or floats; a drowned body lies on the bed with what
  it carried, and recovering it is a job. Armour and heavy kit sink you.
- **What still reaches you underwater is what never needed a voice**: the
  implant's messages and emotes (both the aether) work as anywhere; a slate
  and grease pencil let you write; a bell struck below is heard by every
  diver, from no direction.
- **Light follows the day**: a night dive is dark; a held light or a lamp in
  the bell is what you see by.
- **You can fish by diving** — a spear, the band's fish — and sell to the
  fishmonger.
- **What a diver cannot lift, air can**: a sealed barrel or an air bag
  brings a heavy thing up.
- **Tide tables are goods**: an almanac sold at Wharfside tells you the
  tides; without it you read the shore.
- **Seawater does not quench thirst**; drinking it harms.
- **Aquatic life lives by the water**: every creature's breathing is a rate
  by its apparatus and the medium (so a frog can be underwater a while and
  a fish suffocates in foul water); creatures change apparatus with their
  life stage; senses depend on apparatus and medium; water's oxygen is
  derived and can crash; salinity is a habitat tolerance.

## Placement

- **The physics is a system — `/system/water`** (the water pack) plus the
  kernel's seams: water as a state, the column, the medium's properties,
  the tide. True whether or not anyone dives.
- **Diving is a trade — a new `/trade/diving` pack**: the gear, the
  Discipline, the line signals, the jobs. It owns no resource; it is the
  way to reach work and things that are already in the world.
- **Wharfside is the venue** (the terminus pack): the water below the quay
  at the confluence, a diving master who outfits and hires, the city's
  intake and outfall as works that need someone below the waterline.
- **The magic is rows** in the arcane library beside glowlight.
- ⭐ The test: **a second dive site, a second bell, a second tidal coast
  need no code** — a place citing a water and a depth, and rows.

## Surface decisions

### Water is a state, not a kind of room
A place says which water it is and how deep its floor is below that
water's normal level; what it is *now* follows. The pool kind of room
retires; the cistern's "water air" is replaced. *Why:* a tide, a flood, a
thaw and a dive all change what is underfoot without moving anything
(the slate's principle); a kind of room cannot change.

### The column is rooms, and a party shares one
Below the surface the water is a stack of rooms by what the water does
(lit, dark, the bed), not by metre. *Why:* being together in this engine
is sharing a room; depth as a hidden number would need every channel
filtered.

### Ascent is never gated on skill
Only on breath and ballast. Competence decides how much you bring up and
how deep you can equalize, not whether you drown. *Why:* a death-check is
not a job (the slate's lens 1).

### No forced ascent, and two clocks
The engine never surfaces you; you feel the urge, not the oxygen.
*Why:* the panic reflex would be the engine doing the player's job;
shallow-water blackout is the lesson.

### Not swimming drowns you — by cost, not by a check
A non-swimmer is not refused and not rolled against: staying up costs
them so much effort they cannot last, the breath model takes over, and
they can still grab what is within reach. *Why:* lens 1 — the true version
(most drownings are quick, silent, in calm water, and the advice is *reach,
throw, don't go*) teaches something that saves lives, and it keeps the
slate's invariant: ascent is never gated on skill, only on breath, ballast
and cost.

### A disconnected body floats up and holds at the surface
In water, a player who drops connection floats up and is held at the
surface face-up until they return — the same for an offline body the tide
reaches on the strand. Animals and NPCs keep the physics. *Why:* the dying
clock deliberately does not freeze when you disconnect; drowning someone
for their connection is not a death the game should deal. The general rule
for disconnection is later work (non-goals).

### What reaches you underwater
The implant's messages and emotes are the aether and work as anywhere;
speech and spoken casting need a voice and do not; writing works on a
slate; sound from a struck bell carries to every diver with no direction.

### The air tank and `inhale` stay, reshaped
`inhale` fills your breath store before you go under; the shipped tank is
the worn-supply rung. *Why:* continuity — the same commands keep working.

### Wharfside is the diving venue, slack water
The quay at the confluence, its intake and outfall and the quay's own
footings are the works the city needs inspected and cleared — the slate's
*demand is repair of works that exist*. The basin below the quay is slack
until the river build gives the Kestrel a current.

### Magic is in, by canon
A spell needs a voice, so you cast before you go in; a wand works below;
held workings (breathe water, keep warm, light) are a standing bill and
lapse. A held working that lapses at depth is the breath arithmetic's
problem to solve. *Why:* underwater no flame burns, so this is the one
place magic's held bindings have no cheaper rival — real demand.

### The five life findings ship here
Breathing as a rate, apparatus by life stage, senses by medium, oxygen in
water derived, salinity as tolerance. *Why:* the bands are where they bite
first (a frog under the mere; a fish in a cut-off tidepool), and §17 puts
them in this build.

### The water's own properties are not the weather's
How water carries light, sound, heat and breath is a property of the water
and of the medium, not of the outside air. *Why:* the outside air's
weather says nothing true about a metre down.

## Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — Boyle (lungs, the bell's waterline, neutral depth), CO₂
   vs oxygen and shallow-water blackout, the dive reflex, Haldane's tables,
   equalizing, the lunar day and springs/neaps, zonation in a tidepool,
   dissolved oxygen and eutrophication; ⭐ how people actually drown (calm
   water, silent, a minute — *reach, throw, don't go*) and cold shock.
   Readable: depth, water temperature, the tide (by table and by sight).
2. **Expression** — the ordinary case is a row citing a water and a depth;
   the bespoke is authored rooms beside the column (a sunken works, a
   wreck); gear is rows. ⚠ Generated band rooms risk reading the same —
   prose banks with seeded variation are a budgeted line.
3. **Immersion / participation** — the census is this lens: nothing works
   that the water would stop. Seats: tender, pump crew, diver for hire,
   dive master. A town that needs its intake cleared sends for a diver.
4. **Values** — body composition never a number; the tender's attention is
   not measured; sending someone down is recorded on the accountability
   ledger (*you sent him down*), never judged by it.
5. **Continuity** — `descend` / `ascend` / `haul` / `tug` / `drop belt`
   mean the same at every rung; the tank and the suit share one supply
   path; the stop is staying in a band.
6. **Economy** — demand that existed first: the city's works below the
   waterline, recovering what was lost (a body, an anchor) and the
   fishmonger's market for fish; produced: wages for divers,
   tenders and pump crews; consumed: gear, rope, the pump's labour. ⚠ The
   thinnest lens — the harvest (foraging) and decay (decomposition) are
   what will make diving pay broadly.
7. **Governance** — thin, correctly: the diver for hire and the city's
   works are contracts; bed and foreshore ownership is deferred with its
   questions.

## The drive

Run against the live game at the end of the build. A, B, C, D are the
stages.

**A — the water place**
1. At Wharfside, `look` at the water below the quay: it reads as deep
   water you could go into, with the depth in words.
2. `inhale`, then `descend`: you are in the water at the surface; again:
   you are in the column; again: you are on the bed. Each room reads its
   light (dimming), and a second character who followed is in the same
   room.
3. Stay on the bed: the urge to breathe rises in your own voice — then
   `ascend` band by band to air and breathe. Ascent is never refused for
   lack of skill.
4. Hyperventilate (`inhale` repeatedly), descend and stay: you black out
   **with no warning**. A second character `haul`s or carries you up; at
   the surface you come round, coughing; later you feel the water in your
   lungs.
5. Black out alone and stay down: the dying clock runs (longer in the
   cold); a rescuer within it saves you; past it you die.
6. Carry an iron ingot down: you sink fast; `ascend` with it fails for
   weight; `drop belt` (or drop the ingot) and you rise.
7. Descend fast without equalizing: pain, then a burst eardrum (vertigo);
   descending slower with `equalize` does not hurt.
8. At Hollow Mere in winter, go down through a cut hole: the ice is the
   ceiling; the only way up is the hole.
8a. A character with no swimming goes off the quay into deep water: they
    cannot speak, thrash, and in about a minute go under. A second
    character throws them a line from the quay; they grab it and are
    pulled to the steps. A trained swimmer floats on their back and rests.
8b. Climb out: the quay wall refuses; the steps work. Carry an unconscious
    character up the steps.
8c. Jump into the winter mere's hole: the cold takes your breath — the
    gasp costs breath before you have held it.
8d. Drop a sword from the quay: it lies on the bed (seen there by a diver);
    drop a cork float: it stays at the surface. Go down in armour: you sink
    fast and cannot swim up in it.
8e. Disconnect a character on the bed: they float up and are held at the
    surface; reconnect: they are breathing, on the surface.
9. In the Duncan Hall cistern, the same column — and the air tank still
   keeps you breathing.

**B — the medium**
10. Underwater: `say` comes out as nothing anyone hears; a lamp or `light`
    refuses (no fire); `look` sees less and red reads dark; a paper you
    carry is wet; `wash` works from the water; you cannot `sit`.
10a. Underwater, `dm` and an emote reach their recipients as they would
     anywhere; `write` on a slate with a grease pencil works and another
     diver reads it; a bell struck below is heard by every diver in the
     column, from no direction.
10b. Dive at night: the bed is dark; with glowlight held you see.
10c. Drink from the estuary: it is salt, and it harms you.
11. A fight below: a sword barely hurts, a spear thrust does; grappling
    and `subdue` hold someone under; a drowning character grabs you;
    pulling off a diver's helmet is an attack.

**C — `/trade/diving`**
12. At Wharfside the dive master sells a stone and belt, a line, and
    hires; `tug` sends a line signal the tender reads; the tender `haul`s
    the diver up.
13. The bell is lowered from the quay; inside it you breathe, the water
    rises in it with depth, its air fouls with breathing, and a weighted
    barrel refreshes it.
14. The hard-hat suit: dress, boots, helmet; the pump crew cranks; you walk
    the bed. The pump stops: your supply drains, you must be hauled. Come
    up too fast: after surfacing, the bends; the dive tables (bought) give
    the stops; staying in a band is the stop; recompression treats it.
15. The city posts a job — inspect the intake; a diver takes it, goes down
    to the intake's mouth (an authored room beside the column), inspects
    it, comes back and is paid. A tender's wage is paid too.
15a. The city posts a second job — recover a body (a drowned character's,
     on the bed); a diver brings it up, with what it carried, still its
     owner's.
15b. A third — the city's lost anchor on the bed, too heavy to carry: a
     sealed barrel or an air bag lifts it.
15c. Spear a fish in the lit band and sell it to the fishmonger.
15d. Buy the tide almanac at Wharfside; it tells the next low water at the
     estuary; the shore agrees.

**D — tides and life**
16. At the estuary, the waterline is in one place; come back a day later,
    about fifty minutes later, and it is in the same place; at the other
    tide a rock is covered. At low water the tidepool is cut off; its fish
    is short of oxygen, read with `analyze water`.
17. Throw a floating barrel in at the mouth: at a later high water it lies
    on the strandline.
17a. Leave a character offline on the low strand: when the flood reaches
     them they are floated and held at the surface, and anyone at the
     strand is told the water is coming in.
18. At the estuary mouth the stream runs with the tide and is slack at the
    turn.
19. Under the mere a frog stays a while and must come up; a trout in a
    foul, low-oxygen water dies.

**Magic**
20. `cast` a water-breathing working on the quay, go down and stay past
    your breath; at depth it lapses — you have your breath to get up on.
    `cast` underwater does nothing (no voice); a wand works.

## Acceptance criteria

- A player can go from the Wharfside quay to the bed and back, and two
  players who go down together are in the same room at every step.
- Holding breath ends by choice or by a blackout the player is not warned
  of; no one is ever surfaced by the game.
- Every blackout underwater is rescuable within a window that is longer in
  cold water, and a rescued player knows they were nearly lost.
- Carrying weight changes how fast you go down and up; dropping ballast
  always works.
- Descending too fast hurts; equalizing prevents it.
- Under the mere's ice the only way up is the hole.
- No fire burns, no human voice carries, and no swung weapon fights
  properly underwater, and each refusal says why in the words of what
  failed; the water is a source to wash and fill from.
- The dive master at Wharfside outfits a diver; a tender can haul a diver
  up and read line signals; the bell keeps people alive only as long as
  its air; the hard-hat suit keeps a diver alive only as long as the pump
  turns; surfacing too fast in the suit gives the bends after surfacing,
  and the tables prevent it.
- A city job to work below the waterline can be taken, done and paid.
- At the estuary the tide visibly comes in and goes out, about fifty
  minutes later each day; a tidepool is cut off at low water; things
  floating at sea arrive on the strandline.
- A frog can be underwater for a time and must surface; fish die in water
  that has lost its oxygen.
- A water-breathing working lasts while it is held and stops when it
  lapses; casting needs a voice and so does not work underwater, a wand
  does.
- A non-swimmer in water over their head goes under in about a minute
  without a word, unless they reach something; a swimmer can rest
  floating; anyone can wade and learn.
- The way out of the water is steps, a ladder or a slope, never a wall; an
  unconscious body can be carried out.
- Cold water costs breath on entry.
- Dropped things sink or float by what they are; a drowned body and its
  belongings can be recovered and stay its owner's.
- Underwater, implant messages and emotes work, speech does not, writing on
  a slate does, and a struck bell is heard by every diver.
- A dive at night is dark without a light of your own.
- A diver can spear a fish and sell it; air can lift what a diver cannot.
- The tide almanac is bought and tells the truth about the tide.
- Seawater harms the drinker.
- A disconnected character in water — or on a strand the tide reaches — is
  held safe at the surface until they return.
- The existing pool, cistern, estuary and storage-and-ice behaviours still
  work.
