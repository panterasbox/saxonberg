## Model — the four modes of deliberation (2×2)

From `cooperative-slate.md` § Deliberation. Two axes — **sync / async** ×
**structured / unstructured**:

| | unstructured | structured |
|---|---|---|
| **async** | **social forums** (reddit-style, popularity-OK) | **the argument-map** (claims/objections/rebuttals, no ranking) |
| **sync** | **live chat** | **the live floor** (rules of order, automated floor-bot, debate formats, gallery) |

- **Argument-map** (built): spine = proposal; typed relations **support /
  object / question**; reputation-blind (no voting, no scores, no ranking);
  store/lens split; **open-objection** flag; lay anchor = the **voter guide**.
- **Live floor** (sync structured): floor-bot rations the floor (speaking
  queue, time-limits); competitive-debate formats (opening→rebuttal→cross-ex→
  closing); role-gated (speakers on the floor, audience in a **gallery**);
  bounded/small-group (town-halls, panels, delegate debates). **It feeds the
  argument-map; it doesn't decide.** Governance-as-spectacle.
- **Ungameable principle:** what organizes a *real decision* must be structural
  or chronological — never user-signal ranking (any ranking decays to
  popularity). Casual rooms can be popularity-driven; nothing's at stake there.
- **Flow:** chatter (forums) → poll (advisory, never decides) → deliberate
  (argument-map) → converge → vote. **Live surfaces; async decides.**

## Narration (draft)

**[Beat 1 — the four rooms]**
"I've been talking like there's one place you argue. There isn't — there are
four, sorted by two questions: is it *live* or not, and is it *loose* or
*structured*? Most of the time you're in the loose rooms — real-time **chat**,
threaded **forums** — and they're exactly what they sound like: where ideas
start, where people riff and pile on and crack jokes. Popularity's fine there;
nothing's at stake. But the moment something has to actually be **decided**, it
moves into the two *structured* rooms — and those run on one rule: **what
organizes a real decision has to be ungameable — the shape of the argument, or
the order things happened, never who's loudest.**"

**[Beat 2 — the argument-map = the voter guide, made living]**
"The workhorse is async, and you've already seen its model — it shows up in your
mailbox. Every ballot measure comes with a **voter guide**: the proposal, the
case for, the case against, the rebuttals — the argument laid out by its
*structure*, not by who shouted loudest, and arriving *separately* from the
ballot. All we did was take that guide and make it **living**: instead of a
static pamphlet a few officials wrote, it's a map the whole community builds — a
proposal as the **spine**, and everything anyone adds hanging off it as a
**support**, an **objection**, or a **question**. Organized by the logic of the
argument, not by who's popular or what's newest. No upvotes to climb, nothing to
rank. **It's the ballot pamphlet — except it's alive, and everyone holds the
pen.**"

**[Beat 3 — reputation-blind]**
"And just like that pamphlet — where you read the case for and against without
knowing or caring who wrote them — the map doesn't know who you are. A good
objection from a nobody sits in exactly the same place as one from the biggest
name in the room. The argument stands on its **structure** — what holds up,
what's answered, what isn't — never on whose name is attached. **The crowd
doesn't decide what's true here. The argument does.**"

**[Beat 4 — dissent can't be buried]**
"And here's my favorite part. In a normal forum, an inconvenient objection gets
downvoted into the basement and vanishes. Here there's no voting to bury it with
— so an unanswered objection just *sits there*, flagged, in the open, until
someone actually answers it. **You don't get past a hard question by burying it.
You get past it by answering it.** Dissent isn't noise to silence; it's a
permanent part of the map."

**[Beat 5 — the live floor]**
"The other structured room is live — and honestly it's the fun one. Picture a
real-time debate with actual **rules of order**: a bot runs the floor, hands out
speaking turns, keeps time, and the format's lifted straight from competitive
debate — opening, rebuttal, cross-examination, closing. A few people debate on
the floor; everyone else watches and reacts from the **gallery**. It's a
spectacle — genuinely *watchable* governance. But here's the catch that keeps it
safe: **the live floor doesn't decide anything.** A live room is exactly where
the loudest voice and the quickest mob win — so nothing binding happens there.
What the debate *does* is surface claims and objections, which get captured into
the argument-map, the durable record. The show is live; the decision stays slow,
structured, and async. **Live surfaces; async decides.**"

**[Beat 6 — the honest frontier (scale / AI)]**
"Now — the honest hard part is *scale*. A few dozen voices, the map works; ten
thousand, and it drowns in near-identical claims. This is the one place I'll
point at AI — not as a wave of the hand, but because it's a specific job that
only became doable recently: a model can read thousands of claims, find the
fifty that say the same thing, and **propose** a merge. But it only proposes — a
person confirms, and it only ever touches the *view*, never the record. **AI as
a librarian, never a judge.** That's the live frontier, and it's the thing I
most want people to come argue about."

**[Beat 7 — land + hand forward]**
"So: chat where it's casual, argue where it counts — and whichever room you're
in, the rule holds. **Contribute as equals; decide by weight. Match the mode to
the moment.** But all of it — the talk, the map, the floor, the vote — is just
words and tallies until something actually *happens*. Who turns what the
community decided into the rules of the world? Who builds it? Who enforces it?
That's next."

**Kickers:** *"the ballot pamphlet — except it's alive, and everyone holds the
pen"*; *"the crowd doesn't decide what's true here, the argument does"*; *"you
don't get past a hard question by burying it — you get past it by answering
it"*; *"live surfaces; async decides"*; *"AI as a librarian, never a judge"*;
the creed *"contribute as equals; decide by weight."*
