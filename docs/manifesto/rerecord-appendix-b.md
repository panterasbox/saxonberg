## Narration (draft — descriptive)

**[0 — one scarcity]**
This world has one scarce resource: compute — the cost of running it on a real machine.
Everything here that looks like property, territory, or ownership is, underneath, a way
of dividing and governing that one scarcity. What follows is how that works, and where it
meets the political system.

**[1 — the land: what is held]**
The world is a namespace — source, templates, data — divided into parcels, the way a map
is divided into lots. A parcel is a titled section of the namespace. Each parcel carries
three things: a **title** (who holds it), a **compute allowance** (what it costs to run),
and a **publish state** (whether it is live in the shared world, or isolated in a private
sandbox that cannot affect anything outside itself).

Land divides into public and private. The **public** land is the kernel — the engine
every world runs on, held in common. The **private** land is the estates — authored
content, held by its makers. A private estate runs on the public code and cannot override
it; it sets its own rules only within the limits the kernel defines. That arrangement — a
shared substrate binding every holding, and holdings that govern themselves above it — is
the federal structure, expressed as public code beneath private code.

Holding land does not mean walling it off. Oversight reaches everywhere; anything can be
inspected. What a title grants is not secrecy but security: a holding cannot be taken
without a court. The protection is due process, not exclusion.

**[2 — compute: how the scarcity is rationed]**
The namespace is effectively unlimited — another parcel can always be added — so the land
is not the scarce thing. Compute is. It attaches to every parcel, published or not,
because anything that runs consumes it.

Compute is managed at three levels. The **total** is the size of the machine, paid for
with real money and changed rarely; more funding buys a larger machine, which enlarges
the resource for everyone rather than for any single holder. The **division** of that
total separates two things usually confused: a title and a compute allowance. The title
is permanent. The allowance is derived and continuous — a function of the quality of what
a parcel holds, the engagement it draws, and whether it is active. A well-used parcel's
allowance rises on its own; a neglected one's falls, and the parcel dims toward dormancy
without losing its title. No holding is resized by decree; it runs at the level it earns.
Where a holder must reduce load, the system fixes the budget and the holder chooses what
to change — the allocation is set centrally, the editing is not.

Because the resource is finite, it is sometimes exhausted: a busy location reaches
capacity and admits no one further until it clears. This is not treated as a fault. A
resource that is never scarce carries no weight and requires no management. The single
constraint is legibility — the occupancy, the earned budget, and the allocation formula
are all visible, and demand feeds the formula, so a queue is a market signal rather than
an unexplained refusal.

Allocation is delegated, not central. The commons assigns a **block** to a top-level
holder; that holder subdivides and allocates within the block by its own policy; holders
beneath do the same. The central formula operates only at the boundary between the commons
and a top-level holder — below that boundary, internal division is the holder's own
affair. A holding managed by a group is governed however that group chooses, across the
same range from single authority to full vote that governs the whole polity, applied
recursively: a block within a block.

**[3 — the license: ownership in law]**
Ownership here is also copyright, and it follows the same public/private division. The
**public** code is open — readable by anyone, with improvements returning to the common
pool. This openness is what keeps the record auditable and the system forkable: the code
is guaranteed. **Private** inventions built on the public code — new mechanics, whole
game-layers — may be licensed and held. One condition is fixed: anything that runs is
readable, whether or not it may be copied. Inspection is universal; reuse is the author's
to grant.

The public/private division appears in each layer — the land, the compute, and the law
over both — as one arrangement described three ways.

**[4 — what the political system fixes]**
Almost none of this is constitutionally fixed. The allocation formula, the rules for
holding and acquiring land, whether private estates are permitted, the size of the
machine — each is set by the community and can be set differently. Even the protections
that appear foundational — the separation of money from power, the security of a holding —
are entrenched by choice, not required for the system to run. What the system cannot run
without is small: a way to reach decisions, a way to record who did what, and enough
underlying stability to keep the machine standing. The rest is chosen. The constitution
does not prescribe the arrangement of the land; it secures the machinery through which a
community arranges its own.

## Visual cue sheet

Corner / no-face throughout. Diagrams are *explanatory*, not heroic — labeled schematics
of how the pieces fit, in the flat register of the narration.

- **0 — corner.** One labeled meter: **compute — the one scarce resource.** Everything
  else in the appendix hangs off it.
- **1 — no-face.** A **namespace divided into parcels** (lots over source/template/
  document); one parcel labeled with its three fields — **title / compute allowance /
  publish-state.** Then **public (kernel) vs. private (estate)** shading, a private estate
  drawn resting on the public slab beneath it. Small chip: **inspect ✓ / no seizure
  without a court** (due process, not walls).
- **2 — no-face.** The **three levels**: the machine (enlarged by real money, for
  everyone); the **title/allowance split** (title fixed; an allowance that rises with
  quality+demand+activity and dims to dormancy when neglected, title intact); a **legible
  door** (occupancy / earned budget / visible formula). Then **blocks delegated down the
  parcel tree**, each holder subdividing its own block; the governance range redrawn at
  each level.
- **3 — no-face.** The **license layer** over the same map: the public slab marked
  **open** (readable, forkable), private holdings marked **licensed** (readable ✓,
  copyable per author). The public/private line aligned across the three redrawings.
- **4 — corner.** The whole diagram shaded **"set by the community,"** with a small core
  left lit — **decide · attribute · stay standing** — marked "what the system can't run
  without."
