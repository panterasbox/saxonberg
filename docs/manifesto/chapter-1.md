# Chapter 1 — "Three kinds of say, none supreme"

> **First-draft narration + visual cue sheet.** Voice: personal / witnessed,
> shifting to "you / your community" + operator-aware. Closes the doubt Ch 0
> hands forward; opens Ch 2 ("what is influence, and what stops one person
> owning a whole count?"). This file is canonical for Ch 1 and supersedes the
> Ch 1 entry in `constitution-video.md` (which still uses the old
> "kinds-of-contributor / houses" phrasing). Opening is provisional by process.

## Framing (governs this chapter and the whole video)

**No grievance.** The audience isn't aggrieved. The frame is positive and
**operator-aimed**: self-organization is an **inevitability** at scale (one
person can't make every decision — a full-time job, then a job too big for
anyone). Communities already self-organize ad-hoc; this is **real
infrastructure for what already happens**, and it's **better for everyone — the
operator most of all.** The streamer is the **customer, not the villain.** Old
failure modes (capture, drama) = **the limits of duct tape at scale**, not
wrongs. **Assert, don't defend.**

**The model correction that drives this chapter:** makers / funders / players
are **NOT three groups of people.** They are **three dimensions of every single
person** — everyone makes, funds, and plays, in different proportions. Each kind
of contribution earns its own non-convertible kind of say. A decision is tallied
three ways across the *same* crowd; it passes on **two of three**. (Drop the
word "house" in the video — it makes people picture three separate groups. Use
"three kinds of say / three counts." The formal "chambers" can be named in Ch 4.)

## Narration (draft)

**[Beat 1 — scale forces self-organization]**
"Here's what happens to every online community that gets big enough: it starts
generating more decisions than any one person can handle. Moderation, events,
rules, disputes, where the money goes. Running it stops being a hobby and
becomes a full-time job — and eventually a job too big for one person at all.
So communities do what they always do: they self-organize. Mods, volunteers,
fan projects, unwritten rules. It already happens, everywhere, held together
with duct tape. That's not a flaw to stamp out — it's the healthiest thing a
community does, and it takes load off whoever's in charge. So my question was
never 'should a community have a say' — it already does. It's: what if you gave
that self-organizing real infrastructure, instead of duct tape? An actual
apparatus for the community's decisions. First question about it — who gets a
voice?"

**[Beat 2 — three dimensions of each person]**
"And here's the part that's easy to get wrong: these aren't three groups of
people. They're three things almost everyone does. You make stuff — a room, a
bit, a guide. You chip in to keep the lights on. You show up and play. Make,
fund, play — three ways the same person gives to the place. And each one earns
its own kind of say."

**[Beat 3 — three counts of the same crowd]**
"So the community doesn't decide by counting heads. It counts three ways at
once: what do people want as makers — weighted by how much they've actually
made — as funders, as players? Three readings of the same crowd. To pass, you
win two of the three. So the biggest funder can own the funders' count and
still lose, because money buys nothing in the other two. No single way of
contributing carries a decision alone. That constraint is the whole design."

**[Beat 4 — money: what it actually buys (compute + a voice), never power]**
"And these three kinds of say don't convert into each other — which is where
money comes in. Money's welcome; it's how the lights stay on. And it buys exactly
two things, neither of them power. **One: compute** — the only thing that's
actually scarce in a digital world, where everything else copies for free — so a
dollar grows the world's compute for *everyone*, more room for the place to
exist. **Two: a voice** in the funders' count. That's the whole list — not a
maker's say, not a player's say, not power in the world itself. Funding the world
and running it are two different things, with a wall between them — no token,
nothing to cash out. It's the obvious trap, the one crypto walked straight into,
so I closed that door and moved on. **Money can make you the loudest funder in
the room, and buy the whole world more room to exist. It still can't make you a
maker, a player, or a winner.**"

**[Beat 5 — hand forward, with curiosity]**
"So: an apparatus for the community's decisions, three kinds of say, and money
can't cross between them. But I've been saying 'a voice,' 'weight,' 'influence'
without telling you what those actually *are*. Where does influence come from,
what's it made of — and what stops the most relentless or most popular person
from piling up one kind of it until they personally own that whole count?
That's the next chapter."

Hands Ch 2 the curiosity-hinge: *what influence is, and what stops one person
owning a single count* (the real worry — capture, not the rich).

## Visual cue sheet — gauges, not geography

The image is **"same crowd, measured three ways,"** never "three rooms."
**Framing:** corner throughout (middle chapter — no full-screen); drop to
**no-face** during the hero (Beat 3) and firewall (Beat 4) builds.

- **Beat 1 — corner.** On "duct tape," a literal duct-tape motif holding a
  mess together; on "real infrastructure," it resolves into a clean labeled
  container, *"how the community decides."*
- **Beat 2 — one person, three meters.** A single person-icon with three little
  gauges beside them — **make / fund / play** — filled to different heights.
  Establishes that everyone carries all three.
- **Beat 3 — the hero diagram.** Zoom to a crowd; everyone carries their three
  meters. The meters **sum into three big tallies** (makers / funders / players)
  with a pass-line, and a decision has to clear **2 of 3.** Build: crowd of
  three-meter people → three aggregate bars → the 2-of-3 line.
- **Beat 4 — the firewall.** A **whale**: funder meter maxed, maker/player near
  zero — towers over the funder tally but barely moves the other two, so it
  can't clear 2 of 3 alone. Money flows only into the funder meter; a wall
  stops it reaching the others or "advantage." Firewall recurs as a motif in
  Ch 6 (the floor).
- **Beat 5 — corner**, for the hand-off. Teaser inset: one figure running a single
  meter sky-high (the "owns one whole count" worry). Back to the corner on "that's
  the next chapter."

Motifs planted: **duct tape → infrastructure**, the **three meters/gauges**,
the **firewall.** Diagram changes ~every 10–15s; client never shown.

## Notes

- **Examples foreshadow later chapters** (deliberately): ban/feud → judiciary
  (Ch 4); rule change → the decision body; where-the-money-goes → treasury
  (Ch 6). Worth calling back later. Pick the 2–3 examples that are genuinely yours.
- **Terminology:** "apparatus / the body that makes the community's decisions";
  **"three kinds of say / three counts"** instead of "houses." **"Legislature"
  and the formal "three chambers" deferred to Ch 4**, which names them and
  retroactively labels this thing.
- **Naming:** casual **makers / funders / players** in the video; formal
  **Producer / Patron / Consumer** stays in the constitution. "Patron" is
  load-bearing for the brand — swap to "makers / patrons / players" if wanted.
- **Autocrat → Ch 6.** Scale is the *why* behind the dial: keep whatever stake
  you want and **defer selectively** (abstain) — not all-or-nothing. Optional
  one-line reassurance earlier so operator-viewers don't bounce.
