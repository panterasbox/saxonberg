## Model — the three branches as the lifecycle of a decision

- **Core tenet:** whatever can be enforced by **code**, is (automatic, uniform,
  everyone); **everything else is human** — the *bigger* half (judgment, running
  the place, crises). "Code where it can; people everywhere else." (Art. I §7,
  Art. V §9.)
- **Legislature = requirements**, not code — the *what* and *why*, at **any
  granularity** (broad principle → narrow spec → an exact invariant it may hand
  down). (Art. IV §5.)
- **Executive = the administration** — the people who run the place; mostly
  *human* work; building code is one specialty; the PM leads the administration,
  coder or not. **Functional, never territorial.** (Art. V.)
- **The property limit (federalism):** you can *build* (the maker stake made
  real — sandbox / homestead / claim); what you build is **property that binds
  even the executive** (oversee, never seize, except by due process). *Property,
  not politics.* [Source: build-1 **uncommitted** — `cooperative-slate.md` §"How
  territory is held"; `polity-decision-register.md` Tier-1.]
- **Judiciary = checks both:** *verification* (does the build meet the law? —
  code review + testing + investigation, scaled to the requirement, and
  *deliberated*) + *appeal* (was a human call fair?). Drawn **by lot** (sortition;
  competence-gated for code). **Diagnostic, not prescriptive** — says *where* it
  misses, never the fix; the remand loop is the argument-map's open-objections.
  (Art. VI.)

## Narration (draft)

**[Beat 1 — a decision must become real]**
"So the community decided. Now what? A decision is just words until something
changes — the rule has to start *running*. And here's where most governance
quietly dies: the law passes, then sits in a document while whoever's in charge
applies it however they feel. So how does a decision here become real?

**[Beat 2 — code what you can, human the rest]**
It comes down to one rule, and it's the heart of the executive branch: **whatever
can be enforced by code, is — and then the code does it for you, automatically,
the same for everyone, every time. Everything else stays human.** And that
'everything else' is the *bigger* half: every judgment a rule can't make in
advance, the day-to-day of running a community, the crises, the hard calls. Code
handles the mechanical; people handle everything else.

**[Beat 3 — the lifecycle + the legislature]**
That *codeable* half — turning a decision into running rules — works exactly like
building software, and that's where the three branches come from. Someone says
what it should do, someone builds it, someone checks the build. **Decide, build,
check.** The **legislature** decides — that's the body from chapter one, the same
crowd weighed three ways as makers, funders, and players, a measure carrying on
two of the three. And notice *what* it writes: **requirements**, not code. The *what* and the *why*, never the *how*. And a requirement can be any
size: as broad as *'people get free expression,'* as narrow as *'nerf that NPC by
ten units'* — the legislature can even hand down an exact rule when it wants.
Whatever the size, the *how* is somebody else's job.

**[Beat 4 — the executive is the administration]**
That somebody is the **executive** — the administration: the people who run the
place. Most of what they do is human — the judgment code can't make, the
day-to-day, the crises, the calls no rule anticipated — bounded by the law, in
the open, always reviewable. The parts that *can* be code, they build and ship;
that's one specialty on the team. The Prime Minister leads the whole
administration, coder or not.

**[Beat 5 — you can build here, and what you build is yours]**
And remember the three stakes — make, fund, play? **'Make' is real here: you
don't just *play* in this world, you *build* it** — author content, ship code,
make the place yourself. You start with a **sandbox of your own**, and when you
make something worth more room, you **claim** it — homestead new ground, even
your own managed corner of the world. And because you *built* it, it's **yours**
— not a privilege the admins hand out and take back, but **property.** Which is
why the executive runs by **function, not territory**: no fiefdoms, no governor
of your zone. It can look in on what you've made, but it can't **seize** it
without due process, in the open. **Property, not politics — held, not governed.**
And if you'd rather just play and never build a thing? None of this touches you —
it's a maker's concern.

**[Beat 6 — the judiciary checks both]**
Which is why the third branch — the **judiciary** — has two faces. The first:
when the executive ships a change, a panel **verifies it actually does what the
law asked.** For a precise rule, that's a quick test. For a broad one, it's real
work — reading the code, running it, sometimes real investigation into whether
the build delivers what was promised. Think **code review, but for whether the
*law* came true** — and it's *deliberated*, argued in the open like any proposal.
The second face is the **appeal**: when the executive made a *human* call, was it
fair? Same panel, drawn **by lot** — ordinary members for the fairness calls,
people who can read code for the technical ones. And when a build falls short,
the court doesn't write the fix — it says exactly *where* it misses, and the
executive decides how to close the gap. **Court judges, executive builds; neither
does the other's job.** The folks who check the work are just the folks.

**[Beat 7 — synthesis + hand forward]**
So: **the legislature decides, the executive makes it real — coding what it can,
judging the rest — and the people check all of it.** ...But notice who's holding
a lot of power there. The executive *writes* the code and *runs* the machine. So
what stops them shipping something the law never said, cooking the vote count, or
burying a verdict? How would you even *know*? That's next."

**Kickers:** *"code handles the mechanical; people handle everything else"*; *"a
law can be as broad as free expression or as narrow as nerf that NPC by ten"*;
*"property, not politics — held, not governed"*; *"the people who run the world
can't just take your corner of it"*; *"code review, but for whether the law came
true"*; *"court judges, executive builds; neither does the other's job"*; the
synthesis *"the legislature decides, the executive makes it real, the people check
all of it."*
