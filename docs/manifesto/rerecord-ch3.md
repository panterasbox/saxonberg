## Narration (draft)

**[1 — the third force finally gets a seat]**
Every economy runs on three forces: labor, capital, and the consumer — the people
who make a thing, the people who fund it, and the people it's ultimately *for*. Two
of the three have always had a way to organize. Labor built one — unions. Capital was
born with one — boards, shareholders. But the consumer, the party the whole thing
exists to serve, has never had a seat. Not because no one wanted to give them one —
because there was no way to: you can't seat a hundred million people who've never met.
All the consumer ever had was *exit* — buy it, or don't. Never *voice*. The internet
gave them something, but it was a mob: outrage, review-bombs, pile-ons — noise, not a
seat.

What changed is the technology underneath. Real-time, two-way communication — what the
internet actually is — is the first thing that can gather what consumers think, at
scale, continuously, and turn it into a standing voice instead of a hundred million
private decisions. For the first time, the third force can take a seat. And it breaks a
very old deadlock: political economy has always been a two-body problem — labor versus
capital, a tug-of-war with no one to break the tie. Seat the consumer,
co-equal, and the deadlock ends; the tie falls toward the people the thing is *for*.

But three forces at one table are in permanent tension — what's good for one is often
paid for by another — and nearly every economy resolves that tension the same way:
one force captures the other two, capital buying out the makers and treating the
players as inventory. This government is built so that capture can't happen.

**[2 — co-equal, two of three]**
Each force gets its own count — its own chamber — and the three are co-equal: none
can outvote the other two. A decision needs a majority of chambers, two of three.
So makers can't win without players, players can't win without makers, and no one
wins alone. And the part that's easy to picture wrong: these aren't three tribes.
Everyone makes, funds, and plays, in different proportions — a chamber isn't a room
full of different people, it's the same crowd measured by what they contribute. One
decision, read three ways, passing only when two of the readings agree.

**[3 — money, and where it stops]**
Money is the case everyone watches, so here's the exact line. Capital earns a real voice —
it's how the lights stay on — but the counts are separate, so it stops at its own. Money
can make you the loudest funder in the room; it can't make you a maker, a player, or a
winner. That's a narrow claim, not a grand one: nothing stops you investing or profiting —
money just moves the funders' count and stops there.

**[4 — standing: a product of quantity and quality, given by others]**
So what is a "voice" actually made of? It's called standing — and standing is a
*product* of two things: how much you contribute, and how much others actually
value it. Quantity and quality. And because it's a product, neither counts without
the other — zero of either, and the whole thing is zero. Grind out a mountain of
activity nobody cares about, all quantity and no quality, and it comes to nothing.
Earn a reputation and then stop showing up, all quality and no quantity, and it
comes to nothing too. And what "value" means depends on how you contributed: as a
maker, it's how much others engage with what you *built* — the worth of your work.
As a player, it's how much the community values *you* — your standing among them.
Either way, the quality is conferred by other people, which is why you can't
manufacture it: a thousand fake accounts have nothing real to give. Others confer it,
or it doesn't exist.

**[5 — conviction]**
And standing isn't a pile of chips you own — you can't trade it, cash it out, or spend
it down. Instead you use it as *conviction* — and this part isn't new. It's an
established method, **conviction voting**, from the world of digital governance: you put
your standing behind a position, and the longer you hold it there, the more it weighs.
Patience counts; a last-second flip resets to nothing, so nobody ambushes a vote at the
buzzer. And you're never rationed — full weight on every question. If you can't follow
every issue, you can hand your standing to someone you trust to carry — per topic,
revocable the instant that trust runs out; that's **liquid delegation**, also borrowed,
not invented. You spend it without ever using it up. Earned by contributing, given by
others so you can't fake it, spent as patience instead of hoarded — earned and spent,
never owned.

**[close]**
So no one force can rule the others, and the currency of power can't be bought,
minted, or owned — only earned, and only given.

That's who decides, and what they decide with. It leaves the other half — how a crowd
actually argues its way to a decision, without the loudest voice running the room.
That's next.
