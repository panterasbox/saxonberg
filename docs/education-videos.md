# Education-vertical videos

The education-facing track of the video work: videos that show the
game — and show where an adaptive learning platform's content plugs
into it — to people who think about education products. The sibling
of [game-intro-videos.md](./game-intro-videos.md) (gamer-facing), with
the same settled tone rules and a different audience.

## The organizing principle: each video is an experience

**The game is fun. If a video doesn't come off as fun, nothing else
in it lands** — no completion story, no integration story, no moat
story. Fun is not the garnish on the business case; it is the
carrier. A viewer who doesn't feel "I want to do that" will not be
persuaded by anything said afterward.

So the unit of a video is not a feature, a subsystem, or a value
proposition — it is **an experience the player has**, in Schell's
sense ([lenses/essential-experience.md](./lenses/essential-experience.md):
decide the felt experience first; mechanics, story, and world are
only the means). Each video:

1. **Names the experience** in a sentence — the felt thing, not the
   mechanic.
2. **Shows it happening** — real play, real figures, the
   reference-doc register doing what it does best.
3. **Lets the essence surface** — every video instantiates the two
   ratified essence sentences (platform: *real effort, recognized —
   your doing is seen, what you become is earned, what you make
   persists, you do it among others who remember you, and the world
   itself honors what you understand*; game: *learning as
   adventure*). Never recited; felt.
4. **Derives the exec read last** — engagement, personalization,
   marketing, completion: whatever a study.com executive can
   leverage is a *reading of the experience*, stated briefly at the
   end, in their vocabulary. The value proposition is embodied by
   the experience, not argued alongside it.

The design lenses ([docs/lenses/](./lenses/README.md)) are the
analysis toolkit: each video's brief names the two or three lenses
that govern its experience, and the script is checked against them.
[Transformation](./lenses/transformation.md) applies to *every* video
in this track — both essences are recognition-of-effort, so "is this
good for the learner" is a property of the experience itself (this is
the high-stakes guardrail from the strategy doc, lens-shaped).

## What these videos are — and are not

They **are** a pitch for the game: a fun new text-based RPG with
honest systems, presented in the reference-doc register, speaking to
our specific strengths in the marketplace.

They **are not** a partnership proposal. Nothing in them touches
content licensing, product integration commitments, revenue, or any
negotiated-agreement shape. The videos describe experiences the game
delivers and a seam those experiences expose. What anyone does with
that seam is a conversation the videos deliberately do not start.
(The deal thinking lives in
[study-com-strategy.md](./study-com-strategy.md), internal.)

Corollary rules:

- **Name study.com when a specific product earns it; describe the
  seam generically otherwise.** The seam is vertical-agnostic — "an
  adaptive learning platform with a subject taxonomy and verified
  assessment" — but when a video refers to a concrete product
  (College Saver's credit-recommended courses, teacher-cert prep),
  naming it plainly beats coy circumlocution. What stays out is deal
  shape, not the name.
- **Do speak specifically to the strengths of their products in the
  ed-tech market** — deep subject taxonomy (subject → course →
  chapter → lesson), self-paced completion, credit-recommended
  (ACE/NCCRS) assessment with identity verification. These are the
  properties the seam consumes; naming them precisely is describing
  our own design.
- **Describe design, not build-state, inaccurately never.** The
  advancement/chronicle substrate is shipped; the external-issuer
  adapter is designed, not built. Say exactly that.
- **No assumed gamer literacy.** The decision audience may not game.
  Show the loop on screen; translate to their metrics at the end;
  zero game-culture jargon.

## Register

Inherited whole from the game-intro series: reference-doc tone,
real figures, assert-don't-defend, philosophy as the recurring "why"
bolted to what's on screen, no overclaimed novelty. The
differentiator is honesty depth — the game's physics runs on the
real models the coursework teaches, so knowledge is load-bearing
rather than decorative.

## The seam (context all videos share)

The game stands alone as an educational game — campus, majors, its
own subject taxonomy (the internal/external split ratified in
[lenses/essential-experience.md](./lenses/essential-experience.md)).
An external learning platform is the optional enrichment that turns
the standalone game into a vertical product:

- Its taxonomy aligns against the Discipline catalog's typed edges.
- Coursework completion lands as **claim**-provenance transcript
  evidence — knowledge, learning-zone access.
- Verified assessment lands as **deed**-provenance evidence — the
  rung that raises the ceiling and confers capability. Assessment
  provenance is **graded, not assumed**: a credential carries its
  conditions (proctored / identity-verified open-book / self-report)
  and deed-weight follows — one schema for any issuer.
- The platform is an **issuer**, nothing more. Assessment authority
  stays with the assessor; the game consumes credentials, never
  grants them. The floor stays fully playable on in-game learning;
  external mastery raises the ceiling only.

## The roster

Rules (settled 2026-07-28): **no committed count** — a video exists
when there's meaningful content for it; **under five minutes** each;
**each video is one distinct experience** — overlap is allowed (and
necessary) only for the core-principle slices that recur; content is
picked with the lens work weighing in (the load-bearing ed-tech
lenses per video are named). Series *order* is an interest-curve
decision made later, separate from this list's grouping. Status
honesty per the standing rule: built or fully-designed both qualify;
anything leaning on unbuilt systems is marked HELD.

### Spine (the four, each framed against one of the four execs)

The framing device (settled 2026-07-28): each spine video is
written *to one person's desk* — the frame governs tone, emphasis,
and the closing exec read; the experience stays primary and the
video still works for a general viewer. One roster change fell out

operations president, so **A world that runs** enters the spine and
**Arrival** folds its first-hour personalization beats into
*Playing the part*'s opening act (Arrival survives as a general/
gamer-adjacent cut if it earns a slot later).

1. **The conferral** — framed for **Laura Dansbury (head of
   product)**: she owns plugging the game into the existing
   applications, so this is the *integration story told as a user
   journey.* The wall honestly hit → real study on the real
   platform → the verified final → the return; the conferral fires
   and the knowledge is load-bearing (real Ohm's law). *Tone:*
   product walkthrough — show the seam as a product surface (where
   her catalog appears in-world, what event crosses, what state
   changes), zero architecture talk. *Lenses:* fantasy, curiosity
   (Rico), essential-experience, transformation. *Exec read:*
   completion felt from inside; the whole integration is consuming

2. **Playing the part** — framed for **Chris Mancini (chief growth
   officer)**: personalization and marketing applications. Act 1

   made *in fantasy* (who you are, what you study, how your room

   carries modeled weight. Acts 2–3: months in the role — bands
   move only on evidence, traits accrete from behavior, deeds and
   reputation follow — until **the character is honest evidence
   about the human**: properly aligned, the in-fantasy choices
   reflect the real person's aspiration, diligence, and
   demonstrated competence. *Tone:* the marketer's demo —
   personas, funnels, the six cohort doors into one world; role
   play as engagement no campaign can buy and the aspirational-

   **Ready.**
3. **The long transcript** — framed for **Michael Shmeir
   (president & COO)**: the whole business, told as one customer's
   three years. He oversees every function (product reports to
   him), so his video is not a specialty cut — it's the one that
   shows the entire proposition working *end to end, over time*:
   she arrives a nobody in year one (a subscriber like any other);
   puts down roots (the room, the people, the evening job); the
   studying keeps mattering because her character keeps needing it;
   her real degree posts in year three and the world's conferral
   fires the same week — both transcripts saying the same thing —
   and she logs in that night anyway, because her people are there.
   *Tone:* longitudinal and calm — a customer-lifetime story shown,
   not charted; real time spans, no hype; every function's win
   visible inside one arc (the product seam fires, the identity
   compounds, the mission lands). *Closing read:* the unit
   economics of a *member* versus a *user* — completion up, the
   subscription outliving the goal by years, acquisition arriving
   through community — the company's whole dashboard, embodied in
   one person who stayed. *Lenses:* essential-experience (both
   essences in one arc), interest-curve (the career tier),
   endogenous-value, transformation. **Ready** (the wishbook's
   closing vignette, promoted).
4. **The honest world** — framed for **Adrian Ridner (founder &
   CEO)**: broad strategy and ed-tech positioning. Lifting the
   curtain, elevated to the category thesis: one fountain twice

   the widening: every species real taxonomy, every material real
   mechanics, knowledge load-bearing everywhere, and therefore a
   position no content company can copy — **content can be
   commoditized; an honest world cannot.** *Tone:* thesis-level,
   calm, assert-don't-defend — the closest any video comes to the
   manifesto register, still bolted to concrete demonstrations.
   *Lenses:* transparency, curiosity, infinite-inspiration (copy
   the moves, not the inspiration), resonance (this video likely
   carries the ratified theme's clearest statement). *Exec read:*
   the moat in an LLM era, and the boundless-reach hint — this
   model generalizes to any sufficiently-digitized domain, and
   education is its first and best home. **Ready; strongest
   forwardable.**

### The medium thread — text is an asset, not a liability

**Standing requirement (user, 2026-07-28):** the core
design-philosophy documents ([design-philosophy.md](./design-philosophy.md)
— how we use numbers; [interaction-philosophy.md](./interaction-philosophy.md)
— why text, how we use words) are represented *across* the videos,
cumulatively — no single video carries the whole theory, but by the
end of four or five, the viewer should have concluded on their own
that **text is an asset**.

**The complete case, inventoried** (so no argument gets dropped;
sources: the two philosophy docs + 2026-07-28 additions). Each
argument is assigned to exactly one video below; ✎ marks the two
arguments added to the docs this pass:

1. One model, every surface (command bus; learnability gradient)
2. Every action a legible, attributable event → **auditable and
   moderatable by construction** (the moderation case)
3. Text-first is social-first (conversation IS the medium; emotes
   complete it) — and **education is inherently social** (the gap
   study.com cannot fill from inside a content library ✎ strategy)
4. The uncanny-valley dodge (prose degrades gracefully where
   graphics curdle — the NPC reads as description, not puppet)
5. **Playing is writing practice** ✎ (participation exercises the
   one skill every discipline demands)
6. Anyone who can write can author (the low UGC floor; LLMs lower
   it further; the accretion thesis rides this)
7. Literacy the only floor + the minimal core is mobile — and
   *familiar* mobile (a chat app with a button bar); the floor is
   **per-activity, not just per-device**: combat, forums,
   decorating, authoring all reduce to the stream, so every session
   type survives the smallest screen
8. A text world never ages (no graphics arms race; content
   longevity)
9. Language-or-numbers (prose = faithful rendering of the honest
   numeric model — never flavor text; why `analyze` works)
10. Foundational vs decorational (the game works with decoration
    off; panels are the escape valve for serial-vs-parallel — the
    owned downside)
11. Imagination is the ceiling, not the art budget (which is what
    buys the deep sim)
12. **LLM-ready on both ends** (perceive prose, emit commands — the
    human interface IS the AI interface; agents as social
    participants, auditable in plain text)

**The length budget (settled 2026-07-28, after a load audit found
two videos over):** each video = **one experience + at most two
theory beats said aloud** + one closing exec read; everything else
is *staged* (visible, unnarrated). The 1:1 exec framing survives as
a tone device for videos 1–4, but it is not a content constraint —
all four execs will watch everything, so the set always talks to
everyone. The overflow theory beats turned out to be one family
("everything is a string" demonstrations), which is a fifth video's
experience, not padding for four — see video 5.

**Per-video assignment (rebalanced):**

- **Video 1 (Conferral / Dansbury)** — theory beats: #1 only (the
  form echoes its command — one glance-beat inside the study/act
  flow). The conferral arc fills the video; the ghost command is
  staged throughout, narrated once. Moderation (#2) moves to
  video 5.
- **Video 2 (Playing the Part / Mancini)** — theory beats: #3 (+#4
  staged). Education is inherently social, their product isn't —
  the world supplies the missing half natively; the role is lived
  among people. Arrival's first-hour material trims to a cold
  open (sixty seconds, not an act); the NPC-reads-as-person beat
  (#4) is staged in a conversation, one line at most. Writing
  practice (#5) moves to video 5.
- **Video 3 (Long Transcript / Shmeir)** — theory beats: #7, #8
  (both quick visual beats). The #7 beat, staged right, carries the
  per-activity floor without a word of narration: her lunch-break
  phone session shows a *fight resolving in the stream* or a forum
  argument scrolled on the bus — not just chat — then the same
  evening at her desk with the panels lit. One activity's floor and
  ceiling, same day, same world. #8: year-one prose reread in year
  three. UGC/authoring (#6) moves to video 5, where it belongs with
  the maker arc.
- **Video 4 (Honest World / Ridner)** — theory beats: #9, #11,
  #12 with #10's downside owned in a single line ("text is
  serial; that's what the panels are for"). Closing line: the
  platform is already denominated in the currency AI spends.
- **Video 5 (Made of words — the ensemble video, no single exec
  frame)** — *the medium video proper*: the experience is **a
  player becoming a maker**, told as one arc: she clicks (and sees
  the command it typed), types, scripts her morning routine,
  writes a room description, publishes it, and someone she's never
  met walks through it that evening. Theory beats: #5 (playing is
  writing practice — every step of that arc is composition), #6
  (anyone who can write can author — the low floor, LLMs lowering
  it further), #2 (and everything everyone did along the way was
  a legible, attributable string — moderation and audit as
  substrate). This is the "text is an asset" argument at its most
  direct — and it's still an experience, not a lecture: the
  accretion thesis with a face. *Lenses:* the-toy (the braver
  way), emergence (nouns multiply), infinite-inspiration.
  **Ready** in its early beats (click→type→script are shipped);
  the publish-a-room beat leans on the authoring surfaces (CMS/
  Studio shipped; the full protowizard loop is the honest
  build-state caveat per the script gate).

**The staging rule that carries the argument silently:** every
video shows the *cockpit* — prose center, panels, clickable
affordances previewing commands — never a bare green-on-black
terminal. The frame itself refutes "text = 1985" before the
narration says a word; the thread's job is to convert "huh, that
looks usable" into "oh, the text is *why* it can do all this."

## Video craft rules (from the lens pass, 2026-07-28)

Four lenses read specifically for this track
([resonance](./lenses/resonance.md) / [moments](./lenses/moments.md)
/ [interest-curve](./lenses/interest-curve.md) /
[the-pitch](./lenses/the-pitch.md)) yield the working rules:

- **Every video names its moment before scripting** — a video is one
  top-ten moment made as powerful as film allows; the ranked
  moment list lives in the moments entry and doubles as the
  subject backlog. (Disney rule: if we can't name the top ten
  moments, the pitch meeting is over.)
- **Every script ships with its interest curve drawn** — hook in
  the first fifteen seconds (the claim shown cold, no logos),
  rising demonstration with reference-doc rests, the moment paid
  off as the finale, the exec read riding the descent. Series
  order is an interest-curve decision, separate from stakeholder
  priority.
- **The theme is stated once, felt everywhere — and is not yet
  ratified.** Three candidates await the user's feel-test (see the
  resonance entry); until then, scripts hold the connective
  narration and lead with experiences, which are theme-safe.
- **Build for forwardability** — each video self-contained, titled
  by its moment, sendable cold by an internal champion to an
  executive with one sentence attached.
- **The four things the audience needs to know** (pitch entry):
  the game is fun on its own feet; the learning model already
  exists inside it; their product plugs in as an issuer,
  unchanged; the stakes stay honest. Nothing else is load-bearing.

## Pre-script gap audit (2026-07-28)

Parity check before scripting. Every video has: experience named,
frame + tone set, theory beats assigned, exec read written, lenses
anchored. What varies is **shootability** — whether the live game
can produce the footage. Per video:

- **V1 Conferral — blocked at its climax.** The wall (FloodedCell,
  real Ohm's law) is shipped and shootable; the study montage is
  stageable. The climax — the conferral *firing* — depends on the
  credential-issuer adapter, which is designed, not built. Also
  ungapped but unauthored: the diegetic conferral scene itself
  (where in the University does it land, who says what — the
  threshold-ceremony micro-build already flagged in the moments
  entry). **Needs: mock issuer adapter + one authored ceremony
  beat.**
- **V2 Playing the Part — systems shipped, content thin, texture
  missing.** Traits/bands/chronicle all demonstrable; char-gen cold
  open shootable. The medic *role* needs its stage: the aid-post
  rounds are a wishbook loop, not authored content. And "months in
  the role among peers" needs a world with bodies and history in
  it. **Needs: aid-post content beat + the demo world (below).**
- **V3 Long Transcript — montage-friendly but three soft spots.**
  The year-three "apartment on a titled floor" outruns the built
  residence ladder (dorm exists; apartments don't — reframe or
  caveat). The degree-posts beat shares V1's adapter gap. And the
  lunch-break phone beat assumes the mobile floor actually *looks*
  like the chat-app minimal core today — unverified; the cockpit
  is responsive on paper, but nobody has framed a phone session
  for camera. **Needs: adapter (shared), mobile-floor check, one
  reframe decision.**
- **V4 Honest World — most shootable.** Analyze surfaces are
  shipped; the widening montage (species/materials/nutrition) is
  real data. Small authoring: a fountain with an honest acoustic
  profile and the textbook-checkable fact chosen. One script-gate
  item: the AI-participants beat stays design-level (no LLM
  participant exists — the deferred LLM slate). **Needs: one
  authored object + fact selection.**
- **V5 Made of Words — early beats fully real.** Click→ghost
  command→type→script are shipped and filmable now; the
  write-a-room + publish beat runs on shipped CMS/Studio but needs
  a rehearsed, camera-smooth path and carries the protowizard-loop
  build-state caveat. **Needs: a rehearsed authoring path.**

**Cross-cutting gaps (bigger than any one video):**

1. **The mock issuer adapter** — blocks V1's climax and V3's degree
   beat, and it *is* the pitch demo per the strategy doc. Highest-
   leverage single build in the whole video effort.
2. **The demo world** — nobody has named this until now: all five
   videos film inside some world-state, and four of them need one
   with *age and bodies* — chronicle depth, chain-of-title history,
   named regulars, an aid post with patients, a stranger to walk
   through the published room. A curated, seeded, deliberately aged
   world-state is a production asset that serves every video (and
   every future demo). Worth building once, on purpose.
3. **The resonance feel-test** — still pending; blocks connective
   narration in all five (scripts can draft around it).
4. **The mobile floor** — argument #7's staging assumes it; check
   it on a real phone before scripting V3's beat.

Suggested order: theme decision (user) → mock adapter → demo-world
seeding → the three small content beats (ceremony, aid post,
fountain) → mobile check → scripts.
