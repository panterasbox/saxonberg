## The organizing principle: each video is an experience

**The game is fun. If a video doesn't come off as fun, nothing else
in it lands** — no completion story, no integration story, no moat
story. Fun is not the garnish on the business case; it is the
carrier. A viewer who doesn't feel "I want to do that" will not be
persuaded by anything said afterward.

So the unit of a video is not a feature, a subsystem, or a value
proposition — it is **an experience the player has**, in Schell's
sense ([lenses/essential-experience.md](./lenses/essential-experience.md):
decide the felt experience first; mechanics, story, and world are
only the means). Each video:

1. **Names the experience** in a sentence — the felt thing, not the
   mechanic.
2. **Shows it happening** — real play, real figures, the
   reference-doc register doing what it does best.
3. **Lets the essence surface** — every video instantiates the two
   ratified essence sentences (platform: *real effort, recognized —
   your doing is seen, what you become is earned, what you make
   persists, you do it among others who remember you, and the world
   itself honors what you understand*; game: *learning as
   adventure*). Never recited; felt.
4. **Derives the exec read last** — engagement, personalization,
   marketing, completion: whatever a study.com executive can
   leverage is a *reading of the experience*, stated briefly at the
   end, in their vocabulary. The value proposition is embodied by
   the experience, not argued alongside it.

**Map every beat back to the student (user, 2026-07-29).** At the
core of every video is one person: a learner who wants to learn new
things and apply them in an engaging, dynamic environment. The
internal shorthand is *a Skinner box for surprise and delight, with
a pedagogical bend* — engagement engineering owned plainly, with the
transformation gate as its conscience (never say "Skinner box" in a
video). Practically: each script should be checkable beat-by-beat
for where the learner is standing in it — a world beat earns its
place by provoking the learner's question; a creator beat by
building what the learner meets; the loop is curiosity → study →
the world visibly gives way to what you learned. (V4's Act II
rewrite briefly dropped its one student line — "you study because
your character needs it" — and it had to be restored; treat that
line-class as load-bearing.)

The design lenses ([docs/lenses/](./lenses/README.md)) are the
analysis toolkit: each video's brief names the two or three lenses
that govern its experience, and the script is checked against them.
[Transformation](./lenses/transformation.md) applies to *every* video
in this track — both essences are recognition-of-effort, so "is this
good for the learner" is a property of the experience itself (this is
the high-stakes guardrail from the strategy doc, lens-shaped).

## What these videos are — and are not

They **are** a pitch for the game: a fun new text-based RPG with
honest systems, presented in the reference-doc register, speaking to
our specific strengths in the marketplace.

They **are not** a partnership proposal. Nothing in them touches
content licensing, product integration commitments, revenue, or any
negotiated-agreement shape. The videos describe experiences the game
delivers and a seam those experiences expose. What anyone does with
that seam is a conversation the videos deliberately do not start.
(The deal thinking lives in
[study-com-strategy.md](./study-com-strategy.md), internal.)

Corollary rules:

- **Name study.com when a specific product earns it; describe the
  seam generically otherwise.** The seam is vertical-agnostic — "an
  adaptive learning platform with a subject taxonomy and verified
  assessment" — but when a video refers to a concrete product
  (College Saver's credit-recommended courses, teacher-cert prep),
  naming it plainly beats coy circumlocution. What stays out is deal
  shape, not the name.
- **Do speak specifically to the strengths of their products in the
  ed-tech market** — deep subject taxonomy (**Program → Course → Topic → Lesson**; "chapter" is a derived display index, not a stored level), self-paced completion, credit-recommended
  (ACE/NCCRS) assessment with identity verification. These are the
  properties the seam consumes; naming them precisely is describing
  our own design.
- **Describe design, not build-state, inaccurately never.** The
  advancement/chronicle substrate is shipped; the external-issuer
  adapter is designed, not built. Say exactly that.
- **No assumed gamer literacy.** The decision audience may not game.
  Show the loop on screen; translate to their metrics at the end;
  zero game-culture jargon.

## Register

**Revised (user, 2026-07-29): these are a pitch, and the shape is
the product demo.** The manifesto was functional — matter-of-fact,
reference register. This track optimizes for **cool**
(the north star): Apple-product-demo energy, where the structure is
**drilling in** — each beat reveals another dimension of the product
the viewer didn't know was there, and the wonder comes from the
reveals themselves, not from adjectives. Don't go overboard: no
hype vocabulary, no superlatives — the demo cadence carries the
excitement, the content stays plainly true.

Retained from the game-intro series (these are constraints on the
pitch, not a competing tone): real figures, assert-don't-defend,
philosophy bolted to what's on screen, no overclaimed novelty,
build-state honesty. The differentiator is honesty depth — the
game's physics runs on the real models the coursework teaches, so
knowledge is load-bearing rather than decorative — and the demo
structure exists to make that depth *felt* as successive reveals.

## The seam (context all videos share)

The game stands alone as an educational game — campus, majors, its
own subject taxonomy (the internal/external split ratified in
[lenses/essential-experience.md](./lenses/essential-experience.md)).
An external learning platform is the optional enrichment that turns
the standalone game into a vertical product:

- Its taxonomy aligns against the Discipline catalog's typed edges.
- Coursework completion lands as **claim**-provenance transcript
  evidence — knowledge, learning-zone access.
- Verified assessment lands as **deed**-provenance evidence — the
  rung that raises the ceiling and confers capability. Assessment
  provenance is **graded, not assumed**: a credential carries its
  conditions (proctored / identity-verified open-book / self-report)
  and deed-weight follows — one schema for any issuer.
- The platform is an **issuer**, nothing more. Assessment authority
  stays with the assessor; the game consumes credentials, never
  grants them. The floor stays fully playable on in-game learning;
  external mastery raises the ceiling only.

### The medium thread — text is an asset, not a liability

**Standing requirement (user, 2026-07-28):** the core
design-philosophy documents ([design-philosophy.md](./design-philosophy.md)
— how we use numbers; [interaction-philosophy.md](./interaction-philosophy.md)
— why text, how we use words) are represented *across* the videos,
cumulatively — no single video carries the whole theory, but by the
end of four or five, the viewer should have concluded on their own
that **text is an asset**.

**The complete case, inventoried** (so no argument gets dropped;
sources: the two philosophy docs + 2026-07-28 additions). Each
argument is assigned to exactly one video below; ✎ marks the two
arguments added to the docs this pass:

1. One model, every surface (command bus; learnability gradient)
2. Every action a legible, attributable event → **auditable and
   moderatable by construction** (the moderation case)
3. Text-first is social-first (conversation IS the medium; emotes
   complete it) — and **education is inherently social** (the gap
   study.com cannot fill from inside a content library ✎ strategy)
4. The uncanny-valley dodge (prose degrades gracefully where
   graphics curdle — the NPC reads as description, not puppet)
5. **Playing is writing practice** ✎ (participation exercises the
   one skill every discipline demands)
6. Anyone who can write can author (the low UGC floor; LLMs lower
   it further; the accretion thesis rides this)
7. Literacy the only floor + the minimal core is mobile — and
   *familiar* mobile (a chat app with a button bar); the floor is
   **per-activity, not just per-device**: combat, forums,
   decorating, authoring all reduce to the stream, so every session
   type survives the smallest screen
8. A text world never ages (no graphics arms race; content
   longevity)
9. Language-or-numbers (prose = faithful rendering of the honest
   numeric model — never flavor text; why `analyze` works)
10. Foundational vs decorational (the game works with decoration
    off; panels are the escape valve for serial-vs-parallel — the
    owned downside)
11. Imagination is the ceiling, not the art budget (which is what
    buys the deep sim)
12. **LLM-ready on both ends** (perceive prose, emit commands — the
    human interface IS the AI interface; agents as social
    participants, auditable in plain text)

  (the learner). If a draft's acts can't each point at one of the
  two docs, it has drifted.

  top-ten moment made as powerful as film allows; the ranked
  moment list lives in the moments entry and doubles as the
  subject backlog. (Disney rule: if we can't name the top ten
  moments, the pitch meeting is over.)

  the first fifteen seconds (the claim shown cold, no logos),
  rising demonstration with reference-doc rests, the moment paid
  off as the finale, the exec read riding the descent. Series
  order is an interest-curve decision, separate from stakeholder
  priority.
- **The theme is stated once, felt everywhere — and is not yet
  ratified.** Three candidates await the user's feel-test (see the
  resonance entry); until then, scripts hold the connective
  narration and lead with experiences, which are theme-safe.
- **Build for forwardability** — each video self-contained, titled
  by its moment, sendable cold by an internal champion to an
  executive with one sentence attached.
- **The four things the audience needs to know** (pitch entry):
  the game is fun on its own feet; the learning model already
  exists inside it; their product plugs in as an issuer,
  unchanged; the stakes stay honest. Nothing else is load-bearing.
