# #46 · The Lens of Reward

> One of the [Schell deck](./README.md). Lens named from *A Book of
> Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.[^aogd-rw]
>
> ⚠ **Rewritten 2026-09-29** against the amended
> [measurement.md](../measurement.md). An earlier draft argued we had
> elegantly solved habituation. We had not; we had mis-scoped a rule.

## The lens

Everyone likes to be told they are doing a good job. What rewards does
the game give out now, and could it give others? Are players excited by
them or bored? **Do players understand the rewards they are getting** —
because a reward you do not understand is no reward at all. Are the
rewards too regular; could they be more variable? How are they related
to one another, and how are they building — too fast, too slow, or just
right?

> **From the book.** Schell states the technique without euphemism:
> *"if every monster you defeat gives you ten points, that gets
> predictable and boring pretty quickly—but if every monster you defeat
> has a 2/3 chance of giving you zero points, but a 1/3 chance of
> giving you thirty points, this stays rewarding for a much longer
> time, **even though you are receiving the same number of points on
> average**."* And on escalating reward value as the player progresses:
> *"In a way, this is a cheesy trick, but it works—even when you know
> the designer is doing it and why."* He also names the acclimation
> problem it solves: people habituate to rewards, and what was
> rewarding an hour ago is no big deal now.

## Which of our seven it sharpens

**[Lens 4 · Values](../design-lenses.md)** — the cost side of the
values commitments rather than the test. Secondarily **lens 1**,
because the mechanism it recommends is the one lens 1's instrument
forbids.

## Why our design prompts it

Because this lens sits astride **two separate prohibitions**, and they
are not the same kind of rule:

| | prohibition | tier | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A6** | *No aleatory resolution.* No die between a choice and its outcome | **A — amendable by nobody** | a design invariant; violating it lets the world lie about what you did |
| **B5** | *No variable-ratio reinforcement.* No loot boxes, gacha, pity timers | **B — the founder's** | ⭐ the one RNG application banned on **ethical** rather than design grounds |

So Schell's central technique is doubly out of reach here: the
mechanism by A6, the pattern by B5. Being able to say why, and what it
costs, is the point of the entry.

## What the design answers

**The reward surface is the economy, and it is an honest trade rather
than an engineered drip.** Wages, prices, tips, yields and title are
paid for work delivered. What makes them rewards is that they buy
something, not that they arrive on a cadence tuned for retention —
and [lens 6](../design-lenses.md) already requires each to name its
source and sink, which forecloses the drip as a side effect.

**"How are my rewards related to one another?"** has an unusually
strong answer: goods, capacity, information and standing are
deliberately **not** interconvertible — money may buy goods and
services and may never buy standing. Schell asks the question to stop
rewards feeling arbitrary; ours is a stated non-convertibility, which
is stronger than coherence.

**"Do players understand the rewards they are getting?"** is now more
than a lens question — it is **doctrine**. B3 commits that any
measurement the platform makes of you is one you can read, so:

> ⭐⭐ **A derived reward a player cannot trace is not a reward you gave.
> It is a measurement you are withholding**, which the platform's own
> Tier B forbids.

## The verdict

⭐⭐⭐ **Push back on variance. Adopt the legibility question. And
concede that habituation was never our problem to solve.**

### Push back — we have his mechanism, honestly

[uncertainty.md](../uncertainty.md) bans exactly one of four
provenances: **resolutional**. Environmental, epistemic and generative
randomness are all legal. A seeded ore grade you must assay to learn
**is a variable payout** — you genuinely do not know what this vein
will give you.

> ⭐⭐ **Schell's 2/3-zero, 1/3-thirty is a machine that *simulates* not
> knowing. Ours is a world you *actually* do not know.** Same
> psychological effect, opposite epistemic status — and ours pays a
> dividend his cannot, because the uncertainty is **reducible by
> skill**: a better prospector narrows the distribution. His never
> narrows, because there is nothing under it.

The demand is met, and met better, **in every domain with real
epistemic depth** — prospecting, the bite, the assay, the harvest
grade, what the ground turns out to hold.

⚠ **Where the pushback stops.** His variance is **dense** — every
monster, forever. Ours is **sparse**, confined to domains with genuine
unknowns. A wage is a wage; a known recipe with known inputs pays what
it pays. Those domains cannot honestly acquire variance, and pretending
otherwise is the resolutional roll under a new name.

⭐ **The alternative to randomising the wage: don't.** A predictable
wage is *true*, it is pedagogically honest, and it is how a player
feels the difference between working for somebody and taking a risk.
The question a boring wage raises is not *"how do we make this
exciting"* but **"how much of a player's time is spent in the
deterministic domains"** — content mix, not mechanics.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Concede — and then notice the problem does not apply

An earlier draft of this entry claimed we defeat habituation by
conditioning the unbidden channel on surprise. **That was wrong**, and
wrong in the way this deck exists to prevent: *announce the surprising,
not the every* is a **disposition-narration** rule, scoped to declared
standards, which I generalized into an architecture it never claimed.
`measurement.md` Part 6.2 is now explicit that it does not reach
performance, precisely because generalising it gets the product
backwards:

> ⭐⭐ **For a game whose thesis is applied hours, habituation is the
> outcome being sold.** You *want* the training to become unremarkable.
> A channel that only speaks on surprise is optimised against its own
> goal, and the steadiest player gets the least signal.

⚠⚠ **Altitude check** (added by the 2026-09-29 pass): *habituation is
the outcome* is a claim about **one game**, not about the platform. A
title tuned for speedrunners wants the opposite; so does a one-session
narrative piece. **The platform's obligation is that both be
buildable** — which means the deviation rule must stay a *default an
author can change*, never a substrate opinion. See
[#7](./7-endogenous-value.md) § *asked at the wrong altitude*.

Which reframes Schell rather than refuting him. **His acclimation
problem assumes the reward is why you are there.** For a game, that is
usually true, and variable reward is a real answer. For a platform
where the *activity* is why you are there, habituation is not decay —
it is **success**, and the honest response is the one now in Part 6.4:
routine quantitative reading. A bathroom scale is a number, read daily,
about a routine act, and it exists to support a habit rather than to
defeat one.

⚠ **It does not dissolve entirely.** The activity still has to be worth
doing, and "the routine is the point" is not a licence for the routine
to be dull. That is lens 3's problem, not this one's, but this lens is
where it would be easy to pretend otherwise.

### Adopt outright

*"Getting a reward you don't understand is like getting no reward at
all."* We should be running that as a gate and are not.

## Tensions & risks

⚠ **The deterministic domains are genuinely flat and always will be.**
A defensible choice, but a choice — the density of interesting reward
is a **function of content mix**. If most of a player's hours land in
known-input, known-output work, no mechanism rescues it.

⚠⚠ **Reducible uncertainty must actually reduce.** The pushback is only
honest if skill narrows the distribution. A system shipping epistemic
uncertainty **no competence can sharpen** is a resolutional roll
wearing environmental clothes, and should be caught in review as one.

⚠ **The drip is still reachable by accident.** Nothing prevents paying
out on a timer; conservation forbids a reward with no *source*, not a
reward with a *cadence*.

⚠ **B5 is the softest of the prohibitions here.** A6 is unamendable and
the economy enforces itself; B5 is a promise by whoever ships the code,
and it is the one an engagement problem would come for first.

## Implications

1. ⭐⭐ **Stop saying we refuse variable reward.** We refuse
   *resolutional* variance and take the rest from an honest world. The
   levelling conversation should open with *our randomness is reducible
   by skill and his is not*, not with an apology.
2. ⭐⭐ **Run "can the player trace it?" as a gate on every derived
   reward.** It is no longer a lens preference; B3 makes an untraceable
   derived reward a doctrine violation.
3. ⭐⭐ **Say that habituation is *this title's* product.** It is the
   cleanest one-line statement of how a game selling applied hours
   differs from one selling sessions — and it belongs in that title's
   vision material, ⚠ **not in the platform's**, because another game
   on the same substrate will want the opposite.
4. ⭐⭐ **The levelling question is not "what number goes up."** It is
   *what arrives unbidden, and is it honest?* Framing it the first way
   guarantees a gauge; framing it the second way is the actual problem
   — and routine quantitative reading is now a legitimate answer for
   the performance half.
5. ⚠ **Audit epistemic uncertainty for reducibility.** Any system whose
   unknowns no competence can narrow has smuggled in a resolutional
   roll. A reviewable property, not a feeling.
