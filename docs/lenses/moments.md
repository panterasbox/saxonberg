## The lens

Two questions: **what are the key moments in my game? How can I make
each moment as powerful as possible?**

> **From the book.** "Any entertainment experience is a series of
> moments" — the interest curve charts the most powerful ones, and
> you can't arrange what you haven't named. The teeth: at Disney
> Imagineering, pitching a ride to the CEO guaranteed one question —
> **"What are the top ten moments in your experience?"** — and "if we
> didn't have a clear answer, the pitch meeting was over." Memorable
> moments are the stars; the interest curve is the
> constellation.[^aogd-mn]

## Why our design prompts it

Three consumers at once. The **videos** are, by the settled
organizing principle, experiences — and an experience on film is a
moment made powerful; a video without a nameable moment isn't ready
to shoot. The **pitch** will face the Disney question in some form,
and "well, it's all emergent" is the answer that ends the meeting.
And the **game itself** makes moments two ways — quests author them
(an arc's climax is a designed moment by definition) and systems
mint them unbidden — so someone should check both that the authored
arcs land their climaxes and that the systems actually produce
their share.

## What the design answers

### A first draft of the top ten (the Disney answer)

From the designed-and-built record, the moments with the most
power — these ten happen to be systems-made (quest climaxes will
join the list as arcs get authored; an authored top-ten moment is
just as real):

1. **The conferral** — you pass the real exam, walk into the
   University, and the world recognizes what you now are.
2. **The key in your hand** — Katie walks you in, the door knows
   you now; the first space that is *yours* and persists.
3. **The `analyze` double-take** — prose becomes physics; the
   world was real all along.
4. **The wet firewood lights** — because *you* worked out why it
   wouldn't; reality complies with comprehension.
5. **The coup stayed** — a fight won, a life in your hands, and
   mercy (or a bystander's grip) as a real decision with a ledger.
6. **First introduction** — a stranger learns your name and keeps
   it; the world starts remembering you.
7. **First wage** — the shift ends and honest money lands; you have
   a livelihood in a real economy.
8. **The storm hits the brine pool** — weather → puddle → circuit →
   consequence; the world's systems visibly conspiring.
9. **Your script runs** — the commands you typed become a program
   that acts without you; player becomes maker.
10. **The remodel reveal** — your room, re-themed to your taste,
    seen by a friend who says "this is so you."

(Candidates knocking: the first duel accepted under negotiated
terms; the market day your consigned goods sell; the first time you
read the sky and were right.)

### Making each as powerful as possible

The design already owns the main amplifiers, scattered across the
pass: **narration adapters** (combat's arc is why its moments land;
each top-ten moment deserves its beat treatment), **witnesses**
(reactions, renown reception — a moment observed is a moment
doubled; several top-ten moments are currently private by default),
and **receipts** (the chronicle should *record* the top ten — a
moment that leaves no trace can't be retold; cross-check each
against the chronicle's minters). The unbuilt amplifier is
arrival-timing: several moments (first key, first wage, conferral)
are also *thresholds*, and thresholds bear ceremony — small, honest,
diegetic ceremony (Katie's handover is the model), never a fanfare
sting.

## Tensions & risks

- **Emergent moments can't be scheduled — but they can be missed.**
  The storm-on-the-brine-pool happens whether or not anyone can see
  why (the legibility rules) or is there at all. Moment-making in a
  systems game is mostly *witnessability engineering*: presence,
  sightlines, narration, records.
- **Top-ten lists calcify.** This draft is pre-playtest; the honest
  list is whatever players actually retell (the story-machine's
  valuation instruments — reactions, renown — will *measure* the
  real top ten). Treat this draft as a hypothesis the instruments
  will correct.
- **Ceremony creep.** Threshold ceremony amplifies; repeated
  ceremony numbs and drifts toward manufactured juice. Once per
  threshold, diegetic, skippable.

## Implications

1. **The videos each name their moment before scripting** — the
   experience briefs already do this implicitly; make it explicit:
   *video = one top-ten moment, made as powerful as film allows.*
   The draft list above is also the video-subject backlog, ranked.
2. **Keep the Disney answer current.** The top-ten list lives in
   this entry, revised as systems land and playtests speak; anyone
   pitching (deck, video, hallway) should be able to recite five
   without notes.
3. **Audit the top ten for witnesses and receipts** — for each:
   who can see it, what records it, can it be retold? (Story-
   machine checklist applied to our own best material first.)
4. **Threshold ceremony is a small named build** — the moments that
   are thresholds (key, wage, conferral) get one designed beat
   each, Katie-grade, no fanfare. Cheap, high-yield, honest.
