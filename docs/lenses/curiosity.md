# Lens: Curiosity

> Part of the [design lenses](./README.md) set. Lens named from Jesse
> Schell's *A Book of Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.
>
> **Layer interrogated: the game** (world content plants the questions),
> with a pedagogical payoff.

## The lens

A game runs on the questions it puts in the player's mind. Not the
explicit goals — the *implicit* ones: what's behind that door, who is
that figure, what happens if I try this, what is this place? Curiosity
is the engine that makes a player act without being told to. The
questions: what questions does my game plant? Am I making the player
*want* the answers? And am I rewarding the curiosity I provoke, or
letting it fizzle?

> **From the book.** Schell's framing is that the player's mind is
> always running questions, and good design *plants and stokes* them —
> a well-made game keeps the player wondering, and every answered
> question should open a new one. Curiosity is intrinsic motivation in
> its rawest form; a game that stops provoking questions stops being
> played.[^aogd-cu]

## Why our design prompts it

Because the world is built to provoke questions, and because curiosity
is the cleanest bridge from *wanting to explore* to *wanting to learn* —
which is the whole game. "You're in for anything," a brain in a jar, a
watch with a secret: these are curiosity hooks, and an educational game
that can convert world-curiosity into subject-curiosity has found its
holy grail.

## What the design answers

- **The un-genred campus is a curiosity engine.** "Obviously unreal"
  plants the questions *what is this place, who made it, what else is
  possible here* ([eternal-university](../slates/eternal-university-slate.md)).
  Strangeness-as-finish is, functionally, a question-planting device.
- **Mystery characters.** Dr. Limen (a benign brain in a jar plumbed
  into the campus) is a walking question; Gus's watch hides the
  AUGUSTUS secret beneath an NPC whose name is otherwise freely shown
  (deep lore can hide even when the name doesn't — the
  named-is-for-proper-names discipline).
- **Explorable secrets.** Hidden corners, restricted archives, tunnels
  beneath the old quad ([vision.md](../vision.md)); the fast-travel
  route map with "lines running past the edge of any one reality." The
  world dangles places to wonder about.
- **Tools that reward the question "what's here?"** MQL, `find`, and the
  inspection pane turn idle curiosity into satisfying discovery.

## Tensions & risks

- **Curiosity demands payoff.** A planted question with no answer sours
  into frustration and teaches the player to *stop* wondering. The big
  hooks (Limen's nature, Gus's secret, "who made this place") must have
  designed answers — or be deliberate, framed open mysteries. Teasing
  without resolution burns the very trust curiosity runs on.
- **Text curiosity is authored, not free.** A graphical game tantalizes
  with a shape on the horizon for free; text has to *write* the lure.
  The "finish" technique (a strange surface over a familiar function) is
  the text-native equivalent — but it's authoring-dependent, and an
  unauthored room provokes nothing.
- **World-curiosity and subject-curiosity aren't the same question.**
  The curiosity the world plants (mysteries, lore) may have nothing to
  do with the curiosity the *lesson* needs (about the subject). Aligning
  the two is the pedagogical art, and it isn't automatic — a world full
  of intriguing mysteries that never point at the subject is a great
  game and a failed teacher.

## Implications

1. **Every planted question needs a designed answer — or a deliberate
   open mystery.** Audit the curiosity hooks (Limen, Gus, "who made
   this place") for payoff. Curiosity without resolution is a debt that
   comes due as lost trust.
2. **Build the curiosity→subject bridge on purpose.** The highest-value
   move this lens offers education: design hooks where answering the
   *world* question requires or rewards engaging the *subject*
   (vision's "a knowledge gap becomes an obstacle" is exactly this). This
   is the curiosity / [Motivation](./motivation.md) / pedagogy nexus and
   the cleanest path to intrinsic learning motivation.
3. **Treat the "finish" technique as a reusable lure.** Strange surface
   over familiar function is how text plants a question cheaply; make it
   an explicit authoring tool, not a one-off campus trick.
4. **Layer secrets so there's always a deeper question.** Gus's watch
   (hidden lore beneath a freely-shown name) is the model: durable
   curiosity comes from depth that survives the first answer.
