## The lens

Everyone has secret wishes. Three questions: **what fantasy does my
world fulfill? Who does the player fantasize about being? What does
the player fantasize about doing there?**

> **From the book.** The lens closes Schell's transmedia-worlds
> discussion: the strongest properties (Star Trek, Tolkien, Potter)
> hold their fans not by story or character but by the world itself —
> "a sort of personal utopia that they fantasize about visiting,"
> short-term for some, lifelong for others. The desire to *enter* the
> world is the engine; fantasy "often forms the anchor for your
> world."[^aogd-fa]

## Why our design prompts it

Because the target player's wish is unusually legible, and the
design — read honestly — was built for it without ever naming it.
The study.com student's real life is structured deferral: study
now, become later, alone, invisible, with the payoff years out and
the grind unwitnessed. Their secret wish is the inversion:

> **To be who they are becoming — now, visibly, among others, in a
> world where their competence is real power.**

Not escape *from* the vocation — the vocation **transfigured**. The
nursing student doesn't fantasize about stopping being a nurse; she
fantasizes about nursing *mattering* — being the calm one in the
crisis, before any license says she may. That is the fantasy this
world fulfills, and no HP-bar world can, because only honest models
make real knowledge operative.

## What the design answers

- **What fantasy?** Mastery made visible and consequential: the
  deferred identity, practiced today. The economy's
  vocations-are-the-game choice, combat's status-effects-are-real-
  physiology choice, and skills-only advancement all serve this one
  fantasy — they make *being good at your thing* the content.
- **Who do they fantasize about being?** The competent one: the
  medic who knows why, the electrician who reads the circuit, the
  teacher whose students grow, the broker who reads the title, the
  captain whose line holds, the researcher whose finding bears
  their name. One fantasy per cohort, ten cohorts per catalog —
  see the wishbook.
- **What do they fantasize about doing?** The scenes where the
  competence pays under witness — the wishbook's vignettes, each
  composed concretely from shipped systems.
- **The liberty zone:** the magic discipline grid is the one place
  advancement is ours to shape freely (invented science, real
  laws), and it should be shaped *toward this audience*: schools
  that rhyme with vocations (the fantasy tier flattering the real
  identity), grid edges that feel like curriculum (prereqs,
  specializations, synergies — studying-shaped progress as native
  motion), and frontier nodes as the research endgame — discovery,
  priority, publication, a finding with your name on it. Magic is
  the spectacle budget: the visible awe their real transcript never
  pays out.

## The tenure thesis (user, 2026-07-28)

The lens's end state, stated as the promise of the whole endeavor:

> **Presuming alignment drift is countered, the avatar you've
> invested your tenure into represents the fantasy you started
> with** — the nursing student becomes a real medic, known for her
> craft among her peers in the game, and perfectly set up to meet
> the real challenges of a new world.

The alignment problem is *essential to the promise of
gamification* — not a caveat on the claim but the condition that
makes it a claim at all. Three consequences:

- **Alignment is measurable with instruments we already have.**
  Char-gen seeds the chronicle with claims — the declared fantasy
  is on record as the *prologue*; the deeds are what actually
  happened. Drift is readable as the prologue↔deeds gap: no new
  system, a query over existing ledgers. This is also the honest
  implementation of vision.md's "gameplay informs learning."
- **Drift ≠ revision.** The declared nurse who discovers through
  play that she loves teaching isn't misaligned — the world
  revealed her real fantasy (arguably the most valuable thing an
  education product can do, and far cheaper discovered in a game's
  year one than a degree's year three). Revision is *re-declared*
  — the fantasy amended on the record — so alignment measures
  against the current declaration, never a stale one.
- **Tenure is the unit the promise is denominated in.** The videos'
  time-horizon spine (a night → months → three years → the world)
  ends at this thesis: the Long Transcript video is the tenure
  thesis filmed.

## Tensions & risks

- **Wish fulfillment for the striver can curdle into flattery.**
  The fantasy must stay *earned* (the essence: what you become is
  earned) — a world that hands out the competent-one feeling
  cheapens the exact thing the audience is starving for. Honest
  difficulty is part of the wish.
- **The mature-world texture is a promise the cold-start world
  can't keep.** The wishbook describes year three; a launch player
  arrives in year zero. The accretion thesis is the bridge, but
  early-days experience design (density, NPC liveliness) carries
  the fantasy until the community does.
- **One fantasy, many cohorts.** The unifying wish is real, but a
  nurse's scene and a coder's scene share no content — the world
  must keep *many* competence stages lit at once, which is exactly
  the everything-is-a-business / systems-over-content discipline
  already in force.

## Implications

1. **The wishbook is the design target**: each vignette names the
   systems it composes and its build-status; gaps it exposes are
   prioritized wish-first (see wishbook § gaps).
2. **Shape the magic grid for the audience** (the liberty zone,
   spent deliberately): vocation-rhyming schools, curriculum-shaped
   edges, research-frontier endgame. This is a magic-slate input,
   recorded there when that build resumes.
3. **The videos inherit the wish**: "be who you're becoming, now"
   is the emotional through-line under every experience brief —
   and a resonance-theme candidate (the feel-test list grows to
   four).
4. **Slate checklist, twelfth question:** *whose wish does this
   system serve — who fantasizes about being the person your
   system lets them be?* A system nobody dreams of being good at
   is plumbing.
