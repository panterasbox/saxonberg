# #17 · The Lens of the Toy

> One of the [Schell deck](./README.md). Lens named from *A Book of
> Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.[^aogd-toy]

## The lens

*Stop thinking about whether your game is fun to play, and start
thinking about whether it is fun to play **with**.*

**If my game had no goal, would it be fun at all? And — when people see
it, do they want to start interacting with it *even before they know
what to do*?**

> **From the book.** Toys are fun for their own sake; games have goals
> and are problem-solving. **Many games are built on top of toys** — a
> ball is a toy, baseball is a game; an avatar that runs and jumps is a
> toy, *Donkey Kong* is a game. *"Make sure your toy is fun to play with
> before you design a game around it. You might find that once you
> actually build your toy, **you are surprised by what makes it fun**."*
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ And his worked case is, word for word, this project's:
>
> *"**Grand Theft Auto** was not designed as Grand Theft Auto. **It was
> designed as a medium. It was designed to be a living, breathing city
> that was fun to play.**"* — David Jones. Only once the medium existed
> did they pick a game for it: *"GTA came from Pac-Man. The dots are the
> little people. There's me in my little, yellow car. And the ghosts are
> policemen."* *Lemmings* the same way — *"they weren't sure what the
> game would be, but the world sounded fun, so they built it."*
>
> ⭐ Two ways to use it. On an existing game, to make it *"more
> approachable and more fun to manipulate."* Or **the braver way**: to
> *"invent and create new toys before you even have any idea what games
> will be played with them. This is risky if you are on a schedule—but
> if you are not, it can be a great **divining rod**."*

## Which of our seven it sharpens

**[Lens 1 · Pedagogy](../design-lenses.md)** and
**[lens 2 · Expression](../design-lenses.md)** — and lens 1 badly needed
an instrument that was not borrowed through the essence.

⭐ Because [#7](./7-endogenous-value.md) concluded that the way to
measure understanding without an exam is to **make deriving the fun**.
**#17 is the lens that tests whether it is.** Q1 asks it directly: with
no goal and no reward, is working out how this world behaves something a
person would do anyway?

## ⭐⭐⭐ We have already taken the braver way

*"Invent and create new toys before you even have any idea what games
will be played with them."* That is the build order: a simulation with
an economy, trades, weather, materials and bodies — against **two
written quests**, one of them unbuilt.

Schell calls this the **braver** way and names its cost exactly:
**risky if you are on a schedule.** It is the same fact
[#2](./2-essential-experience.md) states as *an engine on a bench*, and
it is the same fact the [quest slate](../slates/builds/quest-modeling-slate.md)
states as *kernel first, off ≥2 real instances.* Three docs, one
observation, and this is the one that says it is a **strategy** rather
than an accident.

⚠ **But the divining rod has not been used**, because using it requires
the thing we have not done: *"you are surprised by what makes it fun."*
**Nobody has played, so nobody has been surprised** — and the toy's
greatest gift, discovering the game you did not plan, is currently
unavailable to us. We have the medium and have not yet let it tell us
anything.

## ⚠⚠ Q2 is the enterability gap, stated verbatim

> *"When people see my game, do they want to start interacting with it,
> **even before they know what to do**?"*

That is the third sighting of one gap, and unlike the first two it is
not an inference — **he simply asks it:**

| where | how it appeared |
|---|---|
| [#79 Freedom](./79-freedom.md) | *are there places where they are overwhelmed by too much freedom* — the blank canvas lens 2's promise creates |
| [#55 Visible Progress](./55-visible-progress.md) | *a pull nobody knows to pull is not an answer* |
| **#17, here** | **do they want to interact before they know what to do** |

⭐ Per the [revision proposal](../design-lenses-revision-proposal.md)
§ Q0, two sightings was a pattern not yet worth an eighth lens and **a
third would change that.** My recommendation is still **fold it into
lens 2** rather than mint a lens: lens 2 asks what a person can *make*,
and *can they begin* is the same question one step earlier. **One
clause, not one lens.**

> **Lens 2, proposed addition: an expressive surface that is not
> enterable has not landed.**

## ⚠⚠ And text makes Q2 structurally hard

A toy invites manipulation by **showing its affordances**. A ball is
visibly throwable; you do not read a manual. A command line shows
**nothing** — the room prose has to do all the affording, and prose is
the one medium that can describe a thing completely while suggesting no
action at all.

⭐ This is where hypertext earns its keep rather than being decoration:
**clickables preview their command**, which is a *visible handle* on an
otherwise invisible toy. [#31](./31-action.md)'s discovery table is the
rest of the answer — the `affordances` verb, the harvested `help`, the
refusal that names its remedy.

⚠ **What none of those cover is the first thirty seconds**, which is
precisely what Q2 asks about: not *can a motivated player find the
handles* but *does an unmotivated one reach for something*. Every
mechanism we have rewards a player who has already decided to engage.

## ⭐⭐ The GTA/Pac-Man challenge, which is uncomfortable

Once the medium existed, Jones's team did not derive a game from it.
**They borrowed one they knew was good** — the city is a maze, so take
Pac-Man's mechanics and reskin the dots as people.

We derive everything. Lens 1 makes that a virtue at the level of the
**world**, and this is worth saying plainly:

> ⚠ **Lens 1's derive-everything discipline governs the toy. It does not
> obviously govern the game built on top of it.** A borrowed
> known-good game layer over an honestly-modelled medium is what GTA
> actually is, and it is not a compromise of the model.

That is a live question for the quest and progression work, and the
instinct to derive the *game* as well as the *world* has never been
examined as a separate choice.

## Tensions & risks

⚠⚠ **Q1 is unanswered and only a playtest can answer it.** *Is poking at
weather, fire, materials and markets fun with no goal?* A designer's yes
is worth nothing here. ⭐ And [#7](./7-endogenous-value.md) already
supplies the test form: **if people do the work when nothing requires
it, the work is the value.** Same question, and #17 is where it is
asked of the *toy* rather than of the economy.

⚠ **Altitude split.** Q1 is a **title** question — one game's toy may be
delightful and another's dull. **Q2 is platform**: whether anything
built on this substrate can be reached for at all is decided by the
substrate, and by the interface it insists on. An author cannot fix
text's invisibility from content.

⚠ **"Fun to play with" and "honest" are not the same axis**, and it is
easy to assume the second buys the first. An honest thermal model is not
automatically a fun thing to poke — [#93](./93-the-nameless-quality.md)
found the same thing from the aesthetic side, where a derived world
risks reading as machined.

## Implications

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Fold enterability into lens 2 as a clause.** Third sighting,
   one of them verbatim from the book. *An expressive surface that is
   not enterable has not landed.*
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **Run the goal-free playtest before any more progression
   design.** Hand somebody the world with no instructions and **watch
   what they touch.** It answers Q1, it is cheap, and it is the only way
   to be *surprised by what makes it fun* — which is the whole reason
   the braver way is brave.
3. ⭐⭐ **Own the first-thirty-seconds problem as distinct from
   discovery.** Every mechanism we have serves a player who already
   decided to engage. Q2 is about the one who has not.
4. ⭐⭐ **Examine deriving-the-game as a separate choice from
   deriving-the-world.** *GTA came from Pac-Man*: a borrowed game layer
   over an honest medium is not a compromise, and we have never asked
   whether it would be one.
5. ⭐ **Say that the build order is a strategy, not a backlog.** *Medium
   first, game after* is Schell's braver way, and it is more defensible
   stated deliberately than discovered in a status report.
