# #7 · The Lens of Endogenous Value

> One of the [Schell deck](./README.md). Lens named from *A Book of
> Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.[^aogd-ev]

## The lens

*"A game's success hinges on the players' willingness to pretend it is
important."*

**What is valuable to the players in my game? How can I make it more
valuable to them? And what is the relationship between value in the
game and the players' motivations?**

> **From the book.** *Endogenous* is Greg Costikyan's borrowing from
> biology — *"caused by factors inside the organism or system,"
> "internally generated."* His point: **things that have value inside
> the game have value only inside the game.** Monopoly money means
> nothing outside Monopoly, and everything inside it.
>
> ⭐⭐⭐ **Which makes it a measuring instrument**: *"endogenous value is
> an excellent measure of how compelling a game really is."* And the
> test case is brutal —
>
> *"Roulette does not have to be played with real money—it can be
> played with tokens or play money. But the game, on its own, generates
> little endogenous value. **People will only play it when real money
> is at stake, because it just isn't that compelling a game.**"*
>
> At the other end: MMO items that sell for real money outside the
> game, which he reads as proof of how compelling those worlds are.
> *"The value of the items and score in the game is a direct reflection
> of how much players care about succeeding."*

## Which of our seven it sharpens

**[Lens 6 · Economy](../design-lenses.md)** — its second instrument,
alongside [#27 Time](./27-time.md). Secondarily **lens 4**, whose
question *who confers standing* this one asks from the value side.

## ⭐⭐⭐ The roulette test, applied

Schell has handed the education vertical its sharpest possible
formulation, and it is worth stating in his own shape:

> **If the game needs the credential to be worth playing, it is
> roulette.**

Roulette is not condemned for being gambling. It is condemned for
generating **no value of its own** — the stakes have to be imported
because the play produces nothing worth caring about. A game whose
value arrives from outside is a game that failed to make any.

⭐ The project already holds the corresponding commitment — *the game is
educational with zero vertical inputs; external mastery raises only the
ceiling* — and this lens is what turns it from a preference into a
**test with a failure condition**. The question is not *is the
coursework motivating*. It is: **strip every external input and does
anyone still want the sword, the shop, the room, the standing?**

## ⚠⚠ And the bet this lens names precisely

Our value is meant to be **endogenously generated and exogenously
meaningful** — competence derives from in-game evidence (endogenous),
and the claim is that those are *applied hours* which mean something
outside (exogenous). That is the platform's whole wager, and #7 names
its failure mode:

> **If the outside redemption becomes the reason to play, the inside
> stops generating value — and we are roulette with a transcript.**

The direction of travel is what matters. Value generated inside and
*recognised* outside is the bet. Value imported from outside to make
the inside bearable is roulette.

## ⚠ His second question is one we are not allowed to answer

*"How can I make it more valuable to them?"* is a designer deciding
what players ought to value. That is **layer 2**, and
[measurement.md](../measurement.md) says layer 2 is **the subject's** —
*the engine measures, the subject values, the polity imposes.*

So the honest reading is that we may not answer Q2 directly, and the
thing we may do instead is sharper:

> **Make things *matter*; let valuing be theirs.** Consequence,
> scarcity, permanence and irreversibility are the engine's to supply.
> Worth is not.

⭐ That is a real constraint and not a dodge — it rules out most of the
industry's answers to Q2 (make it rare, make it shiny, make it a
badge) while leaving the one that works: **a thing is valuable when
losing it would cost you something the world will remember.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ The value the game promises is the one nothing measures

Question 1 — *what is valuable to the players* — has an answer we do
not like, and the reason we do not like it is the whole entry.

**Money is the most reliable measure of contribution and engagement the
design has.** Not the only one, but by some distance the best
instrumented: it is conserved, audited, chokepointed, and every system
that produces or consumes anything already speaks it. ⚠ **And it became
that by default rather than by choice** — the genre's usual measure of
investment is a combat ladder, we have no hitpoints and will never be
in D&D's lineage, so the economy inherited the job because nothing else
was holding it.

⚠⚠ **But money is explicitly not the value the game promises.**
[Lens 1](../design-lenses.md) is about knowing how the universe works;
[lens 4](../design-lenses.md) is about navigating it with good
judgment. **Neither has anything to do with becoming rich.** So:

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Our best-instrumented value is the one we least want people to
> optimise, and the value we actually promise — understanding, and
> judgment — is the least instrumented thing in the design.**

That is a Goodhart problem of an unusually nasty shape. The familiar
version is *the measure becomes the target*. Ours is **the only good
measure is of a thing we do not want targeted**, which no amount of
care about the measure can fix.

### ⚠⚠ Which inverts the roulette test

Schell's instrument says the more compelling a game is, the more
endogenous value it generates — and *"would people pay real money for
zorkmids"* is his purest form of the question. The honest answer here is
**probably yes, almost to a fault, if this thing works**, which is why
the firewalls between real capital and in-game reward or capability are
being written into **law** rather than left to policy.

> **If money is what players end up caring about, we passed his test and
> failed our own.** The defence we are obliged to build against that
> pressure is the thing he would read as success.

⭐ **And standing does not rescue it.** By his definition standing is the
purest endogenous value here — unbuyable, unexportable, existing nowhere
else in a person's life — but it is still a measure of **regard**, not
of understanding. A person can be widely esteemed and wrong about the
world. It is the better *currency*; it is not the promised *value*.

### ⚠⚠ But the question is asked at the wrong altitude

*"What is valuable to the players **in my game**"* presumes there is
one game and it is ours. **There is not.** The educational game is one
title on a platform that ships an authoring stack and an open licence,
and the deck's whole frame — one designer, one artifact, one set of
values — is the thing [#37](./37-fairness.md) already found it cannot
see past.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Q1 has as many answers as there are games**, and the
> platform's job is not to pick one. It is to make each of them
> **expressible and measurable** by whoever is building.

Which re-homes the Goodhart finding above rather than dissolving it:
**money-outweighing-understanding is the educational game's problem**,
and the platform's version is a *capability* question —

> **Can an author make their game value what they want it to value?**
> If not, that is a failure of [lens 2](../design-lenses.md), not of
> lens 1.

⭐ **And it settles the speedrun worry.** Someone will optimise for
finishing fast; that is not the experience this title sells and would
likely be hollow *in it*. But the toolkit is open, and **a game built
on it and tuned for speedrunners is a legitimate output, not a
leak.** The platform fails only if it can express one set of values.
(The livestream-community audience is the same point with a different
face — game enthusiasts value different things than students, and the
substrate has to survive both.)

### ⭐⭐ So how *would* the promised value be measured — without a test?

⛔ **"Show your work" is already decided and the answer was no.** The
consensus on making a player demonstrate reasoning is that **people
hate it and it does not scale**, and nothing here reopens that.

⭐⭐⭐ **The counter is not a metric, it is just good game design:**

> **Make the experience compelling enough that people do the work when
> they do not have to.** It need not involve doing maths. If deriving
> how the world behaves is the *fun*, then nobody has to be graded on
> having derived it.

And that is **Schell's own instrument, turned back on the problem** —
because the roulette test *is* the measure:

> **If people do the work when nothing requires it, the work is the
> value.** That is endogenous value in Costikyan's exact sense, and it
> is checkable by playtest rather than by instrumentation.

⭐ It also repairs the practice/understanding split from a different
direction. *Competence rises on volume* only measures attendance **if
the volume is joyless**. If the reason to do the thing again is that
working it out is enjoyable, **time spent is time deriving**, and the
measure we already have stops being a proxy for the wrong quantity.

⚠ **The residue is real and stays open.** *Novelty versus repetition* —
did the principle transfer to an unfamiliar case — remains the only
mechanical signal anyone has proposed, and the transcript already holds
what it would need. ⚠ It is also the exact point where a platform is
most tempted to become an exam: `measurement.md`'s layers hold, so
**the engine may measure; it may not grade.**

## The inversion: his top of the scale is our breach

He reads real-money trade in MMO items as the proof of a compelling
world. We treat money reaching standing as a **failure whatever else
the feature does.**

⭐ That is principled rather than squeamish, and the reason is
structural: **his games have no standing layer.** Selling a sword
corrupts nothing, because nothing in the game claims the sword means
you are anybody. Selling standing would corrupt the polity, because
standing is what the polity runs on. Same signal, opposite reading,
because we built a second thing for value to attach to.

⚠ The residue he identifies stays real: **the design's conservation
machinery makes real-money overflow likely *if it succeeds*.** Deciding
the stance — prohibit, channel, embrace — before player wealth exists
is recorded as an open ask in
[lens-deck-salvage.md](../lens-deck-salvage.md) and is still open.

## Tensions & risks

⚠⚠ **Q1 is unanswerable today, and will be until somebody plays.**
*What is valuable to the players* has no informants. Every claim in this
entry about what will be valued is a designer's guess, including the
confident ones.

⚠ **Three kinds of value, three different integrity rules** — the
classification the retired deck left behind and which still holds:
**pure-play** (arbitrary is fine; Monopoly money owes nothing to
anyone), **effort-anchored** (the anchor must hold — a competence that
does not trace to evidence is a lie), **conserved-economic** (supply
must not lie). ⚠ A design that does not say which kind it is minting
will get the integrity rule wrong.

⚠ **The does-nothing failure.** A thing that looks valuable and affects
nothing is the cheapest way to fail this lens: no gauge, no item
property, no standing may exist that changes no outcome. Players find
these fast and the discovery is corrosive, because it teaches that the
other things may be hollow too.

⚠ **Deepening beats minting.** Every new currency of meaning competes
for the same finite willingness-to-pretend. Attaching worth to what
already exists is nearly always better than inventing a parallel scale
— and the parallel scale is always the easier build.

## Implications

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Adopt the roulette test as the vertical's acceptance
   criterion.** *Strip every external input — does anyone still want
   the sword, the shop, the room?* It is a playtest, it is cheap, and a
   failure is the single most important thing this project could learn
   early.
2. ⭐⭐ **Say that Q2 is not ours.** *Make things matter; let valuing be
   theirs* belongs in the rubric — it forecloses the badge-and-rarity
   reflex on principle rather than on taste.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **Ask Q1 per game, not once.** The platform's obligation is
   that an author can make their title value what they choose — money,
   understanding, speed, spectacle — and measure it. **A substrate that
   only expresses one set of values has failed lens 2**, whatever it
   thinks of the values.
4. ⭐⭐ **Treat "is deriving fun?" as the measurement of understanding,
   and playtest it.** The roulette test answers it: *do people do the
   work when nothing requires it?* ⛔ Not *show your work*, which is
   settled and rejected — people hate it and it does not scale.
5. ⭐⭐ **Say out loud that money became the engagement measure by
   default.** No hitpoints, no combat ladder, so the economy inherited
   the job. That is worth knowing when deciding whether to keep it.
6. ⭐ **Make every value-bearing element declare its kind** — pure-play,
   effort-anchored, conserved — since each inherits a different
   integrity rule and getting them crossed is how anchors quietly break.
7. **Settle the real-money stance before wealth exists.** Still open,
   and it gets harder to decide every month there is more to trade.
