# Lens: Resonance

> Part of the [design lenses](./README.md) set. Lens named from Jesse
> Schell's *A Book of Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.
>
> **Fresh pass, entry 13 (2026-07-28), net-new** — first of the
> videos quartet (resonance / moments / interest curve / pitch). The
> consumer is the video work: a resonant theme is stated once and
> felt everywhere, and the videos need to know what it is.
>
> **Layer interrogated: both, deliberately** — the theme must ring at
> the platform layer (any vertical) and the game layer (this one).

## The lens

Look for hidden power. Four questions: **what is it about my game
that feels powerful and special? When I describe it to people, what
ideas get them really excited? If I had no constraints of any kind,
what would this be? I have instincts about how this should be — what
is driving those instincts?**

> **From the book.** The Toontown case study: three subthemes
> (networked play, escapism, "simpler but more powerful") wouldn't
> cohere until the team found play's natural opponent — work — and
> landed the truth-based theme *"work wants to destroy play, but
> play must survive"* (swap in slavery/freedom "and the power of this
> theme becomes clear"). Themes are experience-based or truth-based;
> "resonant themes elevate your work from craft to art"; "an artist
> is someone who takes you where you could never go alone, and a
> theme is the vehicle." And the method warning that governs this
> entry: **"you can never tell which themes are resonant just through
> logic — you have to feel the resonance, deep inside
> yourself."**[^aogd-re]

## Why our design prompts it

The videos need one theme stated once and felt everywhere — the
existing video doctrine (philosophy as the recurring "why," never
lectured) already assumes a theme exists; this lens asks us to name
it and feel-test it. And the design has *many* powerful truths (the
fresh pass has surfaced a dozen) — which is exactly the Toontown
starting position: strong subthemes, no ratified theme.

## What the design answers

### The four questions, answered from the record

- **What feels powerful and special?** The honesty, every time it
  surfaces: the world that doesn't lie — real liters, real Ohm's
  law, receipts for everything. The fifth essence clause ("the world
  itself honors what you understand") is this feeling, ratified.
- **What gets people excited when described?** The demonstrations
  of consequence: the wet firewood that won't light *for the real
  reason*; the armor that betrays you to lightning; the exam that
  changes who your character is. Excitement tracks the moment a
  listener realizes *the model is real and therefore knowledge
  works*.
- **No constraints at all?** The boundless-reach claim: any
  sufficiently-digitized community mirrored into a world that
  recognizes its members' real effort — the university of
  everything, with this game as its first campus.
- **What drives the instincts?** The manifesto's spine
  (assert-don't-defend; mirror real life in real units; never fudge)
  and the two ratified essences. The instincts are consistent; they
  were always circling the same center.

### Candidate themes (for feel-testing, not ratified)

Schell's rule forbids ratifying by argument, so this entry ends in
candidates — each in a form a video could state once and then let
every scene demonstrate:

1. **Truth-based, with the Toontown conflict shape:** *"The world
   wants to tell you what you are; here, what you are is what
   you've done."* (Opponent: ascription — being told your worth.
   Bite without grievance.)
2. **Truth-based, the fifth clause weaponized:** *"A world that
   cannot lie about what you've earned."* (Opponent: every system —
   game or institution — where the score and the substance parted
   ways.)
3. **Experience-based, the Rico conversion:** *"The moment work
   becomes play — because the question became yours."* (The
   education vertical's heart; warmer, less martial.)

The feel-test is the user's: which one, said aloud over the
conferral footage, raises the hair on your arms? That answer — not
this analysis — is the ratification.

## Tensions & risks

- **Many strong subthemes is the Toontown trap** — honesty, earned
  becoming, persistence, community memory are all true and all
  *sub*themes until one theme subordinates them. Videos that each
  pick a favorite will feel like different products.
- **A resonance chosen by committee is craft, not art.** This entry
  can shortlist; it must not vote. If none of the three candidates
  rings, the right move is more listening, not more analysis.
- **The theme must survive both layers.** Toontown's theme worked
  for kids *and* parents; ours must ring for a gamer hearing "what
  you are is what you've done" and an education executive hearing
  the same sentence. Candidates 1 and 2 pass that test on paper;
  paper isn't the test.

## Implications

1. **Run the feel-test and ratify one theme** — then thread it: the
   videos state it once (probably in the series opener), every
   entry's "exec read" derives from it, and the manifesto re-record
   checks against it. Until ratified, video scripting proceeds on
   experiences (which are theme-safe) but holds the connective
   narration.
2. **Subordinate, don't multiply:** once ratified, the other
   candidates become named subthemes — the honesty theme carries
   earned-becoming inside it (or vice versa), the way work-vs-play
   carried escapism and simpler-but-stronger.
3. **The theme is a cut-test for video content:** any beat that
   doesn't demonstrate the theme is a candidate for the cutting
   room, however cool — Schell's "use it for all it is worth" is
   spend advice, and it spends on selection.
