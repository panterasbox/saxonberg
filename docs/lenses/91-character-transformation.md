# #91 · The Lens of Character Transformation

> One of the [Schell deck](./README.md). Lens named from *A Book of
> Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.[^aogd-ct]

## The lens

We pay attention to character transformation because we care about what
might change *us*. How does each character change over the course of
the game? **How are those changes communicated to the player — could
they be communicated more clearly, or more strongly?** Is there enough
change? Are the changes surprising and interesting? **Are the changes
believable?**

> **From the book.** The famous characters, Schell argues, are famous
> because they *"do what nearly every successful movie or book does—let
> events change the main character over time."* His method is a
> **character transformation chart**: characters down the left, the
> sections of your story along the top, and a mark wherever a character
> undergoes some kind of change — *"Some transformations are temporary
> and small, others are great and permanent."* He is candid that
> meaningful change is not possible for the protagonist of every game,
> and suggests giving it to the sidekick or the villain instead.

## Which of our seven it sharpens

**[Lens 4 · Values](../design-lenses.md).** It is the lens that names
lens 4's largest gap, and it does so by asking a question the rest of
the rubric never asks: not *what does the player decide*, but *what does
the player become, and how do they find out?*

## Why our design prompts it

Schell writes it for authored characters. Every question transfers
intact to a player avatar in a simulation — and transferring them is
uncomfortable, because our answer to *does the character change* is
currently **economic**. You own more, you can reach further, your
competence bands have moved. That is accumulation. Whether it is
**transformation** is the open question, and this lens is the only one
in the deck that asks it plainly.

## What the design answers

**The substrate is unusually strong.** Three ledgers describe a person:
what they **HAVE** (assets), what they **CAN DO** (competence), and what
they **ARE** (standing). Traits accrete from what you actually did
rather than from what you chose at creation; the chronicle separates
deed from claim; competence derives on read from evidenced acts.

⭐⭐ **"Are the changes believable?" is where we are strongest, and it is
not close.** Every change traces to something the player did. There is
no bought becoming, no granted becoming, no roll that made you someone
else. Most games cannot answer this question at all; ours answers it by
construction, and the answer is the [lens 1](../design-lenses.md)
derivability property applied to the self.

**"Is there enough change?"** — honest answer: **not yet, and we know
why.** Of the three ledgers, HAVE and CAN DO are online and **ARE is
not.** Standing is the one that would carry transformation, because it
is the one other people can see.

## Tensions & risks

⚠⚠ **The gap is communication, not substrate.** His second question —
*how are those changes communicated to the player, could they be
communicated more clearly or more strongly* — is the one XP answers
cheaply, constantly, and unbidden. We answer it on pull
([#55](./55-visible-progress.md)). **A change nobody notices is,
for this lens, a change that did not happen**, however believable it
was.

⚠ **His method does not port.** The transformation chart wants
characters down one axis and story sections along the other. We have
neither: no cast list for the player, and no acts. What replaces it is
the real question, and the tempting answer — *the ledger is the chart* —
is wrong, because **nobody reads a ledger as an arc.**

⚠ **Temporary vs. permanent is undifferentiated.** We model both — a
condition is temporary, a competence band is durable — but nothing in
how they reach the player marks which is which. Schell's distinction is
narrative, and narratively they arrive the same way.

⚠⚠ **The failure mode to watch for is the annual report.** The obvious
fix for "communicate the change more strongly" is a periodic prose
summary of ledger deltas. **That is a gauge wearing narration** — it
converts becoming back into arithmetic and fails lens 4 exactly as a
progress bar would, while looking literary enough to pass review.

## Implications

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Say plainly that the gap is communication, so the levelling
   conversation does not build more ledger.** We do not need another
   measurement; we need the ones we have to reach the person they are
   about. Building a tenth ledger would be the most natural wrong move
   available.
2. ⭐⭐ **ARE is the missing third, and its absence is the thin
   character-change story.** Standing is what other people can see,
   which makes it the only one of the three that transformation can
   ride. Treat its build as this lens's assigned work.
3. ⭐ **Narration adapters are the honest mechanism** — state → arc →
   per-viewer beats, the combat pattern generalized (recorded in
   [lens-deck-salvage.md](../lens-deck-salvage.md)). A *change* adapter
   is the honest form of a level-up message: it narrates what happened
   to you, from the world's point of view, without printing a delta.
4. ⭐ **Claim believability out loud.** *Every change traces to a deed*
   is a claim almost no game can make, and it is currently a silent
   property of the architecture rather than something a player is ever
   told. It is also the answer to anyone who asks why we refuse the
   cheaper progression tools.
5. ⚠ **Mark temporary vs. permanent where the change is delivered.** If
   a player cannot tell a condition from a becoming, the lens's
   "surprising and interesting" question has no honest answer.
6. ⚠ **Add the annual-report failure to review.** Any proposal to
   summarize a player's ledger deltas in prose is a gauge until proven
   otherwise.
