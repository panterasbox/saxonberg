# #13 · The Lens of Infinite Inspiration

> One of the [Schell deck](./README.md). Lens named from *A Book of
> Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.[^aogd-ii]
>
> ⭐ **Restored 2026-10-01 at the owner's request** — *"it's my favorite
> lens in the book and I wanna make sure it's in our deck."* The retired
> entry left exactly one ratified line behind in
> [lens-deck-salvage.md](../lens-deck-salvage.md): **reality-as-taught is
> the inspiration library; curricula are content roadmaps.** That line is
> still right and it is only half the lens — the half that is safe to say.

## The lens

The chapter is about where ideas come from, and the lens arrives attached
to the best story in the book. Schell, a teenager at a juggling
convention, watches a man doing moves nobody else is doing and asks him
where he gets them.

> *"They all get their tricks from each other. **Which is fine — you can
> learn a lot that way. But it will never make you stand out.**"*
>
> *"So where do you get them?" I asked. "Books?"*
>
> *"Ha! Books. That's a good one. No, not books. You wanna know the
> secret? **The secret is: don't look to other jugglers for inspiration —
> look everywhere else.**"*
>
> A looping pattern, arms spiralling, occasional pirouettes — *"I learned
> that one watching **a ballet** in New York."* Balls popping up and down
> under fluttering hands — *"I learned that from **a flock of geese** I saw
> take off from a lake up in Maine."* A weird mechanical movement where the
> balls seem to turn right angles — *"I learned that from **a paper punch
> machine** on Long Island."*
>
> *"People try to copy these moves, but they can't."* He points at a
> juggler across the gym doing the ballet move. ⭐⭐⭐ ***"But it just
> looked dumb. Something was missing, but I couldn't say what."***
>
> ***"See, these guys can copy my moves, but they can't copy my
> inspiration."***

**The lens**, epigraph *"When you know how to listen, everybody is the
guru" — Ram Dass*:

> **To use this lens, stop looking at your game and stop looking at games
> like it. Instead, look everywhere else.**
>
> 1. **What is an experience I have had in my life that I would want to
>    share with others?**
> 2. **In what small way can I capture the essence of that experience and
>    put it into my game?**

> *"You must be willing to try the impossible — for surely it is
> impossible for a roll of the dice to capture the excitement of a
> swordfight or for a videogame to make a player feel afraid of the dark —
> isn't it?… When you have concrete visions based on real life that guide
> your decision making…"*

⚠ Note what kind of lens this is. Almost every other instrument in the
deck examines **the artifact**. This one examines **the designer**, and
its questions are in the first person singular. It cannot be run by
committee, and it cannot be run on somebody else's behalf.

## Which of our seven it sharpens

**[Lens 2 · Creative expression](../design-lenses.md)**, and the
distinction is the whole value of having it:

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Lens 2 asks whether an author *can* express themselves. This lens
> asks whether they have anything to express.** Those are different
> questions, and only the first one is an architecture problem.
>
> **A perfect expression substrate handed to someone with no inspiration
> produces convergence** — which is a measurable outcome, not a worry (see
> below).

Secondarily **lens 1 · pedagogy**, and in an uncomfortable direction —
⚠ our pedagogy *supplies subject matter*, which is the most convincing
possible substitute for inspiration.

## ⚠⚠ The conflict this lens has with our stated strategy

We have a declared method, and it is the thing the juggler is warning
against:

> **Owner, on the trade chains:** *"none of these things we invented, we
> basically explicitly ripped off EU5's concept of RGOs and applied the
> concept to an MMO. **most of our systems are inspired by other games
> actually, what's unique is our particular synthesis** of all these
> different ideas."*

That is *"they all get their tricks from each other."* And the defence —
**synthesis** — is real: [`30`](./30-emergence.md) carries it as a rule
(*borrow to the depth the combination needs, not the depth the source
went*), and Schell's own body text licenses it (*"each can build on
different inspirations, blending them together to create something
entirely new"*).

⭐⭐⭐ **But the resolution is finer than "synthesis is fine," and it turns
on what an RGO actually is.** An RGO is not a juggling trick. It is **a
model of a real thing** — a goods-producing site, which is agriculture
and extraction and von Thünen. So:

> **Borrowing a model is borrowing somebody else's reading of the world.**
> It works, and it is strictly *downstream* of looking at the thing
> itself. The game you took it from did the looking.

Which locates the risk precisely. Not *"we borrow from games"* — borrowing
a **model** is cheap and sound. The risk is borrowing a **shape**: a
building list, a stat spread, a quest structure, an interface convention.
**A model has a referent you can go check. A shape has only its source.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ Reality-as-taught is a real advantage, and the most seductive substitute

The salvaged line stands and it is genuinely unusual: **for an educational
platform, "look everywhere else" is not an exhortation, it is the product
spec.** The curriculum is a standing list of non-game experiences we are
*obliged* to render, inexhaustible, pre-structured, and externally
validated. No other game gets that.

⚠⚠ **And that is exactly why it is dangerous.** Re-read the lens's first
question:

> *What is an experience **I have had in my life** that I would want to
> share with others?*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **A curriculum is not an experience anyone had. It is a list of
> topics.** Reality-as-taught gives us **subject matter**; it cannot give
> us **inspiration.** The syllabus says *blood typing is a topic*; it does
> not say what it was like to watch a transfusion.

That is the half of this lens the salvaged line left out, and it is the
half with teeth. **Subject matter arrives for free and feels like
inspiration.** A design that mistakes the two produces content that is
accurate, well-modelled, pedagogically sound, and dead — the
textbook-shaped failure, which is precisely the thing the vertical pitch
exists to avoid.

## ⭐⭐⭐ The evidence, and it is measured rather than asserted

A 45,000-file multi-author corpus was read and counted in
[eotl-craft.md](../eotl-craft.md) / [eotl-census.md](../eotl-census.md),
and the result is this lens's thesis with numbers on it.

**What came from outside games, and was memorable:**

| | from |
|---|---|
| an abattoir where *"this is where the humans are taken to the slaughter"* and the prince's box seats are **fine tanned leather** | Swift, and slaughterhouses |
| a mine noticeboard rotating *"Fourth level closed due to drow sightings"* and ⭐ ***"Accidents down from last month"*** | **every workplace noticeboard anyone has stood in front of** |
| a 150-word character study of a middle manager who *"will still be at his post while anyone under him will suffer the incrimination"* | an office |
| five levels over the 49-day interval after death, each failure naming the defilement its deity transmutes | **a religious text** |
| a ski resort awarding *partial stats* only on a slope difficult **relative to your current stats** | learning to ski |
| *"Paving technology must not have been invented yet in this region"* | a dirt road |

**What came from inside games, and converged:**

```
temple 81% · bank 76% · inn 71% · weapons 71% ·
armoury 67% · general 67% · market 62%          ← the checklist
                                                   derived by authors
library 29% · magic 19% · … pawn 10% ·             LOOKING AT EACH OTHER
jeweller 10% · casino 5%                        ← where the distinctiveness
                                                   was, and nobody went
```

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Every memorable thing in that corpus came from outside games.
> Every forgettable thing came from inside.** Mean pairwise overlap between
> cities: **0.39**, concentrated entirely in the mandatory seven.
>
> **The checklist city is the juggler with the ponytail.** It has all the
> moves. ***It just looked dumb. Something was missing, but I couldn't say
> what.***

⭐⭐ And the control case is the one area the owner remembered three
decades later, unprompted, by its author's name: **no temple, no armoury,
no weapons shop, no general store, no market** — a bakery, a jeweller and
a library, three of the four rarest institutions in 45,000 files.
**You cannot buy armour there.** Its density is why it is *good*; **its
missing checklist is why it is itself.**

## ⚠⚠ What it says about how this deck is being written

This lens implicates its own research method, and the entry should say so.

A long session was spent reading EotL closely and extracting fourteen
techniques — refrain-plus-absence, the detail chain, specify-by-household-object.
**That is looking at another juggler**, and Schell's verdict on it is
explicit and not dismissive: *"Which is fine — you can learn a lot that
way. **But it will never make you stand out.**"*

> ⭐⭐ **So [eotl-craft.md](../eotl-craft.md) is the "learn a lot that way"
> half, and it cannot be anything else.** It is a book of patterns. Nothing
> in it is inspiration and nothing in it could be — **a technique is a
> move.** The doc is more useful for knowing that about itself.

⭐ And the ancestor itself knew the difference. Its design notes cite
**non-game sources, directly, as the brief**: *"If you don't know what I
mean by the desc of the place, **watch Clash of the Titans.** Yah, figure
it out."* · *"based on my personal knowledge, and a really old edition of
the AD&D book **Deities and Demigods**."* · a witches' eye *"about the size
of **a mug coaster**… resembles **a paper weight**."*

> **That last one is the lens working at the smallest possible scale.** A
> mythological artifact specified by two objects on the writer's desk —
> *look everywhere else*, in nine words, and it transmits perfectly.

## Tensions & risks

⚠⚠ **It cannot be delegated, and the person who holds it has said the
work is undone.** *"I have a good idea of the kind of content I'd like to
see **I just haven't written it down anywhere**. I certainly have my own
unique style as a content author even though I haven't gotten to do much
of it in this project because it's all platform level right now."*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The content-vision document is this lens's two questions, asked
> of one person, and that is why nobody else can write it.** Everything a
> collaborator can supply — a census, a technique catalogue, a precedent
> survey, a declaration schema — is the *second* question's machinery. The
> first question has one possible author.

⚠ **And "everyone is an author" multiplies the problem rather than
solving it.** A thousand authors with a substrate and no inspiration
produce the convergence table above — **that is literally how it was
produced.** The platform cannot supply inspiration, and the two levers it
*does* have are weak: ⭐ the declaration form can **ask** what a thing is
from, and the review can **notice** when the answer is another area.

⚠ **The honest limit of the lens.** Its questions presume a designer with
a life to draw on and the leisure to introspect. The corpus's own record
is of authors with *"a bunch of life stuff to do that kept me occupied
enough not to code."* **Inspiration is not the binding constraint on
content volume; attention is** — and this lens has nothing to say about
that, which is a reason to hold it beside **#18 Passion** — unwritten, and now
the entry this one most wants next to it.

## Implications

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Split subject matter from inspiration, in writing.** The
   curriculum is a **content roadmap**; it is not a source of feeling.
   Treating them as one is the specific failure mode an educational game
   is most exposed to, and it currently has no name in our docs.
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **Add *what is this from?* to the declaration form**
   ([content-declaration-slate](../slates/builds/content-declaration-slate.md)).
   Not mechanical provenance — **the inspiration.** The ancestor's best
   authors volunteered it unprompted (*watch Clash of the Titans*), and it
   is the one field that makes *"this is a checklist of what other areas
   have"* visible to a reviewer.
3. ⭐⭐ **The distinctiveness rule the convergence table implies**:
   *a settlement is distinctive in proportion to how many of its
   institutions appear nowhere else* — already recorded in
   `eotl-craft.md`, and this lens is why it is a design rule rather than
   an observation.
4. ⭐ **Borrow models, not shapes.** A model has a referent you can go
   check; a shape has only its source. [`30`](./30-emergence.md)'s
   borrowing rule covers depth; this adds the *kind*.
5. ⚠ **Say what the craft doc is.** It is a pattern book, which Schell
   calls useful and insufficient in the same breath. The caveat belongs in
   the doc.
