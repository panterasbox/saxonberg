# Lens: The Pitch

> Part of the [design lenses](./README.md) set. Lens named from Jesse
> Schell's *A Book of Lenses*; questions paraphrased, analysis our own.
>
> **Fresh pass, entry 16 (2026-07-28), net-new** — last of the videos
> quartet. Consumer: the study.com approach and the education-video
> track that fronts it.
>
> **Layer interrogated: neither — this one aims at us**, the people
> carrying the thing into a room.

## The lens

Four questions: **why are you pitching this game to this client?
What will you consider "a successful pitch"? What's in it for the
people you are pitching to? What do the people you are pitching to
need to know about your game?**

> **From the book.** "Every game begins with an idea, but it gets
> funded with a pitch" — so **"design your pitch as well as you
> design your game."** The audience rule that matters most for us:
> "if you are pitching to an executive, the most important thing to
> them will be whether the game can make money, and how much."
> Schell also points at Brian Upton's *30 Things I Hate About Your
> Game Pitch* as the canonical pet-peeve list — worth a read before
> any live meeting. (His surrounding crowdfunding notes carry one
> transferable point: pitches travel when the audience gains by
> spreading them.)[^aogd-pi]

## Why our design prompts it

Because the pitch is now a designed artifact in progress — the
education-video track, the strategy doc, the demo beat — and this
lens is the checklist that keeps all of it aimed. Also because the
insider interview established the exact room: four executives, no
assumed gamer literacy, at a company whose game-shaped ideas have
historically died as hackathon demos. The pitch design problem is
real and specific.

## What the design answers

### Q1 — Why this game to this client?

Because the fit is structural, not opportunistic: their taxonomy
and verified assessment are exactly what the credential seam
consumes; their stated strategy (completion, confidence, barrier
removal) is exactly what the world's motivation engine addresses;
and their content library is, per the infinite-inspiration
corollary, literally an index of what the world can become. No
other partner makes the seam's first instantiation this clean —
and the founder-insider (the user) exists only here. That's the
honest answer, and it's strong.

### Q2 — What is a successful pitch?

**A greenlit pilot** — one cohort, one course, a control group,
agreed metrics — plus permission to keep the working demo alive
against real credential events. Not a partnership agreement, not
budget, not a roadmap slot. (The strategy doc's models B/C/E are
*outcomes of a successful pilot*, never the ask.) Defining success
this small is what makes yes cheap — the anti-hackathon-death move
is a proposal an executive can approve without spending political
capital.

### Q3 — What's in it for them?

The strategy doc's stakeholder map, compressed to Schell's
executive rule ("can it make money, and how much"): **churn** (the
transcript becomes an identity that outlives the goal), **completion**
(the metric they already hurt on, moved by a consequence layer
outside the content), **moat** (a persistent credentialed world
no content competitor or LLM can commoditize), **efficacy story**
(publishable applied-transfer outcomes). Per-exec emphasis:
Dansbury completion, Mancini acquisition/moat, Shmeir cost-and-risk
(one outbound event feed; pilot off a warehouse export), Ridner
mission and differentiation.

### Q4 — What do they need to know?

Four things, in this order, and almost nothing else:

1. **The game is fun on its own feet** (the toy verdict; fun is
   the carrier — shown, not claimed: this is what the videos are
   *for*).
2. **The learning model already exists inside it** (claim/deed,
   bands, the University — education is the core, not a skin).
3. **Their product plugs in as an issuer, unchanged** (assessment
   authority untouched; floor playable without them; integration =
   consuming events they already record).
4. **The stakes stay honest** (the high-stakes reconciliation: the
   avatar borrows its stakes; no manufactured urgency; rigor as an
   unlock, not a barrier).

What they do *not* need: architecture, the polity, the manifesto,
the lens library. Everything the fresh pass produced serves the
pitch by making these four sentences *true and demonstrable*, not
by appearing in it.

### The videos are the pitch, designed

"Design your pitch as well as you design your game" — the video
track is exactly that: each video one moment made powerful
(moments), curve-checked (interest curve), carrying one slice of
the theme (resonance), with the exec read derived at the end in
their vocabulary (the experience-first principle). The Disney
question gets its answer from the moments entry's top-ten list.
And Schell's virality note applies inside a company too: the pitch
should be **forwardable** — a video an engineer-champion can send
to a VP with one sentence attached is worth ten meetings we're not
in.

## Tensions & risks

- **The room decides on money; the product decides on fun.** The
  pitch must hold both without letting either corrupt the other —
  the videos stay reference-doc honest (no hype), the business
  case stays out of the videos (the are/are-not rules). The
  lens's executive rule is about *emphasis in the room*, not about
  changing the artifact.
- **Q2's smallness is fragile.** Every conversation will pull
  toward bigger asks (new SKUs, roadmaps). The discipline is
  pre-committed: pilot or nothing; everything else is "after the
  pilot reads out."
- **The insider is also the pitcher** — the user's employment cuts
  both ways (access and credibility vs. perceived conflict).
  How to sequence disclosure and who fronts the meeting are
  judgment calls this entry flags and does not make.
- **Upton's list is unread.** Before any live pitch, read it; a
  known pet-peeve catalog is free error-avoidance.

## Implications

1. **The pitch is a designed artifact with an owner and a spec:**
   the four Q4 sentences, demonstrated by the videos, sized to the
   Q2 ask, tuned per Q3 stakeholder. Anything added must displace
   something — the pitch has a budget like any design.
2. **Build for forwardability:** every video ends in a state where
   a champion can send it upward cold — self-contained, titled by
   its moment, exec read included. The internal-champion path
   (designers and engineers who already heard the idea) is a
   distribution channel to design for, not an anecdote.
3. **Prepare the Disney answer** (moments entry) and **read Upton**
   before any live room.
4. **Success-criteria discipline:** the pilot ask is written down
   (strategy doc) and doesn't inflate in the room. If the room
   offers more, take notes, not commitments — the demo's integrity
   is the leverage.
