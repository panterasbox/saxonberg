## Narration (draft — plain explainer)

**[1 — this one's for the organizer]**
Every other chapter spoke to a community. This one is for the person starting one. And
if you're standing it up, the elaborate version raises a fair worry: three chambers,
courts, a whole republic — do you switch all that on at once, and hand the keys to a
crowd that doesn't exist yet? No. The same process runs at any size, a population of one
or a million; what changes is only how much the *community* drives and how much *you*
do. That's the dial — from *you decide everything* to *the community decides
everything* — and this chapter is how you turn it: bootstrapping from one person to a
full republic on your own schedule, never forced to let go before you're ready.

**[2 — the honest floor the dial sits on]**
Start with the part that doesn't move. Wherever you set the dial — running the place
yourself, or handing it all to the community — three things are guaranteed, identical
at every setting, and not even you can switch them off. **Money can never buy power** —
the firewall. **The record can never be faked** — anyone can check what really
happened. And **anyone can always leave** — walk out with their account, their
standing, and everything they've built, and set up somewhere else. Three slabs.
Bedrock. The honest floor the dial sits on.

**[3 — the dial: you already run a government]**
Now the dial itself — everything above the floor is yours to set. And this is where it
stops being one game and becomes a machine any community can pick up. A Discord, a
guild, a stream. Here's the part worth sitting with: you're already running a
government — you just call it
moderation. The operator is the executive. The mods are the police. The unwritten
rules are the law. The ban appeals are the courts. The mod-log is the record. This
doesn't drop a government onto a blank slate; it formalizes the one every community
already has. And rule-of-law is a gift to whoever runs the place: "you broke rule
four, here's the log, you can appeal" lets the rules take the heat instead of you —
and discretion stops scaling the moment you get big, while this doesn't. So you adopt
the floor for free, then turn the dial as far as you trust your people — from "the
operator decides everything," a streamer running their own show, all the way to "full
republic," where the community decides everything. Where you sit is your call. It was
never all-or-nothing.

**[4 — the founder's control falls out of contribution]**
So how much does the founder start with — and how much do they have to hand over? At
the start: all of it, and none of it. They hold every branch from day one, and handing
any of it over is purely their call. The holding itself isn't a carve-out, though — it
*falls out* of the same machine everyone else runs on. Remember: the laws are code, you
write them, and authority here is the natural effect of that — it follows the work. Early on, the founder is doing all the work: building the
world, funding it. So the ordinary rules hand them the ordinary reward, which happens to
be control. Here's how that falls out, chamber by chamber.

Take the **capital** chamber — the funders' count. The founder puts up the early money
and labor, and they're compensated for it — but paid in stake, not cash. Picture a dollar
of pay plowed straight back into the game as capital: it never reaches their pocket, it
just mints standing. That's the ordinary startup move — founders granted equity for the
work they put in — applied to the chamber; and the grant is sized to the community's own
funding, matched dollar for dollar plus one unit on top, so their share always lands a
hair past half: a standing majority of the funders, held for as long as they keep putting
in. No real money changes hands, so the capital pool itself never grows — only the
standing does. Which is exactly why it's *control*, not cash: a **vote**, never a payout.

The same work earns standing in the **labor** chamber — the makers' count. At the
outset the founder is the only maker, so that's a hundred percent. Then, as
collaborators come on board and build alongside them, it **dilutes** — automatically,
by the same rule that credited the founder in the first place. Their grip on the makers
loosens exactly as fast as other people actually make things.

The **consumer** chamber they get nothing automatic — that standing is for people who
show up and play, earned like anyone's, or not at all. But they don't need it. A
decision takes a majority of the chambers, two out of three — and capital and labor are
already two. Consumer is the one the founder can let go from day one and still run the
place.

**[5 — control of the chambers hands you the branches]**
And holding two of three chambers hands you the other two branches as well. The
executive comes for free — the PM is the legislature's to elect, so the chambers *are*
the executive, same stroke. The judiciary is the one that doesn't come by math:
remember, no judge to appoint, just juries drawn by lot from citizens past a seniority
bar. But the founder is the most senior citizen there is, by construction — set the bar
at day one and the whole pool is you, so every jury is you. That's the branch you hold
only by keeping the pool small; lower the bar and the courts pass to real citizens on
their own. A clock you can't fake — the first thing to leave your hands as you open up.

The same control has a plainer payoff, too. The chambers are the legislature, and the
legislature writes the budget — so an operator who was already making a living keeps
making it. Their income becomes a line in the budget they control, paid out the way a
salary is: the same revenue as before, with a couple of extra steps.

So early on, one person genuinely runs it — all three branches — not by exception, but
because they're doing the contributing, and contribution is what confers authority.
Transparent, every unit on the record.

**[6 — graduated participation]**
And holding all of it doesn't mean wielding all of it. An operator can keep the reins and
still hand specific decisions to the community, one at a time — the mechanism is just
**abstention.** On a question they'd otherwise decide, they hold their weight off the
scale; the vote still reaches quorum and resolves on everyone else's, so the community's
judgment carries. Defer the calls you want to, hold the ones you don't. A member has the
mirror image — the delegation from the standing chapter: hand your vote to someone you
trust so you needn't track every issue. Run it all yourself, or hand off as much as you
like — nobody's forced to carry the whole load.

**[7 — tentative until ratification]**
None of it is permanent yet. It all stays provisional until **ratification** — the point
the community locks in what it wants to keep, written into the constitution as kernel-level
rules. And you don't draft that from scratch. There's a **roster of ready-made
amendments** — a due-process right, free expression, term limits, and the rest — that
the founder and community pick from, à la carte, and bolt in. That's really what the
dial *is*: each amendment is a notch, and ratification is where you set it and weld it
in place. And the weld holds because a constitutional rule sits *above* ordinary law: a
normal law a simple majority can change next week, but an amendment takes a supermajority
across every chamber to enact — and the same to repeal. That higher bar is what makes
ratification a *commit* and not just another vote. Nothing forces the founder to install
any of them — lock in the top-down arrangement, and that's a legitimate constitution
too — but this is the moment the permanent shape gets chosen.

You hold the dial: turn it the day you're ready, or don't. **The structure doesn't
force you to become a democrat. It just makes it safe to.**

**Kickers:** "the same process runs at a population of one or a million" · "a floor
nobody can break, a dial you control" · "you already run a government — you just call
it moderation" · "the founder's stake is an equity award — a vote, never cash" · "the
structure doesn't force you to become a democrat — it just makes it safe to."
