Hey DGG. [How long you've been around DGG, in your words.] I'm a software engineer in the Bay Area, and for the past year I've been building an open-source online game where the community actually governs the place. I think this is the crowd most likely to pull it apart in interesting ways, so here it is. Short version: every player gets counted three ways, a law needs two of the three to pass, and that means money can never win on its own.

The longer version. Every online community already governs itself somehow. A streamer and their chat, a Discord and its mods, this sub with GTAB. What almost none of them do is let the people in them take part in the actual deciding. You hang out, someone else runs the place. I wanted to see what it looks like when running the place is something the players do, out in the open, with every rule visible to the people it binds. I built it as a game because a game is the one thing that reliably gets people to show up and care.

So here's the machine. There are three chambers, one for each kind of contributor: labor (the people who make stuff in the game), consumers (the people who play it) and capital (the people who fund it). It's not three tribes, it's the same crowd counted three ways, since pretty much everyone makes, plays and funds in some mix. A law needs a majority in two of the three chambers. You can't pass anything by capturing just one.

Your voice in a chamber is called standing, and in two of them you can't just grab it. In labor you earn it when other people value what you make. In consumer you earn it when other people value how you play. You can't mint it for yourself, you can't buy it, and it fades if you stop showing up. Capital is the exception: that one you get by putting in real money, which is exactly why it's fenced in. Money gets a real voice in its own chamber and nothing else. It never votes in the other two and never buys you anything inside the game.

A law here isn't a press release, it's a change to the code the world runs on. So the branches work like a software team. The legislature writes the requirements, what should be true but never how. The executive builds it and runs the live world. The courts work like code review: did the build actually do what the law asked? And when a call was made by a person instead of the code, the courts hear the appeal. Anything the machine can enforce, it enforces, the same for everyone. Anything that takes judgment is on the record and appealable.

Every step goes on a record nobody can quietly edit: the proposal, each chamber's count, the code change, the ruling. You don't have to trust that the vote was honest. You can check it yourself.

And yeah, right now it's me. It starts with one founder holding everything, because at the start there's literally nobody else. The whole design is a dial that turns from one operator deciding everything to the players deciding everything, and I've locked in when I give up my majority: at 1.0 the players get a ratification vote on the constitution, and from then on I'm just a big contributor like anyone else.

Since someone will ask: this was built with AI, start to finish. It's the only reason one person could build something that touches this many fields at once, and I'm not hiding it. Every commit in the repo has Claude on it.

It's also just a game. It's a MUD, which is basically an MMO in text, with a real simulation underneath. Food spoils, rivers flow, money runs through a central bank whose ledger you can audit. If that's more your thing than the politics, the site has the game side: [link].

If you watch one thing, chapter 1 is 5 minutes and covers all of this: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n52hOI2IeZM

The rest of the series is about half an hour and takes each piece apart: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLFFj5IiyqKDI

There's a live demo at https://mud.panterasbox.com (it resets every night, so come break it), and I stream building it [day and time, PT] on Twitch: https://www.twitch.tv/bobalu_smallberries

The part I most want to see argued about: should money get a chamber at all? I went back and forth on it a lot. Fencing it in seemed more honest than pretending money doesn't buy influence anyway. I'm still not sure that's right.
