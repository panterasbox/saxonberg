## Part 0 — The gap

The site already carries the Compact well: three chambers, standing as a
rate, two-of-three to carry, the append-only record, *"the dial runs from
operator to republic, and it only turns one way."* That is strong and
does not need reworking.

⚠ **What is missing everywhere — site, docs, videos — is any claim that
the model is form-independent.** The text interface is presented as a
virtue with reasons (it is), but nothing anywhere says *the text is one
rendering and the governance would hold without it*. A reader's honest
takeaway today is "a well-designed MUD," which undersells the thing by a
category.

That gap is the whole subject of this doc.

## Part 1 — The claim, and how true it is

> **Saxonberg is a governance substrate that ships a text world as its
> first client.**

**What genuinely travels**, and why:

- The server is **authoritative**; the client renders MML. A different
  client is a different renderer, nothing more.
- The Compact, offices, seats, elections, delegation, chronicle, renown,
  participation, influence, the entrenchment tiers — all data-layer.
  None of it knows what it is being drawn with.
- The measurement doctrine (*engine measures · subject values · polity
  imposes*) contains nothing text-specific.

⚠ **What does not travel cleanly, and should not be claimed:** the
*interaction* model. Verbs, the command bus, MQL, the shell — a 3D or
voxel client needs its own affordance layer, and porting that is real
work. The honest public claim is about **the model**, never *"drop our
engine into Unreal."* Keep the claim on governance and record; do not let
it drift into a portability promise about the engine.

## Part 2 — Who we are actually competing with (internal)

Not MUDs. Not AAA games. The real comparison set:

| Them | What they have | What they lack |
|---|---|---|
| **Discord / Slack** | Where communities actually live | No polity layer at all — roles and permissions are admin fiat. Every server reinvents governance badly in a pinned message. |
| **Roblox / Minecraft** | Worlds, creators, enormous reach | Platform governance is unilateral. Creators have economics, never authority. |
| **Reddit** | Moderation as de facto governance | Entirely informal; 2023 showed users hold no structural power. |
| **DAO tooling** (Snapshot, Aragon) | Real governance primitives | No world, no reason to show up, dismal turnout. |
| **Unity / Unreal** | The toolkit everyone reaches for | ⭐ **Outputs never coexist.** Two games built with Unity share no market, economy or neighbourhood, so their quality is nobody's business but their author's. |

⭐ **The sharpest internal one-liner:** *DAOs have governance with no
reason to participate. Games have participation with no governance. We
have both, and the participation is what makes the governance work.*

That is also the honest answer to "who are you competing against" —
**Discord, mostly.** Communities already exist there and already argue
about who decided what. We are not trying to win a genre; we are trying
to give an existing thing a memory and a constitution.

### ⭐⭐ "Like Unity?" — the answer that is not hand-waving

*"A game"* is the short answer and it is wrong; *"a game platform"* gets
*"like Unity?"*, which is also wrong, and the difference is worth being
able to say in one move:

> **Unity gets to be neutral because its outputs never coexist.** Ours
> all live in the same world — one economy, one polity — so a bad thing
> is not merely bad, **it is a cost to other people.** Neutrality is
> affordable only when outputs do not interact.

Roblox and Minecraft are closer, because **making the thing is as much
the point as playing it.** But they stop one step short:

> **Unity does not care. Roblox counts the audience. We are trying to
> have an opinion that is not the house's.**

Roblox measures *how many people played your thing* — attention, which
is the Feed's metric. The claim here is to measure whether it was any
**good**, and to say *who says so* — which is exactly why
[measurement.md](./measurement.md) separates the engine that counts, the
subject that values and the polity that imposes, and why **B8 forbids
the engine from ever computing *how good is this person* itself.**

### ⭐⭐ The other half of the answer: the synthesis is the moat

Added 2026-09-29 from [lens #30 · Emergence](./lenses/30-emergence.md).
Almost nothing here was invented — the RGO is Europa Universalis' concept
applied to an MMO, the settlement model credits the colony sims by name,
logistics credits Transport Fever and Manor Lords, crafting credits Dwarf
Fortress and EVE. The claim is **not** that any one of them is better
here; ⚠ each is deeper in its own game than it will ever be in ours, and
the public frame should say so rather than imply otherwise.

> **Europa Universalis' RGOs have no labour market with people in them.
> Transport Fever's network has no polity deciding where the road goes. A
> MUD has rooms and verbs and nothing underneath them. None of these
> systems has ever had to coexist with the others in one world people
> live in.**

⭐⭐ **So the defensible thing is the combination, not any component** — a
competitor has to copy a synthesis rather than a feature. Which is also
the internal permission that makes the scope survivable: **borrow to the
depth the combination needs, not the depth the source went.**

⚠ **And the cost, which belongs with Part 0's gap:** a synthesis has no
genre, so **the thing that makes the design interesting is the thing that
makes it hard to describe.** *"A well-designed MUD"* is what a reader
concludes when the interesting claim is about how six borrowed systems
meet.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Which makes the freedom to decide badly load-bearing

The consequence is not soft, and it is the sentence to reach for when
someone asks why the platform allows something obviously unwise:

> **A measurement only has content if the thing measured could have gone
> badly.** Prevent the bad decisions and you are measuring the
> prevention, not the person. **A decision economy with no bad decisions
> carries no information.**

So the freedom to make poor choices is not tolerance; it is a
**precondition**. And it runs straight into the constitution:

> **freedom → consequence → harm → governance.** Bad decisions must be
> possible; outputs coexist, so someone else's bad judgment can cost
> you; which is *who can be wronged, on what basis, and can they answer*
> — and that is why there is a polity at all.

⭐ That is a better one-line answer to *"why does a game need a
constitution"* than any currently in the governance material.

## Part 3 — The ambition, and how much to say

The full private ambition — *every company you deal with as a consumer
could someday run some version of this* — is the right north star and
**too big to state flatly in launch copy**. Stated whole it reads as
grandiosity; withheld entirely, the audience concludes we make games.

The calibration: **say the model is bigger than the game; do not say what
we intend to do with that.**

✅ **Say:**
- Text is a choice with reasons, not a limit of the model.
- The same Compact would hold behind a 3D client, or inside Minecraft or
  Roblox.
- This is infrastructure for communities that make decisions, not a game
  genre.
- The ambition is that organizations with constituencies could run
  something like this.

⛔ **Do not say (yet):**
- Anything about competing with public companies or capital markets.
- Anything about real equity, shares, or investment. The site's existing
  *"Standing, not equity"* line is load-bearing and stays exactly as it
  is.
- Any claim that the engine ports trivially to another medium (Part 1).
- Any number about market size or displacement.

### 4a — The section

> **Eyebrow:** What this actually is
>
> **Title:** The text is a choice. The model isn't.
>
> Saxonberg is a text world because text is quick to build, reaches any

> of it knows what it is being drawn with. The server decides what is
> true; the client only renders it. Put a 3D world in front of this, or
> run it inside a game your community already plays, and the governance
> is unchanged.
>
> **We built a way for a community to govern something it shares, and
> shipped a world as the first thing worth governing.**

### 4b — The ambition line (short form, for the hero or the close)

> Every group that has members and makes decisions has the same problem:
> the people affected by a choice and the people who make it are
> different people. That is not a game problem. We are starting with a
> world because a world is where you can watch it work.

### 4c — One-liner variants

- *A governance substrate that happens to ship a world.*
- *We are not building a better MUD. We are building the part every
  community is missing, and a MUD is where you can see it.*
- *The world is the demo. The Compact is the product.*

### 4d — The Discord-adjacent framing (for the communities section)

> Your community already has somewhere to talk. What it does not have is
> anywhere that **remembers** — who decided what, who did the work, what
> the rule was before someone changed it. Roles and permissions are not
> governance; they are one person's settings.

## Part 5 — Guardrails

1. ⚠ **Never the word "manifesto" in site copy.** Standing rule; assert,
   do not defend.
2. **Assert-don't-defend applies here too.** The substrate claim is
   stated, not argued for. No "you might think this is just a game, but…"
3. ⭐ **The claim must stay checkable.** Everything in Part 4 is either
   architecturally true today or explicitly framed as ambition. The
   whole credibility of the Compact messaging rests on the existing
   material being verifiable — do not spend that on a portability
   promise we have not tested.
4. **The institutional pitch keeps its own exclusion list.** This doc
   widens the *public* frame; it does not amend
   the institutional pitch, which keeps the polity out of the exec room
   on purpose.

## Part 7 — For streamers (added 2026-11-18)

> **Status: messaging doctrine and draft copy**, out of the broadcasting design
> ([broadcasting-slate](./slates/trades/broadcasting-slate.md) § 7). The owner: *"I want to have a public
> document somewhere that isn't shy about marketing our platform to the livestreamer without saying we're
> directly competing … the stuff about them owning their own audience I actually think is a huge deal — it's
> the sort of thing that changes markets and business models."*

### 7a — The claim

**A stream is one place a community gathers; it shouldn't be the only place, and it shouldn't be the place
that owns them.** Saxonberg gives a streamer's community a world to live in — with a memory, a constitution,
and standing that belongs to the people who earned it. Video comes from wherever the streamer already
streams.

### 7b — What to say, and how true it is

| say | true today? |
|---|---|
| **Your stream plays on screens inside the world** — from Twitch, YouTube or Kick | ✅ built: `watch` and `tune` over the three transports; screens in the world show a stream |
| **A viewer can look around before signing in** | ✅ built: the anonymous-guest path |
| **Your community is yours** — members, their standing and what they built belong to them and to you, not to a video platform | ⭐ a constitutional guarantee: *anyone can always leave, walking out with their account, their standing, and everything they built* |
| **Viewers step into the world your stream is showing** | designed: frame as *what we're building* |
| **A community that moderates itself, with due process** — holds that expire, appeals, a public log | designed ([moderation-slate](./slates/society/moderation-slate.md)): frame as *what we're building* |
| **Run your own world** — a big community on its own realm, federated with the rest | ambition: frame as ambition |
| **Support that means something** — *standing, not equity* | ⚠ say only *standing, not equity*, exactly as the site does (Part 3) |

### 7c — Copy blocks

> **Eyebrow:** For streamers
>
> **Title:** Your community is yours.
>
> Your stream is where people find you. It doesn't have to be where they live. Saxonberg puts your stream on
> screens inside a world your audience can walk into — and everything they build there, every bit of standing
> they earn, belongs to them and to you. Not to a video platform.
>
> Stream from wherever you already stream. Twitch, YouTube, Kick — your stream plays here either way. You
> don't move your stream. You give your audience somewhere to be.
>
> And if a platform ever changes its rules on you, your community doesn't go with it. Anyone can always leave
> here with their account, their standing and everything they built — which is exactly why they'll stay.

**One-liners:**

- *Your stream is where they find you. This is where they live.*
- *The video is theirs. The community is yours.*
- *A stream ends. A world remembers.*

### 7d — Guardrails for this part

1. ⛔ **Never frame a video platform as a competitor**, and never disparage one. Name Twitch, YouTube and Kick
   only as places a streamer already streams from, which the world supports. No *killer*, no *instead of*.
2. ⭐ **Audience ownership is the headline** — it is true, checkable, and the thing that changes the business
   model for a creator.
3. **Claims stay checkable** (Part 5, guardrail 3): each line in 7b is built, a constitutional guarantee, or
   framed as what we're building or as ambition.
4. **Never imply that support buys equity or power** — *standing, not equity*; money never carries alone.
5. **Assert, don't defend** (Part 5, guardrail 2).
