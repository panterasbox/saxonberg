## Part 0 — The gap

The site already carries the Compact well: three chambers, standing as a
rate, two-of-three to carry, the append-only record, *"the dial runs from
operator to republic, and it only turns one way."* That is strong and
does not need reworking.

⚠ **What is missing everywhere — site, docs, videos — is any claim that
the model is form-independent.** The text interface is presented as a
virtue with reasons (it is), but nothing anywhere says *the text is one
rendering and the governance would hold without it*. A reader's honest
takeaway today is "a well-designed MUD," which undersells the thing by a
category.

That gap is the whole subject of this doc.

---

## Part 1 — The claim, and how true it is

> **Saxonberg is a governance substrate that ships a text world as its
> first client.**

**What genuinely travels**, and why:

- The server is **authoritative**; the client renders MML. A different
  client is a different renderer, nothing more.
- The Compact, offices, seats, elections, delegation, chronicle, renown,
  participation, influence, the entrenchment tiers — all data-layer.
  None of it knows what it is being drawn with.
- The measurement doctrine (*engine measures · subject values · polity
  imposes*) contains nothing text-specific.

⚠ **What does not travel cleanly, and should not be claimed:** the
*interaction* model. Verbs, the command bus, MQL, the shell — a 3D or
voxel client needs its own affordance layer, and porting that is real
work. The honest public claim is about **the model**, never *"drop our
engine into Unreal."* Keep the claim on governance and record; do not let
it drift into a portability promise about the engine.

## Part 2 — Who we are actually competing with (internal)

Not MUDs. Not AAA games. The real comparison set:

| Them | What they have | What they lack |
|---|---|---|
| **Discord / Slack** | Where communities actually live | No polity layer at all — roles and permissions are admin fiat. Every server reinvents governance badly in a pinned message. |
| **Roblox / Minecraft** | Worlds, creators, enormous reach | Platform governance is unilateral. Creators have economics, never authority. |
| **Reddit** | Moderation as de facto governance | Entirely informal; 2023 showed users hold no structural power. |
| **DAO tooling** (Snapshot, Aragon) | Real governance primitives | No world, no reason to show up, dismal turnout. |

⭐ **The sharpest internal one-liner:** *DAOs have governance with no
reason to participate. Games have participation with no governance. We
have both, and the participation is what makes the governance work.*

That is also the honest answer to "who are you competing against" —
**Discord, mostly.** Communities already exist there and already argue
about who decided what. We are not trying to win a genre; we are trying
to give an existing thing a memory and a constitution.

## Part 3 — The ambition, and how much to say

The full private ambition — *every company you deal with as a consumer
could someday run some version of this* — is the right north star and
**too big to state flatly in launch copy**. Stated whole it reads as
grandiosity; withheld entirely, the audience concludes we make games.

The calibration: **say the model is bigger than the game; do not say what
we intend to do with that.**

✅ **Say:**
- Text is a choice with reasons, not a limit of the model.
- The same Compact would hold behind a 3D client, or inside Minecraft or
  Roblox.
- This is infrastructure for communities that make decisions, not a game
  genre.
- The ambition is that organizations with constituencies could run
  something like this.

⛔ **Do not say (yet):**
- Anything about competing with public companies or capital markets.
- Anything about real equity, shares, or investment. The site's existing
  *"Standing, not equity"* line is load-bearing and stays exactly as it
  is.
- Any claim that the engine ports trivially to another medium (Part 1).
- Any number about market size or displacement.

## Part 4 — Copy blocks

Drafts in the established site voice — plain, declarative, concrete,
em-dashes, no hype. Place as a new section after the Compact section and
before *For communities*.

### 4a — The section

> **Eyebrow:** What this actually is
>
> **Title:** The text is a choice. The model isn't.
>
> Saxonberg is a text world because text is quick to build, reaches any
> device, has a ceiling set by your imagination rather than an art
> budget — and happens to be the exact channel a language model already
> speaks.
>
> None of that is load-bearing. The Compact, the three chambers,
> standing that fades when you stop, the record anyone can read — none
> of it knows what it is being drawn with. The server decides what is
> true; the client only renders it. Put a 3D world in front of this, or
> run it inside a game your community already plays, and the governance
> is unchanged.
>
> **We built a way for a community to govern something it shares, and
> shipped a world as the first thing worth governing.**

### 4b — The ambition line (short form, for the hero or the close)

> Every group that has members and makes decisions has the same problem:
> the people affected by a choice and the people who make it are
> different people. That is not a game problem. We are starting with a
> world because a world is where you can watch it work.

### 4c — One-liner variants

- *A governance substrate that happens to ship a world.*
- *We are not building a better MUD. We are building the part every
  community is missing, and a MUD is where you can see it.*
- *The world is the demo. The Compact is the product.*

### 4d — The Discord-adjacent framing (for the communities section)

> Your community already has somewhere to talk. What it does not have is
> anywhere that **remembers** — who decided what, who did the work, what
> the rule was before someone changed it. Roles and permissions are not
> governance; they are one person's settings.

## Part 5 — Guardrails

1. ⚠ **Never the word "manifesto" in site copy.** Standing rule; assert,
   do not defend.
2. **Assert-don't-defend applies here too.** The substrate claim is
   stated, not argued for. No "you might think this is just a game, but…"
3. ⭐ **The claim must stay checkable.** Everything in Part 4 is either
   architecturally true today or explicitly framed as ambition. The
   whole credibility of the Compact messaging rests on the existing
   material being verifiable — do not spend that on a portability
   promise we have not tested.
4. **The institutional pitch keeps its own exclusion list.** This doc
   widens the *public* frame; it does not amend
   [lenses/the-pitch.md](./lenses/the-pitch.md), which keeps the polity
   out of the exec room on purpose.
