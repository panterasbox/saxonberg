# Terminus — the city (street-level design, staging)

> **Status: exploration, 2026-07-12. NOT canon yet.** A wide-angle pass over
> Terminus at street level (the city *outside* the university campus), building
> toward districts → neighborhoods → venues → NPCs. The load-bearing output is
> the **city-economy model** (§6) — the anti-ghost-town floor — which is
> buildable on shipped substrates (Business/P&L, banking conservation + CB,
> residency). Everything above it is the setting that model runs on. Extends the
> bible's geography + §The administered realm; ratify into canon only on
> sign-off.
>
> Criteria used throughout: **immersion** (the world answers curiosity; nothing
> is a hollow shell) **+ function** (every realized venue is wired to a runtime
> system). Sibling threads: the underground note (drains/wire), the attuned
> allegory. Corpo roster/sectors: `docs/slates/builds/corpos-slate.md`.

## 1. What kind of city it is

**A patchwork of privately-carved quarters, not a planned civic grid.** Built in
an era with "not much government," the houses and then the five corpos each
developed their *own* quarter to their *own* sector-logic; the quarters abut with
no master plan. Consequences: district heterogeneity is explained; **the corpo
rivalries become the readable social geography** (walking the city reads the
factional map); and the city-administration job is the young polity
*retroactively* trying to govern a city built by people who wanted no governance.

- **Topography (hills — SF):** Eternal City was flat; Terminus rings it and
  *climbs.* Core low and flat (confluence, port, campus, oldest); hills rise
  outward. Tension: "nearness to the ruin is rank" → old prestige clusters **low**
  by the sacred core, new corpo money bought the **heights** for views.
- **Density:** dense and walkable (navigation), densest in the flats, thinning up
  the hills.
- **Safety:** mostly civil — danger is *economic/social* (shakedowns, the corpo
  squeeze, the Gray), not lethal. Lethal combat faces *outward* (see §4).
- **Weather:** mild coastal-river seasons; signature **fog off the confluence**
  (the Mere's stillness as mist).

## 2. Geographic spine + map

The spine runs **south → north** (the college-town shape): the **Gate** (TPA
terminal · port · city gate · the sea) at the south; **University Avenue** running
**east–west** across the middle (the civic/commercial heart); the **Campus** (on
the ruins, the **Confluence** just behind it) at the north head. The two rivers
(**Marrow**, **Mere**) descend from the highlands and meet at the Confluence by
the campus; the combined river runs **south** through the city, splitting **West
Bank** (working) from **East Bank** (prestige), crossed where University Avenue
spans it. Thresholds: the **Gate** (south — arrivals/refugees + the port) and the
**landward valley roads** (north, up past the campus — domains, Marches, the
front); the **Garrison** guards the northern approaches. *(Note to confirm into
canon: this puts the Confluence at the northern head by the campus and the port
at the southern river-mouth — more natural than "port at the confluence," and it
preserves campus-on-the-ruins.)*

## 3. Districts → neighborhoods

The rivers **divide** (class war across the water); the wedge **unites** (shared
civic/sacred/arrival heart). Each bank's neighborhood is the class-inverted twin
of the other's. Names provisional (owner's to finalize).

**West Bank (working/everyman):**
- **The Shallows** — working residential heart: tenements + Hollis retail; the
  stewardship rental floor, the corner local, a clergy shrine. *housing + retail.*
- **Wharfside** — dockers' riverfront: fish market, **dockers' hall** (labor),
  river-trade broker (delivery intake). *labor + logistics.*
- **The Gray** — shady edge (poor/informal, not lethal): fence/pawnshop,
  flophouse, manhole/dead-drop (underground seam), shakedown corner.
  *gray economy + underground.*
- **The Nightside** — after-dark: music hall, **fight-pit** (consensual combat +
  betting), gambling, the red-light row. *entertainment.*
- **Goodkin's Enclave** — the company town (velvet cage): company store/housing/
  clinic, all Goodkin. *corpo paternalism critique, walkable.*

**East Bank (prestige/new-money/tech):**
- **The Aevex Quarter** — new-tech: **augment clinic** (augmentation on-ramp),
  Aevex flagship, **Aevex Combat Academy** (guild), maker-lab. *tech/augments.*
- **Vionne Heights** — luxury hill: atelier, gallery/media house, **members' club**
  (recognition-gated), luxury terrace. *luxury/media/status.*
- **The Hill Streets** — single-family belt: owned homes (ladder peak), hilltop
  park, neighborhood association (private governance). *housing top.*
- **The Terraces** — professional/diplomatic shelf: **good hospital**, consulate
  row (diplomacy/refugees), professional guild hall, private-security HQ.
  *services + diplomacy — the uneven-services theme from the privileged side.*

**The Core / Wedge (shared, neutral):**
- **The Gate** — arrivals: TPA terminal + departures board, refugee intake,
  traveler's exchange, newcomer's bureau. *onboarding threshold; first thing
  every player sees.*
- **The Counting-Houses** — downtown financial: exchange/bank, corpo HQ towers
  (all five, neutral ground), counting-house, contracts/notary. *banking/capital.*
- **The Old Quarter** — Mission-era oldest-of-the-new-town: the **first Chapel**
  (worship), Independent artisan (Crowsfoot kin), antiquarian, pilgrim's hostel;
  the campus + underground seam. *worship + independents.*
- **The Civic Quarter** — the new government, retrofit: the **Forum**, **Lands &
  Works** office, the **Museum**, municipal hall. *governance + the admin-sim seat.*

## 4. Military / the war home-front

Pointed **outward** (so "the city is safe" holds): the war vs. the **Ordinance**
is fought at the frontier/dead-zones/away-realm, offstage-first. The city hosts
the war's **home front** as an industry: **Garrison/Muster** (contract board +
staging — the combat-loop hook: players *fill contracts*, don't enlist), **Arms
works** (Veshko/Aevex materiel), **combat academies/guild halls**, **veterans'
quarter + refugee/diplomatic intake**. In-city combat = *consensual spectacle*
(the Nightside fight-pit); lethal combat = the front.

## 5. Institutions as archetypes (the content-justification)

You don't author a dozen shops — you author **one archetype** (a template
parameterized by *tier*) and the economy instantiates/prunes the right count. The
class-tuning is *data*, not bespoke builds, so a dozen taverns ≈ the cost of one.

- **Demand elasticity sets the count.** *Need-based* (food/drink/bed/clinic):
  ubiquitous + tiered, ~one per walkable catchment, every neighborhood.
  *Want-based* (pet shop, gallery, luxury): rare + concentrated where money is.
  (So: the town sustains ~1–2 pet shops, in the Hill Streets/Vionne; a third
  starves out.)
- **A repeated institution is the best class probe** — holding *function*
  constant makes *tier* legible (who's excluded, who's off-ledger, who's the
  honest middle). Exemplar: the **tavern** traced Shallows→Wharfside→Nightside→
  Old Quarter→Gray→Vionne, one `Business` archetype, six tiers.
- **Recipe for any runtime institution:** `{ demand-driver · catchment/density ·
  tier-spread · breakeven }` → hand to the economy; count + placement emerge; the
  residency cull keeps it honest.

## 6. The city economy — the Atmosphere / Ledger model *(the core deliverable)*

**The problem:** build the whole city without it being a hollow shell, and
without it decaying to a **ghost town on a light-DAU month.** The fix: the city's
economic life must **not** be coupled to DAU.

**Two economies with a firewall between them:**

- **Layer A — the Atmosphere (notional, DAU-independent).** A coarse aggregate
  NPC economy modeled *per neighborhood* (not per NPC). Pure simulation numbers,
  never real money. The floor that keeps the city alive at zero DAU.
- **Layer B — the Ledger (real, conserved).** The shipped banking economy —
  players + the *few real* NPCs (named live-venue staff) trading real,
  conservation-locked money.
- **The firewall:** notional money **never** becomes real money. A business's
  *vitality* is a Layer-A fact; its *bank balance* is a Layer-B fact. Enforce
  architecturally — the Atmosphere layer literally cannot call `postTransaction`.
  Break this and you print money; hold it and conservation is trivial.

**Layer A — the per-neighborhood circular flow.** Designer-set constants per
neighborhood: **Residents `R`** (the floor; doesn't churn with DAU), an **income
floor**, a **propensity vector** (income split across institution types, tuned by
class tier) → **baseline demand** per institution type. A stationary circular
flow (income → demand → revenue → wages → income), conserved trivially (closed
numbers). Give it **rhythms** off the world clock (tavern-at-night,
market-at-morning, seasonal) so the city breathes without depending on players.

**Business viability = the emergent count (anti-collapse).** Each `Business`
computes **baseline capture** (its share of neighborhood demand for its type,
split among competitors weighted by tier-fit); **viable** iff capture ≥ breakeven
(notional rent + wages + inputs). Count persists to where the marginal one's
capture = breakeven; oversupply closes the weakest. **Runs entirely on the NPC
floor**, so a light DAU month changes nothing. Closure fires only on *structural*
collapse (depopulation/over-build), and is a *different mechanism* from residency
eviction (which is for *abandoned* content — a staffed business is never
abandoned).

**Layer B + the one conserved bridge.** Players and the few real NPCs hold real
accounts; player purchases credit a live venue's real account. The only place the
two economies touch — a player earning from / spending to the NPC economy — goes
through a **CB-managed Circulation Reserve** (a real, conserved account):
- player **earns** from NPCs → **faucet** (draw the reserve);
- player **spends** to NPCs → **sink** (fill the reserve).
Keeping the reserve in-band is **monetary policy** — the central bank's job (the
governor office + `reserve` mint/drain verb already exist). Run it on
**autopilot** (auto-hold the band, default-safe without governance), governor can
override. (Autopilot detail = §7.)

**Player perturbation = the ceiling.** Floor (Layer A) is stable + DAU-
independent. Players are **strictly additive**: custom raises a venue's real
revenue and, at scale, lifts effective demand → new businesses become viable
(booms). Boom businesses ride *player* demand and *can* fail when the boom ends
(honest bubbles) — **but the floor holds**, so a bust reverts to a living
baseline city, never a ghost town. **Invariant: DAU adds; it never subtracts
below the NPC floor.**

**Cheap + plugs in.** Layer A = a handful of numbers per neighborhood per tick
(`ScheduleApi`/world clock, coarse). Only the *few* live venues + few real NPCs
are real Stuff; the rest is facade + notional atmosphere (the player never sees
numbers — they see a busy, staffed neighborhood). Reuses **`Business`+P&L**
(add the baseline-demand revenue term), **banking conservation + CB reserve verb
+ governor office**, **residency cull** (orphaned content only), districts as the
demand unit. **The admin-sim falls out for free**: per-neighborhood
demand/supply/vacancy is the "run the city" dashboard.

**Two load-bearing risks:** (1) the firewall must be *structurally impossible* to
leak, not merely avoided; (2) the reserve autopilot must **default-safe** — an
*ungoverned* server still can't ghost-town or hyperinflate; governance is the
steering, not the life support.

## 7. The Circulation Reserve autopilot

Everything players do at the NPC boundary flows through one conserved account, so
its **level is the whole macro signal.**

**Faucets** (real money, reserve → players): wages for player labor at NPC
businesses (`payWage`), NPCs buying player-made goods, contract/bounty/quest
payouts. *Player income from the NPC world.* **Sinks** (players → reserve):
buying from NPC businesses, **rent** (stewardship housing), the **sales tax**
(shipped `salesTaxRate` → treasury), service fees, fines. *Player spending in.*
Supply is fixed, so the reserve **drains** when players net-extract and **fills**
when they net-pay — both extremes fail (empty → can't pay players/freeze;
overflowing → money vacuumed out of player circulation/starvation).

**Default autopilot — floating terms-of-trade.** Don't mint/drain by default;
let NPC prices toward players float as negative feedback. One scalar `τ` (terms)
multiplies all player↔NPC prices:
- reserve **below** setpoint (over-extraction) → **tighten** (`τ`: NPC pays less,
  charges more) → net extraction falls, sink rises;
- reserve **above** setpoint → **loosen**.
Nudge `τ` a small, rate-limited step toward `(S* − S)` each tick — a damped
proportional controller with a **deadband** inside the band. Self-limiting (the
more players collectively extract, the worse the rate, until faucet ≈ sink);
never freezes (terms tighten as the reserve nears empty); never inflates (supply
fixed). And a **diegetic signal**: over-extraction visibly makes the city
*poorer* (wages down, prices up) — emergent macro you feel; neutral + invisible
when healthy.

**Emergent macro that falls out:**
- **A natural cap on aggregate player wealth** extractable from NPCs (set by
  reserve size). To let the playerbase get collectively richer, or onboard *more*
  players, the **CB must mint into the reserve** — deliberate **growth policy**,
  exactly like a real central bank expanding supply with population. "Growing the
  economy" becomes a governance act.
- **DAU-scaling both ways:** more players → tighter terms (signal) *or* CB mint
  (accommodate); fewer players → low pressure → reserve stable, terms neutral,
  businesses fine on the notional floor. No ghost town, no runaway.

**Governor levers on top** (autopilot = life support; governor = steering):
**mint** into the reserve (stimulate/accommodate growth), **drain** (tighten),
set the **setpoint + band width** (policy stance), **targeted subsidy** (the
shipped CB subsidy / deficit-P&L — prop a struggling district's wages without a
global mint). Default-safe: an ungoverned server holds the band on `τ` alone.

**Disciplines that keep it from breaking:**
1. **Bound `τ`** (wage floors, price ceilings) so the NPC economy never goes
   absurd — and hitting the bound *while still draining* is precisely the alarm
   that a mint is genuinely required.
2. **No arbitrage:** NPC buy-price must sit below NPC sell-price for the same good
   (a spread), or players round-trip the reserve to zero.
3. **Damping:** small gain + rate-limit + deadband, or `τ` oscillates.
4. **One global reserve to start** (single CB); regionalize only if districts ever
   need independent monetary conditions.

## 8. The government stack — locked fundamentals (2026-07-30)

The institutional skeleton, locked ahead of content so offices, NPCs,
and venues can fill in later. Substrate: the civics build
([civics.md](../subsystems/civics.md)) — each tier below is a
`Government` instance on the jurisdiction chain. **Governing
principle: a functional city beats adherence to legacy narrative** —
the corpo-carved-patchwork lore stays as flavor and informal politics,
but the corpos hold NO structural seats: a corpo-run government would
be dysfunctional, or claiming it isn't would itself be a statement
about the world. The locked statement is the opposite one: *the city
works.*

### Terminus is a city AND a realm — two governments, one name, one seat

- **Not a city-state.** The realm and the city are distinct
  governments in a nested chain; "Terminus" naming both is Rome-style
  metonymy. The realm is **just another hierarchy tier** — plural like
  every diegetic government (more realms can and will exist) — and is
  **not** the Compact's face (no tier is; the bible's projection stack
  was corrected to thesis-rhyme only).
- **The state-capital model.** The realm government's *seat* is
  Terminus city — a capitol presence in the Civic Quarter alongside
  the municipal hall (Sacramento: city hall and the statehouse share a
  locality, never a jurisdiction). Colocation is physical (venues),
  never institutional.
- **Three tiers, three instances, one address chain**:
  `terminus` (the Realm of Terminus) → `terminus/city` (the City of
  Terminus) → `terminus/city/campus` (the Eternal University — the
  third layer, its own government: town-gown jurisdiction inside the
  city inside the realm). A dorm room's chain resolves
  [university, city, realm], most-local first.

### The Realm of Terminus (thin lock — detail deferred)

Exists as a `Government` instance claiming the realm root; seat in the
city (capitol venue = future content). It charters the city
corporation and the University, runs the assizes above the city
magistracy, and fronts the frontier war. Institutional detail
(ministries, the steward's diegetic dressing) deliberately unlocked —
later design, its own pass.

### The City of Terminus — a chartered municipal corporation

- **Form: commission under charter** — realm-chartered municipal
  corporation run by an appointed professional **City Administrator**
  with department heads. No elections yet (nobody in this city ever
  voted for anything; the administration was imposed on a patchwork
  that wanted no governing) — a representative council and elections
  are a *future story the polity can dramatize*, not a retcon.
- **Departments** (each a Business): the **Registry** (civics-build
  flagship), the **Watch** (public safety), **Works** (streets,
  drains, the wire — the city-tier echo of, never the same office as,
  the realm's lands administration), the **Almonry** (welfare — the
  shelter's eventual department).
- **Seats** (each a department position): **Administrator**,
  **Magistrate** (flagship — city law, the gaol, market disputes;
  grave matters escalate to realm assizes), **Treasurer** (the
  pre-planned landing seat for the Circulation-Reserve lever now held
  by the meta CB Governor), **Registrar**, **Captain of the Watch**.
  Named now, filled later; the charter Document names the full
  structure while the Government template grows entries as departments
  get built.
- **Treasury**: the municipal budget (the shipped
  `/domain/terminus/budget` Business account).
- **Corpos**: informal power only — lobbying, quarter identity, the
  fault-line map as *politics*, never as seats.

### The Eternal University (thin lock)

Its own `Government` instance claiming the campus subtree — the
town-gown layer. Institutional detail (a Chancellor seat, college
structure) deferred; v1 is identity + jurisdiction so the three-deep
chain is real from day one.

### Boundary calls

- Quarters get **no sub-governments** — if a ward ever wants home
  rule, that's a story (a jurisdiction repoint), not a default.
- Sibling address roots (`counting-houses`, `university-avenue`,
  `lounge`) remain outside every government until re-rooted by city
  content work — deliberate, not a bug.
