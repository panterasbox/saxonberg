## 0. The sources, and what each one is for

**Perfection, Nevada** (*Tremors*) · **Deadwood** · **Matewan**.

Not a blend — a **sequence**, one per act, each a different lens on the
same town:

| act | source | lens | what it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Tremors | survival | the town has no mechanism for unowned ground |
| **II** | Deadwood | civics | a charter, worth what Terminus says it's worth |
| **III** | Matewan | political economy | who the charter covers, and who it doesn't |

⭐ Each act *causes* the next, and none of it needs anyone to decide to be
a villain. Each is separately shippable. **Act I is greenlit.**

**Names.** Val, Earl and Rhonda are literal — they are the reason this
town is this town. Everyone else takes the Cornish/Welsh hard-rock
register (Bawden, Hocking, Tregear, Rundle, Nancarrow, Odgers,
Chenoweth), which is what a mining district's names actually sound like.

## 1. What kind of place it is

**A town nobody chose.**

The name is about the ground, not the people: the first prospectors up
this valley were *rejected* — thin gravels, three seasons of washing
nothing — and most left. Somebody stayed long enough to find the Ferrow.
The name stuck because the people who stayed thought it was funny, and
then it stopped being funny and stayed anyway.

Everything follows from **you go where the ore is, not where the people
are**:

- Nobody is *from* Rejection. Almost no children, no grandparents. The
  population is working-age because the town has no other use for anyone.
- ⭐ **It is the most species-mixed place in the realm, for the inverse of
  the usual reason.** A city has enclaves because people who arrive with a
  choice settle near their own. Rejection has none, because nobody arrived
  with a choice — the work does not ask what you are, only whether you can
  hold a bar. ⚠ Do **not** write this as a khazadicus town; the trope is
  right there and it is exactly the essentialism the species doctrine
  refuses. The mixing *is* the characterisation, and no character remarks
  on it.
- Everyone is somebody's second choice of life, and the town knows it.

### ⚠ Two registers, and the split is class

**The miners talk like Tremors** — flat, dry, declarative, funny about
bad odds, precise where it matters. Nobody at the face is eloquent and
everybody is exact.

**The saloon talks like Deadwood** — the people who trade in *words*
rather than rock (the publican, the newspaperman, the Institute's
secretary) are elaborate, formal and profane. It is the town's one real
social division and you can hear it across a room.

**Normal here, shocking elsewhere:** working a face alone · a woman
running a burn · owing the store four weeks · discussing a man's lungs to
his face.
**Shocking here, normal elsewhere:** an unattended flame · a stranger who
won't say what he's after · a promise about the ground.

## 2. ⭐⭐ The geology, which is the whole of Act I

**A mine manufactures soft ground.**

Hard-rock working produces enormous volumes of broken waste and fine
tailings, dumped downslope; below that lie the placer gravels the first
prospectors washed. So the district is a lode **in rock**, and beneath the
town a made deposit of deep, unconsolidated material — exactly the medium
a burrower needs and cannot leave.

Two consequences, and everything in Act I hangs off them:

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The safe place is down the adit.**

The mine — dust, damps, falls, the thing the town's entire creed is built
around fearing — is where you **run**. Everything they know is backwards,
and that inversion is the horror.

> ⭐⭐ **The spoil bank is nobody's claim.**

It is not on anyone's stake; it is where the waste went. The habitat is a
by-product of the industry, sitting on the one piece of ground the town
has no owner for — which is the Act I problem stated as geography.

it at the adit, everything crowded onto it because it is the only flat
ground. One street long, because the shelf is one street long. ⭐ The Row

## 4. Culture

**The candle.** The organising object. A miner buys his own light, burns
it by the hour, and knows to the stub how much working time he has left.
The town's whole relationship to cost and time runs through it. *"A
two-candle job."* *"He's short of candle"* — out of money, or out of time,
or dying; the ambiguity is the point.

**The creed, and how it blinds them.** The onsetter already says it in
shipped content:

> *Ground will stop you working and will not kill you. Air will kill you
> and will warn you.*

Everyone can recite it; it is what they say instead of goodbye. ⭐⭐⭐ And
in Act I it is **the reason the town is slow**: a mining camp has a
category for *the ground took him*, so the first three deaths get filed —
correctly, honestly, by a competent surgeon — as ordinary industrial
accidents. The creed does not merely get falsified at the end. It hides
the thing at the start.

**The Hush.** The natural chamber below is not spoken of casually. Nobody
claims it is haunted; everybody declines to eat down there. The
superstition is **procedural, not theological** — a list of things you do
not do, kept by people who will tell you flatly they believe in nothing.

**Debt is the social structure.** Wages weekly, in coin, at the adit; the
store carries people between paydays and the Institute's fund carries them
past an injury. Who owes the store, who owes the Rest, whose subscription
has lapsed — ⭐ all written in three books kept by three people who have
never compared them. **Whoever holds the debt is the government until
there is one**, and in Act II that turns out to be literal.

**Policing: the hue and cry.** ⭐⭐ Per the policing slate's Tiebout axis,
the frontier runs *a neighbour with a lantern* — policing as everyone's
unpaid duty. Terminus is **Peelers** (by consent, identifiable, minimum
force). Two arguments about law, one road between them, and you feel it
by walking.

**The year.** Nothing seasonal underground, which is itself the point: the
surface has weather and the work does not care. Two dates — **pay Friday**
and **setting day** (the deferred tribute pitches).

## 5. Act I — the hunt

### The shape

Something is taking people off the spoil. **Val and Earl** are hired to
find out, because it is not anybody's job. They survive it, and the town
discovers it has no mechanism for a problem that is not on anybody's
claim.

### ⭐⭐ It is not a boss fight, and that is the accurate adaptation

*Tremors* is not a fight film. The graboids are killed with a bulldozer,
pipe bombs, and a cliff they are tricked into charging off; the one time
somebody shoots one, it does not work. The climax is a **terrain and
logistics puzzle** whose win condition is *get to ground it cannot follow
you into*.

**Readiness (verified 2026-09-02).** Combat ships a 1v1 poise session plus
cycle 2's N-party melee (threat graph, focus-fire, `defend`, fleeing), and
ranged bands (`close·reach·near·far`) that **derive from the room's real
extent in metres**. What is missing for a *fought* graboid is real:
**scale** (no representation for a creature bigger than the room, no
multi-cell occupancy, no fighting a part of something) and **submergence**
(present/absent/affecting-you is not a state concealment has).

⚠ But `HazardMixin` is most of a graboid already: armed→sprung,
**triggered by the locomotion traverse**, delivery through the weapon
grammar (so armour mitigates it exactly as it does a blow), detection
gated by concealment (so you can *spot* one). What is missing is that it
moves and learns — **not** that it hurts you.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The rule is: you cannot be on soft ground.** The creature needs
> no stats. Everything else falls out of that one line.

### What falls out

- **Ground denial.** The Row is rock; the yard, the road and the spoil are
  not. The map acquires a texture it never had.
- ⭐ **Haulage becomes the game.** The mine keeps producing — you are safe
  underground — but the fifty metres between the adit and the smelter are
  lethal. How does ore cross the yard? The conveyance and freight
  substrate as a survival puzzle, which is a thing this engine is good at.
- **Who can reach the infirmary.** Rundle is on the wrong side of the yard.
- **The deduction.** Rhonda's traces, read against the ground you stand on.
- **The collective decision** — the fix is not killing it, it is the town
  agreeing what to do about ground nobody owns.

### The ecology it completes

⭐ The metal-chain slate's unbuilt list says *"no deep ecology, no apex,
no Hush cast."* The Ferrow zone ships a `stocks:` table — `Gryllus
tenebrarum`, `Rattus fodinae`, **`Subterracavia pallida`** — authored as
taxonomy only, with no creature rows. That is a prey pyramid with the top
missing. This is the slot, not an addition.

⚠ The table is **inert as shipped and fails silently** (`ResidencyLogic`
reads it by the candidate's `censusKey`). Fix that in the same wave or the
ecology stays a comment.

### ⚠ The three deaths

Author them as **accidents, correctly diagnosed.** A fall on the bank. A
man who did not come up from the lower road. A hand at the fuel yard.
Rundle's case book has all three with honest causes. Nothing in the prose
winks. The player who re-reads the case book after Act I is the point.

## 6. Act II — the charter, and what it is worth

### The Deadwood engine, already latent in shipped rows

Rejection ships a claims register where **first come is the whole rule**
and a registrar who *"does not adjudicate anything"*; **no
`_governmentKey`**; and — the fact nobody in town has noticed — its
businesses declare `banksAt: goodkin`, in Terminus.

⭐⭐ So the town's titles are honoured by a book only Rejection respects,
and the town's money already lives in a city that owes it nothing. **The
pressure to charter is not internal. It is the need to be legible to
Terminus.** They organise so there is something for a larger power to
ratify — which is exactly why Deadwood organised, and it is a cynical,
correct account of where institutions come from.

### ⭐⭐⭐ DECIDED: the charter is only worth what Terminus says it is worth

The charter is a **petition, not a victory.** What the town builds is
**de facto** authority — real inside Rejection, worth nothing at its edge.
**De jure** is somebody else's to grant.

It renders concretely: *an unrecognized sheriff can arrest a man in
Rejection and cannot follow him to Terminus.*

⭐⭐ And the consequence that gives the town its entire atmosphere:
**nothing that happens in Rejection is cognizable anywhere that matters.**
No jurisdiction, no extradition, no record. Not colourful lawlessness —
**the reason men get sent there.**

### The knife

You cannot purchase what nobody owns; enclosure requires title first. So:

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Terminus recognizes the charter because the corpo needs clean
> title to buy.**

The town's legitimacy is granted *for the purpose of making it sellable*,
handed to them by the people who intend to take it, because it makes the
taking lawful. **They win Act II. That is how they lose Act III.**

### The mechanical shape

Recognition is **an act by an office in Terminus** (civics ships
seats-as-positions and Locality-declared jurisdiction), and that seat is
**holdable by a player**. ⭐⭐ A player in Rejection petitions; a player in
Terminus decides; the corpo lobbies the seat and the town has nothing to
lobby with. A two-town political scenario with no NPC arbitration — which
is what the platform is for.

⚠ **Design against both degenerate ends.** Always-decline is a treadmill
and players stop petitioning; always-grant has no tension. It must be a
real decision by someone with real interests — ore supply, the cost of
governing, and a corpo in the room.

### The counterweight

⭐ **Rejection is where the game shows you why the Compact exists** — a
polity whose legitimacy is entirely at another's discretion, which is what
nearly every real polity is. The fiction may be bleak about it precisely
because the platform is not: players who live in Rejection hold standing
in the Compact that no in-fiction charter grants or revokes. The world
models how it goes; the Compact is the argument about how it could go
instead. ⚠ Never say this in-world.

## 7. Act III — the buyer, and who the charter covers

**The corpo.** Write it per the corpo doctrine: **the steelman of a
working form** — effective, often genuinely good at what it does,
extractive by *structure*, not villainy. It buys distressed claims from
people who have stopped believing, which is the historical method, and
⭐ **Act I supplies the distress**: a town that has just lost six people
and cannot work its own ground is a town that sells cheap.

**The Pinkertons.** The pattern is already designed in the policing slate
— *predator and instrument*: a corpo that wants a strike broken **does not
send its own security, it hires muscle from across the way.** Deniability
is the whole reason, exactly as in life.

Three facts to hold:

1. ⭐ **Private force has capacity and no authority.** Authority comes from
   an office (`holdsOffice`/`holdsSeat`). Hired men are just men.
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **The frightening one is the deputized one.** Baldwin-Felts
   agents at Matewan were routinely sworn as deputy sheriffs. Private
   force borrowing a public office is the horror — and your own alignment
   model already names the evil pole **capture**.
3. ⭐⭐ **Rejection has no office to capture, which is worse.** With no
   law, the corpo's men break nothing. They can hold the adit, evict from
   the Rest, seize the register, and no one has standing to refuse —
   because standing is a thing an office confers.

⭐ **The Pinkertons are the reason you need a sheriff.** And the corpo
would *prefer* the town stayed unchartered — so the interests invert
cleanly: the act that makes the titles saleable is also the only act that
gives the town standing to refuse.

### The leverage, and the people it does not fit

An unrecognized polity's only weapon is **what it can withhold**, and
Rejection is the realm's sole source of metal. So the instrument is the
Matewan one — stopping work — not as a moral gesture but as the only
thing available to people with no standing.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Val and Earl cannot strike.** Nobody employs them. There is
> nothing to withhold from.

The two men who did the work no one owned are the two the town's one
weapon does not fit. **The charter's first real test is whether it covers
people with no employer**, and the honest historical answer is that
charters usually did not.

### The detector

Per the policing slate: police catch what someone **saw**, journalists
catch what is **in the books**, and *"the press is the only detector for
an entire class of it."* A corpo's move on Rejection will be legal on its
face. ⭐ Odgers reading ledgers is the only thing that finds it — so the
print shop is not camp colour, it is Act III's instrument.

### Act I

**Val** — handyman. Young, competent, vain about it, and keeps a running
list of reasons to leave for the city that he has never acted on. Not on
any roster; takes piecework from whoever asks.
*Dispositions:* `boldness +65`, `ambition +50`, `diligence +45`,
`humility −40`.
*Routine:* wherever the work is. No fixed core — ⭐ which is the character.

**Earl** — handyman, older, Val's partner. Has been about to leave for
nine years. Does the thinking; lets Val do the talking. The one who
notices things and says them a beat too late.
*Dispositions:* `patience +60`, `worldview −50`, `constancy +55`,
`boldness −30`.
*Routine:* with Val.

⭐⭐ **Val and Earl are the labor question with faces.** Every job in
Rejection has an owner — the co-op owns the face, the fuel yard owns the
clamp, the smelter owns the furnace. They do the work with **no owner**:
clearing spoil, digging ditch, hauling the dead. No wage rate, no
protection, and no Institute subscription because casual labour cannot
afford a check-off. *Who is responsible for the work nobody employs anyone
to do?* — asked without a strike in it.

**Rhonda LeBeck** — graduate student, Eternal University, here on a
seismic survey of the district.
⭐ Her instruments are **the ones the game ships**: `measure strike`,
`measure dip`, `analyze ground`, against the `Deposit` field. She is
mapping the orebody's structure for a thesis, and her traces have
something in them that is not geology.
⭐⭐ The dramatic geometry is the film's: **Val and Earl have standing and
no instruments; Rhonda has instruments and no standing.** The deduction
only closes with both halves in the room.
*Dispositions:* `curiosity +85`, `honesty +70`, `humility +40`,
`boldness −25`.
*Routine:* out at her stations dawn and dusk; the Rest otherwise.

**Absalom Rundle** — surgeon-apothecary. Competent at crush, burns, breaks
and what a rope does to a hand; entirely defeated by the dust, which he
diagnosed correctly eleven years ago. ⚠ **Good at his job and losing
anyway** — never a quack, never a saint. Keeps a case book of every man's
lungs and shows it to anyone who asks, which the town finds either
admirable or unforgivable. ⭐ In Act I his three honest, correct,
completely wrong death certificates are the trap.
*Dispositions:* `honesty +85`, `compassion +60`, `worldview −40`.
*Routine:* 07:00–19:00, and awake for anything the whistle means.

### Act II

**Jory Hocking — the Tallow.** ⭐⭐ Fourteen years a hewer, then his lung
went, and he bought the licence with what the Institute's fund paid out.
Coughs between sentences and does not apologise. He is the only person in
town everybody tells the truth to — and he holds **the slate**, which is
every man's tab and therefore every man's business.
So when the town decides something, it decides **in his room, on his
terms, after he has already spoken to everyone individually.** That is
Swearengen's actual method and it needs no cruelty. Write him **liked,
trusted, and not remotely disinterested.**
*Dispositions:* `honesty +55`, `sociability +70`, `worldview −60`,
`ambition +45`.
*Routine:* opens 16:00, closes 02:00. Busiest at the core changes.

**Enid Nancarrow — the Institute.** Secretary; not elected — the last one
left and she knew where the minute book was. Keeps the subscription
ledger, the forty books, the stove, and the minutes of a body that meets
when there is a reason. ⭐⭐ **She writes the charter**, and she knows it,
and she has been waiting for somebody to ask.
*Dispositions:* `diligence +70`, `fairness +75`, `worldview +50` (the only
idealist in town), `boldness −35`.
*Routine:* the hall 18:00–22:00 on core days; all day Sunday.

**Merritt Odgers — the print shop.** An outsider, and it shows in how he
talks. Runs a hand press and the weekly *Ferrow Advertiser* — mostly
assays, shipping and notices. ⭐ The register says who owns what; **the
paper says it happened.** In Act III he is the only detector.
*Dispositions:* `curiosity +75`, `honesty +65`, `boldness +40`,
`humility −30`.
*Routine:* sets Thursday, prints Friday, drinks Friday night.

**Morwenna Bawden — the Rest.** Widow of a hewer the Ferrow took eight
years ago; took the compensation, bought the building, has never said
whether that was a good trade. Rents **by the week, bed not room.** Will
not take a man who has been drinking, and will carry a man who cannot pay,
and the two rules together are the whole of her.
*Dispositions:* `fairness +70`, `constancy +65`, `compassion +45`,
`sociability −30`.
*Routine:* awake 05:00–22:00. Serves 05:30 and 18:00.
⚠ Not the first rental market — Mayfield Row ships that. This is the rung
*below* a lease: a bed by the week, no instrument, no term.

### Act III

**Pasco and Loveday Chenoweth** — the Gummer archetype, local names.
Have prepared, at length and at cost, for the mine to kill everyone:
powder, a shored cellar, a plan, and a standing offer of both that nobody
has taken. Cranks until they are not. ⭐ They sit **directly opposite the
Institute** — private preparedness against mutual aid — and the town gets
to have that argument out loud, with both sides partly right.
*Pasco:* `boldness +80`, `trust −70`, `constancy +75`, `sociability −45`.
*Loveday:* `diligence +75`, `compassion +40`, `trust −55`, `humility −40`.

**The corpo's agent, and the men.** The agent is polite, well-briefed and
offering a fair price by any measure the town can apply. The men are hired
through somebody else and are, on paper, nobody's. ⚠ Neither is written as
a villain; the structure does the work.

### ⭐ The vacant seat

**There is no sheriff.** That is Bullock's chair and it is **the player's**
— the town has none, needs one when the crisis comes, and whoever takes it
is whoever decides to. In Act III the corpo will try to fill it, buy it, or
see it filled by someone accommodating.

## 9. The economy

**In:** tools · timber · food · candles · coin (wages) · **people**.
**Out:** ingots. Only ingots.
**Made and consumed here:** charcoal.
**Scarce:** flat ground · timber · air · sleep · **rock to stand on** (Act I).

⚠ **The wood contest is live and unwired.** The fuel yard's coppice is
also the mine's timber supply — two consumers, one seven-year rotation, no
rule. Ordinary business that runs underneath all three acts, and the thing
the Tallow argues about on a normal night.

**Terminus relation.** Rejection sends metal and takes everything else. Its
money already banks at goodkin, in a city it does not govern and which owes
it nothing — which is the whole of Act II in one shipped field.

## 11. Open forks

- **Does the co-op become the government?** The company-town question. Left
  open; it is the most interesting thing about the town.
- **Whose timber?** The wood contest wants a rule, or deliberately none.
- **What the Institute's fund pays** — injury, death benefit, or both, and
  who decides. ⭐ And whether it can ever cover a man with no employer.
- **Does the graboid recur?** Act I can be a *season* — a thing that
  happened, whose consequences persist — with the spoil bank permanently
  regarded differently. Recurrence wants the scale layer.
- **Children.** Almost none. Is there a schoolroom, or do families simply
  not stay? Currently they do not stay, which is sadder and cheaper.
- **The Hush cast.** Deferred by metal-chain. The superstition is authored
  here without committing to what is down there.
