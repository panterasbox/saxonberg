## The keystone (who they are)

If Dunny noticed everyone, **the roommate noticed no one — and not from
coldness.** They're the heads-down kind: self-contained, busy, perfectly
polite, living in their own lane and never thinking to look up. They didn't
ignore Dunny; it never occurred to them to *track* him. In a story entirely
about noticing people, the roommate is the one who didn't — which makes them
the player's **mirror-warning.** The win-condition is being known and knowing
others (§10); the roommate is the quiet failure mode of it. And the gut of it
is they're not a villain — they're *us on a bad week.* Everyone has been the
roommate.

What keeps their non-noticing humane rather than a flaw to judge: **they were
quietly going through their own hard thing** — a private weight, kept out of
pride, that ate all their bandwidth. They and Dunny passed like ships not
from carelessness but because both were drowning in separate water. That's
the ordinary truth the carve runs on: everyone's fighting something, and we
miss each other.

## What they actually have (the honest, frustrating witness)

They give you the **shape** of Dunny, never the **content:** the laugh
through the wall, the muttered counting, the comings and goings at all hours,
that he'd been more wound-up and tired the last few weeks, that papers and
number-columns piled up on his side and they never once looked. They do
**not** know the work, the secret, the people, the danger — so they can't
crack the case. And being asked "what was he like?" over and over rubs the
wound, because the honest answer is *I don't know, and I should.* That shame
reads as evasion if the player just mines them. Patience is the key: treated
as a person and not a source, they open — and what they give isn't facts,
it's **texture and feeling**, plus one real memory (below).

## The unwitting clue (where the half-packed room pays off)

Their investigative spine, and the seeded detail made load-bearing: **in his
last scared weeks, Dunny hid a fragment of the work in the roommate's
belongings** — a copy of the key page, or a small object — knowing the
roommate never looked at anything, knowing it'd be the last place anyone
would think to seal. Then the body's found, the roommate is hastily moved "to
preserve evidence," and **the fragment rides out in their boxes**, past the
proctor's tape, neither of them aware. So:

- The sealed room is *incomplete* — one piece escaped the seal in a
  stranger's luggage.
- The roommate is investigatively essential while knowing nothing — they hold
  a clue they don't know is a clue (the honest-witness principle, literalized
  as an object in a box).
- The reveal's gut-punch: *he trusted me with this, and I didn't even know
  him.* The person who didn't know him is the one who unwittingly kept his
  secret safe. Guilt twists toward grief.

> **Open:** deliberate dead-drop, or pure accident (a paper slipped among
> their things in the rush)? Deliberate is more poignant and shows Dunny knew
> he was in danger; accident is more naturalistic. **Lean: deliberate.**

## Emotional state & arc

When the player finds them: **displaced** (a strange new room, boxes still
unopened days later — no heart for it), **numb and a little resentful**,
quietly haunted — a stranger you were intimate-by-proximity with is dead,
which is its own uncanny grief. Defensive about the not-knowing, maybe a
thread of *why him and not me / did I miss the thing that would've saved
him.* The arc is **closed → open under patience**: early they've got nothing
and they're ashamed of it; given decency, they surface the shape, then the
feeling, then the one warm beat — and, if the player earns it, the fragment
they never knew they carried.

## Concrete texture (clean lines)

- **The new room that isn't theirs** — one corner made functional (their
  work), the rest still in boxes. They live in a lane, not a home; the room
  says so.
- **Always mid-task** — they answer you while doing something with their
  hands, half-turned away. Self-protective busyness.
- **"I didn't really know him"** — the shield, said too often, with a small
  flinch. They apologize for being useless.
- **An object of their own** — an unanswered letter from home, a bill,
  something that hints at the private weight without ever explaining it.
  Negative space; never spelled out.
- **The one real memory** — a single specific 3am moment they shared once (he
  covered for them; or they talked about nothing; or he said one kind, odd
  thing). The hindsight that he was a good person they failed to know. The
  emotional core of their testimony.

## Restraint notes

Private crisis stays **implied** — texture, never a subplot or a new NPC. The
proctor who moved them stays a **flat wall** (a process, not a person). The
roommate is **mundanely real**; the unease is entirely "I shared a room with
a ghost."
