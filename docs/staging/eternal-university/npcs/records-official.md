## The two-attribute portrait

`Cautious × Deceitful` — **the fear makes the lie.** She isn't a deceiver by
nature; she's a coward who got trapped into deceiving. Where Vance is *expert and
won't look* and Bram is *expert and looks past it*, Pell is **expert and lies on
purpose, terrified the whole time.** Scare her *more* than the upstream does (or
offer her safety), and the Cautious–Fickle cracks → she flips.

## Keystone: the trapped functionary

She didn't start it and doesn't run it. It was a **slow slide** — one irregular
sign-off, told it was a one-off, a favor for upstream; then another; then the
line had moved and one day she understood she was approving the paperwork of
*murders* and it was far too late to stop, because by then she was *in it.* Now
she's caught between two walls: **exposure** (if it comes out, she goes down too)
and the **faceless upstream** she's terrified of and has never met. A decent-ish
person who took the first small step and can never find the last one out. The
banality of evil's middle management.

## The escalation — and the pawn's white-collar twin

The dorm/morgue/enrollment people are *banal and unknowing* (Katie's love,
Vance's numbness, Pim's cheer, Bram's wisdom — none of them *know*). **Pell
knows.** She's the first knowing participant the player meets — the step from
*unwitting enablers* to *a witting, trapped accomplice.* And she's the
**bureaucratic mirror of the killer-pawn** (§11): both are ordinary humans
*groomed* into serving a **faceless handler** they fear and have never seen
(§8 — the orders come from an authority that can't be authenticated). The pawn
corrects the rolls with a knife; Pell corrects them with a stamp. Two hands of
the same banal machine — so meeting her *foreshadows* the pawn (§15.3), his
white-collar twin, equally pitiable.

## The kill-chain link (the lean — and the knife)

The strong version of her connection to Dunny, and the lever: processing the
cooked rolls, **she saw his discrepancies coming up** — the kid was flagging the
fraud, and the flags reached her desk. Scared, she did what the trap trained her
to do: she **passed it upstream** ("someone's noticing"). And Dunny died. She's
the **administrative seam in the kill-chain** — the §9 "the roll *is* the
kill-list": the handler reads the roll, the pawn strikes, and **Pell is the human
who passed the flagged name up.** She didn't pull the trigger; she *pointed* — and
she knows it, and it's eating her. So her flip isn't only self-preservation; it's
**atonement.** *(Lean: yes — it gives her a concrete tie to the murder and the
sharpest possible lever. Dial: how central / how confirmed.)*

## The lever

Not professional shame (Vance), not being-seen (Katie) — **fear, turned.** You
break her by becoming the *less frightening* option: corner her so flipping is
her only out, or offer her protection from the upstream — and lean on the
**buried conscience** (Dunny). A frightened, guilt-ridden accomplice who finally
flips is the player's **biggest source**: she *knows* the cooking, she *signed*
it, and she can name the seam. (She's also *dangerous* unbroken — she'll warn the
upstream, or feed the disposal chute.)

## Concrete texture (clean lines)

- **The stamp.** Thunk — approved. She uses it without looking, because looking
  is unbearable. Her tell and the evidence.
- **The jump.** She watches the door; the comms makes her flinch; the unlit
  cigarette.
- **The good suit, kept up.** Appearances are the last thing she controls.
- **"Take it upstairs."** Her deflection — *everything* is someone else's
  authority. She has spent years making sure nothing is hers, which is exactly
  why it's all hers.

## Role in the experience

The records-floor **source and obstacle** (registrar.md): the cooked ledger and
the SPECIAL-HANDLING tray are *hers*; the morgue's certificate trail leads to her
sign-off. The charged beat is **breaking her fear** — out-scare or protect her,
work the Dunny guilt — converting the woman who signed it into the witness who
*names* it (the city-cooking proof + the kill-chain seam). Unbroken, she's a
threat (warns upstream / destroys evidence — the disposal-chute clock).
