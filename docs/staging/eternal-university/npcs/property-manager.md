## Engine attributes (the buildable spine)

Authored *target* positions — the trait system seeds dispositions via
`BehavedMixin` claim-evidence; the sheet names the destination.

- **Species:** **Troll** — cast because the persona *is* her function (a
  threshold-guardian who decides who crosses and can be won over) *and* for the
  **overlooked-caretaker-caste** allegory it carries. Living people, mundane
  (§17.D); the "brutish/lowly" framing is the *prejudice*, not the truth. *Light
  flavor/dial:* big and solid (good for maintenance and for blocking a door);
  an indoor creature whose whole world is the building (the folkloric
  sun-shyness, made mundane as rootedness).
- **Dispositions** (salient axes — `key` → pole · band):
  - `compassion` → **Compassionate · entrenched** — genuine, deep warmth;
    treasures her tenants, knows their parents' names. The tragedy: a truly
    caring person enforcing silence.
  - `constancy` → **Stubborn · entrenched** — territorial, immovable, loyal to
    **the building** above all. The troll's immovability — and the axis that
    drives her to choose the institution over the kid.
  - `curiosity` → **Curious · defined** — she *notices* everything in her
    building. The pointed contrast with Vance: **Katie sees and looks away by
    choice; Vance stopped seeing.** Her complicity is the harder kind — not
    blindness, a seeing person choosing the building.
  - `diligence` → **Diligent · entrenched** — always mid-fix; the caretaker who
    keeps the place alive (the keys, the flickering hall light).
  - `fairness` → **Arbitrary · defined** — the one-petty-rule-with-relish
    streak; small-domain authority that can be capricious. Complicates the
    warmth.
  - *Meaningful tension —* `honesty`: **not Deceitful** by nature. She's an
    honest woman enforcing an *accepted* story — which is exactly why the
    boss-mask **costs** her.
- **Competence:** practical building-craft / maintenance (discipline TBD — not
  yet in the Catalog, and **not** her defining axis, unlike Vance's forensics).
- **Behavior (brains):** lobby/greets (the onboarding touchpoint), a maintenance
  wander loop (always mid-fix), the official-accident-line + convince-her social
  route. Bespoke responder/dialogue deferred to npc-dialogue.
- **Regard baseline:** **warm/high** for most players (Compassionate +
  gregarious) — the *opposite* of Vance. But the **room-gate** (letting you in)
  needs more than baseline warmth: it needs the personal knife (below).
- **Carries / on her person:** the **master keys** (her sound) and the
  **manifest** — a physical ledger/clipboard, the Warren's room-roster you can
  actually hold (a micro-census, and a later **clue-surface**: a room listed with
  no one ever seen in it = a phantom); plus a multitool (always mid-fix), a rag,
  treats for tenants. Worn — lived-in practical work clothes, a tool-belt, shoes
  worn from the rounds. Implant: a basic comms (parents, maintenance). Brands:
  durable, independent, no-name — not flashy.

## The defining tension

Where Vance's character is two numbers, Katie's is two **entrenched** poles in
collision: `compassion: Compassionate` × `constancy: Stubborn` — **a deeply
caring woman, immovably loyal to the institution.** Add `curiosity: Curious` and
the indictment sharpens: *she is not blind and not cold. She sees, she cares,
and she enforces the silence anyway* — because the building outranks the kid.
That's the whole engine of her, and it's a harder, sadder failure than Vance's.

## Keystone: the live-in caretaker who's both

She lives on-prem like everyone else, treasures her tenants, knows their names
and their parents' names — **and** she's the building's enforcer: fixes what's
broken, keeps the rules, holds the order. She can be one of the crew when she
wants and the authority when she has to, and she switches by **necessity.** That
switch is the whole character — the warmth is real and the boss is real, and
which one you get depends on what the moment demands. *(Gang when she wants,
boss when she has to.)*

## The triad position: she noticed, and looks away

The story's central axis is *seeing people.* **Dunny** noticed everyone (and
died for it); **Wren** noticed no one; **Katie noticed — and chose not to see.**
Hers is *active*: her boss-side **enforces the quiet** — turns the room over,
keeps the floor from gossiping, reassures parents — over a kid her gang-side
cared about. The procedural horror (§1, the institution closing ranks) doesn't
need a villain; it needs a **nice person doing her job.**

## The allegory: the overlooked caretaker caste

Trolls carry an **abstracted dynamic of prejudice** (per the
species-as-race-allegory stance — *not* a reskin of any real group): the
**live-in help** — the super, the caretaker, the janitor — essential, present,
*knowing*, and treated as a **fixture, not a person**; condescended to; judged
brutish and lowly by size and class. The prejudice ("trolls are dumb, violent
brutes; they'll eat you") is the **projection — false-as-a-law.** The discipline
(the essentialism trap): her boss-side firmness is real, but its meaning stays
**honestly underdetermined** (petty tyrant *or* loyal guardian doing a hard job
— a decent player can read it either way), and the "brute" slander is one the
fiction **refutes.** The irony that drives it home: **the overlooked caretaker
sees more than the people who overlook her** — she is *the one who notices*,
which is the arc's whole currency.

**The payoff fuses the two engines — and it's already her knife.** The cover-up
rides quietly on the assumption that *the super doesn't matter and doesn't
notice.* Contempt is the cover here too. And it's literalized in the personal
lever below: Dunny *counted her as a person.*

**Lean into the convention, swerve before cliché:** the gruff-gatekeeper-with-a-
heart is the genre move — but her swerve is that the heart is *exactly* what
makes her complicit (she enforces the quiet out of love for the building and its
people). Not a cuddly softie. A warm person doing a quietly terrible thing.

## The personal knife (the lever)

Dunny was one of the few tenants who treated Katie as a **person, not staff** —
knew her name, asked about her day. So she's enforcing the smoothing-over of the
death of one of the only kids who ever *saw* her (he noticed everyone — including
the live-in super). Invoke Dunny-as-a-person and her boss-mask slips. The knife
*is* the allegory: the overlooked caretaker, made to bury the one who refused to
overlook her.

## She has the keys (the investigation gate)

As maintenance authority she holds **legitimate master access to everything** —
so by default she's the **gatekeeper** to the victim's room, and the obstacle is
her institutional loyalty, not the lock. Her arc: earn her gang-side and she
breaks her own rules and lets you in, at real personal cost (boss → gang, under
pressure). **This absorbs the "physical access" function the inherited §12 gave
to Pidge.** This is the **social route** into the room — the clean-but-slow door,
one of several (§17.G immsim access) — and the *gentle* introduction to access-
literacy that the city morgue (#7) later escalates.

## The clearing-hand (her complicity, made operational)

Post-murder, her routine gains a task — and it's **executing the cover-up's
erasure at ground level.** *She* sealed Dunny's room; *she* relocated Wren "to
preserve evidence"; *she* clears the room on the schedule (the sealed-room
clearing clock is **Katie's hand**). So §1's procedural horror — "a nice person
doing her job" — becomes literal: the local erasure-by-routine is her daily work,
done warmly, over a kid who was one of the few who ever saw her. Her complicity
stops being abstract; it's in her rounds. And it sharpens the social route:
you're asking the woman *executing the erasure* to let you into the room she's
erasing.

## Her role: orientation → on-ramp (and the manifest)

Katie is **the one character a player has already met** when they decide to
investigate — which is why she's load-bearing. Every player meets her **day one**
(the onboarding climax at Duncan Hall): she greets you and **assigns your room
from the manifest** — and her manifest *is* the Warren (Duncan Hall is an elastic
graph of dynamically-generated rooms; her clipboard is its **diegetic face** —
when she "finds you a room," the engine buds a node). She is the building's
**intake of the living** — the warm mirror of Vance's intake of the dead. That
day-one warmth is the point: it makes her later complicity *cost* something, and
it's the same warmth she gave Dunny. (The roommate she assigns from the manifest
— a procedural agent — plants the who-counts thesis on night one; bible §17.H.)

## Her domain (the space she inhabits)

- **Her apartment by the lobby — the warm heart.** Ground-floor, on-prem, door
  half-open; the kettle, the plants, a **corkboard of decades of tenants**
  (photos, parents' numbers, kids she's looked after for thirty years), a wall of
  labeled keys. The room *is* her thesis: rootedness, memory, care.
- **The lobby — her station and the building's threshold.** Where she greets
  every arrival and watches everyone come and go (she misses nothing — her
  `Curious`). The troll at the bridge, literally.
- **The building as territory.** The halls she patrols and fixes, the basement
  workshop (the boiler, the master keys to *everything*), the floors she knows by
  name — and **the service stairs where Dunny was "found"** are hers too (she
  maintains them; she may even have been the one who found him — a dial).

## Her routine (runs without the player)

The morgue's "the place runs without you" principle, but warm. Her loop
(npc-behavior brains: greets, a maintenance wander, enforcement triggers, lobby
idle): **opens up and walks the rounds** (fixes the overnight breakages — she
can't pass a flickering light, her `Diligent`); **greets and assigns** new
tenants (calls home if a kid's struggling); **holds court** (the warm gossip hub
— where Pim is incurious-cheerful, she is curious-and-caring); **enforces** (quiet
hours, sign-your-guests, the petty rule with relish); **closes up, back to the
kettle.** The player always meets her *mid-rounds*, interrupting a super with a
building to keep.

## How she pairs with Vance

The two are a deliberate rhyme: **two stigmatized service-caste species** — the
caretaker troll and the death-worker ghoul — both **overlooked, treated as
not-quite-people**, both **complicit** in the cover-up, both carrying others'
contempt. The contrast is the axis: **warm/intimate vs. cold/industrial**;
`Compassionate + Curious` (sees, and looks away) vs. `Callous + Incurious`
(stopped seeing). In both, *the cover rides on the overlooked.*

## Concrete texture (clean lines)

- **A real home by the lobby** — ground-floor apartment, door half-open, kettle
  on, plants. The opposite of Wren's lane-not-home; Katie's whole self is
  *rootedness.*
- **The keys are her sound** — the jangle on her belt, always mid-fix (a
  flickering hall light, a stuck door). Dunny had the laugh, Gus the whistle,
  Katie the keys.
- **The code-switch tell** — warm-and-loose drops to straight-and-final in a
  beat; the player learns to read which Katie they're getting, and dreads the
  flip.
- **One petty rule she enforces with relish** — quiet hours / no candles / sign
  your guests. Charming… until you realize the same instinct is what keeps a
  death quiet.
- **Genuine warmth toward the player** — glad you're here, checks in — which is
  exactly what makes boss-mode sting.
