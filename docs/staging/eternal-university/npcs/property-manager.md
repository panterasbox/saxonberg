## Keystone: the live-in caretaker who's both

She lives on-prem like everyone else, treasures her tenants, knows their
names and their parents' names — **and** she's the building's enforcer:
fixes what's broken, keeps the rules, holds the order. She can be one of the
crew when she wants and the authority when she has to, and she switches by
**necessity.** That switch is the whole character — the warmth is real and
the boss is real, and which one you get depends on what the moment demands.
(Calibration: gang when she wants, boss when she has to.)

## The triad position — she noticed, and looks away

The story's central axis is *seeing people.* **Dunny** noticed everyone (and
died for it); **Wren** (the roommate) noticed no one; **Katie noticed — and
chose not to see.** Hers is *active*: her boss-side **enforces the quiet** —
turns the room over, keeps the floor from gossiping, reassures parents — over
a kid her gang-side cared about. The cover-up gets smoothed by someone who
loved him. The procedural horror (§1, the institution closing ranks) doesn't
need a villain; it needs a **nice person doing her job.**

**The personal knife (her best lever):** Dunny was one of the few tenants who
treated Katie as a *person, not staff* — knew her name, asked about her day.
So she's enforcing the smoothing-over of the death of one of the only kids
who ever saw her. Invoke Dunny-as-a-person and her boss-mask slips. (Ties to
Dunny's keystone — he noticed everyone, including the live-in super.)

## She has the keys (the investigation gate)

As maintenance authority she holds **legitimate master access to
everything.** By default that makes her the **gatekeeper** to the victim's
room — and the obstacle is her institutional loyalty, not the lock itself.
Her arc: if the player earns her gang-side, she breaks her own rules and lets
them in, at real personal cost (boss → gang, under pressure). **This absorbs
the "physical access" function the inherited §12 gave to Pidge** — so Pidge
can be deferred or cut; her separate value (the "borderline-real") is a
different question for if/when it's ever needed.

This is the *social route* into the room — one of several (see §14's immsim
investigation approach: convince Katie, break in, time the cleaners, go
around). Katie is the clean-but-slow door; she is **not** the only door.

## Concrete texture (clean lines)

- **A real home by the lobby** — ground-floor apartment, door half-open,
  kettle on, plants. The opposite of Wren's lane-not-home; Katie's whole self
  is *rootedness.*
- **The keys are her sound** — the jangle on her belt, always mid-fix (a
  flickering hall light, a stuck door). Dunny had the laugh, Gus the whistle,
  Katie the keys.
- **The code-switch tell** — warm-and-loose drops to straight-and-final in a
  beat; the player learns to read which Katie they're getting, and dreads the
  flip.
- **One petty rule she enforces with relish** — quiet hours / no candles /
  sign your guests. Charming… until you realize the same instinct is what
  keeps a death quiet.
- **Genuine warmth toward the player** — glad you're here, checks in — which
  is exactly what makes boss-mode sting.
