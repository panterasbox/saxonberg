## 0. The line it sits on **[DECIDED — the owner's rule, read]**

The performance and visual-arts slates hold one rule: **the game stages art and never captures it**
([performance-slate](../world/performance-slate.md) § 0 — *"the performance happens off the page … the game
supplies the fiction around it"*; [visual-arts-slate](../world/visual-arts-slate.md) § 1 — *"the piece is the
artist's, and it is never generated"*). A photograph sits on that line, because two things share the name:

1. **A real photograph** — taken by a person in the real world and brought into the game. That is art: the
   game frames it (the print it hangs on, the gallery, the audience) and never makes or captures it.
2. **A photograph taken inside the game**, with an in-world camera, of the game world. It *does* capture —
   but what it captures is **the world's own rendering, which the game already owns**, never anyone's
   real-world art.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The game never captures art, but it can capture itself.** This slate is the second kind.

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ What an in-world photograph captures **[DECIDED in shape]**

**What the camera could see, and nothing else:**

- **only the visual channel** — no sound, no smell;
- **from where the camera stood**, framing what is in view;
- ⭐ **under the light at that moment** — light is real physics, and an unlit interior is pitch black, so a
  photo of a dark room is black;
- ⭐ **never more than perception allows** — a hidden person is not in it; a disguised person appears
  disguised. **A photo is a frozen perception, never an omniscient snapshot** — the evidence firewall holds,
  which is what makes a photo honest evidence.

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ Appearance, not identity **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 3a]**

Recognition is per-viewer: you know who someone is only if you have met them. So **a photo stores how things
looked** — *"a tall man in a gray coat beside the fountain"* — and **whoever looks at it later applies their
own recognition**. Someone who knows Bob's face sees Bob; a stranger sees a tall man in a gray coat. The same
photograph means more to the people who know more, as a real one does.

## 3. ⭐⭐ It is frozen, and it is a thing

- **Frozen in time** — paper's thesis ([paper.md](../../subsystems/paper.md)): it shows the scene as it was,
  goes stale, contradicts the present, can be dated.
- **A thing** — printed on paper or a plate; carried, given, lost, burnt.
- **Copied from a negative** — a reusable original: the printing slate's fixed-cost curve
  ([printing-slate](./printing-slate.md) § 3).
- **Forgeable** — staged scenes, retouching. Forgery stays play: the record never certifies an in-fiction
  object ([record-integrity-slate](../platform/record-integrity-slate.md) § *The interface*).

## 4. ⭐⭐ Exposure is physics **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

- **Light × time** — too little and the photo is dark, too much and it is washed out.
- ⭐ **A long exposure loses what moves.** Real history: Daguerre's *Boulevard du Temple* (1838), the earliest
  photograph of a street, shows an empty boulevard — the traffic moved too fast to register; the only person in
  it is a man standing still to have his boots shined. A slow camera in a busy square makes a photo of an empty
  square.
- Every outcome can be worked out in advance; none is rolled ([uncertainty.md](../../uncertainty.md)).

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ It never captures a performer's real media **[DECIDED]**

Photograph a stage where someone streams a real piano performance and the photo shows **the stage** — the hall,
the crowd, *"a pianist at the grand piano"* — never a frame of their real video. Photograph a screen showing an
aether stream and you get *"a screen showing* Saturday Night at the Bowl*"*. The owner's line, held at its
sharpest point.

## 6. Craft, epoch, trades

- **Competence resolves detail, never access** — a better photographer gets a sharper, better-exposed frame of
  the same scene, resolving more. ⛔ **The game never judges composition or beauty** — that would be judging
  art; only the technical result is measured.
- **Epoch** — the camera **runs on** muscle (no power) but is **made with** Engine-era optics and chemistry,
  from the daguerreotype (1839). A digital camera or the phone's camera **runs on** Signal; its photos are
  digital — copied with no negative, carrying their own metadata
  ([content-declaration-slate](../platform/content-declaration-slate.md) § 3.0).
- **Trades it pulls on** — glass for lenses, silver and chemistry for the process, paper for prints; and
  **photography** itself as a vocation: portraits, photojournalism, forensics, records.

## 7. Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — optics, exposure as light × time, silver chemistry, Daguerre and the empty boulevard, the
   history of doctored photographs (Stalin's commissars disappearing from official pictures). **Lens 1 chose
   exposure as real physics, and appearance not identity.**
2. **Expression** — the ordinary case is point and capture: the camera a tool row, the photo derived from the
   scene, nothing authored per photo. ⭐ **One new verb, `photograph`** **[DECIDED — lens 2]** — `snap` is
   glassworking and `shoot` is ranged combat; nothing holds `photograph`.
3. **3a Immersion** — the photo obeys perception and light, so it cannot contradict the fiction; it never
   captures real media; **covenants apply to operating devices**, so a phone camera (runs on Signal) is subject
   to a covenant town's law, and a film camera (runs on nothing) is not. **3b Participation** — photojournalism
   for the Post, portrait studios, forensic photos at a scene, **blackmail** (Vionne's hidden hand —
   [corpos-slate](../society/corpos-slate.md)), staged forgeries and the work of exposing them.
4. **Values** — ⭐ **photographing inside a private extent is its holder's rule** **[DECIDED — lens 4]**: the
   right to exclude applied to cameras — a club or a home may ban them, enforced as that extent enforces its
   rules. In the fiction's public spaces there is no consent mechanism (*leaving the lounge is the opt-in* —
   [moderation-slate](../society/moderation-slate.md) § 1a).
5. **Continuity** — ⭐ **one command across the epochs** **[DECIDED — lens 5]**: daguerreotype, film, digital,
   phone — `photograph` answers on every rung; only the dynamics change (exposure time, a negative or none,
   copies free or not).
6. **Economy** — a real vocation with demand already designed (journalism, portraits, forensics, records); the
   pull on glass, silver, chemistry and paper. ⭐ **Value moves across the epochs** **[DECIDED — lens 6]**: a
   film print costs a negative and a printing session, so copies have value; a digital photo copies free, so
   its value moves to *the shot itself* and to *being first* — timeliness, journalism's whole business.
7. **Governance** — ⭐ **an in-fiction photo is admissible in an in-fiction case, weighed like testimony**
   **[DECIDED — lens 7]**: frozen perception, so epistemic and fair; forgeable, so weighed, never trusted
   blindly. **Never evidence in a conduct case**, which uses only what was delivered to someone
   ([separation-of-powers-slate](../society/separation-of-powers-slate.md) § 7o; moderation-slate § 23).
   Taking a photo judges nobody; publishing a staged one to defame someone is a civil case.

## 9. ⭐⭐⭐ "Photographer," and off-page work **[DECIDED — the owner; lens pass]**

The owner: *"One thing is how to handle the ambiguity when we say 'photographer' in the game. Do we mean the
fictional kind or the real-life kind, because they both exist and are two different disciplines … or are they?
Is a real-life photographer actually a fictional photographer automatically, because they can show deeds that
prove their ability? My gut says no, because they're not really the same skill — one's gameplay and one's using
actual cameras. But then there's even more reason why we need to disambiguate them. Maybe even coin a new term
for 'real-life media producer' in the game's jargon that we can use in code and content."*

### Not the same skill, but bridged **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

- **Understanding transfers.** In-world photography runs on real physics, so a real photographer already
  understands it and **can pass the in-world photography exam on day one** — the tech tree's understanding
  layer, however it was learned ([assessment-slate](../society/assessment-slate.md): one student model, two
  kinds of evidence).
- **Deeds do not.** In-world competence is evidenced in the world — framing a scene, timing an exposure in a
  dark room. A real photograph proves real skill, but **the game cannot count it as an in-world deed, because the
  game never judges art.**
- So: **real expertise earns the credential by exam; deeds come only from play.** Not automatic — the owner's
  gut — but a real bridge, which a real photographer will likely cross faster than anyone.

### Three kinds of people

| who | what they do | how the game knows them |
|---|---|---|
| **a tradesperson in the fiction** | a character practicing photography in the world | the **Discipline** — deeds in the world, and exams |
| **an author** | someone who writes the world's content — rows, places, code | the authoring record; the tech tree's author credentials |
| ⭐ **an off-page artist** | a person whose real work enters the game — a musician streaming, a painter uploading, a photographer, a streamer | **audience reception**, never a judgment of the work; Disciplines only for the staging around it |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The term: off-page **[DECIDED — lens 2]**

From the owner's own doctrine — *"performance is explicitly not on the page … the performance happens off the
page"* ([performance-slate](../world/performance-slate.md) § 0). In a text game **the page is the game's own
medium**:

- **off-page work** — real-world creative work brought into the game: a real photo, a real performance, a
  stream;
- **an off-page artist** — the person who made it;
- **on-page** — anything the game itself renders, so **an in-world photograph is on-page**.

Short, from the doctrine, plain in content (*"an off-page photograph hangs in the gallery"*), usable as a code
flag (`offPage`), and it says the important thing on its face: **off-page work is the artist's, and the game
never touches its content.**

### What it does to the word

- **In the game, "photographer" always means the in-world trade** — a Discipline holder. A real photographer is
  **an off-page photographer**, and their work off-page work.
- ⭐ **Disciplines name in-world crafts only** **[DECIDED — lens 1]** — no Discipline exists for off-page art,
  because the engine cannot measure it. **An off-page artist's standing comes from audience reception and
  credentials**, and the game measures **the staging around** their work as a Discipline — a streamer's craft
  measured by agendas kept ([broadcasting-slate](./broadcasting-slate.md) § 7) is exactly this.

### The rest of the lens pass

- **Expression** — off-page artists grow in-game through the staging around their work; their art stays theirs.
- **3a Immersion** — off-page work appears in the fiction framed (a photograph on a gallery wall, a performer on
  a stage), and in prose is just *"a photograph"*; ⭐ **off-page work carries the out-of-fiction mark in the
  interface** **[DECIDED — lens 3a]**, the flag streams carry — a viewer can always tell it is a real person's
  real work. **3b Participation** — real artists join on their own terms, and may also take up the in-world
  trade: two roads, both open.
- **Values** — what counts as good off-page art is never the engine's call; the audience decides.
- **Continuity** — off-page work persists as media, independent of any Discipline.
- **Economy** — off-page artists earn through tips, patrons and the audience; in-world photographers through
  their trade. ⭐ **Off-page work earns standing only if it is original** **[DECIDED — lenses 6, 7]** — the
  existing rights rule: labor standing is for original work only, and the platform never rewards uploading
  someone else's.
- **Governance** — the engine never judges art (*the engine measures, people value*); **authorship is attested
  by whoever brings the work in and challenged through notice-and-takedown**; a false claim of authorship is a
  conduct matter; what may be shown publicly is the visual-arts slate's open moderation question.

## 10. ⭐⭐⭐ The general model — every recording medium **[DECIDED in shape — the owner; lens pass]**

The owner: *"Let's extend all this to the other recording media — motion picture film and sound recordings and
anything else that qualifies — and then map this all back to broadcast and the rest of the conversation."*

> **A recording captures what a device could perceive — which senses, from where, under what conditions — over a
> span of time, and plays it back later to whoever is present.**

| medium | senses | span | first rung | runs on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **photograph** | sight | an instant | Engine (the daguerreotype, 1839) | muscle |
| **sound recording** | hearing | minutes to hours | Engine (Edison's phonograph, 1877, hand-cranked) | muscle |
| **motion picture** | sight, then sight and hearing | minutes | Engine (hand-cranked cameras, 1890s) | muscle to shoot; light to project |
| **magnetic tape** | hearing, then sight | hours | Electric | electricity |
| **digital** (camera, recorder, phone) | sight and hearing | as long as storage allows | Signal | electricity — copied free |

### What carries over from the photograph

Only the device's channels, from where it is; **within perception, under real physics** — light for sight, and
⭐ **real acoustics for sound** **[DECIDED — lens 1]** (speech fades with distance, a wall muffles, a whisper across
the room never reaches the microphone, a sneaking player makes no sound to record); **appearance, not identity** —
a face *"a tall man in a gray coat"*, a voice *"a low man's voice"*, recognition by whoever plays it back; frozen, a
thing (reel, cylinder, tape, file), copyable from a master, forgeable; admissible in in-fiction cases, never in
conduct cases.

### What is new with sound and motion

1. **Duration** — a recording is a sequence, played back in time: *"on the screen, a tall man walks into the
   square…"*
2. **Fidelity is physics, tied to the medium** — a wax cylinder holds minutes with hiss; a reel of film runs about
   eleven minutes.
3. ⭐ **Editing is a craft that makes new work** — cutting and reordering captured material; the editor's choices are
   an argument (the Kuleshov effect: one actor's face reads as hunger, grief or desire by what it is cut against).
4. **Editing to deceive is forgery**, and stays play; the record never certifies an in-fiction recording.
5. ⭐ **Continuous recording is surveillance** — a crime under a camera is easy to solve. **Inside a private extent,
   the holder's rule; in public, the polity's law** — a city wired with cameras solves crime and loses privacy; one
   without stays free and harder to police. A real political choice, and players choose where to live.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The rule, sharpened **[DECIDED — the owner's rule, lens 3a]**

Players acting a play in text make art in the game's own medium — **on-page art**; recording it is the game
recording its own rendering. So:

> **The game never captures off-page work. Anything on-page — the world, events, and on-page art like a play
> performed in text — may be recorded.**

A real pianist streaming on a stage is off-page: a recording of that stage gets the stage, never the music. Players
performing *Hamlet* in text are on-page: their performance may be filmed, edited and screened. ⛔ **The game never
judges art, on-page or off** **[DECIDED — lens 1]** — recordable is not ratable; only technical results (exposure,
fidelity, an unbroken take) are measured, and the audience judges the work.

### Mapping it back

### Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — acoustics, photochemistry, magnetism, the sampling theorem; editing and the Kuleshov effect;
   **real acoustics for sound; the game never judges art**.
2. **Expression** — ⭐ **one recording model for every medium** **[DECIDED — lens 2]**: a device is a tool row
   declaring its senses, its span and its fidelity — a camera, a phonograph, a tape deck, a phone; no mechanics
   per medium; editing makes new work with no code; **one slate for recording** (this one).
3. **3a Immersion** — playback in time, in the world's prose; **the sharpened rule**; the per-player memory stays
   meta. **3b Participation** — player films and radio dramas, oral histories, surveillance politics, recordings as
   blackmail, forged recordings and the detective work of exposing them.
4. **Values** — surveillance in public is the polity's, in private the holder's; what a player performs and shows
   falls under the conduct code like any expression.
5. **Continuity** — record, play back, edit: the same commands on every rung. ⭐ **Playback needs a compatible
   device** **[DECIDED — lenses 5, 1]**: a wax cylinder needs a phonograph, a reel a projector — format

6. **Economy** — devices, consumable media (film stock, tape), studios, editing, cinemas, archives, surveillance as

7. **Governance** — surveillance law is the polity's; ⭐ **recording a private conversation is the polity's law**

## 11. ⭐⭐⭐ Sound recordings **[DECIDED in shape — lens pass]**

### What an on-page sound recording captures

The acoustic channel at the microphone, under real acoustics: **speech** — what was said, the speaker's voice
described, never named; **sound** — footsteps, a door, rain, the mill's machinery (places and weather already emit
sound); **never off-page sound** — record a stage where a real pianist streams and you get *"a pianist plays a slow
nocturne; the audience is silent"*, never the music. Playback renders as lines in the world: *"From the phonograph,
a low man's voice: 'Meet me at the wharf.'"*

⭐ **Voice recognition is added to belief** **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 3a]** — recognition is by sight today; knowing
someone by their voice is real and learnable, and recorded speech is evidence of *who* only if a listener can
recognize the voice. Voice disguise comes with it ([belief.md](../../subsystems/belief.md)).

### ⭐⭐ Fidelity as signal and noise **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

Every medium has a **noise floor** — a wax cylinder hisses, tape less, digital barely. **A sound is captured clearly
only if it is louder than the noise**: a nearby voice comes through whole; a distant one **in fragments** —
*"…the wharf… after dark…"*; a whisper on a wax cylinder is lost. **Deterministic, never rolled** — how much of a
line survives follows from loudness, distance, walls and the medium: signal-to-noise ratio, a real engineering
concept, making *"can we make out what he said on the tape?"* an investigative question with an answer.

### Copies, pressings and splices

- ⭐ **Analog copies degrade** — each tape-to-tape copy adds noise (generation loss), so the original is worth more
  than any copy and a tenth-generation bootleg is visibly worse; digital copies are perfect.
- **Pressings follow the printing curve** — a master cut, a stamper made, records pressed cheaply after: a large
  fixed cost and a small marginal one, how the record industry worked. Shellac — a real material from the
  secretion of lac insects — then vinyl.
- **Splicing** rearranges what was said; ⭐ **splicing to forge** — making someone "say" a sentence they never said —
  is forgery as play, and **on analog tape a splice leaves a physical trace an expert can find**; digital edits are
  far harder to detect, which is real too.

### Music, on-page and off

- **On-page music is ordinary craft** **[DECIDED — lens 2]** — a player sings in the world (lyrics as speech, a
  described performance): on-page art, recorded, a master cut, records pressed and sold — **a record industry for
  the world's own performers**.
- **Off-page audio follows the page boundary**, as video does: an off-page record plays real audio, **offered to the
  listener as an opt-in player** — the room gets a line, the listener chooses to hear it; never required, never
  autoplayed. ⭐ **The catalog is public-domain recordings plus off-page artists' own original recordings**, never
  anyone else's work **[DECIDED — lenses 6, 7]**; ⭐ **only recordings public domain where the host runs** **[DECIDED
  — lens 7]** — status differs by country (in the US, recordings made before 1923 entered the public domain in 2022,
  later years on a rolling schedule), and anything uncertain stays out.
- **Radio carries both** — on-page records as prose, off-page audio as real sound to receivers within range: real
  music, local by physics ([broadcasting-slate](./broadcasting-slate.md)).

### Lens pass

Pedagogy — acoustics, decibels, **signal-to-noise**, generation loss, Edison's cylinder (1877) and Berliner's disc
(1887), digital sampling, forensic audio. Expression — devices as rows; on-page music as ordinary craft; off-page
records as media rows. Immersion — playback as lines; off-page audio opt-in; the jukebox an object holding both
sides of the page. Participation — player musicians, labels, bootlegs, DJs, forensic audio, wiretaps. Values — the
jukebox's contents are the venue's; wiretap law the polity's. Continuity — the same commands from cylinder to
digital; format obsolescence holds. Economy — records as goods, pressing plants, labels, radio. Governance — public
domain where the host runs; splices findable on analog; disputed music by notice-and-takedown; the off-page player
never required.

## 12. ⭐⭐⭐ The jukebox — a shared *now playing* **[DECIDED in shape — the owner]**

The owner: *"I really want the jukebox to play real music if I can do it legally … if we can say 'Now playing: Huey
Lewis and the News — Power of Love' and make it very easy for someone using our web client to actually play that
song in Spotify using whatever capabilities are at their disposal, that would be great. Some ad hoc way to get some
sense of a group experience. To me that's the important part of the jukebox, not the recording part."*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The game never plays the music

- **A song's title and artist are facts** — *"Now playing: Huey Lewis and the News, 'The Power of Love'"* says what is
  playing.
- **The audio comes from each player's own service**, under their own subscription. We stream, host and perform
  nothing, so the public-performance question that ASCAP and BMI license mostly does not arise — the game is not a
  jukebox in the legal sense but a sign saying what the jukebox would play. ⚠ **A lawyer's confirmation before
  launch.**
- ⭐ **The precedent is Discord's *Listen Along*** — showing what a friend plays on Spotify and letting you join on your
  own account — inside a bar.
- **It is the purest form of the rule**: the game does not even host the off-page work; it only points at it.

### How it works

1. **A coin in, a song picked** — a coin per play, as real jukeboxes worked: income for Dave's Bar; request rules are
   house rules, the holder's.
2. ⭐ **Songs identified by open data** **[DECIDED — lens 2]** — MusicBrainz, a free, openly licensed music database;
   its identifiers tie the jukebox to no streaming company, and each player's client finds the song on the service
   they use.
3. **Everyone in the bar sees the same line** — *"The jukebox plays Huey Lewis and the News, 'The Power of Love'."* The
   shared line is the group experience, and works for someone who plays nothing.
4. ⭐ **Joining in, in time** — the jukebox knows when the song started and its length, so it hands each client **the
   current position**: with Spotify Premium and the account connected, the client starts the song on the player's
   device at that point, everyone roughly together; without Premium, an *open in Spotify* link; without Spotify, the
   line alone.
5. **The queue advances** when the song would end, by its length.
6. **Nothing is required** — no account, no autoplay; the bar is the same for someone who never presses the button.

### Things to know before building

- **Spotify's developer rules** — a new app starts restricted to a small number of users until Spotify approves wider
  use, and starting playback on a user's device needs Premium: **plan the approval before launch**; follow Spotify's
  branding and attribution rules.
- **Apple Music** has its own web toolkit — the same approach works. ⚠ **YouTube is the risky one** — its embedding
  rules do not allow hiding the player to use it as audio only, so a YouTube *listen along* must show the video.
- **This changes § 11's emphasis**: the public-domain catalog becomes a nice extra, not the jukebox's point. The point
  is *the song everyone is hearing right now*.

### Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the jukebox teaches music rights by how it is built: why the bar cannot play the song itself (public
   performance is licensed), and the honest way around it; the coin-operated jukebox's history from the 1930s.
2. **Expression** — players express themselves through what they play; venues through house rules; **open
   identifiers**, so the jukebox works for anyone, whatever service they use.
3. **3a Immersion** — an object in the world, *now playing* a line of prose; ⭐ **a real song is off-page, so its line
   carries the out-of-fiction mark** **[DECIDED — lens 3a]** — Huey Lewis in an industrial city is a signal from beyond
   the world, marked, not a crack in the fiction; **a jukebox is Electric-rung**, so a Wheel-covenant tavern has none.
   **3b Participation** — song battles, requests, the bar's playlists, regulars with their song.
4. **Values** — taste is the venue's: the coin price, the request rules.
5. **Continuity** — ⭐ **one *what's playing* line, whatever the source** **[DECIDED — lens 5]**: the jukebox (a link to the
   player's own service), a live performer streaming off-page, or a player singing on-page — the same line, each
   source saying its side of the page; on lower rungs the performer or singer fills the place.
6. **Economy** — a coin per play for the bar, the demand there first (the backstop's social space); **artists are paid
   through each listener's own stream** — real plays to real artists, nobody here paying; the cost is the Spotify
   integration and its approval.
7. **Governance** — requests are expression in a venue: house rules and the conduct code, moderated locally; ⭐ **the
   jukebox shares only what the jukebox plays, never a player's own listening** **[DECIDED — lens 7]** — Discord
   broadcasts what you listen to; we will not; a connected account plays music *for* you and never announces your taste
   unless you choose to.
