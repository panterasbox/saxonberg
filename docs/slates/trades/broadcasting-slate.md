## 0. Why reach comes from the transmitter **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 3a]**

The owner, on area channels: *"now you're talking about things we can actually model — broadcast reach and
radio waves and such. The aether supports all that, but we've done nothing with it. We could either use radio
for real or try to make the aether actually reflect some kind of fictional reach."*

- **The aether's founding postulate is that it is everywhere** — information crosses it with no distance,
  which is why a DM always works ([aether-load-bearing purpose](../engine/aether-devices-slate.md)). A channel
  fading at the city limits *because of the aether* would betray it (lens 3a).
- **So reach comes from a transmitter**, by real physics — derivable and teachable (lens 1). An area channel
  is administration, not physics: membership by presence, the town's aether node's policy
  (moderation § 24).

## 1. The model **[DECIDED in shape]**

- **A station is a thing in the world** — a transmitter with a power and a frequency, drawing on the grid.
- **A listener needs a receiver** — a radio set, or the phone, already a tuner.
- **Signal at the receiver falls off by the inverse square law**, plus attenuation by terrain and buildings;
  above the receiver's sensitivity you hear it, and near the edge you hear it through static.
- **One-to-many, not chat** — a call-in show is the station taking a phone call (a DM to the studio) on air.
- **Epoch content** — broadcast radio is the Electric rung (the 1920s town with light and a radio but no
  phones); a covenant may forbid it, and a Wheel-era town has no stations.
- **Within one world** — radio does not cross dimensions; the aether does. The two keep honestly different
  jobs.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Spectrum is a commons, so it needs law **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

Two stations on one frequency jam each other — why real countries license spectrum (the FCC in the US).

- **A polity allocating bands is a Tier C consumer** — a typed clause per assignment (*this band to this
  station within this extent*), a licensing tool, enforcement against pirate stations
  ([separation-of-powers-slate](../society/separation-of-powers-slate.md) §§ 3, 7f).
- **Granting or revoking a license judges a person** — a tool with a notice and an appeal.
- **How to allocate is the polity's** — licenses, auctions (Ronald Coase argued in 1959 that the FCC should
  auction spectrum as property; auctions are what the US adopted), or open unlicensed bands like Wi-Fi's.
- **Censorship by licensing has real precedent**; the Compact's floor and the appeal upward answer it.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The trades — broadcasting is probably two **[LEAN — the owner]**

As printing splits the machine from the medium, so does radio:

| trade | what it does | rung |
|---|---|---|
| **broadcasting** | running a station: programming, scheduling, on-air talk, call-in shows — the medium, a sibling of printing | Electric and up |
| **radio engineering** | building transmitters and receivers — the hardware; probably part of a broader electronics trade, not radio's alone | Electric and Signal |

**Journalism is the craft and broadcasting one of its media** ([journalism-slate](../society/journalism-slate.md)
§ *Vocabulary*): a station is a publisher with a transmitter. The Saxonberg Post could run one; a Terminus station
would be local by physics, not by decree.

## 7. ⭐⭐⭐ 2026-11-18 — what broadcasting is, in a game that is not only text

The owner: *"This interesting thing about this one is that we actually are launching with real broadcasting.
Me, on Twitch and YouTube, manifest on fictionalized screens within the game's narrative, but actual
streaming video content in the client. It's not radio, it's the aether, but it's still me actually doing what
the game considers a 'deed' for a given discipline. How we measure any of it I dunno, but the audience will
hopefully tell us … it's not just a text game. Not really."*

### The definition **[DECIDED — the owner]**

> **Broadcasting is delivering a program to an audience: one source, many receivers, on a schedule.**

### Two carriers **[DECIDED — the owner]**

| carrier | how it reaches | media | at launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the aether** | everywhere — its founding property: no distance | **real media**: Twitch, YouTube and Kick video played on in-game screens | **yes** — the owner's show, and any player who streams |
| **physical** | by physics — a voice across a square, a radio wave falling off with distance | text and sound in the fiction: the crier, radio, later television | the crier yes; radio when wanted |

- **The aether carries the real-world stream because it is everywhere**, so an aether broadcast has no
  geographic reach — the in-fiction reason the show plays on every screen in every world. Radio is local by
  physics. Different products.
- **The covenants still apply**: a stream needs a powered screen; in a Wheel-covenant town whose screens are
  powered down by law the show does not play, and the crier or the Post's paper edition carries the news
  ([journalism-slate](../society/journalism-slate.md) § *the Saxonberg Post*).
- **Much already ships** — the body that streams and the streamer axis ([livestream.md](../../subsystems/livestream.md)),
  `stream` with **segments and an agenda**, `watch` and `tune` over the three transports
  ([streaming.md](../../subsystems/streaming.md)), and screens in the world showing a stream
  ([display.md](../../subsystems/display.md)).

### ⭐⭐ One trade across the ladder **[DECIDED — lens 2]**

The act — one person announcing to everyone within reach — exists on every rung; only the carrier changes:

| rung | how it reaches | what already models it |
|---|---|---|
| Muscle, Draft | **the town crier** — a real job in medieval towns, still a ceremonial one | acoustic speech and `shout`'s reach |
| Wheel, Engine | **bells and beacon fires** | sound; light and its falloff |
| Engine, Electric | **the printed broadside** — posters, the newspaper | printing |
| Electric | **radio** | this slate |
| Signal | **television** — a station sending pictures to screens | the display subsystem's stream source, reached by broadcast |
| the aether | **the stream** | streaming and display, shipped |

The same act answers the same command on every rung (lens 5's test). The crier is a job with no code.

### ⭐ Radio is light at another frequency **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

The light subsystem already computes a signal's falloff with distance and its absorption through materials
(inverse square, Beer–Lambert). Radio and light are the same physics — electromagnetic radiation — so
**radio propagation is the light model at another frequency**, materials absorbing it differently (walls stop
light and mostly pass radio, which is why a station is heard indoors). One law with a frequency parameter, and
a true lesson: a radio wave and a sunbeam are the same thing.

### What is on the air

- **The studio is a room whose speech is relayed** to every receiver in range — a DJ or a news reader just
  talks; text is the platform's native medium, so radio is the easiest broadcast medium to make real.
- **Call-ins** are a phone call to the studio, put on air; **programming** is a schedule (news on the hour,
  the real weather field's report, a talk show, a serial); **recorded programs** use the recording instrument
  (journalism-slate); **sponsorship** — a corpo underwriting a show — is the natural first revenue.

### ⭐⭐ Craft and audience are separate axes **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 7]**

As the journalism slate's *craft and credibility are orthogonal*:

| axis | the question | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **craft** (the Discipline) | can you produce a program? | ⭐ **a published agenda, kept** — segments run as scheduled, guests hosted, call-ins taken: the stream subsystem already records segments and agendas. **Never hours live** — that rewards presence, which the abstraction law forbids |
| **audience** (reputation) | do people come? | viewers, reactions, return visits — renown's reception signals |

*"The audience will tell us"* is the second axis exactly: standing, never skill. ⭐ **Audience measures belong
to the broadcaster** — the engine counts and shows them to the broadcaster; publishing them is the
broadcaster's choice (the engine measures, the subject values); renown still derives as it does.

### Outlets: venues own their screens **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

A screen belongs to whoever holds its place: Dave's Bar decides what its screen shows, as a real bar picks
the game. **Broadcasters** make programs, **venues** choose what is on their screens (their pen over their
displays), **sponsors** underwrite. On a public building — Saxonberg's windows — what a screen shows is
government speech.

### The first broadcasting institution is the owner's show

A committee with seats — the owner and the stream moderators whose grants reach into Twitch and YouTube
([moderation-slate](../society/moderation-slate.md) § 24). **Court day**, the moderation court held on
the stream (moderation § 14a), is broadcast content: the judiciary on air.
