## 0. Why reach comes from the transmitter **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 3a]**

The owner, on area channels: *"now you're talking about things we can actually model — broadcast reach and
radio waves and such. The aether supports all that, but we've done nothing with it. We could either use radio
for real or try to make the aether actually reflect some kind of fictional reach."*

- **The aether's founding postulate is that it is everywhere** — information crosses it with no distance,
  which is why a DM always works ([aether-load-bearing purpose](../engine/aether-devices-slate.md)). A channel
  fading at the city limits *because of the aether* would betray it (lens 3a).
- **So reach comes from a transmitter**, by real physics — derivable and teachable (lens 1). An area channel
  is administration, not physics: membership by presence, the town's aether node's policy
  (moderation § 24).

## 1. The model **[DECIDED in shape]**

- **A station is a thing in the world** — a transmitter with a power and a frequency, drawing on the grid.
- **A listener needs a receiver** — a radio set, or the phone, already a tuner.
- **Signal at the receiver falls off by the inverse square law**, plus attenuation by terrain and buildings;
  above the receiver's sensitivity you hear it, and near the edge you hear it through static.
- **One-to-many, not chat** — a call-in show is the station taking a phone call (a DM to the studio) on air.
- **Epoch content** — broadcast radio is the Electric rung (the 1920s town with light and a radio but no
  phones); a covenant may forbid it, and a Wheel-era town has no stations.
- **Within one world** — radio does not cross dimensions; the aether does. The two keep honestly different
  jobs.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Spectrum is a commons, so it needs law **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

Two stations on one frequency jam each other — why real countries license spectrum (the FCC in the US).

- **A polity allocating bands is a Tier C consumer** — a typed clause per assignment (*this band to this
  station within this extent*), a licensing tool, enforcement against pirate stations
  ([separation-of-powers-slate](../society/separation-of-powers-slate.md) §§ 3, 7f).
- **Granting or revoking a license judges a person** — a tool with a notice and an appeal.
- **How to allocate is the polity's** — licenses, auctions (Ronald Coase argued in 1959 that the FCC should
  auction spectrum as property; auctions are what the US adopted), or open unlicensed bands like Wi-Fi's.
- **Censorship by licensing has real precedent**; the Compact's floor and the appeal upward answer it.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The trades — broadcasting is probably two **[LEAN — the owner]**

As printing splits the machine from the medium, so does radio:

| trade | what it does | rung |
|---|---|---|
| **broadcasting** | running a station: programming, scheduling, on-air talk, call-in shows — the medium, a sibling of printing | Electric and up |
| **radio engineering** | building transmitters and receivers — the hardware; probably part of a broader electronics trade, not radio's alone | Electric and Signal |

**Journalism is the craft and broadcasting one of its media** ([journalism-slate](../society/journalism-slate.md)
§ *Vocabulary*): a station is a publisher with a transmitter. The Saxonberg Post could run one; a Terminus station
would be local by physics, not by decree.

## 5. Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the inverse square law, propagation, spectrum scarcity and the license-or-auction argument;
   reception is derivable — a player can work out where they will hear a station.
2. **Expression** — a station is content: a transmitter row with power and frequency.
3. **3a Immersion** — reach never from the aether. **3b Participation** — stations, pirate radio, jamming wars,
   spectrum politics.
4. **Values** — how to allocate spectrum is the polity's.
5. **Continuity** — radio exists from the Electric rung up.
6. **Economy** — a trade: transmitters, receivers, stations as businesses, sponsorship (a natural place for
   corpos); built when the demand exists.
7. **Governance** — spectrum as Tier C law; a license is a tool with notice and appeal.
