## 0. Why reach comes from the transmitter **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 3a]**

The owner, on area channels: *"now you're talking about things we can actually model — broadcast reach and
radio waves and such. The aether supports all that, but we've done nothing with it. We could either use radio
for real or try to make the aether actually reflect some kind of fictional reach."*

- **The aether's founding postulate is that it is everywhere** — information crosses it with no distance,
  which is why a DM always works ([aether-load-bearing purpose](../engine/aether-devices-slate.md)). A channel
  fading at the city limits *because of the aether* would betray it (lens 3a).
- **So reach comes from a transmitter**, by real physics — derivable and teachable (lens 1). An area channel
  is administration, not physics: membership by presence, the town's aether node's policy
  (moderation § 24).

## 1. The model **[DECIDED in shape]**

- **A station is a thing in the world** — a transmitter with a power and a frequency, drawing on the grid.
- **A listener needs a receiver** — a radio set, or the phone, already a tuner.
- **Signal at the receiver falls off by the inverse square law**, plus attenuation by terrain and buildings;
  above the receiver's sensitivity you hear it, and near the edge you hear it through static.
- **One-to-many, not chat** — a call-in show is the station taking a phone call (a DM to the studio) on air.
- **Epoch content** — broadcast radio is the Electric rung (the 1920s town with light and a radio but no
  phones); a covenant may forbid it, and a Wheel-era town has no stations.
- **Within one world** — radio does not cross dimensions; the aether does. The two keep honestly different
  jobs.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Spectrum is a commons, so it needs law **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

Two stations on one frequency jam each other — why real countries license spectrum (the FCC in the US).

- **A polity allocating bands is a Tier C consumer** — a typed clause per assignment (*this band to this
  station within this extent*), a licensing tool, enforcement against pirate stations
  ([separation-of-powers-slate](../society/separation-of-powers-slate.md) §§ 3, 7f).
- **Granting or revoking a license judges a person** — a tool with a notice and an appeal.
- **How to allocate is the polity's** — licenses, auctions (Ronald Coase argued in 1959 that the FCC should
  auction spectrum as property; auctions are what the US adopted), or open unlicensed bands like Wi-Fi's.
- **Censorship by licensing has real precedent**; the Compact's floor and the appeal upward answer it.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The trades — broadcasting is probably two **[LEAN — the owner]**

As printing splits the machine from the medium, so does radio:

| trade | what it does | rung |
|---|---|---|
| **broadcasting** | running a station: programming, scheduling, on-air talk, call-in shows — the medium, a sibling of printing | Electric and up |
| **radio engineering** | building transmitters and receivers — the hardware; probably part of a broader electronics trade, not radio's alone | Electric and Signal |

**Journalism is the craft and broadcasting one of its media** ([journalism-slate](../society/journalism-slate.md)
§ *Vocabulary*): a station is a publisher with a transmitter. The Saxonberg Post could run one; a Terminus station
would be local by physics, not by decree.

## 4. When it gets built **[DECIDED — lens 6]**

**Designed now, built when Electric-rung content needs it** — nothing on that rung exists to want it yet.
⚠ **Corrected in § 7**: the rung exists (Terminus is on the grid); radio waits for *demand*, not the rung. The
campus's Electric and Signal zones teach it from day one, in the general labs
([eternal-university-slate](../world/eternal-university-slate.md) § 2026-11-18).

2. **Expression** — a station is content: a transmitter row with power and frequency.
3. **3a Immersion** — reach never from the aether. **3b Participation** — stations, pirate radio, jamming wars,

## 7. ⭐⭐⭐ 2026-11-18 — what broadcasting is, in a game that is not only text

The owner: *"This interesting thing about this one is that we actually are launching with real broadcasting.
Me, on Twitch and YouTube, manifest on fictionalized screens within the game's narrative, but actual
streaming video content in the client. It's not radio, it's the aether, but it's still me actually doing what
the game considers a 'deed' for a given discipline. How we measure any of it I dunno, but the audience will
hopefully tell us … it's not just a text game. Not really."*

### The definition **[DECIDED — the owner]**

> **Broadcasting is delivering a program to an audience: one source, many receivers, on a schedule.**

### Two carriers **[DECIDED — the owner]**

| carrier | how it reaches | media | at launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the aether** | everywhere — its founding property: no distance | **real media**: Twitch, YouTube and Kick video played on in-game screens | **yes** — the owner's show, and any player who streams |
| **physical** | by physics — a voice across a square, a radio wave falling off with distance | text and sound in the fiction: the crier, radio, later television | the crier yes; radio when wanted |

- **The aether carries the real-world stream because it is everywhere**, so an aether broadcast has no
  geographic reach — the in-fiction reason the show plays on every screen in every world. Radio is local by
  physics. Different products.
- **The covenants still apply**: a stream needs a powered screen; in a Wheel-covenant town whose screens are
  powered down by law the show does not play, and the crier or the Post's paper edition carries the news
  ([journalism-slate](../society/journalism-slate.md) § *the Saxonberg Post*).
- **Much already ships** — the body that streams and the streamer axis ([livestream.md](../../subsystems/livestream.md)),
  `stream` with **segments and an agenda**, `watch` and `tune` over the three transports
  ([streaming.md](../../subsystems/streaming.md)), and screens in the world showing a stream
  ([display.md](../../subsystems/display.md)).

### ⭐ Radio is light at another frequency **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

The light subsystem already computes a signal's falloff with distance and its absorption through materials
(inverse square, Beer–Lambert). Radio and light are the same physics — electromagnetic radiation — so
**radio propagation is the light model at another frequency**, materials absorbing it differently (walls stop
light and mostly pass radio, which is why a station is heard indoors). One law with a frequency parameter, and
a true lesson: a radio wave and a sunbeam are the same thing.

### What is on the air

- **The studio is a room whose speech is relayed** to every receiver in range — a DJ or a news reader just
  talks; text is the platform's native medium, so radio is the easiest broadcast medium to make real.
- **Call-ins** are a phone call to the studio, put on air; **programming** is a schedule (news on the hour,
  the real weather field's report, a talk show, a serial); **recorded programs** use the recording instrument
  (journalism-slate); **sponsorship** — a corpo underwriting a show — is the natural first revenue.

### ⭐⭐ Craft and audience are separate axes **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 7]**

As the journalism slate's *craft and credibility are orthogonal*:

| axis | the question | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **craft** (the Discipline) | can you produce a program? | ⭐ **a published agenda, kept** — segments run as scheduled, guests hosted, call-ins taken: the stream subsystem already records segments and agendas. **Never hours live** — that rewards presence, which the abstraction law forbids |
| **audience** (reputation) | do people come? | viewers, reactions, return visits — renown's reception signals |

*"The audience will tell us"* is the second axis exactly: standing, never skill. ⭐ **Audience measures belong
to the broadcaster** — the engine counts and shows them to the broadcaster; publishing them is the
broadcaster's choice (the engine measures, the subject values); renown still derives as it does.

## 8. ⭐⭐ The town crier **[DECIDED in shape]**

**No new mechanics.** `shout` already carries acoustically across nearby rooms, and `ring` on a bell is *"loud
enough to be heard in the next street."* A crier rings a bell, shouts a proclamation, and posts the paper
notice after — three existing acts and paper. **The crier is a job** ([lens 2]); what matters is who employs
it and what it is for.

### What a crier did

Proclaimed the law (new ordinances, proclamations, market days) and then nailed the notice to a post — where
*posting* news comes from; announced for hire (merchants' sales, lost property, events); was protected —
harming a crier was an offense against whoever sent them, the original *don't shoot the messenger*; still
exists ceremonially, with competitions for the best cry (*"Oyez, oyez, oyez"*).

### What it is for, when the aether delivers everything free

1. **Places the aether's screens do not reach** — in a Wheel-covenant town, tuners powered down by law, news
   arrives by voice, the Post's paper edition, or the crier. On the lower rungs the crier *is* broadcasting.
2. ⭐ **Notice of the law, in the fiction** — due process rests on people being able to know the law; in real
   systems a law binds once **promulgated**, and the crier was how. Ours are in force on enactment
   ([separation-of-powers-slate](../society/separation-of-powers-slate.md) § 3), but **a polity may choose a
   promulgation rule** — a covenant town's ordinances bind once cried in the square and posted.
3. **Presence** — news spoken by a person in a square is part of the place.
4. **Advertising** — a merchant pays to have a sale cried, the oldest ad market there is.
5. **Ceremony** — opening a market, announcing a verdict; on any rung, even a city full of screens.

### Who holds the seat, given the pen

- **The city's crier is a public office**: its proclamations are government speech; it cries what the city's
  executive gives it. **A private crier** works for whoever hires them — merchants, the Post, a candidate:
  private speech.
- **NPC or player** — crying prepared text is mechanical, so an NPC may hold the floor, ⭐ **but only for
  official notices** **[DECIDED — lens 7]**: judging whether a paid private announcement is libel or breaks the
  conduct code is judgment, a person's. A private announcement needs a person to accept it — a player crier,
  or the employer's staff approving it before the NPC reads it. **A player crier adds judgment and
  performance** — a voice people stop to hear.

### Craft and economy

The Discipline is **public speaking and oratory** — projection, delivery, memory. Pay is the city's wage for
official notices and fees from private clients; the Post may hire criers to carry its headlines into
covenant towns. Craft is measured by **announcements delivered as agreed** — the agenda kept (§ 7), never
time spent shouting.

### Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — promulgation and notice (*ignorance of the law is no excuse* holds only because laws are
   published; real governments keep an official gazette for it; Rome posted laws in the Forum); oratory; the
   cry's reach derivable from the acoustic model.
2. **Expression** — **a job over existing verbs**, no new mechanics; anyone may hire a crier; promulgation a
   clause in a polity's own law.
3. **3a Immersion** — presence in the fiction. ⭐ **Nothing out of fiction depends on a crier**
   **[DECIDED — lens 3a]**: the Compact's notices, help and messages stay on the aether, so the never-strand
   rule holds even where screens are dark (`help` always works); the crier carries the fiction's local news
   and law, never the meta layer's. **3b Participation** — player criers with personality, crying
   competitions, rival criers, criers hired for an election, heckling.
4. **Values** — whether a town requires promulgation, and which notices are cried: the polity's.
5. **Continuity** — every rung from Muscle up; in a city of screens, ceremony.
6. **Economy** — wages and fees. ⭐ **A seat a locality creates when it wants one, not a launch requirement**
   **[DECIDED — lens 6]**: Terminus has screens at launch, and the real demand — covenant towns, ceremony, ads
   in a square — is small.
7. **Governance** — ⭐ **if a polity adopts promulgation, crying an enacted law is a *ministerial* duty with a
   deadline, and silence enacts** **[DECIDED — lens 7]**: otherwise an executive blocks a law by never crying
   it — the slow-walk again. The crier has no discretion over enacted law; uncried by the deadline, the law
   takes effect anyway, as the PM's window and the court's. *Marbury v. Madison* was about a commission
   nobody delivered, and the Court called delivering it ministerial. **Only a person accepts a private
   announcement.** Protecting criers at work is the polity's law; the crier's acts are logged like any
   official's.

## 9. ⭐⭐⭐ Television **[DECIDED in shape — lens pass]**

Television is mostly built from pieces already designed — broadcasting's carrier, the on-page relay
([display-manifestation-slate](../client/display-manifestation-slate.md) § *The page boundary*), the recording model
([recording-slate](./recording-slate.md)). The question is what it adds that the aether's streams and the cinema do not.

### What only television gives

1. **It is local** — a station reaches the screens within its signal, not everywhere: Terminus TV is Terminus's.
2. **It is scheduled** — a lineup, a time slot, *what's on tonight*.
3. **It has channels** — a dial with a few stations, because spectrum is scarce.
4. **It is in the home** — a set in the living room, watched together; and on the bar's wall for the big game.
5. **It is paid for by commercials** — the thirty-second spot.

### ⭐⭐ Reach is horizon geometry **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

Television signals travel roughly in line of sight, so **a station's reach ends at the horizon**, set by its antenna's
height. The geography build made the world **a third of Earth's radius**, so the horizon is nearer and a tower reaches
less far than on Earth — a consequence nobody authors: put the antenna on a hill and the station covers more of the
realm. The light model again (§ 7), at television's frequencies. **A station is a row** — transmitter power,
frequency, antenna height **[DECIDED — lens 2]**.

### What is on — programming in a text world

| program | here |
|---|---|
| **news** | anchors (players) at a desk; live on-page relays from reporters in the field |
| ⭐ **game shows** | the most text-native format there is — quizzes, with an obvious link to the university's quiz bowl |
| **serial drama** | player actors in a soap opera, recorded or live |
| ⭐ **sports** | live relays of in-world sports — the drill ground, races, varsity — with players as commentators |
| **weather** | from the real weather field |
| **the Assembly** | the floor, live — the C-SPAN idea |
| **films** | the cinema catalog, broadcast ([recording-slate](./recording-slate.md) § 13) |
| **an off-page stream** | a station may carry one locally; viewers in range get video mode, marked |

**Networks** link stations in different towns to share programs; **before videotape, programs traveled on film** — a
kinescope was a film shot off a television screen and shipped to distant stations — so here recorded programs travel
as goods: logistics.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Spectrum scarcity brings public obligations — and the press is different **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

- **Broadcasters use scarce public spectrum**, so in the US they could be held to public-interest obligations: the
  **Fairness Doctrine** (1949–1987) required licensees to air contrasting views on controversial issues, and the Supreme
  Court upheld it in ***Red Lion v. FCC*** (1969) *because* spectrum is scarce.
- **Newspapers use no scarce public resource**, so the same Court struck down a right-of-reply law for them in ***Miami
  Herald v. Tornillo*** (1974).
- So: **a television or radio license may carry public-interest conditions the polity sets — fairness, equal time for
  candidates, children's programming, each a typed clause — and the Saxonberg Post never can**
  ([journalism-slate](../society/journalism-slate.md)). Whether to impose them is a real choice; the scarcity is real, a
  world's spectrum finite.

### Ratings — with consent **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

Real television ratings came from Nielsen's **panels of households that agreed to be measured**. Each broadcaster sees
its own audience (§ 7); **a ratings service measures across stations only from viewers who opt into its panel** —
consent-based measurement, as it really worked (*the engine measures, the subject values*). Ratings sell advertising, so
a ratings service is a real business.

### Epoch and timing **[DECIDED — lens 6]**

Mechanical television (spinning disks, the 1920s) was Electric-rung; electronic television is Signal; covenants apply.
**Like radio, it waits for demand**: it needs on-page programs worth watching, which need the on-page relay first. Its
first real audiences are **sports in bars and the Assembly in public places**.

### Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the radio horizon (a formula over the planet's radius and the antenna's height); spectrum scarcity;
   two Supreme Court cases pointing opposite ways for opposite reasons; **sampling** in a ratings panel; mechanical
   against electronic television; the kinescope. **Reach is horizon geometry.**
2. **Expression** — programs are performances, relays and recordings, all on-page, no code; **a station is a row**.
3. **3a Immersion** — sets in homes and on bar walls; Signal-rung, so covenants apply; programs as prose by glance and
   watch; carried off-page streams marked. **3b Participation** — anchors, game-show hosts and contestants,
   sportscasters, soap stars, local news crews; the Assembly televised.
4. **Values** — public-interest obligations; advertising standards (the US banned cigarette advertising on television
   and radio in 1971); what each station airs.
5. **Continuity** — mechanical, electronic, digital, one command (watch it on a screen); distribution from kinescope to
   videotape to digital.
6. **Economy** — stations, networks, commercials sold to sponsors (corpos the obvious buyers), syndication, the ratings
   business; **waits for demand**, bar sports and the Assembly first.
7. **Governance** — **license conditions the polity's option, never imposed on the press**; **cross-station ratings
   only from opt-in panels**; spectrum law the polity's; licenses tools with notice and appeal.
