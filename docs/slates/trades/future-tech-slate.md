## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The rung: real physics at extreme parameters, with a story **[DECIDED in shape]**

- **The Future rung is the top of the prime-mover ladder** (Muscle · Draft · Wheel · Engine ·
  Electric · Signal · **Future**). It is a threshold, and anything past it may live here if it has
  math and a story.
- ⭐ **Start from real physics at extreme parameters** where it serves — fusion, the fabricator and
  the automaton each plug into equations the engine already computes. **Where it does not serve,
  invent** (§ 1a: *"we're making all this stuff up"*) — the sums must add up and a story explains
  them. ⚠ **[OPEN]** whether an invented future mechanism counts against
  [arcane-science.md](../../arcane-science.md)'s budget of three invented things, which calls a
  fourth a modelling error.
- **Where the infinities live** — faster-than-light travel, time travel, free energy (Galt's motor in
  *Atlas Shrugged*: power drawn from the air with no fuel and no cost). None of those is on this
  rung. Faster-than-light **information** is already the aether's one exception (the second
  postulate), and nothing else crosses it.
- ⛔ **Magic is not this rung** ([content-declaration-slate](../platform/content-declaration-slate.md)
  § 3.0): magic is a flag on any rung that borrows specific capabilities from higher ones (§ 5).
- **Each idea extends a shipped mechanic** and answers the same commands (design-lenses § 5,
  continuity): a fabricator answers the crafting verbs, an automaton holds a seat, a fusion plant
  feeds the grid.

## 1a. ⭐⭐⭐ Three roots, a triangle, and a tree **[DECIDED — the owner, 2026-11-18]**

The owner: *"What I like about having multiple things like this is we can try our best to give every
one a downside, so there's some decision matrix at work as to what specific future tech you want to
employ for any given job. That we have three root tech nodes at the bottom of the rest of the tree
actually makes it interesting — it could even be a rock-paper-scissors kind of relationship between
the three. Again, we're making all this stuff up, so we can really do whatever we want with it.
That's why we also talk about it and magic in the same breath."*

**Invented, like magic — so the same rule binds both:** the sums must add up, and a story explains
the sums. The difference stands: future tech is a **rung**, magic is a **flag**.

### Each root is strong where the other two are weak

| root | its strength | its downside |
|---|---|---|
| **Fusion** | **scale** — enormous, cheap power | only at huge, fixed sites; capital-heavy; makes nothing and moves nowhere |
| **Fabricator** | **specificity** — makes *anything* from a design | energy-hungry per kilogram; slow on bulk; needs pure feedstock; designs are someone's property |
| **Automaton** | **adaptability** — goes anywhere, decides | draws power, wears out and needs parts; someone answers for it; it displaces people |

### Rock-paper-scissors, by job

- **Smelt a mountain of ore** — fusion wins; the fabricator is too slow, an automaton lacks the power.
- **A part you need right now** — the fabricator wins.
- **Tending, repair in the field, judgment work** — the automaton wins.

Each root loses the job it is not shaped for. The choice of root is the decision matrix.

### The cycle underneath: each root's downside is the next one's strength

- **Fusion powers the fabricator's** energy bill.
- **The fabricator makes the automaton's** parts.
- **Automata run the fusion plant** where people cannot safely work.

So the roots counter each other job by job and depend on each other overall: nobody wants only one,
and a polity that refuses one weakens the other two.

### The tree grows from pairs

| pair | what grows | its story |
|---|---|---|
| fusion + fabricator | **transmutation** — making elements | eases the fabricator's feedstock limit; the RGOs that supply rare elements lose their monopoly |
| fabricator + automaton | **machines that build machines** — the self-replicator | governance's nightmare: growth nobody ordered |
| automaton + fusion | **machines that go where people can't** — the deep sea, orbit | the [orbit-slate](../world/orbit-slate.md)'s station |
| all three | the top of the top | story to taste |

## 2. Fusion — the energy pack's generation, with a threshold **[LEAN]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **extends** | generation on the grid (`energy.md` — the Wharfside hydro feeding feeders and meters) |
| **the math** | D–T fusion releases about **3.4 × 10¹⁴ J per kg** of fuel; coal about **2.4 × 10⁷ J/kg** — some **ten million times** more |
| ⭐ **the threshold** | the **Lawson criterion**: density × confinement time × temperature must pass about **3 × 10²¹ keV·s/m³**, or the plant consumes more than it makes. Real, teachable, and literally a threshold |
| **feedstock** | deuterium from water (the water pack, the sea); tritium bred from **lithium** (a mining hook) |
| **who loses their living** | the fuel economy of the Engine band — collieries, the gasworks, the oil works |

**Lens 1:** the triple product is a real rule a student can reason about. A small plant fails and a
large one succeeds, so fusion has a **minimum scale**, which makes it civic infrastructure rather
than a household good (design-lenses § 7: *which private capability does the thing make common*).

**Open:** capital cost and minimum size; whether power this cheap breaks the energy pack's civic bill
(*too cheap to meter* was the fission promise, and it did not come true).

## 3. The fabricator — crafting with *any design* as the recipe **[LEAN]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **extends** | a crafting station; its recipe is a **design** — a row that names a product's composition and form |
| **the math** | **mass is conserved**: feedstock in = product out. Energy ∝ mass × bonds made (the material's enthalpy of formation). Time ∝ complexity ÷ throughput |
| **the threshold** | making anything from its elements and a design |
| **feedstock** | elements, which still come from mines, wells and farms — the RGOs survive |
| **who loses their living** | every making trade |

⭐ **Value moves to designs.** When anyone with feedstock and power can make anything, the scarce
thing is the design. That is Galt's story as content: an inventor who withholds a design from a
society they think does not deserve it (lens 7, governance; the labor chamber).

**Open:** ⚠ **who owns a design.** Recipes today are content rows, not property. A design that is
property needs a title (the chattel or parcel shapes), and copying it is the question every
intellectual-property regime fights over.

## 4. The automaton — a seat held by a made mind **[DECIDED in shape — the owner]**

- **The mechanic already exists.** Every NPC is an automaton telling a different story: seats do not
  care whether a person or a program holds them (design-lenses § 3b — *NPC/player interchangeability
  at the position level*).
- **What changes is the story, and the story is governance and economy:**
  - **it draws power, not wages** — work = power × time; a person sustains about 100 W, a machine
    draws kilowatts and needs maintenance ([exertion.md](../../subsystems/exertion.md));
  - **someone owns it** — a bought capital good (the chattel shape), so guarantee P7 (*a player
    answers for every NPC*) names its owner;
  - **it may displace a player** — the labor chamber's fight, and the sharpest version of *who holds
    the obsolete half*;
  - **a polity may refuse it** — the same law on operation as every other covenant (*power it
    down*), obeyed or broken in front of witnesses.
- **Open:** how an automaton is priced against a wage; whether an automaton-held seat reads
  differently in the roster.

## 5. What magic may borrow from this rung **[OPEN]**

Magic borrows *specific* capabilities from higher rungs, portioned, with a story and a cost. From

## 8. Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the Lawson criterion; conservation of mass at the fabricator; work as power ×
   time for the automaton. Real math at every idea.
2. **Expression** — the rung is an open bucket: an author adds an idea with math and a story, as a
   row over a shipped mechanic.
3. **Immersion** — every idea is a thing in the world. **Participation** — the designer, the plant
   operator, the automaton's owner; a polity may refuse any of them.
4. **Values** — *should* a made mind take a person's job? No right answer; the polity decides.
5. **Continuity** — each idea answers the same commands as the mechanic it extends. The obsolete
   half is largest here: the fuel economy, every making trade, labor itself.
6. **Economy** — fusion makes power cheap at scale; the fabricator moves value to designs and
   feedstock; the automaton moves wages to power bills and capital.
7. **Governance** — design ownership; a ban on automata; fusion as civic infrastructure.
