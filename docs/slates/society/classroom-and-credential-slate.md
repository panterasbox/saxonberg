## 0. The ask **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"We have a university intake path … you come from the lounge, walk onto campus and someone will
help you enroll in the university and get a dorm room if you want to live on campus. That's the
basic story we're telling but it ends at 'okay I'm in my dorm now what?' You probably should
enroll in a course."*

And the thing that decides the shape:

> *"The coursework and the exam isn't the product the university produces, not really. Their
> product is the credential. … We have a real record of 'hey I did this in class and it's on my
> permanent record' … the question is if he reads that directly or if it's only evidenced through
> something else. … These make the different credentials mean different things and we have to
> work through all of it, because … it's the credential that's the thing that actually has any
> real durative value."*

## 1. After the dorm: enroll in a course **[LEAN]**

The intake story continues at the registrar. You enroll in a course, and the first course worth
offering is the one with a job at the end of it: **bartending**, because Dave's Bar is the
backstop ([bar-audition § 0](../world/bar-audition-slate.md)).

- Enrollment is a **contract** (campus-grounds Part 3–4) whose consideration may be money, time,
  or both. A broke student pays in time, through an obligation clause.
- **Self-paced by default.** There is no term, no timetable, no fixed roster. Cohorts are not
  scheduled; they *form* (§ 3).

## 2. The bartending course **[LEAN]**

Shaped as the college slate's **one recursive course node**: a course holds topics, topics hold
lessons, to any depth, and an assessment can attach at any node.

- **Topics are the bar's Discipline subtree** ([bar-audition § 3](../world/bar-audition-slate.md)):
  technique, recipes, presentation, prep, product, sanitation, responsible service; operations for
  the keeper's track.
- **Lessons cite, never restate.** Each lesson points at the real articles. ⚠ Wikipedia is out of
  fiction (anchors slate: *"a player inside the world does not get a hyperlink to Earth"*), so the
  **course reader is an out-of-fiction surface**, like `help`, the wiki and the exam interface. The
  lab, the registrar and the wallet are in the fiction. *(Grain.)*
- **⭐⭐ Teach families and principles, not spec lists (lens 1 chose this).** Taught as spec lists,
  the dominant skill would be menu memorization, which is design-lenses' named failure. Real
  bartending is largely derivable:
  - the sour is a ratio template;
  - shake when there is citrus, egg or cream, and stir when it is all spirit;
  - dilution and chill follow from the ice, and the shaker already knows it chills about 8 K and
    adds about 20 mL.

  So the specs become derivable and the guide becomes a reference, not a crutch. ⚠ That only
  holds if the game **answers** a method error: a stirred daiquiri has to come out *wrong* in a way
  a player could predict (recipes slate § 3, method faults). If it does not, the principle cannot
  be tested by playing.

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ The lab, and the cohort that forms in it **[DECIDED — the owner; mechanism LEAN]**

*"The 'bartending lab' with the wet bar is like a single location on campus, only one player can
use it at a time; as soon as there's another client, we've organically formed a cohort. So I'd
want the design to accommodate that even if it's not what we're assuming is the default."*

The cohort is **not a scheduling concept. It is what happens when a scarce place is shared.**

- **One station, one hand at a time.** The wet bar is the contended resource. The shape that
  already exists for "one at a time, the rest wait" is the attendant substrate: you are poked when
  it is your turn and you mill about meanwhile, never frozen at a modal. Whether the station is an
  attendant queue or an engagement-slot lease is the plan's question.
- **⭐ Waiting is learning, for free.** Every witness in the room is written **known-of** when
  somebody makes a drink (crafting's knowledge ladder, shipped). So whoever is waiting their turn
  watches the person at the bar and learns from it. Contention produces peer demonstration with
  no new mechanism.
- **The instructor teaches whoever is present.** An NPC demonstrates at the bar, so the room's
  population at that moment *is* the class.
- **What a cohort can do that a lone student cannot** is the design's to make room for, not to
  schedule: watch, compare drinks, taste each other's work, argue about a spec in the room, share
  the guide. The lounge slate's furniture rule applies: *conversation scaffolding, not content*.
- **Accommodate, don't assume.** The design works for one student alone (the default) and gets
  richer with two. Nothing requires a second student, and nothing gets in its way.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ Four things that get called "the credential" **[LEAN]**

| | what it is | who holds it | how long it lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **learning** | what the *player* now knows how to do | the human | as long as they remember |
| **evidence** | the transcript: deeds and claims | the engine | permanent |
| **competence** | the band derived from the evidence | the engine, shown only to you | moves with the evidence |
| **credential** | an issuer's **dated attestation** about you, which you carry and present | you, in your wallet | ⭐ permanent: a fact about the past, by someone else |

- **An A never improves a drink by fiat (lens 1, invariant).** A drink's grade comes from what the
  player actually did. If a grade raised quality, *passed the exam, can't do it* would become
  physics. What makes the drink better is the **learning behind the A**: the A and the better drink
  are two effects of one cause.
- **The credential's value is durable because it is portable, third-party, dated and verifiable.**
  It is worth what its readers trust its issuer for (§ 8). It also teaches real economics: a
  credential is a **cheaper signal than a trial**, which is why employers take them.

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ Transcripts are private unless you present them **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"Transcripts are private unless you present them."*

- **People and institutions read only what you present.** That includes Dave, an employer, a
  guild and a committee. **The world reads your skill directly**: a lock's floor checking your
  band is physics, and stays.
- **The transcript presents whole (LEAN, lens 4).** No cherry-picked rows, because a selective
  transcript is just a credential, and the whole one is worth trusting for being whole. Real
  official transcripts work the same way.
- **So a reader can act on exactly three things:** a credential you present, a transcript you
  present, or what you do in front of them (the audition). Whether to reveal everything, show one
  credential, or show nothing and perform is an **undecidable choice** for the player, which is
  lens 4 working as intended.
- **Altitude, proposed: invariant.** It is a consent rule, and consent is one of the few things
  the platform imposes on purpose.

⚠ **Shipped code that this decision contradicts:** a seat's `requires: {band}` reads the
applicant's band on `job apply` with no presentation step (§ 6).

## 6. ⭐⭐ The credential, as data **[LEAN — lens 2 chose it]**

- **A new credential kind** in the wallet: a *qualification*, held as a record like `payment`,
  `travel` and `key`. Adding a kind is adding data. Its validity is the issuer's to confirm
  (credential.md: *carrying it is not the same as it being true*).
- **Verification is an act.** The aether is identity-blind, so Dave checks with the registrar. A
  forged certificate fails the lookup, and the lookup is something a player can see happen.
- **Credentials are granular,** per Discipline node or a named set of them. Real bars draw the
  same line between a food-handler card and a mixology certificate. A seat asks for exactly the
  nodes it needs.
- **⚠ Keyed to the Discipline node, never the course row.** Courses are content and content gets
  rewritten (content independence: [live-instance-testing § 5c](../tech-debt/live-instance-testing-slate.md)).
  A credential keyed to a deleted course becomes an orphan.
- **The hiring criterion moves from reading your band to presented credentials.** An employer
  authoring *which* credentials it accepts is expressive; a band threshold is not. Lens 2 chose
  this, and § 5 requires it. This changes employment's closed vocabulary
  (`{gigs, discipline, band}`) on purpose. It passes the vocabulary's test: a credential is
  something a player can go and DO.

## 7. ⭐⭐ Who pays: an initial endowment **[DECIDED — the owner, for now]**

*"The university can start with some initial endowment and we'll deal with long-term economic
viability later."*

- The university opens with an **endowment**: a house account funded once at founding, through
  the ledger's conservation chokepoint. It is a recorded issuance or a grant from the Compact's
  budget, never money from nowhere.
- It pays for the wet bar's well, the instructor and the proctor's time.
- Tuition from those who can pay flows in. Students who pay in time cost the endowment.
- **Long-term viability is DEFERRED by the owner.** Recorded so it is not mistaken for decided:
  an endowment that only drains is a clock.

## 8. Issuer trust is earned, never declared **[LEAN]**

- **A reader honors a credential as far as they trust its issuer,** and they learn that trust from
  how its graduates actually perform: behind Dave's bar, a school whose A-students cannot make a
  Negroni gets discounted.
- **⛔ No engine-published issuer score (lens 4).** A public rating is a gauge; it would turn
  *should I trust this school* into a calculation and delete the decision. The engine measures
  graduates' performance; **each reader values it**; players can publish their own reviews through
  the press and forums. *(Grain, and a strong one.)*
- **This is load-bearing, not decoration.** Players *will* found credential mills (lens 3b), and
  the design survives them only because trust comes from track record.
- **⚠ Recency-weighted, never a bare count.** A school must be able to recover its standing (lens
  7's worked example: a gate over an append-only record can only ever rise).
- **⭐ The audition is the universal appeal (proposed invariant for the backstop).** If Dave
  discounts a school, a *good* graduate of a bad school is refused because of other people's
  record. That is acceptable only if the audition is always open to anyone, because it is the one
  route that judges you and not your paperwork.

## 9. The lens pass

1. **Pedagogy:** the bar's subtree, every node anchored. Dominant: **judgment from principles**,
   not recall, *if* the curriculum teaches families (§ 2) and the game answers a method error.
   The credential teaches real economics (signaling). Lens 1 chose families over spec lists and
   ruled out an A improving a drink.
2. **Expression:** courses, item banks, issuers and accepted-credential lists are rows; a player
   can found a school or a guild, Dave can issue his own certificate, and a skilled player can
   write a course. What stays constant: the apparatus and the privacy rule. What varies:
   curriculum, pass marks, which issuers a reader honors. Lens 2 chose credentials over a band
   threshold.
3. **3a Immersion:** the reader and the exam are out of fiction; the lab, the registrar, the
   wallet and the verification are in it. A classroom needs an instructor at the bar, or it is an
   empty set. **3b Participation:** rival schools, guilds, credential mills, licensure by a city
   (assessment § 12.5, tier C), a Chancellor who refuses someone.
4. **Values:** study or practice; the strong exam or the weak one; **show everything, one
   credential, or nothing**. Standing is conferred by issuers and valued by readers, with no
   public gauge.
5. **Continuity:** the master's hall, the university, the online test are the same act. A
   lamplighter's license after the grid stays true and loses value, and nobody revokes it.
   Credentials keyed to nodes survive content rewrites.
6. **Economy:** produces trained people and trust; consumes ingredients, instructor and proctor
   time; paid by the endowment, with long-term viability deferred. ⚠ **Demand is thin: Dave's
   bartender seat has headcount 5 and four rostered NPCs, so the backstop has one open job** (§ 10).
7. **Governance:** admission (open, by contract, tier B); grading (item bank and pass mark,
   appealed by a retake or another issuer); ⚠ **expulsion for a breached obligation has no named
   appeal yet**; Dave's hire (accepted credentials or the audition, appealed by another
   credential, the audition or the university); issuer distrust (track record, recency-weighted,
   appealed by the audition).

## 10. Open

1. **Expulsion's appeal.** A breached obligation ends enrollment; who hears the student's side?
2. **One open job.** Players displacing NPC staff (and a roster that allows it), more bar work (a
   barback, the keeper, a second venue), or a backstop climb that is a one-person queue.
3. **The station's shape:** an attendant queue or an engagement-slot lease (the plan's).
4. **Credential expiry.** A credential is true forever; whether a reader discounts an old one is
   the reader's (assessment § 12.3).
5. **The privacy rule's altitude.** Invariant, as proposed in § 5, is the owner's call.
6. **The endowment's size and source** (a founding issuance or a Compact grant), and what happens
   when it runs low.
