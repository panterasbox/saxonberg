> market, the labour supply, who gets heard. Every other design decision
> is downstream of this one. — the founder, this session

The balance slate already supplies the vocabulary: *every global ledger
is a currency, and every parcel that writes to one is a **mint***
([balance-slate.md](./balance-slate.md) Part 1). Standing is a global
ledger. So the question is not whether standing is a currency — it is —
but **who holds the mint, on what conduct, at what weights, amendable by
whom.**

## Part 1 — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Yes, it is a morality score. Say it.

Not a euphemism for one. A morality score is a ranking of persons by
conduct against a value system, and that is exactly what standing is.
Every hedge available ("it's just influence", "it's revealed
preference", "it's an engagement metric") is a way of not saying the
thing, and the project does not hedge.

What follows from saying it plainly is a **better** design, because the
question stops being *"is this a morality score?"* (yes) and becomes
*"what distinguishes an acceptable one from social credit?"* That
question has a structural answer, and it is not "ours measures nicer
things."

**The five tests.** A conduct score is defensible to the degree it has:

1. **Exit.** Can you leave, or fork? AGPL + self-host says yes. *A
   social credit system you can fork is not one.*
2. **Published weights.** *The first screen is the syllabus*
   ([measurement.md](../../measurement.md) Part 7). Conditioning
   requires opacity; publishing the weights is what makes it teaching.
3. **Amendability, and by whom.** Tier C (the polity) is legitimate;
   Tier B (whoever ships the code) is where we must be most
   conservative; Tier A should contain almost nothing behavioural.
4. **Plurality.** Many raters with different weights is a *reputation*;
   one rater with universal reach is a *score*.
5. **No coupling to unrelated goods.** If standing gates travel or
   housing, it is social credit. If it is what people think of you and
   whether they will deal with you — that is society.

Hold those five and the project is defensible in public without
flinching. Lose any of them — particularly 4 — and it is not.

## Part 5 — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Two layers of argument, and only one of them is settleable

This session's argument had two layers that look alike and are not:

| | **mechanical** | **political** |
|---|---|---|
| asks | does the measure measure what it claims? | *whose work counts?* |
| examples | Goodhart, crowding-out, off-measurement collapse, validity | should bartending confer more than smithing |
| settled by | evidence, in-world, over time | deliberation — **never** |
| our standing to speak | high; this is engineering | **none beyond one vote** |

⚠ **And the trap:** mechanical arguments get *used as* political
weapons. "Crowding-out" will be cited, correctly and cynically, by
whoever benefits from not paying. Every argument in this slate will
become somebody's ammunition. That is what political arguments are made
of, and it is not a reason to withhold them — it is a reason to publish
them **in full, on both sides** (Part 9).

⚠⚠ **The bartender is not a villain.** A bartender arguing that
bartending should confer more standing is doing exactly what a polity is
*for*. Hospitality labour is systematically undervalued in real
economies; the bartender may simply be **right**. Special pleading is
ordinary interest-representation, not corruption. Any design that treats
"you benefit from this proposal" as disqualifying disqualifies everyone,
since everyone benefits from something — see the veto risk in Part 6.

## Part 6 — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Legibility is the only lever we actually have

The simulation cannot resolve the political layer. It can do exactly one
thing: **make special interests legible.** That is the founder's claim
and it is correct, and it turns out to be buildable and rare.

**The distributional impact statement.** When a weight change is
proposed, the engine can compute *before the vote*:

- who gains and who loses, by name and by magnitude — because the engine
  already knows who holds which positions, which deeds, which scope;
- the standing distribution under current weights vs proposed;
- who proposed it, and their own exposure to it, from the chronicle;
- who funded any campaign for it, from the authorship ledger.

Real analogue: the **CBO score / fiscal note / regulatory impact
assessment** — "make the interest legible without resolving the
politics." Real legislatures mostly *cannot* do this; we can do it
exactly, automatically, for every proposal. It is plausibly the single
most valuable civic instrument the platform can ship, and the fights
over *its methodology* would be the same fights as the real ones, which
is the point.

⚠ **The veto risk.** Legibility must not become a technocratic veto. If
the tool becomes a way to delegitimise any proposal by anyone who
benefits, it has destroyed deliberation rather than served it. The
framing that survives:

> **Having an interest is not disqualifying. Concealing it is.**

## Part 7 — ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The pathologies are required reachable states, not caveats

*Make the simulation honest* is the governing rule, and taken seriously
it **generates** the objections as content rather than defeating them. A
scored world that does not reach these is flattering, not honest — the
same failure as an abstraction that costs nobody the activity
([uncertainty.md](../../uncertainty.md)):

- **Goodhart.** When the measure becomes the target it stops measuring.
  Scored recycling gets farmed — buy cans to return cans, manufacture
  deeds. Already a live mechanism in-world: the Michigan/Indiana
  cross-border deposit arb (can slate § 2).
- **Auditing**, which becomes a real vocation *only because* the score
  exists — the second vocation the can mints, after the collector.
- **Rater capture** ([polity-decision-register.md](../../polity-decision-register.md) D9).
- **Crowding-out, as a ratchet.** Titmuss, *The Gift Relationship*
  (1970); Gneezy & Rustichini, *A Fine is a Price* (2000) — the daycare
  fines made lateness worse and stayed worse **after** the fine was
  withdrawn. A price can permanently destroy the cheaper mechanism it
  replaced.
- **Off-measurement collapse** (Part 8).

## Part 8 — ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Intrinsic vs extrinsic: same at the measured point, different everywhere else

The founder's challenge: *whether you recycle for intrinsic or extrinsic
reasons, the behaviour is the same — what's the difference?*

At the measured point, none. The difference is mechanical, not moral,
and it is entirely in the **unmeasured neighbourhood**:

1. ⭐⭐ **The unmeasured neighbours.** Paid per can, you return the can
   and leave the wrapper beside it. To the intrinsically motivated the
   can and the wrapper are one act; to the extrinsically motivated they
   are two, and one pays. **The commons is mostly made of unmeasured
   neighbours.**
2. **Unobserved behaviour.** Where measurement lapses — no scheme in
   this locality, nobody watching — extrinsic goes to zero and intrinsic
   does not. In a world with real partial observability (concealment,
   perception, belief are all shipped), that is *testable*, not
   philosophical.
3. **The ratchet.** Extrinsic can destroy the norm it replaced, and the
   norm does not come back.
4. **Adversarial gradient.** Extrinsic motivation produces *the
   appearance of the act at minimum cost*, which becomes fraud whenever
   fraud is cheaper. Intrinsic has no such gradient. That asymmetry is
   why scored systems need auditors and norms do not.

All four are simulable, and the lesson they deliver is one most adults
never learn: **incentives buy compliance at the measurement point and
nothing else.**

## Part 9 — ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The wiki carries the ARGUMENTS, not the conclusions

The founder's closing observation — *the argument the polity will have
is this argument, only political* — has a direct design consequence.

A player proposing a weight change should be able to find the
crowding-out argument. **Their opponent should be able to find the
revealed-preference rejoinder, stated just as well.** Both are real;
both have evidence; neither wins on the merits alone.

So the wiki stocks the **arsenal on both sides**, fairly stated — which
is *model the mechanism, never the verdict* applied to discourse instead
of to mechanics. This session's transcript is a worked example of the
shape: mechanical objection → political rejoinder → synthesis, with a
recorded retraction (see the [polity-decision-register.md](../../polity-decision-register.md)
D9 rationale) because retractions are what make an argument trustworthy.

This is also the polity-decision-register's own commitment: *the
eventual polity inherits arguments, not just settings.*

### ⭐⭐⭐ The three rules that outlive this slate

1. **Keep the measurable vocabulary small and physical.** We choose what
   is measurable *at all*; the polity only chooses weights. That is
   structural power no licence dissolves, because forking costs more
   than any player will pay.
2. **Prefer deeds over dispositions.** A deed is checkable and
   contestable; a disposition is an accusation.
3. **Treat every new measurable as a constitutional change, not a
   feature.**

## Part 11 — ⭐⭐⭐ Who earns what: the line between the stocks, and the standing cascade (2026-11-18/19) **[DECIDED in shape — the owner; lens pass]**

Talked through with the owner after the governance design
([separation-of-powers-slate](./separation-of-powers-slate.md)). Built today: consumer standing is
**engagement × renown** ([participation.md](../../subsystems/participation.md) — recognized commands from a real
player, decaying in real time; [renown.md](../../subsystems/renown.md) — how valued your presence is), labor standing
from authorship ([influence.md](../../subsystems/influence.md), [provenance.md](../../subsystems/provenance.md)),
capital from real money.

### The value-neutral mint, restated

The owner: *"help communities make choices it values as good. So a player base of Nazis can legitimately earn standing
for exterminating Jews, and that's the platform working. It sounds perverse, but it's actually instrumental in making
sure the platform can't become captured by its own inner workings … I don't actually want to make a Nazi MUD, so we also
have to talk about the real incentives we actually want to build for our actual instance."* Part 1's five tests are why
it is safe: **the kernel never decides what is good**; the weights are the polity's.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The line: in the fiction, or staging it **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"Labor previously meant worldcrafting — maintaining and enhancing the game. Simply 'playing it,' even if you're a seller,
is still consumption … for a consumer to do [labor's work] is actually kind of game-breaking, because that's all happening
outside the fiction, and the consumer exists to participate in the fiction."*

| stock | rewards | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **consumer** | **taking part in the fiction** | playing, trading, selling, socializing, giving blood |
| **labor** | **work outside the fiction that stages it** | fixing a bug, publishing an extent, codifying a law, moderating, judging cases |
| **capital** | real money funding the platform | subscriptions, Patreon, sponsorship |

A shopkeeper selling bread is playing — both sides of the sale are consumer activity. **The game needs both**: labor
stages it, consumers play in it.

**Why labor earns Compact standing at all** — *"the game isn't going to break down if Joe the plumber doesn't show up for
work. But if people stop maintaining the codebase, that's game-breaking. And no one's getting paid for any of that"*:
**the one thing the Compact can always give for unpaid work done for it is standing**
(separation-of-powers § 7h). **Compact standing is for Compact-scope work**; local work flows through the cascade (below).

### Consumer spending — spending, not earning **[DECIDED — the owner]**

Zorkmids and real money never convert, so in-game wealth is a result of play, and **what you spend matters more than
what you earn**: a consumer's defining act is consumption. Spending joins engagement, with **breadth** — spending across
many distinct sellers counts more than pouring everything into one friend (as tips count by distinct tipper), which also
blunts two players trading money back and forth. Zorkmids are conserved, so every flow is measured exactly; wash trading
is a required reachable state for auditors (Part 7). **[OPEN — absolute with diminishing returns, or relative to your
means.]**

### Quality, per stock

- **Labor** — **durability** (survived review and the challenge window) and ⭐ **use**: how much players engage with what
  you made — consumers playing in a place is the evidence its labor was good (the reputation slate's *content popularity
  is a labor signal*).
- **Consumer** — **regard**, renown's half of the product; spending measures engagement, not quality.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Civic service — the founder's thumb on the scale, honestly **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 7]**

The owner: *"To me the prototypical consumer standing comes from something like giving blood. Universal demand, it takes
time which no one else can serve, you don't get compensated for it, and barring blood type every donor is equal in the
eyes of the market. You do it just because it decreases suffering, and that's its only incentive. For that, you should
earn standing. But that's exactly me putting my thumb on the scale."*

**Three layers keep the thumb honest:**

1. **The kernel measures and never values** — it records that a donation happened ([blood.md](../../subsystems/blood.md)),
   with everything else countable; a Nazi realm and ours run the same engine.
2. ⭐ **Our instance's weights are received law** — in force since the founding, not enacted by vote, published and
   repealable ([legal-code-slate](./legal-code-slate.md)): **the founder's first vote, never a hidden constant**.
3. **The polity amends them.**

⭐ **Written as a criterion, not a list** — an act earns civic standing when it has the five properties of giving blood:
**universal demand** · **a personal cost no one else can pay for you** · **uncompensated** · **every giver equal** · **its
only purpose relieving others**. Derivable and general: giving blood, jury duty, mentoring a newcomer in the lounge, free
teaching, carrying the injured. ⭐ **It mirrors labor's logic exactly: standing is what the Compact gives where money does
not** — for labor, unpaid work staging the world; for consumers, unpaid service to others in it.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The standing cascade — local labor through the parcel tree **[DECIDED in shape — the owner]**

The owner: *"That person is doing labor outside the fiction, so by your criteria they deserve compensation. But it's not
direct by the Compact. It should flow through the parcel tree … at some nodes on the tree (as adjudicated by the Compact)
we inject a bunch of standing, an amount based on some kind of performance metrics, and then the operators of that extent
determine how to distribute that standing out to their own constituents. We probably need a few fixed models for them to
choose from … this also introduces conflict-of-interest laws because you shouldn't really be earning consumer standing and
labor standing on the same content … we need legislation for all of this."*

It rides the **allowance cascade**'s shape ([stewardship-slate](./stewardship-slate.md) § *The allowance cascade*):

```
Compact ──injects──▶ a node on the tree ──apportions──▶ its constituents
(the only mint)     (adjudicated, by       (by a model from a fixed menu)
                     performance metrics)
```

- **The Compact is the only mint**; an extent passes on what it was given and never creates standing.
- **Injection at nodes the Compact adjudicates** — a statute names the eligible (maintained localities, institutions);
  disputes go to the court.
- **By a published formula** — ⭐ **a share of the period's labor-standing pool, divided by measured performance**
  **[DECIDED — lens 1]**, so an extent can work out what it should receive. The metrics are what the engine counts:
  **use** (distinct players, repeat visits, participation inside the extent), **durability** (its content works, its code
  passed review), **maintenance delivered** (fixes and changes merged and surviving the challenge window). Never *is it
  good art*.

⭐ **The apportionment menu** **[DECIDED — lens 2]** — an extent's charter picks one, published; a change is logged; a sixth
only by bill:

| model | divides by | suits |
|---|---|---|
| **authorship share** | who authored what in the subtree (the authorship ledger) | content-heavy extents with many contributors |
| **bounty** | the extent's own maintenance bounties, paid by formula (separation-of-powers § 7h) | an extent with a work queue |
| **seat** | the maintaining seats, equally or by role | a small team |
| **allowance** | a committee's awards — fixed, targeted, logged, lapsing unspent | the human work no formula sees |
| **pass-through** | handed down to sub-extents, each applying its own model | a large tree such as Terminus |

⭐ **The conflict rule** **[DECIDED — lens 7]** — **activity by an extent's pen-holders (its authors and committee) inside
that extent counts for nothing**: it earns them no consumer standing, and it does not count toward the extent's use.
Computed from the authorship ledger and titles, so nobody judges intent. An author plays anywhere else normally; friends who
genuinely play are real use; organized fake visits are for auditors.

### The Standing Act — received law, tiered by clause

| clause | tier |
|---|---|
| the Compact is the only mint; extents transfer, never mint | **A** — the machinery's integrity |
| standing never comes from real money except through the capital chamber | **A** — the manifesto's floor |
| ⭐ one labor pool per period, its split between Compact work and the cascade **[DECIDED — lens 6]** | C |
| which nodes are eligible for injection | C |
| the injection formula and its metrics | C |
| the apportionment menu | C |
| the conflict rule | C, with a floor: it may be tightened, never removed |
| the civic-service criterion and the founder's other weights | C — received law |
| disputes | the court |

### Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — fiscal federalism: **formula grants**, and the block-versus-categorical argument mapped onto the menu;
   **injection by published formula**; **civic service as a derivable criterion**.
2. **Expression** — each extent's apportionment model is an expression of how it governs; **the five-model menu**.
3. **3a Immersion** — ⭐ **the standing cascade has no costume in the fiction** **[DECIDED — lens 3a]** — an honestly labeled
   out-of-fiction meter, as the compute allowance (*never give it a costume*); consumer acts happen in the fiction, the
   standing they earn is meta. **3b Participation** — extents compete for maintainers (Tiebout for labor); local politics
   over apportionment; auditing as a vocation.
4. **Values** — the founder's weights made visible and repealable; what counts as performance; spending absolute or
   relative.
5. **Continuity** — attribution outlives rewrites (the authorship ledger is kept forever); ⚠ injection binds to nodes, so
   grants binding to the title, not the path, applies here too.
6. **Economy** — **one pool per period, split by law**; reward follows demand (use); ⚠ the cascade's competition needs **a
   second city** to be real, the allowance cascade's same dependency.
7. **Governance** — **the conflict rule is mechanical**; **the founder's weights are received law**; ⭐ **standing has no
   floor** **[DECIDED — lenses 7, 4]** — unlike the compute allowance, standing is earned, not a need, so no extent is
   obliged to pass any down; eligibility by statute, disputes to the court.

## Part 12 — ⭐⭐⭐ Labor standing — draw, the toolkit, and keeping the stocks independent (2026-11-19) **[DECIDED in shape — the owner; lens passes]**

### What is built: labor standing is draw

The labor faucet ships ([influence.md](../../subsystems/influence.md) § *The labor stock*): **how much other players
engage with content you authored** — a player acting in a place credits its author, **once per player per time
bucket** (anti-inflation), **never for engaging your own content** (the A≠P exclusion — half of Part 11's conflict rule,
already code), decaying in real time (current draw), with **no regard term** (an author earns from use, not from being
liked). It is exactly Part 11's *use* metric, routed straight to authors.

- ⭐ **Authorship share is the cascade's default model** **[DECIDED — lens 2]** — draw is measured per extent (the
  cascade's use metric) and, with no model chosen, apportioned by authorship share: precisely what the faucet does
  today. Nothing built is thrown away.

### Draw is the honest measure of experience **[DECIDED — the owner]**

The owner, on a draft that called sewers more important than a beloved tavern: *"If the beloved tavern draws more
standing than the sewer system, do the sewers matter more? … content isn't free; if it were, then yes, I would grant your
premise, but immersion has a cost. That said, I would never ever offline something just because no one was experiencing
it. That's just a problem that has to be solved by more labor."*

- **The sewer as a place** — rooms players walk through: draw measures it honestly.
- **The sewer as a mechanism** — the water system's code (a sewer is a conduit run in reverse): its value shows in the
  draw of everything that depends on it.
- ⭐ **Dependency credit** **[DECIDED — lens 1]** — a share of a place's draw flows to the systems it depends on: the packs
  it builds on, the mechanisms its rows use. Derived demand (Alfred Marshall: demand for an input comes from demand for
  what it produces) — a royalty chain, as a song pays its samples. Infrastructure earns exactly as much as things people
  experience use it.
- ⭐ **Only dependencies actually exercised** **[DECIDED — lens 7]** — a pack could declare a dependency it never uses to
  route credit to a friend, so credit follows the systems a place's content actually runs on, as the tech tree's `@law`
  annotations record ([tech-tree-slate](./tech-tree-slate.md) § 6b). Measured, never declared.
- ⭐ **Never offline for low draw** **[DECIDED — the owner; lens 5]** — low draw is a labor problem, solved by more labor:
  low draw is visible to holders and to anyone seeking work; **reviving neglected content can be a bounty**; unmaintained
  content follows the dormancy rules — title may revert after notice, the place keeps running under the continuity
  license ([institutions-slate](./institutions-slate.md)).
- ⭐ **Raw draw, never weighed against cost** **[DECIDED — lens 6]** — content's cost is governed by the compute allowance
  and its cascade; weighing labor standing by cost too would penalize rich, immersive work twice and mix two meters kept
  apart.

### Labor without draw has its own faucets

| labor | measured by |
|---|---|
| authoring places and content | draw (built) |
| platform code | codification bounties — per clause, durable, support × waiting ([separation-of-powers-slate](./separation-of-powers-slate.md) § 7h) |
| maintenance and bug fixes | per fix, durable through the challenge window |
| judging, juries, clerks, counsel | per case, from the court's appropriation (§ 7j) |
| moderation | per action upheld on review, or the seat model |
| writing courses | draw on the course, or a bounty per course |
| committee and office work | allowances — fixed, targeted, logged, lapsing |

All feed **one labor pool per period**, its split among faucets set by law (Part 11). The polity answering *is
maintenance underpaid?* is one amendment.

### ⭐⭐⭐ One toolkit, everywhere **[DECIDED — the owner; lens 2]**

The owner: *"Some of it will be awarded by formula and some by human judgment, but even in the case of the latter humans
should still all be working from the same toolkit … whether you're talking about sewer systems or specific taverns,
players should have similar expectations about how their activity is converting into labor standing … there's no labor
and there's no consumers and there's no capital. Everyone is doing all three all the time."*

| tool | who decides | how |
|---|---|---|
| **draw** | nobody — automatic | players engaging with your work, crediting its authors and, by dependency, the systems under it |
| **bounty** | a formula | per clause, fix, case or other unit the worker cannot inflate |
| **allowance** | a person, from a fixed budget | targeted, logged with a reason, lapsing unspent |

The cascade's apportionment menu (Part 11) is these tools applied by an extent; the same conflict rules apply to all.
⭐ **Every award comes with a receipt** **[DECIDED — lens 1]** — the standing view shows each award's tool, rule, source
and reason, so a player sees exactly how activity became standing, and can expect the same everywhere.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Patronage is directed play — nothing minted to hand out **[DECIDED — the owner]**

The owner rejected a per-player patronage budget: *"I'm not really down with minting some amount of standing and some
fungible thing you can award … in that model I do think there be dragons. It's more like, we know what kind of consumer
activity contributes to labor standing. If it's well known and easily 'gamable', a consumer can deliberately take the
actions that would award standing to the wizard of their preference … it's just gameplay executed at the meta but still
honest within the fiction … that you did it for the votes is hopefully inconsequential for everyone other than the person
getting the standing."*

- **The routing is public knowledge**: playing in a place credits its authors (and, by dependency, its systems). **Love a
  wizard's work? Go play in it.** Real play, honest in the fiction; the motive is yours.
- **Nothing is minted and nothing can be handed over** — anything awardable becomes a currency: traded, sold, hoarded, a
  vote market.
- ⭐ **The bucket makes it egalitarian by construction** — each player credits an author at most once per bucket, so a
  devoted fan cannot pour in more than one person's worth; only real play counts (recognized commands from a real player);
  no crediting yourself or what you hold the pen over; alts do not multiply (one account).
- **Legibility is what it needs** — ⭐ **credits on every place and piece of content** (players have favorite wizards and
  illustrators and follow their work), authors see where their draw comes from in aggregate, and the rules are published,
  so *playing for the votes* is known and agreed, not an exploit.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Paid presence — keeping the stocks independent **[DECIDED — lenses 6, 7]**

The owner first allowed paying visitors (*"if any player wants to spend their zorkmids to get people to play in their
zones, they're welcome to try"*), then asked for harder pushback: *"surely there's pitfalls … we're trying to keep these
measures independent; the integrity of the legislature depends on it."* The pitfalls:

1. **It evades the conflict rule** — paying visitors to your own place is self-dealing through a middleman: one act earns
   the author consumer standing (spending) and labor standing (bought draw) on the same content.
2. ⭐ **It correlates the chambers, and 2 of 3 depends on their independence** — if in-game wealth moves both the consumer
   and the labor tally, **wealth controls two of three by arithmetic**. Zorkmids never convert to real money, so the
   manifesto's letter holds, but its purpose fails: the wealthiest are the corpo barons — a check failing against exactly
   whom it exists to check.
3. **"It doesn't scale" holds for everyone but the barons** — the bucket caps each visitor, never the number; money buys
   visitors.
4. **It breaks the measure** — draw is honest only while it cannot be bought; Goodhart at the stock's foundation.
5. **Paid visitors are workers getting consumer credit** — blurring the line between taking part and staging.

The fix forbids nothing:

- ⭐ **Consideration voids credit** — draw from anyone the author paid within the period does not count for that author;
  *the author* includes their committee and their corpo, and payment includes routing through a third party. Computed
  from the bank ledger and the participation log; evasion through intermediaries is for auditors and the court. Paying
  people stays legal — it buys no standing. The real analog: disclosure rules for paid endorsements — a paid review is not a
  review.
- ⭐ **Staff do not count** — an author's employees working in their venue are staff, not visitors.
- **Commerce in the fiction is honest draw** — free beer at the tavern draws a crowd who come for the beer. **The line:
  payment for presence (void) against goods and experiences that attract people (honest).**
- ⭐ **Tally independence is published** — for every bill, how closely the three chambers' tallies moved together, so the
  legislature's integrity is a number anyone reads, and convergence (the same people winning all three) is visible before
  the check fails.

**The conflict rule now has three parts**: pen-holders' own activity counts for nothing; paid presence counts for nothing;
staff presence counts for nothing.

### The straddling work, and credits **[DECIDED — lens 3a]**

- **In-world verbs are consumer; authored content, code and law are labor** — a curator arranging a museum in the world is
  playing; one writing its exhibit documents is working.
- **Maker credits are out-of-fiction UI** — authors are people, not characters: credits sit with the plaque showing the pen
  ([separation-of-powers-slate](./separation-of-powers-slate.md) § 7p), apart from in-fiction maker's marks on goods.
