Working slate for **Eternal University** — the campus new players walk
into after char-gen + the lounge. It's where the deferred char-gen
choices get made (you visit campus **services** to set them), where the
first-login onboarding journey happens, and where the freshman dorm
(Duncan Hall) lives. It is the **first authored place the player ever
inhabits**, so its *feel* is the single most load-bearing content brief in
the game.

The load-bearing decisions:

1. **A real-feeling college campus that's obviously not a real place.**
   The *campus-ness* is real (recognizable forms, feel, and functions —
   you instantly know it's a campus); the *place-ness* is not (nowhere on
   Earth, strange-textured, plainly built). "Except for the real part."

2. **Un-genred, not multi-genre.** The thing loved about Eternal City
   wasn't a sci-fi zone beside a horror zone — *nothing* in it was cleanly
   any genre. It's a zany assortment of unplaceable patterns; the one
   thing held perfectly consistent is the **refusal to commit**. The canon
   is that nothing is canon-bound to a genre.

3. **Strangeness is a finish, not a structure.** The weirdness lives in
   **texture, materials, setting, and roads** — *never* in the buildings
   or their functions. The registrar is a registrar; the dorm is a dorm.
   The roads can glow pink; the registrar's office stays a registrar's
   office.

4. **Decentralized and walkable — no kill-zone Heart.** Eternal City's
   central Heart funneled everyone and became a PvP kill-zone. Our
   **lounge already owns "everyone passes through"** (it's its own
   universe), so the campus is freed from needing a deadly center. It's a
   walkable place with a **sparse 2–3 terminals** at high-traffic anchors;
   everything else is on foot.

5. **Build it all together.** The defer-your-choices model makes the
   **services load-bearing**: if char-gen sends you to the registrar to
   set things later, the registrar must exist at launch. So entry + Quad +
   services + Duncan Hall + the journey are **one drop** — which moves
   services out of char-gen's "Wave 2" into the first campus build.

6. **Why this is the most load-bearing why in the game.** Un-genre-ness is
   the *aesthetic form* of two pillars at once (below): the **obvious
   fabrication** (the educational mission made diegetic) and **vertical-
   agnosticism** (the universal stage). The campus's refusal to pick a
   genre is what the whole platform thesis rests on — and it's the first
   thing a player feels.

## Principle

1. **A real campus that's obviously unreal** — campus-ness real,
   place-ness fabricated.
2. **Un-genred** — defined by its refusal to commit to a genre.
3. **Strangeness is a finish, not a structure** — weird texture, legible
   forms.
4. **Walkable + decentralized** — no funnel, sparse terminals, the place
   is *traversed*.
5. **Services are load-bearing** — built with the campus, not after.
6. **First content carries the thesis** — the fabrication is the mission
   made diegetic; the un-genre-ness is the universal stage.

## The north-star — why Eternal City (previously undocumented)

The design brief for the *feel* of the campus, drawn from why the old
Eternal City is loved:

**Genre-spanning, but *un-genred*.** EotL let authors bring laser guns and
anti-grav boots to Middle Earth, and wove it together through **solid
design + social reinforcement**, not genre walls. But the genres
themselves were never well-defined — the well-defined thing was that *they
aren't*. The silver street isn't "futuristic"; the pink spongy lane isn't
"medieval"; they're just **strange** — evocative patterns that suggest
possibility without committing to a reality.

**The obvious fabrication is a feature.** For all the real units and real
education the platform does, there is **no real place like Eternal City** —
it's a complete and obvious fabrication. No city would emerge like this; it
must have been *designed*, maybe by a God. That artificiality is honest
(this *is* a built thing, made to teach you anything) and is exactly what
grants the **"you're in for anything"** license.

### The two pillars un-genre-ness serves

This is why it's load-bearing, not just vibe:

1. **Un-genre-ness is the aesthetic form of the obvious fabrication.** No
   real place is built from unplaceable patterns — so the strangeness *is*
   the maker's fingerprint. Liberal diegesis: the meta-truth "someone built
   this platform to teach you" wears the fiction-coat "the world was made
   on purpose, maybe by a God."

2. **Un-genre-ness is the aesthetic form of vertical-agnosticism.** Because
   the world commits to no genre, **every vertical's narrative drapes over
   it without clashing** — a nursing scenario, a Middle-Earth quest, a
   cyberpunk heist all feel at home. The campus's refusal to pick a genre
   is what makes it the **universal stage** — the engine thesis (swap the
   narrative, not the engine) made visible.

### The synthesis: legible institution, unplaceable finish

A college campus is a recognizable real thing (grounding, purpose,
navigability); Eternal City's genius is the un-pinned surreality. The
campus resolves the tension by **grounding the forms and functions** (you
enroll, get housing, take classes — recognizable, low-friction, welcoming)
while **the finish is unplaceable** (strange materials, a sky that's wrong,
roads that glow). **Familiar function, strange form.** Grounding the
buildings isn't a compromise of the vision — it's *required* by onboarding:
new students must feel oriented immediately, and a campus of surreal
shrines would disorient the exact people we're welcoming.

### The authoring craft of un-genre-ness

Un-genre-ness is *hard* — it collapses into either bland abstraction or
accidental genre-commitment unless authored deliberately. The legacy's
techniques, applied **within the "finish not structure" rule**:

- **Strange materials** that suggest without naming (silver metal-plate
  paving, spongy pink ground, gem-like gravel).
- **Morphing / era-shifting** atmosphere (a setting that won't sit still in
  one time).
- **Impossible juxtaposition** (adjacencies no real place would have).
- **Whimsy as a genre-solvent** — the secret weapon. A high-fiving God, a
  statue named Bob: humor keeps anything from ever locking into serious
  genre.

All of it as **set dressing and texture** over recognizable campus forms,
never as a remodel of the registrar into a temple.

## Lore grounding (the deep history)

The campus's *feel* is the north-star above; its *backstory* now lives in
[story-bible.md](../../story-bible.md), and it grounds a few specifics. The
University was raised by the Mission clergy **on the ruins of the dead
Eternal City**, atop the **Sanctuary** (the sacred dead heart) — so the
campus literally sits on the old eternal core, which is *why* its finish is
impossibly strange (it seeps up from beneath). Two roster buildings carry
that history: the **Health Center** dispenses **attunement** because the
University is where attunement was *mastered* (it installs the TPA
implant-update on the onboarding journey — see
[onboarding-slate.md](../builds/onboarding-slate.md)); and the **Chapel** is
the **Mission clergy's residue** — keepers of the old worship faded to
echoes (religion model: the bible + the
[alignment slate](./alignment-slate.md)).

## The surround — the city past the gates (deferred)

EU sits inside a city. The temptation is to make that city a revival of
the old Eternal City — but that **dilutes the aesthetic by splitting the
EotL influence across two places**, and neither ends up the strong one.
The decision is the opposite:

**EU holds the *entire* EotL homage.** All of it — the un-genred strange
finish, the named roads (Tanelorn), the impossible storefronts, the
deadpan whimsy — lives on campus. The campus is where we "blow our load,"
deliberately. The surround does **not** get EC canon, because the surround
is not EC.

**The surround is a deliberately distinct city, and the contrast is
*grounding*.** Step through the gate and the world gets more **real** —
lived-in, weighty, an ordinary sky overhead. This is the chosen
relationship (over "strange in a different register"), because:

- it makes the campus's strangeness *pop* — you only register the
  sourceless sky because the city outside has a normal one;
- it's honest: a campus *is* a bubble; the world outside it is the world;
- it gives the eventual city a *direction* without owing a feel now (it
  grounds, it doesn't out-weird the campus);
- it gives onboarding a graduation arc — start in the safe weird enclave,
  the bigger realer world waits past the gates.

*Superseded: the surround is Terminus, shipped with its streets; the
locked campus gate on University Avenue is the boundary, and the gatehouse
behind it is the stub the campus entry replaces →
[holding.md § The packaging](../../subsystems/holding.md#the-packaging).*

**The geography is a gradient** — worth reserving *structurally* now even
with the city's feel deferred: the **campus enclave** (its sourceless sky)
sits inside the **urban** city, and past the city is **wilderness**. In
biome terms: an `urban` baseline → { the city (deferred) ; the
`eternal-campus-grounds` leaf that overrides the sky } , with `wilderness`
→ forest / meadow / … as a sibling branch under `outdoor`. Locking the
gradient gives the biome model a real three-tier consumer and reserves the
right shape; the city's *contents* stay deferred.

*Resolved: the city is Terminus, and the campus fronts University Avenue
at `/world/terminus/university-avenue` — a Terminus street, the gate the
boundary, the campus a district at `terminus/city/campus` →
[holding.md § The packaging](../../subsystems/holding.md#the-packaging).*

## The model — the v1 campus

### Topology: walkable, decentralized, sparse terminals

Walking **is the point**, twice over: it's the sense of a navigable,
authored place you hold in your head (the EC love), and the first-login
journey *teaches movement by being walked* (onboarding). TP everywhere and
you erase both. So the campus is a walkable place; terminals are sparse
conveniences at high-traffic anchors, **2–3 tops**, pre-selected by what
fast-travel already needs:

- **Arrival** *(terminal #1)* — where the lounge TPA drops you (first-timers
  and returners). Mandatory; it's the lounge-exit's destination.
- **Duncan Hall lobby** *(terminal #2)* — your home-recall anchor; you TP to
  the lobby, then *walk* to your room.
- **The Quad** *(optional terminal #3)* — only if real traffic warrants;
  otherwise skip it.

No central funnel (the lounge owns "everyone passes through"); no kill-zone.

### The v1 roster — recognizable campus, strange finish

- **Arrival** — a campus gate / transit stop on the edge of the green. Its
  whole job is the first impression: unmistakably a campus, but the ground
  and sky announce "nowhere real." This is where "you're in for anything"
  lands first.
- **The Quad** — the walkable heart of campus life and the navigable spine
  (a *center to orient by*, not a funnel). Greens, walkways, benches, a
  landmark (the whimsical statue lives here). Strange paving; grounded
  layout.
- **Student Services / Admin** — the **registrar** (identity / rename) +
  the **housing office** (dorm assignment). The "go change your deferred
  choices" desk, as a normal admin building.
- **Health Center** — **implants / augments**: the demo-augment install on
  the journey, and ongoing implant changes. A campus clinic, not a
  body-shop.
- **Campus Store / Outfitter** — **appearance presets**, gear. A
  bookstore-ish shop.
- **An academic hall** — a recognizable lecture building, present as campus
  *texture* and a **future hook** where education-vertical content plugs
  in. v1 ships **no lessons inside it** (that's the vertical's job).
- **Duncan Hall** — the **freshman dorm**, itself a roster-persistent
  Warren (full treatment below): lobby terminal, rooms a walk away. The
  housing payoff + the **themed-customization** on-ramp.
- **Walkways / roads** — where the weirdness lives (EC-spirit strange
  materials, a named main path) and the route the onboarding journey walks
  (signs + greeter + the clinic stop).

(No big **student union** — the lounge already owns the social-hub role
from its own universe, so the Quad covers casual campus social and we stay
lean.)

### Campus services — the pattern

The deferred-choices model leans entirely on this: services are **places
you visit** that present **closed-choice dialogue + run existing mutation
verbs** — the registrar sets identity fields, the clinic issues augments,
housing assigns the dorm, the outfitter applies appearance presets. Per
char-gen, this is **content** (service NPCs / kiosks), **not a new
engine**. The same services handle **intake and ongoing change** — you
enroll through them and return to them to alter things. The legacy's
service-as-place locations (Temple of the Ages, the Oracle machine, Dr.
Frankenstein's Body Shop) are prior art for the **pattern**, rendered here
as **campus buildings**, not city-fantastical ones.

### Duncan Hall — the dorm (a roster-persistent Warren)

*Superseded by the code: `DormWarren` is dorm-when-empty — an empty room
captures and reaps, and the building reconstitutes from the durable slot
set →
[residence.md § Reap](../../subsystems/residence.md#reap-residency-dormancy).*

**What persists vs. regenerates.**

- **Durable singletons:** the **lobby** (the Warren host — holds the TPA
  terminal), the **amenities** (front desk, laundry, common room…), and the
  **dorm rooms** themselves (co-occupied, themed, persistent).

**Vertical circulation.** Multi-floor, so the topology is a vertical stack
(not the lounge's flat star): the lobby at ground, **stairs** linking
adjacent floors, and a **real elevator** — a **Vessel** (the conveyance
subsystem; a car that actually travels between floor-stops and gains a stop
each time a floor buds). First real Vessel consumer.

**Growth & vacancy.** Lazy growth — materialize a room when assigned, bud a
floor when the current one fills. Vacancies **refill preferentially**, and a
room left vacant too long is **purged** (the lounge's reap mechanic on a
much longer, *vacancy-keyed* grace — days, not a beat). Mostly-monotonic
with slow GC of dead rooms.

**Rooms, roommates, assignment.**

- Rooms are **shared, max two occupants**, both with **room-level write**.
  Roommate pairing taps the **lounge flavor tags** — matched by
  *compatibility* (you get along), with the *themes* carrying the
  genre-blend (compatible roommates still pick different themes).
- *Assignment via Katie — shipped →
  [residence.md § Provisioning](../../subsystems/residence.md#provisioning--the-stored-slot);
  rent stays a deferred money leg →
  [holding.md § Deferred seams](../../subsystems/holding.md#deferred-seams).*
- **Start-loc vs. terminal.** The TPA terminal is **public** (lobby only —
  no terminals in rooms); your **start-loc is personal** and *can* be your
  private room (a persistent singleton, so a valid start-loc resolving to
  itself — no walk forced). Different systems: public travel vs. personal
  spawn.

*Superseded by the code: the theme is picked per ROOM (one leaseholder),
prose-only, by vocation — "curated, not freeform" held →
[residence.md § The shell personalization](../../subsystems/residence.md#the-shell-personalization-theme-overlay);
the per-side / roommate design lives in
[dorm-warren-slate](../tails/dorm-warren-slate.md).*

### Dorm-room customization — the Detail schema

*Superseded by the code: fixtures shipped as real Stuff (`Bed`, `Desk`,
`Footlocker` — `residence.md § History`), themes as prose bundles applied
by fixture role, `remodel` as a prompt wheel; no Detail tree, no kiosk →
[residence.md § The shell personalization](../../subsystems/residence.md#the-shell-personalization-theme-overlay).*

### Prose & the mood-board

The legacy descriptors are the **source to *update*, not replace** — we
**preserve the EC aesthetic and improve the language** (a faithful
modernization, *not* a from-scratch rewrite — don't lose the feel). The
roads come **from EC, names kept**: the real **Eternal Way / Tanelorn Road /
Silver Street / Limbo Lane / Glass Way / Infinity Way / Dimension Drive /
Old Road / Dark Alley** (per the legacy source + `docs/eternal/MAP.TXT`),
each with its **original prose polished**, not invented. Keep the *spirit*:
material specificity, day/night atmospheric layering, readable sign/object
detail, and the **Guide wristcomp** — a carried object that gated
newbie-school progression — as strong prior art for an onboarding-gating
device. (The EC **map is not replicated** — its central kill-zone Heart is
rejected; the campus is its own layout, borrowing the road names + their
polished prose.)

## Worked scenario — first login onto campus

The lounge TPA deposits a first-time student at **Arrival**. The first room
reads instantly as a campus — and just as instantly as *nowhere real* (the
ground, the sky). A **greeter NPC** welcomes them; **signs** point inward
(following them teaches movement). The walk down the main path is the
campus revealing itself — recognizable buildings with an unplaceable
finish. A stop at the **Health Center** installs the **demo augment** (the
implant lesson). The path ends at **Duncan Hall**; they walk from the lobby
to their room and hit the **scoped-authoring** customization (authoring
their own space — the climax). Later, wanting to change their name, they
walk to **Student Services** and visit the registrar. Nothing teleported
them anywhere except the two anchor hops; the campus was *walked*, and that
walk was the onboarding.
