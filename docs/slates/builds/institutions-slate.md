## The principle everything below is an instance of

> **The platform never decides *who*. It only sets *how long*.**

Charters have terms. Seats have terms. Positions vacate on absence.
Title is held by activity. Standing is a rate. Nothing in the kernel
reaches in and moves an outcome; everything in it puts a clock on tenure.
That is mechanical (Art. V §9-clean), it is the actual lesson of term
limits and antitrust, and it is what makes *"wisdom of the masses"* a
mechanism rather than a hope: circulation of tenure is what lets
patronage matter.

The corollary for corpos, which had authored rivalries and were about to
compete for real: ⭐ **author the ethos, never the outcome.** A rivalry is
a disposition — what Veshko's clerks refuse to stock, how Vionne's talk
about Hollis. What becomes of Veshko is the ledger's business.

# Part 1 — The entity-form ladder

Every form answers four questions: **who owns** (the residual), **who
controls** (appoints, removes, sells), **who is liable**, and **can
ownership move** — to whom, with whose consent.

| form | owns | controls | liable | ownership moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **sole trader** | one | the owner | limited — the business's estate | the whole business, by transfer |
| **partnership** | several, in shares of the residual | the partners by their agreement (default: a majority of shares) | limited — **never joint and several** | an interest moves **only with the other partners' consent** |
| **company** | many, in shares | a majority of shares appoints the managing position; management runs it | limited | **freely, through the registry** |
| **mutual** | its members — one member one vote | the members elect management | limited | you leave by withdrawing your share |
| **chartered corporation** | as the charter says | per charter | limited | per charter |

What is genuinely new at each rung is one thing: several owners → transfer
without consent → a charter instead of a filing. The mutual is the
sideways rung: ownership *is* membership, and it is the form Goodkin
turns out to have been all along (Part 6).

## Two rungs already decided by doctrine

- **Liability is limited everywhere.** The economic bootstrap decided the
  estate is the limit and nothing chases the person. So a single-owner
  business is a single-member limited company — the 1855 idea applied
  universally, and the right default for a polity that does not know its
  own risk appetite.
- **There is no general partnership.** Partners liable for each other's
  debts is *enforceable joint liability*, which the credit slate already
  ruled 13th-module bondage. The partnership rung is limited by
  construction. ⚠ What is lost is the lesson that made limited liability
  revolutionary — the partner ruined by a partner. A lesson told, not
  lived; a deliberate cut.

## The primitive: a cap table

All four questions derive from one thing a business does not have today —
a **table of owners and their shares**. A sole trader is one row at 100%.
A partnership is several. A company is many, and the registry holds it. A
mutual's table is its membership roll, one vote each.

- **The draw becomes a distribution**, pro rata over the table. A dividend
  is the draw of a company. One act, already a leg kind, fanning out.
- **Control derives from the table** — a majority may appoint the managing
  position. No board, no resolution engine, no ballot: a share carries a
  vote, an appointment stands when holders of a majority have endorsed
  it, read from the table when somebody tries. No majority → enough
  endorsements to make one, which is what a coalition of partners *is*.
- **Transferability is a term on the table**: consent-of-others, or
  none-needed.
- ⭐ It gives the corpos a real representation for the first time: the
  corpo is the 100% row on each of its businesses' tables. "Goodkin owns
  the bank" becomes a fact in the world rather than a config key.

## Formation is an act at an institution

- **A sole trader just starts** — the business already stands up lazily.
- **A partnership is signed** — a deed is the oldest contract there is,
  and the contract substrate already does multi-party agreement with a
  stake. Bringing a partner in is issuing an interest — **for capital, or
  for work**. ⭐ Sweat equity (decided in) is how an apprentice becomes a
  partner; Dave bringing Mara in is the succession the cast already
  implies. ⚠ Requirements must state it next to the 13th module: an
  interest issued for labor is a *grant against work done*, never an
  obligation to work — non-recourse, exactly as the Arrival Note.
- **A company is filed** at a **registry** — a chartered institution,
  NPC-run, one for the Compact to start (localities competing on
  incorporation law is a real lever, not at dozens of players). ⭐⭐
  **Whether the registry accepts filings from anyone is the
  general-incorporation act.** Until the polity passes it, the only
  companies are the chartered ones.
- **A charter is granted** by the polity, one at a time (Part 2).

## One trading rule, every form

> **A business trades while an active person holds its managing
> position.**

For a sole trader that is the owner, so the closed sign falls out of
dormancy as decided. For a partnership, whichever partner holds it. For a
company, whoever the shareholders appointed — a position with a wage, so
what owners collect is what is left *after paying the person who runs
it*. Absentee ownership is allowed and costs a manager. This is what
dissolves the passive-income worry structurally rather than by threshold.

## Equity, minimally

- **Primary issue**: new shares sold, subscribers pay in, money lands in
  the business's account as capital — a neutral movement between real
  accounts, no new money — and the table dilutes.
- **Secondary**: shares move owner to owner through the registry; money
  moves owner to owner; the business is untouched.
- ⭐ **Dividends ≤ retained earnings.** From actual surplus, never from
  new subscriptions. That one conservation-shaped check is the anti-Ponzi
  rule, readable by anyone.
- **The ledger is the prospectus.** A shareholder reads the P&L, because
  the books are real — the thing securities law spends most of its effort
  forcing, free.
- **Spot only.** No margin, no shorting, no derivatives, no clocks.
- **The wall**: a chartered bank may not hold a row on the table of a
  business it lends to. Module, default on, repealable (Part 2).
- **Escheated interests** go to the state by situs, reclaimable forever,
  and **do not vote** — otherwise the treasury accumulates control of half
  the town's companies by attrition.
- In-game equity is a zorkmid claim on a zorkmid business and confers a
  vote *in the company*. It never confers standing in the polity. Capital
  grows the pie; it still never buys a slice.

# Part 2 — Charters

> **A charter grants a privilege and imposes a duty, for a term.**

Before ~1844 nobody could simply *form* a corporation; you needed a
charter from the sovereign — the East India Company, the Bank of England,
Hudson's Bay — usually with a monopoly attached. The corpos are exactly
that: **state-chartered, NPC-run incumbents who hold the bank charters**,
whose privilege is legal, visible and old.

- **Grants**: the right to be a company before general incorporation; a
  bank charter (the *specific* kind that permits taking deposits and
  presenting paper at the reserve's window); brands.
- **Obliges**: the **NPC-floor duties** — run the bank under the ladder's
  rules, keep the general store stocked, keep the cash-and-carry buying.
  The charter says what the private sector must do when nobody is
  online, and the corpo is who does it. ⭐ Duties are written as things
  the ledger can check, so breach is **mechanical** (a corpo that stops
  stocking the store is in breach), never a judgment.
- **Term**: charters expire and come up for **renewal**. The East India
  Company's was renewed in 1793, 1813 and 1833, and each renewal stripped
  it of something — that is how the monopoly ended. At 12×, a
  five-game-year term comes up every five real months: a forcing function
  on the polity to look at something.

## Renewal is how power circulates

This is the thumb on the scale, and it satisfies every constraint at
once: **not picking winners** (the terms are published and apply to all;
meet them and keep it; fail and lose it; the polity grants it to the
next); **power circulates** by renewal, never by authored plot; and
**"life support" is measured, not narrated** — the credit slate's
seed-vs-life-support rule (how many subsidy legs has this account taken)
reads the same for a corpo as for a bakery. A corpo on its fourth bailout
is on life support by its own ledger, and its charter is what is at
stake.

## Bank charters, and "never player banks"

A bank charter is granted only to a chartered corporation, under rule.
That is the shipped default, and the reason is the record — Ginko
Financial, EBANK, every EVE stock exchange. **The door is the polity's,
not a mechanic's**: "never" is a default the Compact holds, which is where
a door like that belongs.

⭐ **A player at Goodkin is not a player-run bank.** A player-run bank is a
player holding a *charter*. A player at Goodkin holds a *position under
Goodkin's charter*: the charter's rule binds them (the loan officer
approves by the ladder's gate, a ledger read — there is no discretion in
the seat to abuse), the corpo answers for them, and the polity can pull
the charter from the corpo. Three checks that do not exist over a sole
proprietor with a vault. What the player can do is be good at it, rise
to the managing position, be fired, and **embezzle** — which is a crime
and the courts' first real docket.

**Player moneylenders** are the ladder's rung 3 and they are legal:
lending your own money on your own terms, taking no deposits. They
compete where banks are weakest — the unsecured, no-history borrower the
gate refuses — on local knowledge. The ugly half of loan-sharking is
enforcement, and non-recourse plus the 13th module remove it by
construction: a lender may repossess what was pledged and nothing else.
The design produces moneylenders and makes them toothless, with no rule
about them.

## The wall

Glass-Steagall in one line: **a chartered bank may not hold equity in a
business it lends to.** (The other half — no securities bought with
deposits — is automatic under narrow banking.) Without it Veshko buys the
shops it finances and the oligarchy stops being a fiction. An
amendment-library module, default on, repealable; a polity that repeals
it finds out what happens, which is the lesson, and the corpos are the
natural party to want it repealed, which is the politics.

# Part 3 — The committee and the show

> **Title over an extent is the only real power. Every seat, office,
> charter and position is theater performed on that extent — and theater
> is the product.**

A committee is whoever holds parcel title over a subdivision (access.md).
Its job is to make the extent *play*. It is subject to the show's own
rules while it runs it — the reserve, the ladder, the estate — and it is
not a god, for one structural reason stated below.

## ⚠⚠ The finding: for corpos, the committee and the show are the same people

An organization-held title *"admits everyone holding a non-exited
position in it."* `/corpo/<key>` is held by the corpo's organization. So
today, **being hired as a teller at Goodkin makes you a member of the
committee that authors Goodkin.** The code-trust lockdown stops a teller
touching code; it does not stop them editing the bank's prose and rows.
For the executive holding the platform's roots that is fine; for a corpo
whose diegetic positions are about to fill from the labor market it is
not.

✅ **Decided: separate them.** `/corpo/<key>` is held by a
`<key>-committee` group, like a locality's. The corpo's organization is
the show. A committee member may also play the CEO — the showrunner
holding a role — but a hired clerk is not thereby an author of the bank.
⭐ This also resolves a circularity the Goodkin row documents: its
appointing authority *"cannot be the committee over its own extent,
because that committee IS the organization,"* so the PM's office fills
its chief-executive seat. Once the committee is a group, the org's
authority can be that committee — and the showrunner appoints the CEO,
which is what a showrunner does.

## Committees are gods in exactly one place, and it is a row field

A committee with title can write a Business row with an opening capital
of a million and the row mints it. The bootstrap requirements close that
one case (opening capital is the treasury's advance on the treasury's
terms) — but the principle wants naming as a rule the lint family holds:

> ✅ **No authored field may create money.** Every faucet is a rule of the
> reserve or the treasury, never a number in content.

That is the line between *puts on the show* and *prints tickets*, and it
is the one that keeps "they're not gods" true.

## Staffing: federalism by subdivision

The PM's job is not to run localities; it is to **seat** them. Title is
recursive — a holder may subdivide and grant — so each locality's
committee seats the institutions under it, and each institution's
committee seats its rooms. Nobody runs more than one level down, and the
one who grants an extent is the one who can see whether it is run well,
because it is inside theirs.

- ✅ **Vacancy is derived.** A committee is a group; its members are
  players; players have the three states. A committee all of whose
  members are dormant is dormant. No new concept.
- ✅ **Title escheats up.** When a committee escheats, its extent reverts
  to the *parent extent's* committee — situs, one level up. The
  Counting-Houses revert to Terminus; Terminus to the realm; the realm's
  committee is the PM, the founder default generalized to every extent.
  ⚠ The nightly reprovision must not re-grant a reverted extent to its
  dormant holder.
- ✅ **Vacant extents post to the board.** The work board already posts
  positions; a vacant committee seat is a posting on it, with the
  extent's record attached.

## Intake: measured by the platform, decided by the parent

**The floor is mechanical** (Schedule rows, applies to everyone): active
— seen within the window; standing — producer standing above a minimum,
the Compact's own measure of *has done work here*; clean — no unresolved
breach, no revoked charter, nothing on the docket.

**Above the floor, the parent committee chooses.** Judgment is human;
that is Art. V §9 and it is the point. The platform's job is to make the
record legible — what extents an applicant has held, how those fared
while they held them (visits, transactions, players employed, residents'
regard), what they authored — and the Gazette prints it. *The engine
measures and may publish; it must never be the only rater.*

**The ladder of extents is the ladder of trust.** Nobody is granted
Terminus. A position in the show first, then a building's committee,
then a locality — each granted by someone who watched the last one. That
is apprenticeship, and it is how the make-it-play skill transfers.

On the measure the polity hopes for — standing for those who *advance
all gameplay* — the mechanical proxies exist; the **weighting** is the
political layer the standing-mint work already ruled is the polity's to
fight over. Publish the proxies; let the polity weight them.

# Part 4 — The publish covenant

The opposite problem: the founder authored everything, so the founder is
every committee. The fix is the one already designed for a *studio* that
builds a city in a sandbox and asks the legislature to publish it — and
it applies to the founder, who is a studio too.

The doctrine is settled in land-compute-and-license. A studio's city is a
**private estate**: its committee holds title and staffs it by whatever
policy it likes — a dictatorship of one, if it wants — because
**subsidiarity already gives every committee absolute policy inside its
extent**. It needs no special rights over committee assignments. The
polity's leverage is only at the seam: entitlement, the publish gate, due
process.

What ships: a pack's manifest declares its title claims and who holds
them; install grants them; the nightly reprovision re-grants; a
conflicting claim is recorded, never overridden. ⚠ **What is missing is
the legislature's admit act** — the attestation slate's go-live
predicate. Today a pack enters the live world by an operator adding a
dependency line.

## The bundle, separated

| right | who holds it | when the author leaves | when they return | tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **authorship** (attribution) | the author, forever | untouched | untouched; producer standing earned from it is kept | **kernel** — the producer chamber runs on attribution, not property |
| **license** (reuse outside this instance) | the author's choice at publish — CC by default, or their own; code source-visible always | untouched | untouched | module (the license regime) |
| ⭐ **continuity** (the instance may keep running it, and maintain it for continuity) | the instance, irrevocably, granted *at publish* | this is what lets the city stay open | — | module — the covenant's core term |
| **title** (who edits the live copy) | the author's committee | reverts up on dormancy, after notice | reclaimable during the window; after re-grant, not | floor = due process (chosen eternity); terms = module |
| **appointment** (who seats the committee) | the title-holder, by their own policy | passes with title | passes with title | subsidiarity — not the polity's to touch |
| **the work itself** | in the state's custody on escheat, credited | never destroyed | always theirs | situs |

**Is this amendment territory?** The floor is — attribution is kernel;
notice-before-reversion is a chosen eternity clause. **The terms are a
module**, Tier C: the dormancy window, whether continuity is irrevocable,
the reclaim window. **Bespoke deals are not.** The legislature's vote is
*admit or don't* under the standing covenant; a studio wanting more asks
for a general amendment, visibly, for everyone. Per-publisher terms are
the capture vector; *not picking winners* applies to publication as to
charters.

⭐ **The continuity license is what makes leaving survivable.** Without it
a studio that licensed restrictively and went dormant leaves a dead city
nobody may touch. With it the instance keeps it open while title sits
reverted, and the author, on return, finds it alive — the orphan-works
problem solved the way it should have been: the license to *keep running
what was published* is a condition of publication, not a favour.

# Part 5 — Localities

Three options were on the table — NPC institutions steered by player
committees, players in every seat, or mixed — and they resolve to mixed
by a rule the constitution already states:

> **Judgment seats are a person's. Mechanical seats may be an NPC's.
> Every seat is a position, and an NPC in it is the floor until a player
> displaces them.**

Art. V §9 sorts a locality's chart on its own: the mayor, the council, the
zoning committee decide — player seats, founder-default when empty. The
clerk who records deeds, the pound keeper who feeds escheated dogs, the
assessor who reads shell bands — NPCs executing rules, and a player may
take any of them for the wage. Civics already has the shape (a
Government's departments are organizations; seats point at positions);
this is the *holder policy*: **NPC floor, player apex**, the same phrase
the corpo memory uses for brands.

⭐ **A locality is now a property owner.** Escheat sends it the dead
frontage, the pound, the weathering house; it already has a treasury
taking TPA fares. The obvious next step is good: a locality that must
fix the frontage or feed the dogs issues **municipal paper on the same
registry**, bought by its residents — the citizens own the town's debt,
which is how towns funded themselves for three centuries and the most
direct *your money and your neighbours' money are the same money* lesson
available.

# Part 6 — The five corpos, rewritten as forms

Each corpo is a **form**, a **charter**, a **strategy** its NPC brains
execute, a **committee** that runs the show, and a **transition** that is
its arc. The authored ethos lines survive intact — they were dispositions
all along. `rivals:` becomes disposition-only and never an outcome.

| corpo | form | charter | strategy (the disposition, as a brain) | the arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Goodkin** — *the Paternalist* | ⭐ **credit union** (mutual): depositors are members, one member one vote, members elect the board, surplus returns as low fees and a member dividend | the retail bank charter — **exercised** | lends to members on what it knows of them; near-free; pays above the floor | **demutualization** — the members vote on cashing out the velvet cage |
| **Veshko** — *the Ruthless Optimizer* | **closely-held company** — shares exist, the founder bloc controls | ⭐ the retail bank charter — **exercised, as a real bank** | fees on everything; higher rates, bigger lines, faster; lends to anyone the ladder admits; repossesses without sentiment; integrates vertically — *buys its suppliers*, which is why the wall exists | the **bloc fragments** and control is contestable for the first time |
| **Vionne** — *the Prestige House* | **partnership** — interests move only by the partners' consent | the private-banking charter | holds price, restricts supply, lends only to names it knows | a partner **dies out or is bought out**; the house decides who may enter |
| **Hollis** — *the Populist* | **public company** — widely held, nobody controls it, management runs it | ⭐ a bank charter, **held and unexercised**: the frontage's "FREE!" is a store-credit desk (Sears/Discover — the credit half without the deposit half) | volume, thin margins, low wages, cheerful | the **proxy fight**, and ⭐ **the day it exercises the charter** — the people's company banking the unbanked at no fees, which is both populist and a megacorp swallowing a mutual's market |
| **Aevex** — *the Disruptor* | **sole trader** — one founder's company | the payments/fintech charter — "the terms are free; you pay in data" | undercuts fees, adopts first, **finances growth with deficits** | the **IPO** — and ⭐ Aevex is the corpo the seed-vs-life-support test bites first, because its strategy *is* life support called growth; charter renewal is where that gets judged |
| the Independents | sole traders, and someday a worker co-op | none | — | the player's home rung |

## ⭐⭐ Goodkin and Veshko: paragon and renegade

Making Goodkin a credit union without a real bank beside it shows nobody
the difference. So Veshko's house — already authored as *"a bunker with
nothing on its front but its name,"* across the avenue from Goodkin's
propped-open doors — is the **shareholder retail bank**, and the two are
the same product at opposite poles:

| | Goodkin (credit union) | Veshko (bank) |
|---|---|---|
| owned by | its depositors | its shareholders (the founder bloc) |
| votes | one member, one vote | one share, one vote |
| surplus | member dividend, low fees | shareholder profit |
| fees | near-free | on everything |
| loan rate | lower | higher |
| lends to | members it knows | anyone the ladder admits |
| speed and size | slower, smaller | faster, bigger |
| on default | works it out | repossesses |
| leaving | costs your share and your vote | costs nothing |

The Mass Effect property: ninety percent of players will bank at Goodkin,
and Goodkin only *feels* like a choice because Veshko is across the
street. Veshko is also the bank a player turns to when Goodkin's gate
says no — bigger line, worse terms, no mercy — which is exactly the
renegade bargain and needs no authoring beyond the disposition.

## Goodkin's common bond

Real credit unions restrict membership to a field. Goodkin's bond is
*"anyone who lives, works or shops in a Goodkin town"* — broad enough that
every new player qualifies (it is the first bank), narrow enough to feel
like belonging. The leash is structural: your money is your vote, your
vote is your membership, and **leaving means withdrawing your share**.
Nobody authors the leash; the form is the leash. Circle membership *is*
membership; the Circle parlour is where the AGM happens — a scene players
can be in. Members are **depositors, not staff**; the worker co-op is a
different animal and the Independents' someday, not a corpo's.

⭐ Goodkin is therefore where **local underwriting** lives by
construction: a credit union lends to members on what it knows about
them — the Raiffeisen model the credit slate called *where the game is*,
and the natural first lender at the ladder's rung 2.

## What the rewrite touches

A content pass, not a design one, once Parts 1–3 land: each corpo's mark
row gains its form and charter; each `/corpo/<key>` claim moves to a
`<key>-committee` group; each organization's appointing authority becomes
its form's control rule (Goodkin: a member vote); the four disposition
brains are authored; the Counting-Houses prose is already right and
needs only Hollis's frontage to read as a store-credit desk.

# Open questions

1. **Charter term length.** Five game-years is the East India cadence
   (five real months). Shorter forces engagement; longer lets a strategy
   play out. A Schedule row; the starting value is a requirements call.
2. **Use-it-or-lose-it.** Is an unexercised charter (Hollis) grounds for
   non-renewal by rule, or only a fact the polity may weigh? *Leans the
   latter* — a rule here would pre-decide Hollis's arc.
3. **May players buy shares in corpo companies?** Default no — the
   charter says closely held. A corpo choosing to list is the most
   interesting late-game event this design produces; make it the
   charter's term, never impossible.
4. **Localities competing on incorporation law** (Delaware). Not at
   dozens of players; named so nobody builds a second registry by
   accident.
5. **The registry's operator.** A Compact institution, NPC-run. Aevex as
   its technology vendor is tempting and too cute; leave it the state's.
