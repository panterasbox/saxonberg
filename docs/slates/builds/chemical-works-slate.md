## 2. The products **[DECIDED]**

- ⭐ **Sulfuric acid (vitriol)** — the industry's keystone; the **lead-chamber process**
  (Roebuck, 1746): burn sulfur (caprock sulfur ships), oxidize the gas with niter in
  lead-lined chambers. Lead finally has a big industrial buyer.
- **Nitric acid** — saltpeter distilled with sulfuric acid (the nitre bed, gunpowder's).
- **Soda** (sodium carbonate) — the **Leblanc process** (1791): salt (ships) + sulfuric
  acid → salt cake → roasted with chalk and coal. Then the cleaner **Solvay** process
  (1860s) — a later rung.
- **Bleach** — chlorine (from the Leblanc process's hydrochloric acid) absorbed in lime:
  **bleaching powder** (Tennant, 1799).
- **Alum** — from alum shale, roasted and leached; the dyer's mordant.

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ The governance lesson — the Alkali Act **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

The Leblanc works vented **hydrochloric acid gas**, which killed vegetation for miles
around. Britain's **Alkali Act (1863)** required works to condense most of it and
created the **Alkali Inspectorate** — among the first modern environmental regulators,
with inspectors who **measured** emissions. The three-layer measurement pattern again
(the engine measures, an institution certifies, a polity imposes), and the honest
consequence of a LULU — *a spill that already costs somebody*
([settlement-model](../../settlement-model.md)). ⭐ And the condensed acid became a
**product** (bleach) — regulation turning waste into a good.
