# Guild slate (the vocation institutions)

> **Status: design settled in conversation (2026-07-28), pre-requirements.**
> This slate is the institution-level guild design. It **supersedes and
> extends** [advancement-slate § Guilds + § Declared focus](./advancement-slate.md)
> — the focus-tagged-Transcript *mechanic* designed there stands as the
> substrate, but one framing there is corrected here: a guild's domain is
> **chartered and authored**, never derived from member behavior. The
> old [affiliation-slate § Guild](../deferred-rpg/affiliation-slate.md)
> sketch is two supersessions deep; both now point here.

## What a guild is

Guilds are the class system — there are no hardcoded classes; the
[Discipline Catalog](../../subsystems/advancement.md) is the impersonal
map and a guild is a **chartered, authored institution** that claims a
region of it. The charter declares the domain; membership never defines
it. (This was the load-bearing correction of the design pass: earlier
drafts kept trying to *derive* the guild's shape from what members do —
spend, practice, teach — and every derivation turned into churn and a
gameable surface. Institutions are declared; people are measured.)

The design center is **wish fulfillment, not skill acceleration**.
Nobody fantasizes about a training multiplier. Unpack the vocational
daydreams people actually have (rangers, mage colleges, thieves' guilds,
knightly orders, master smiths) and they share three parts:

1. **A door into a hidden world** — members see rooms, texts,
   techniques, and *problems* outsiders don't know exist. The interior
   is the product.
2. **Being called** — when a certain kind of trouble appears, *your
   kind* is summoned.
3. **Mutual recognition** — the cloak, the countersign. Two members who
   have never met owe each other something.

So a guild's spine is its **mysteries, calls, and marks** — the
mechanical layer (below) exists to make membership *feel real in
advancement*, never to be the point.

## The formation rule — vocation = discipline × livelihood

The Catalog is unbounded; guilds are scarce. A guild does not exist
because a discipline exists — it forms where **a discipline meets
demand**: someone will pay for the practice, certify it, or summon it.

- Skill with demand → **vocation** → can support a guild.
- Skill without demand → hobby → a **club**, which is just a chat group
  with a name (settled in the affiliation slate). Clubs are infinite,
  free, claim nothing, owe nothing, and dilute nothing.

The grain of a guild is **a career, not a leaf**. A world full of
game-genre communities doesn't get one guild per genre; it gets clubs,
and at most *one* institution at the vocation grain if the world
actually pays for the practice. This kills the paradox of choice at the
root: guilds only exist at the grain where a life fits inside one.

Two corollaries:

- **Open canon** (retained from the advancement slate): a guild never
  gates *learning*. Anything is trainable guildless; the guild gates
  recognition, acceleration, and the interior. The self-taught prodigy
  exists; she just can't walk into the archive or sign with the mark.
- **The mint-time rule**: *don't charter a guild until the world already
  pays for its vocation.* The biggest real inequality between guilds is
  demand-side — a guild over a rich economic loop vs. one over a system
  that isn't built yet — and no dial fixes that; only content does.
  ("Never half-grown," applied to charters. Build the livelihood loop
  first, then the institution over it.)

## The four layers — where to be liberal, where to be scarce

| Layer | Cardinality | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocations** | liberal, unbounded | a map region — costs nothing, dilutes nothing |
| **Institutions** | scarce, population-priced | halls, oaths, interiors — the layer where three-person guilds happen |
| **Wings** | per-institution | corpo flavor *inside* an institution |
| **Clubs** | infinite | a chat group |

The membership-thinness failure ("five corpo-flavored versions of the
same guild, each with three members") is prevented structurally:

- **Corpo flavor attaches as wings, chairs, and endowed halls inside
  the one institution — never as forks of it.** Five corpos who want a
  piece of the smiths get the Veshko Foundry Wing and an Acme-endowed
  mastership — one room, five logos, sponsorships competing on prestige
  *within* a shared institution (exactly the market-and-prestige
  competition the [corpos-slate](./corpos-slate.md) wants).
- **Rivalry is an earned event, not an authored default.** A second
  institution in a region only comes into existence by **schism**: a
  master (or bloc) with real standing and fresh capital leads a
  breakaway. Below critical mass a schism is visibly doomed; above it,
  it's a world-historical event (bulletin + forums material). Rivalry
  becomes endgame drama instead of launch-day fragmentation.
- Thin institutions **die of ordinary economics** — see Lifecycle.

## No magic guild

Magic is a top-level branch of the taxonomy parallel to everything
else — a capability layer, like literacy. A "mage's college" would be
the genre-guild mistake wearing robes: an institution formed around a
*branch of the map* instead of a vocation.

Instead, **every natural vocation carries supernatural elements, and a
guild's synergized magic disciplines are precisely its mystery tier.**
The outer hall teaches the mundane craft; the interior holds the
supernatural tradition of it — the smiths' forge that burns without
coal (fire/create), the healers' hands that read a body from within
(body/perceive), the wardens' speech with beasts (beast/plant/sense),
the delvers' stone that confesses where it's weak (earth). Medieval
guilds literally called their craft secrets *the mysteries* (from
*mestier*, craft) — this makes that literal. The claim should follow
real authored `synergizes` edges in the Catalog, not taste.

Consequences:

- **"Where do pure mages go?" — nowhere, correctly.** Magic *as a
  subject of study* belongs to the scholars' vocation (a researcher of
  magic is a real vocation; "mage" is not). The University teaches
  magic fundamentals to everyone as feeder material, like letters.
- **Magic makes vocations need each other**: lightning lives with the
  soldiers and earth with the delvers, so the expedition needing both
  is a *party* drawing on two guilds — the interlock, delivered free.
- Some magic may stay **institutionally homeless** (mind, arcana
  residue) — learnable but guildless: the hedge-witch corner of the
  map, where the tapped-and-secret institutions recruit.

## Invariants and divergence axes

**True of every guild:** claims a vocation-sized region; has an
interior invisible to outsiders; marks members legibly; asks something
of them (dues, conduct, answering calls); can examine and expel; never
grants competence, only recognizes it; never gates learning.

**Divergence axes** — one substrate, per-charter settings:

| Axis | Range |
|---|---|
| **Charter** | public (delegated by an [Office](../../subsystems/governance.md)) · private (self-chartered) · sponsored · religious |
| **Loyalty** | plural-tolerant · exclusive-primacy |
| **Steepness** | flat brotherhood · electoral stewards · appointed hierarchy |
| **Breadth** | one craft deep · a union of disciplines under one command |
| **Ends** | livelihood · service · knowledge · transcendence |
| **Selectivity** | open door · examined · sponsored · **tapped** (you don't apply; you get noticed) |
| **Listing** | public roster · unlisted (the guild's existence is itself a mystery) |

The **military** is not a counterexample — it's a guild at extreme
settings: publicly chartered, exclusive-loyalty, steep, broad,
service-ended. Public institutions are guilds whose authority is
*delegated* by the sovereign; the governance wall survives (powerful ≠
sovereign, and a public charter is revocable by the chartering Office).

**Specifically not a guild:** the club (interest without duty), the
party (venture without durability — form/join/earn wall unchanged), the
house (belonging without vocation), the corpo (standing without
membership), the office (sovereignty), faith itself (meaning without
vocation — though a religious *order* is a chartered guild), and the
**University** — the map-keeper and feeder of all guilds, belonging to
none.

## Membership — three tiers, friction inverts as you climb

Borrowing the Twitch shape (basic | affiliate | partner):

- **Associate** — the guild's door is open *to you*: outer hall, public
  lectures, the outer job board, the recruiter knows your name. Free,
  unbounded, reversible; claims no identity, confers no focus. The
  guild is courting you. "Join everything" lives here safely.
- **Member** — you belong. Earned admission (a demonstrated band or a
  sponsor), an **oath** — meaningful because breach is *derivable* from
  the [accountability ledger](../../subsystems/accountability.md), not
  vibes — dues, a key, the channel, and the focus declaration. Costs
  money, conduct, and the shape of your growth. Two or three is a full
  life (priced by the focus ramp's time-cost, no artificial slot count).
- **Master** — the inversion completes: the guild has a stake in *you*.
  You carry its authority (examine, sponsor, sign, answer calls in its
  name); your conduct is its reputation; and you got here partly
  through **teaching deeds that only mint when done for others** (the
  `command`-Discipline pattern) — so masters structurally seek juniors,
  and the endgame is the learn → master → teach lifecycle by
  construction. Typically one, ever.

Associate wide, member few, master one. Rank rule throughout:
**eligibility is derived, conferral is witnessed** — the engine can say
you're ready (band thresholds + counted deed kinds, legible and
ungameable), but rank lands only through an exam: a witnessed in-world
act minting a [chronicle](../../subsystems/chronicle.md) deed (the
trusted-recording shape; a rank exam is a *legitimate* θ read per the
advancement slate's spoiler doctrine). Leaving or expulsion: frozen
focus tags stay frozen, the chronicle keeps what you earned, the living
recognition (key, board, vote, countersign) lapses.

## The conferred advantage — a chartered training budget

The mechanical heart, and the design pass's hardest-won piece. The
guild's training effect is a **skill-points-shaped table on the
charter**, legible at the recruitment poster: *"The Ironwrights —
primary: forging, fire-work; secondary: appraisal, delving."*

- **Every guild gets the same fixed training budget**, denominated
  coarsely (primary/secondary emphasis tiers, not decimals), allocated
  at charter across its claimed region. The spread is the guild's
  defining mechanical statement: stacked deep or spread wide.
- The budget **weights the focus modulation** riding the settled
  advancement-slate substrate: focus-context frozen per
  `TranscriptEntry` at append, modulation applied inside
  `Competence.derive`, immutable/prospective, per-discipline **clamp**,
  bonus-never-penalty. Nothing here is stored competence.
- The declared-focus **floor** (works anywhere) is deliberately
  trivial — an identity signal. The weighted table is where the felt
  effect lives.

Why this doesn't violate the honesty firewall (the question that burned
three design rounds): the firewall's hard line is **no fabricated
evidence and no stored competence** — a multiplier on real practice was
*always* allowed (that's what declared focus is). A per-discipline
weight table is authored institutional configuration, the same shape as
a Discipline's `conferrals` table. The doctrine governs *measurement*,
not institution authoring. What remains genuinely non-negotiable:

1. **Points weight training, never mint it.** The budget modulates
   learning-from-real-acts; it can never purchase band progress. (The
   one thing that would break the game's spine.)
2. **The budget is uniform across guilds, forever.** One global number;
   a "bigger budget" promotional guild inflates the whole layer.
3. **Coarse tiers on the surface** — primary/secondary, never "1.7×"
   (the never-a-number presentation rule).

**Broad vs. narrow answered:** same budget, so the 10-discipline guild
and the 1-discipline guild are wide-and-shallow vs. narrow-and-deep — a
real character choice, not a ranking. **Inflation impossible:** realized
advantage is minted only by practice — total acceleration in the world
is bounded by (player practice-hours × clamp), invariant to guild
count, claims, or wealth; charters redistribute *where* the fixed
supply lands. **Concentration self-limits:** a mega-guild is never
mechanically richer, just more crowded — congestion on masters and
halls (the attendant/queue substrate makes it felt), credential
dilution, and schism pressure peaking exactly at oversubscription.

## Proportionality — the guild points at the world

Membership must affect real advancement while the player has a life
outside the guild hall. The shipped estimator already enforces this:
**easy, safe, repeatable checks are nearly worthless evidence**
(difficulty-modulated observation + ZPD gating in
[advancement.md](../../subsystems/advancement.md)) — so **the practice
hall cannot be ground, by construction**. A novice's ZPD is low enough
that hall drills are genuinely at their edge; past novice, informative
evidence exists only out in the world. The apprentice belongs in the
hall; the *journeyman* — etymologically the one who journeys — is sent
out. The rank fiction falls out of the difficulty curve.

So the guild's instructional product is **direction, not a room**: the
mentor's ZPD-holding job is *matching you to the right world problem* —
which is honestly self-balancing, because well-matched difficulty is
already faster learning under the existing math, no multiplier needed.
The hall keeps three ungrindable jobs: **onboarding** (the one phase
where drills are real evidence), **the safely-impossible** (a sparring
floor under consented non-lethal terms), and **punctuation** — report,
rite, naming, next direction. Teaching deeds mint for the mentor when
the assignment *lands* (the `command` pattern again). The guild is a
place you're **from**, not a place you're **at** — and the min-maxer
and the roleplayer end up doing the same thing: their vocation, in the
world.

## The guild job board — why you frequent, not just join

Identity is why you join; **the board is why you walk in on a random
Tuesday**. It is the tier ladder made concrete, on the shipped
[contract substrate](../../subsystems/contract.md):

- **Outer board** (associate/public): small overflow work — the
  recruiting funnel made of actual paid vocation-work instead of a
  sales pitch (frequent → associate → member).
- **Member board**: the real commissions, behind **claim gates**
  (credential/band/membership requirements on who may claim — the
  contract build's named-deferred seam; this is the consumer that
  justifies building it). *This is what dues buy.*
- **Directed**: the mentor handing you the right commission is the
  board with judgment attached — board and mentor are one function at
  two touch levels.

Work faucets, in order of importance: (1) **outside posters choosing
the guild board over the public noticeboard** — the guild as
*clearinghouse*: qualified readership, credential-gated claimants, and
the guild **vouches and arbitrates for a cut** (quality assurance +
dispute-backing as an economic service; a revenue line that scales with
being useful); (2) polity/authored demand (public works, the deferred
contract generator); (3) the guild's own subsidized **training
commissions** (the WPA floor at guild scale).

**Anti-enclosure discipline** (a discipline, not a rule): posters
always choose where to post, the public noticeboard stays alive, and
authored NPC demand is never piped exclusively through guild boards.
Guild boards win share by service quality — vouching, matching,
arbitration — never by capture. A guild's stickiness is then exactly
proportional to real vocational demand through its board, which is the
mint-time rule enforcing itself: a guild over a vocation nobody pays
for has an empty board, and an empty board is a visibly dead hall.

## The balance ledger — what is balanced, and how

After the firewalls, **exactly one thing is numerically balanced, and
it's global, not per-guild**:

1. **The proportionality dials** — budget size, primary:secondary
   ratio, clamp magnitude, floor weight. Uniform across guilds, so
   there is no inter-guild tuning — one question: *how much should
   membership matter?* Balanced by (a) a written target sentence
   ("a focused member reaches the next band noticeably sooner — a
   season's difference, never a class barrier; unaffiliated is behind,
   not excluded") and (b) an **advancement gym** — the combat-gym
   precedent: the estimator is deterministic and headless-benchable;
   simulate Transcript accrual under focused/unfocused play, print
   band-arrival deltas, tune to the sentence, pin in CI.
2. **Charter-time structural review** — region coherence (career
   grain, connected via real Catalog edges) + budget-spread
   arithmetic. A validation pass + human judgment at mint (the
   skill-signature review-gate precedent); no ongoing accounting.
3. **Self-balancing markets** — rank strictness / credential value
   (floats on the guild's [renown](../../subsystems/renown.md); a
   diploma mill deflates itself), fees/dues (market competition; the
   guild pays rent), concentration (congestion + dilution + schism),
   mentor quality (auto-honest via the estimator). Our job is
   **rendering the signals** (credential renown readable, queues felt,
   dormancy visible) — monitoring, never knobs.
4. **Firewalls (balance by prohibition):** sponsorship never touches
   the budget · points weight, never mint · no mechanical compensation
   for exclusivity clauses (the military demands primacy and gets
   *nothing* mechanical for it; the interior is the compensation) ·
   credential never confers clearance or office · open canon.

**Everything else is flavor**: names, marks, oaths, calls, selectivity,
loyalty, interior governance, mysteries-as-content, secrecy, hall
placement, NPC casting, succession, chapter policy. Shapes identity and
story; touches no conserved quantity; author freely.

## Wizards and the Worldwrights

**Wizardhood is a security clearance, not an expertise** — the
`isWizard` code-trust axis is a fact about a *principal*, orthogonal to
any character competence ([access.md](../../subsystems/access.md)). So:

- A wizard's **character** joins guilds like anyone.
- **Authoring is a real disciplined vocation**: worldwrighting is
  diegetic labor (scripting, Studio, the provenance ledger, producer
  credit) — discipline × demand, so a **Worldwrights' guild** is its
  natural institution, the player → contributor on-ramp given a hall
  and a ladder (associates script; members are protowizard content
  authors; masters are those the realm's work runs on).
- **Hard wall, same shape as the governance wall:** a Worldwrights'
  mastership is *evidence an archwizard consults* when deciding to
  flip the bit — the bar exam, not the judgeship. Guild rank never
  confers `isWizard`; an in-world social process must not mint
  out-of-fiction root access.
- **"House developer" is employment, not membership.** Guilds
  self-serve most software needs through their own members (content
  tier is protowizard-capable; a guild without worldwrights among its
  members has a recruiting problem, which is fun). Genuinely code-tier
  needs are commissioned (work contract) or rostered (a waged position
  on the guild-as-business). Being the Ironwrights' house wizard is a
  *job at* the Ironwrights held by a *member of* the Worldwrights —
  who you serve ≠ what you are (the employment-vs-membership split the
  corpo design already established).

## Corpos — labor and capital

**Guilds are labor; corpos are capital.** Three edges, all
prosocial-competitive: corpos **sponsor** wings/endowments inside
institutions (never memberships, never forks — settled); corpos **hire
preferentially** on guild credentials (the credential's market value
*is* corpo demand); and the live tension seam — **corpo in-house
training vs. guild certification**, competing for the same young
practitioner. A union that bargains *with* Veshko is a better fantasy
than a guild that is secretly Veshko's HR department.

## The charter schema — the decision surface at mint

Minting a guild is a **content act, not a code act** — nearly every row
below is authored data on a charter document.

- **Identity**: name, marks, livery, title forms; listed vs. unlisted.
- **The claim**: vocation + discipline region (validated once for
  career-grain coherence); the budget spread (primary/secondary); the
  mysteries (which magic synergies + interior content at which access
  tier — the keyway tiers are its lock).
- **Constitution**: charter type (and thus who can revoke); loyalty
  clause; interior governance (who admits/examines/expels;
  succession); the rank ladder (count, names, eligibility recipes —
  bands + deed kinds + teaching duty; eligibility derived, conferral
  witnessed).
- **Membership policy**: selectivity; entry floor (raw novices vs.
  demonstrated bands); the oath (conduct code written against the
  accountability ledger; expulsion process); obligations (dues cadence,
  calls, teaching duty at senior rank).
- **Economy & presence**: the hall (which parcel, which locality);
  fee schedule (admission, dues, exams, the board's cut); waged
  positions and which one or two roles are NPC carves (NPCs are
  expensive — each charter names the mentor, maybe the greeter, and
  stops); sponsorship policy (which corpo money, if any — refusing all
  of it is as strong an identity as being gilded by three).
- **External relations**: what the credential certifies and gates;
  the calls (what summons the guild; what a member drops); the campus
  recruiting touchpoint; declared relations (rivals, treaties —
  optional, but a rivalry written into two charters is content gold).
- **Lifecycle provisions**: founding path; dormancy terms; who
  inherits hall and treasury (escheat to the chartering authority? a
  designated merge partner?) — decided at mint so dormancy is a story,
  not a support ticket.

**The non-choices** — physics every charter inherits, load-bearing by
their absence from the schema: budget size and clamp (global, uniform);
open canon; no competence grants; credential-never-clearance; the
governance wall (no charter makes a guild sovereign).

## Lifecycle

- **Founding paths**: authored at world-build · player-founded (the
  three capitals: masters with real bands — unpurchasable human
  capital; a hall parcel + opening float; a charter filing) · schism.
  Same schema regardless; only the origin differs.
- **Dormancy through ordinary economics** — no vitality index: the
  hall is a parcel with rent, staff draw wages, dues lapse. A guild
  that can't cover rent goes visibly dark via the existing property
  and lease mechanics — dusty hall, mothballed charter, joinable in
  name, the world showing the truth.
- **Merger**: dormant guilds amalgamate (the "Amalgamated Society of
  Smiths and Delvers" is a deeply real institution shape).
- **Chapters** (lean, unconfirmed): none at v1 — a chapter shares the
  charter where a schism forks it, and chapters reopen the
  membership-thinness question; make chapters the pressure valve a
  congested guild earns *before* schism.
- **Revocation** (lean, unconfirmed): revoking a public charter is an
  Office power — that's most of what "public charter" means.

## Substrate mapping — what exists, what's new

Almost everything is already built; the Party and Business shapes
solved the hard parts.

| Piece | Rides |
|---|---|
| `Guild` | an Idea backed by a Document — the [Party](../../subsystems/party.md) pattern verbatim (durable mirror, boot re-materialization, membership owned by the guild, participant-gated transitions); durable-only, no ad-hoc tier |
| roster + ranks | a `guild:<path>` GroupProvider — [grouping.md](../../subsystems/grouping.md) literally anticipates "a future guild provider with richer hierarchies" around `roleOf`; chat = a bound channel (party precedent) |
| guild-as-business | composes the [Business](../../subsystems/employment.md) shape: account, dues as banking legs, waged positions, sponsorship as treasury transfer |
| hall | a parcel **titled to the guild's group principal** ([parcel.md](../../subsystems/parcel.md) supports group owners); interior tiers via [lock/key keyways](../../subsystems/credential.md) (the dorm build proved member-keyed doors end to end) |
| board | a [JobBoard](../../subsystems/contract.md) instance + **claim gates** (the contract build's named-deferred seam — this build is its consumer) |
| rank exam | a witnessed [chronicle](../../subsystems/chronicle.md) deed (the trusted-recording shape); credential *derived* from guild records (validity derived, presentation separate — the credential slate's doctrine), never a mutable stamp |
| oath / expulsion | conduct derivable from the [accountability ledger](../../subsystems/accountability.md) |
| **new** | the focus-context tag on `TranscriptEntry` + the charter-weighted modulation inside `Competence.derive` (frozen facts, tunable formula — a field and read-time dials, not a subsystem); the charter document + its validation pass; claim gates on contracts; the advancement gym |

## The launch roster

> Drafted 2026-07-28 against the **whole slate corpus treated as
> shipped** (mining / fishing / farming / ranching / property-tenancy /
> courts / inquiry / streaming / alignment all live by go-live). Names
> are sketches. Players must not start from zero — the roster is the
> exemplar set that *teaches what a guild is*, so its charters
> deliberately spread across the divergence axes; player chartering
> opens after the exemplars have taught the form.

**One new mechanism: the standing charter.** Not every institution
opens on day one — some launch as **authored-but-dormant**: the hall
exists in the world (mothballed, storied, visitable), the charter is
filed, and the guild *opens* when its vocation's economy activates.
This runs the dormancy mechanic in reverse: a guild reopening — the
Delvers' hall unshuttering when the mines open — is a live world event
(bulletin + forums material), and the world ships feeling old instead
of new. Day-one thinness is absorbed by the associate tier (associate
wide, member few) plus the dormant set.

### The audit test

Every charter must answer three questions, or it's a genre import:

1. **Who pays?** Name the paymaster — market demand real at launch
   scale, polity paper (public contracts, DAU-independent where
   possible), or faith/patronage — and check the payer actually exists
   in the shipped/slated world.
2. **Under which law?** Realm (in-world legislation, the offices) vs
   meta-state (coop governance, moderation). Flag any straddle.
3. **World-native fantasy?** Does the wish attach to machinery this
   world generates, or to a memory of other games?

The roster below is the post-audit version (the first draft's Muster
fell to question 3, the Factors and Carriers to question 1 — see
*What fell out of the audit*).

### Force in an administered realm (the Muster, deconstructed)

One realm → no interstate frontier → no war → **no standing military**,
and the realm is an *administration* (The Ordinance), not a
nation-state — administrations issue paper, not armies. Legitimate
force decomposes into four world-native pieces:

- **Writ execution** — courts and polity generate paper (summons,
  seizure, escheat, judgment). Per *consequence recorded, not
  mechanized*, there is no police force: a writ becomes a sealed
  contract claimable only by holders of a **marshal's credential**.
  The **Marshalcy is a credential + a public contract stream, not an
  institution** — the credentialed labor comes from existing guilds
  (Free Company, Wardens).
- **The frontier against the hollow** — the only organized violence
  *with a cause* this world generates, and it's alignment content by
  construction: the army-fantasy (banner, hardship, cause) homes in
  the **Warding Order** (standing charter, below).
- **Protection & escort** — private, already chartered.
- **The civic call** — *posse comitatus in the original sense already
  exists as the calls mechanism*: the polity or a court raises the
  commons; answering is voluntary, paid, credential-weighted. The
  modern military/police wall holds **by construction** — there are no
  standing armed bodies to separate; every use of force is per-writ,
  credentialed, and accountability-stamped (consent + terms recorded
  on every harm).

Citizenship stays deliberately thin: you are *of* the realm by
**residence** (a lease or title in administered lands — the dorm gives
every player this on day one) plus participation standing. Anything
thicker is polity legislation later. Use-of-force rules are **realm
law**; harm to *people* is **meta-state** (moderation) — the membrane.

### The Landwrights — the real-estate vocation, in depth

Property is not thin — the slate corpus gives it more machinery than
almost any vocation: parcels + title + an append-only chain-of-title
([parcel.md](../../subsystems/parcel.md)), bilateral-consent
`transfer` / `subdivide`, leases (`UseGrant`), the residence ladder
(dorm → **apartments**, the elastic-Warren build-out), parcel tax,
escheat, and tenancy (property-slate phases). That's a full career:

- **Survey** — extents, subdivision geometry, boundary walking. The
  measurement pedagogy angle is real (geometry/earth-science ISCED
  anchors); `analyze address` and the coverage index are its
  instruments.
- **Valuation** — appraisal specialized to land: income approach over
  a venue's actual P&L, comparables over the title record, and the
  honest twist that a parcel's worth includes what can *run* on it.
- **Conveyancing** — title search (reading `parcel_events`),
  transfer brokerage (the bilateral-consent verb makes a broker
  natural), escrow via banking; disputes hand off to the Advocates.
- **Development** — the apartment ladder: assembling title + capital
  + commissioned Worldwright labor + a tenancy plan into a building
  that grows on demand (the DormWarren precedent, grown up).
  Developer ≠ author (the property slate's un-fusing) — the
  Landwright *orchestrates*; the Worldwrights build.
- **Tenancy stewardship** — lease administration, rent collection,
  the eviction/lapse mechanics, upkeep against reset sweeps. The
  dorm→apartment pipeline every player rides is Landwright
  journeyman work.

Its **demand anchor is DAU-independent** (the Terminus-city economic
rule): the polity's **assessment contract** — somebody must keep the
tax rolls, value escheated parcels, and survey new subdivisions, and
the polity pays for it whether or not the market is hot. A private
charter *holding recurring public contracts* — deliberately distinct
from the Muster's public charter, teaching the difference between
*being* the state and *working for* it. Interlocks everywhere:
commissions Worldwrights, escrows through banking, litigates through
Advocates, assesses every other guild's hall.

(One discipline note: the compute-allowance layer of property stays
**meta, never diegetic** — the Landwrights' fiction is title, ground,
and tenancy; allowance administration is paperwork the platform
surfaces plainly, not a mystery.)

### What fell out of the audit

Four findings bigger than any single charter:

1. **The engine automates the middlemen.** In a world with honest
   physics, *trust-work is engine-work; labor-work is player-work*.
   Engine-verified contracts kill the notary, the conserved ledger
   kills the auditor, derive-on-read blame kills the investigator's
   paperwork — so guilds whose historical product was **trust
   intermediation** (Factors) get automated out of a launch paymaster,
   and only the human residue (interpretation, persuasion — the
   Advocates) survives. The vocations that thrive are the ones
   producing **goods, care, knowledge, content, or force**.
2. **Metabolism is the bedrock paymaster** (everyone eats; per-capita
   demand never zero) — the Grange → Victuallers → Watermen chain
   stands on it. **The wear economy is second** (`DurableMixin`
   condition makes repair demand universal) — the Ironwrights stand on
   that. **Polity paper provides the DAU-independent anchors**
   (assessment, marshal writs, floor-buys, frontier contracts).
3. **Two membrane-straddles are sanctioned, not bugs**: the
   Worldwrights (realm-money commissions ⊕ producer influence) and the
   Chorus (realm-money tips ⊕ renown/platform patronage) each span the
   realm/meta line exactly the way the three-chambers design intends —
   they *demonstrate* the membrane.
4. **Placeholder mechanics can silently erase vocations.** The retail
   reset-sweep's self-restock is a placeholder occupying the Carriers'
   entire niche; engine conveniences that stand in for future labor
   markets should be flagged as such, so replacing them with real
   logistics is understood as *opening a hall*, not breaking a
   feature.

### What the roster is for

Three practical payoffs beyond the fiction:

1. **It is the employment-content worklist.** Each demand-anchor cell
   is exactly the venue + job-contract authoring the economy build
   needs next — enumerate guilds, derive the backlog. The derived
   backlog is maintained as **[launch-worklist.md](../../launch-worklist.md)**
   (economy-essential first; guilds themselves deferrable past
   launch).
2. **It teaches the axes by contrast** — public/exclusive (Muster) vs
   private/plural (Wardens) vs union (Delvers) vs public-contract
   (Landwrights) vs open-door (Victuallers, Chorus) vs examined
   (Physic, Inquiry) vs sealed (the unlisted one) — so when player
   chartering opens, the form is understood.
3. **It fixes the vanilla discipline pack's spine**: the taxonomy
   fill-out should be authored so these ~18 regions come out as
   coherent career-grain subgraphs with the right `synergizes` edges
   (each mystery tier above is a synergy claim to honor in data).
