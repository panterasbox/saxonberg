## 0. The ask **[the owner, 2026-11-02]**

*"Is it basically just like real life where some things take keys and some unlock with your phone?
Do we model things like IoT? Is that redundant in a world that's already 100% digital, or do we want
some aether-based IoT that's expressible in the narrative? What would that even be — it feels very
meta."*

## 1. ⭐⭐ Not redundant — the aether is a field in the fiction, not the game's interface **[LEAN]**

In the fiction the aether is a **physical field that carries information**; a device that reads or
acts over it is an instrument with one more way to be reached, exactly as a display already is. Every
aether property has a consequence in the world, which is what keeps it narrative:

- **coverage** — off grid or in a dead zone, nothing is reached by mind;
- **power** — no power, no reach, and a fail mode the author chose;
- **distance collapses** — the reason a covenant may refuse it below the modern rung;
- **a log** — the device knows who drove it and when, and the person a line is about can read it;
- **people are the weak point** — whoever edits the access list.

## 2. ⚠⚠ The two lines this design must not cross **[LEAN]**

1. **No out-of-fiction control panel.** Every act on a device is a verb through a carrier the actor
   is holding (phone, implant, radio). A dashboard that drives the world from outside the fiction
   breaks *the room never pulls* and makes presence pointless.
2. **No automation through the back door.** *"Lock the door at ten every night"* is a standing
   instruction — [standing-instructions-slate](./standing-instructions-slate.md)'s question (*may a
   player automate labour at all?*) comes first. A device driven by mind is driven **now, by
   someone**.
