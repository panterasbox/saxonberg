## 1. ⭐⭐ The demand is shipped; the material is wrong **[DECIDED]**

**Twenty-one things are made of `ceramic`** and stocked from nowhere (the
shape of Veshko's rum): the dairy's jug, butter crock, rennet jar, starter
crock and setting pan; the tallow crock; the honey jar; **farming's three
plant pots**; the bowl, platter and plated dish; the mug; **smelting's
crucible and ingot mould**; the fuel trade's **retort**; mining's streak
plate. And pottery **breaks** (`brittle`), so crockery is recurring demand.

⚠ **But `ceramic` is one material doing five jobs:**

| body | fired | what it is | of the 21 |
|---|---|---|---|
| terracotta, unglazed earthenware | low, ~1000 °C | porous — breathes and wicks | the plant pots (porous pots keep roots cool and drain), the setting pan |
| glazed earthenware | low | porous body, watertight skin | crocks, jugs, jars, the mug, bowls, platters |
| stoneware | high, ~1250 °C | vitrified, watertight unglazed, strong | crocks and jugs at the better grade |
| refractory (fireclay) | high | survives furnace heat | the crucible, the retort, the ingot mould |
| porcelain | ~1300 °C+, needs **kaolin** | white, translucent | none — an epoch and geography good |

⭐ **The brick rule converges exactly: firing temperature derives water
absorption.** A body's porosity and strength come from **its clay**
(ordinary, fireclay, kaolin) **and the heat it reached** — one fired-clay
derivation over two inputs, not five authored materials (moulding's *the
matter decides what setting is*, applied to the kiln). **The shipped rows
are re-pointed** to the body they really are.

## 2. Forming — `shape` **[DECIDED]**

The `make` verb is the recipe-script runner (the decouple branch makes it
the shell's `do`), and its findings rule *"a recipe without a verb is a
missing act, and the act belongs to the trade."* Potters *throw*, but
`throw` is the platform's hurling verb, and ranged rightly keeps it. Worked
on the collision ladder: rung 2 — **unify**. Glass ships **`shape`** (a hot
gather on the blowpipe *into a bottle or a cylinder*), and throwing a pot is
the same act — forming a plastic material into a vessel form by hand with a
tool:

> **`shape <material> into <form>`** — the gather on the blowpipe (shipped),
> clay on the **wheel** (throwing), clay **by hand** (coiling), clay pressed
> into a **brick mould** (the moulder's day, durative — brickmaking § 3,
> corrected from `make` in this MR).

The help says *potters call it throwing*. The **instrument and material
decide the possible forms** (a wheel throws round ones, hands coil any, a
brick mould makes its brick).

| rung | how |
|---|---|
| hand-building (coil, pinch) | `shape` with hands only — slow, crude, anyone |
| ⭐ the wheel (kick or treadle) | `shape` at the wheel |
| moulds | press-moulding, and later **slip casting** in plaster (moulding § 0a's absorbency; plaster from gypsum, an 18th-century European rung) |

⭐ **Competence decides the pot, deterministically — never a roll**: **wall
thickness and evenness** (a beginner's mug is heavy and thick-walled, a
master's light — legible in the hand), **the largest form you can raise**
(below the band, a smaller or thicker pot rather than a failure; a form
simply beyond you is refused, and the refusal is the progression UI), and
the **grade**. Weight then matters downstream — capacity per weight, thermal
mass, firing risk. **Forms are rows**: jug, crock, bowl, mug, plant pot,
crucible, sugar cone.

## 3. Leather-hard, trimming, handles **[DECIDED]**

Drying is brick's, on the wetness model, with one window that matters:

- ⭐ **Leather-hard is a moisture window** — too wet deforms, too dry will not
  cut or join.
- **Trimming** (the foot ring, cut on the wheel) is **`carve`** — the
  woodworker's shipped verb for cutting away to a form, on leather-hard clay;
  *sgraffito* decoration too.
- ⭐ **A jug is an assembly** — body, handle, spout — joined with slip by
  assembly's one verb, **`fit handle to jug`**, a **slip joint** made
  irreversible by firing. ⭐ **Joints crack on moisture mismatch** (a wet
  handle on a drier body shrinks differently) — derived. A broken-off handle
  is localized damage the tinker mends (§ 6).
- **Bone-dry before firing**, or it bursts (brick's physics); **too fast
  cracks it**, and a beginner's uneven walls crack more.

## 4. Firing **[DECIDED]**

- **Bisque** (~900 °C — hard, still porous, takes glaze) then **glaze**
  (the glost firing, at the glaze's maturing temperature, which also sets the
  body). Once-firing on raw ware is the cheaper, lossier alternative.
- **Kilns, all read at the end** (rubber's way, the clamp's timing): **pit or
  bonfire** firing (the clamp's open shape, the hand-building rung), the
  **updraft** kiln (Stoke's bottle kilns — the ceramics pack's kiln, the
  brickfield's Scotch kiln rung), the **climbing** kiln (East Asia — later or
  elsewhere).
- ⭐ **Atmosphere from the draught** — oxidizing for warm colours, reducing
  for **celadon** green from iron and copper reds. One dial, four trades
  (charcoal, ash, brick, pottery).
- ⭐ **Thermal shock** — heat or cool too fast and pots *dunt*; opening the
  kiln early to sell sooner is a temptation with a derived price (the thermal
  gradient against the body; uneven walls suffer most).
- ⭐ **Wood ash glazes the pots by itself** — the fuel's ash lands on the
  shoulders and melts (the anagama look): the potash slate's wood ash,
  fluxing silica, from the fuel's ash fraction and the heat.
- **Saggars** — fireclay boxes protecting fine ware — from the brickfield's
  fireclay.
- **Wasters** — cracked, slumped or stuck pots — sorted out and ground back
  as **grog**, which strengthens new clay. The loop closes.

## 5. Glazes **[DECIDED]**

⭐ **A glaze is a glass** — silica, flux and stabilizer melted as a skin: the
glass batch's physics, the potash slate's **`flux`** role, and glass's
**colour from iron** (Beer–Lambert; copper and cobalt later). **Applied by
`dip`** — chandlery's shipped verb (candles dipped in tallow) — or poured;
`glaze` stays the glazier's (setting a pane).

| glaze | flux | notable |
|---|---|---|
| **ash** | wood ash | ⭐ the ash-burner's product in a glaze bucket — § 4's natural glaze on purpose |
| ⚠ **lead** | lead oxide | glossy, low, cheap — and **toxic** (below) |
| **salt** | salt thrown in at peak heat | ⭐ the saltern's salt; sodium vapour glazes stoneware orange-peel and **vents hydrochloric acid** (Leblanc's story at the kiln) |
| **tin** | tin oxide, opaque white | maiolica and delftware — Europe's porcelain imitation; tin from mining |

⚠⚠ **Lead leaches into acidic food** — wine, vinegar, fruit, pickles kept in
a lead-glazed crock; centuries of endemic poisoning (Rome boiled wine down
in lead). **Derived**: the contents' **acidity** × the glaze's lead × time in
contact is a dose through metabolism's shipped **toxin route**. ⭐ **Silent**,
like contamination — *no sensory tell, on purpose*; learned from knowledge
or from the sickness. Lens 4 and 7: cheap lead against safe glaze, and a
polity that bans lead-glazed food vessels passes a real public-health law
with a measurable effect.

**Faults, derived**: ⭐ **crazing** (a glaze shrinking more than its body on
cooling crackles — two materials' thermal expansion; crazed ware harbours
dirt, cleanliness's interest), **running** (over-fired), **crawling** (over
dust or grease).

## 6. Breakage, mending, and pottery in use **[DECIDED]**

- **Breakage** is materials-response on a **brittle** body — impact energy
  against the body's strength, so a thick beginner's pot survives a knock a
  master's eggshell bowl does not (skill's trade-off). The pieces are
  **sherds**, ground back to grog. ⭐ **Breakage is the recurring demand.**
- **Mending**: ⭐ the **china mender** drilled the pieces and bound them with
  metal **staples** — repair's ruling *the first mender is a tinker on the
  road*, `repair` with rivets on assembly's localized damage; and ⭐
  **kintsugi** — lacquer and gold, **a visible mend that adds value and
  history** (lens 2 and 4: a broken heirloom becomes more itself) — a repair
  method row with its own look (lacquer from a tapped tree).
- **The body decides the use, derived**: ⭐ **cooking on the flame** —
  open-bodied earthenware with grog survives a direct fire's shock, dense
  stoneware cracks (the bean pot, the tagine; the wrong pot is lost);
  **porous ware absorbs** fat and flavour and harbours microbes (cleanliness,
  spoilage) while glazed and stoneware are hygienic; ⭐ **porous jars cool
  water** — an unglazed jar sweats and the evaporation chills its contents
  (the *botijo*, the *zeer*): the shipped wetness evaporation plus the body's
  absorption, no new mechanism — the Coolbox's simpler cousin.

## 7. The venue **[DECIDED]**

- ⭐ **The potter at the Delight brickfield** — the same pit and kiln rung, so
  the ceramics pack's one venue is **a works with two trades**; the potter
  picks finer clay from the same pit (derived quality). Rejection's fireclay
  serves both — crucibles and retorts here, firebrick there.
- **Wares go downstream by barge** with the bricks — light, valuable,
  fragile, so packing (straw, crates) is a carrier's real concern and the
  drive can show breakage in transit.
- **A potter's stall in Terminus market** — retail, where crockery is bought
  and replaced. ⭐ **The 21 stocked goods' lines are re-pointed to the
  pottery** instead of conjuring stock, as molasses replaces Veshko's rum.

## 8. The lens pass (2026-10-18)

1. **Pedagogy** — firing temperature → vitrification → porosity (the brick
   rule); leather-hard as a window; moisture mismatch cracking joints;
   bisque vs glaze; reduction colour; thermal shock; a glaze is a glass;
   expansion mismatch (crazing); acidity × lead (leaching); evaporative
   cooling in a porous jar.
2. **Expression** — the ordinary case is rows (forms, glazes, a second
   pottery); ⭐ a potter's hand shows in the pot (wall thickness, form, glaze);
   an impression of any pot can be slip-cast (moulding § 0a); kintsugi turns
   damage into expression.
3. **Immersion** — a beginner's heavy mug, crazed glaze, the smoking kiln, a
   sweating water jar. **Participation** — the potter, the kiln-burner, the
   china mender, the stallholder, the bargee; ⭐ *can the polity do something
   we didn't want?* — **ban lead glaze** (or fail to), with a measurable
   public-health effect.
4. **Values** — ⚠ lead glaze (cheap now, poison later, silently); kintsugi
   against replacement; deskilling when slip-casting undercuts the thrower.
5. **Continuity** — coil → wheel → slip cast → jigger; pit → updraft →
   tunnel kiln: the same `shape`, `carve`, `fit`, `dip`, `repair`.
6. **Economy** — ⭐ demand shipped first (21 stocked goods) and recurring
   (breakage); consumes clay, fireclay, ash, lead, salt, tin, fuel; produces
   ware by body and grade, sherds and grog; the 21 stock lines re-pointed.
7. **Governance** — lead-glaze bans; salt-glaze fumes as a nuisance; the
   china mender licensed or not; each a criterion and an appeal when
   designed.

**Altitude** — *invariant*: a body's properties derive from its clay and the
heat it reached; skill decides the pot deterministically. *Grain*: the
firing thresholds, the wall-thickness bands, the lead leach rate. *Title*:
the Delight works, the Terminus stall.
