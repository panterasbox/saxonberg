## ⭐⭐ The object

A pump moves a **fluid** from A to B **against gravity**. That is the
whole concept, and it is why it has more customers than anything else in
the backlog: the village well, the mine sump, the oil lift, the brine
line, the bilge, irrigation, and the fire engine are **one machine with
different numbers**.

Four numbers, which is the authoring surface: **lift** (how high),
**throughput** (how fast), **power** (what drives it), **seal** (what
keeps the fluid on the right side of the piston).

### The ladder

| rung | what it is | lift |
|---|---|---|
| **shadoof · screw** | muscle, continuous, no valves | a little, repeatedly |
| **bucket chain · noria** | animal or water powered | the height of the wheel |
| **suction (lift) pump** | a piston and valves, pulling | ⭐ **~10 m and no further, ever** |
| **force pump** | piston at the **bottom**, pushing | as high as the cylinder and the power allow |
| **sucker-rod / beam pump** | rods down a bore to a plunger | the oil answer; the nodding donkey |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The limit, which is the best pedagogical object in the backlog

> **A suction pump cannot lift water more than about 10 metres.** It works
> by atmospheric pressure pushing water up into a vacuum, and a 10.3 m
> water column is exactly one atmosphere. Miners knew it empirically for
> centuries, and it was a **hard ceiling on how deep a mine could go.**

What that ceiling produced is the whole story of industry: it is why
Torricelli and Pascal went after the vacuum in the 1640s, which ran
through Boyle to **Newcomen's 1712 atmospheric engine — a steam engine
invented specifically to pump water out of mines.**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The thing that could not be pumped produced the science that
> produced the engine that pumped it.**

⭐ **Candidate: a better first case for the discovery loop than
vulcanization** (→ [inquiry-slate](./inquiry-slate.md)). The limit is
**felt before it is understood** — your pump simply stops working at
depth — it is a **measurable number**, and understanding it unlocks **a
different machine** rather than a recipe. The world announces a law *by
refusing, with a number*: not *"you lack the Law of Vulcanization"* but
*"the water stops coming at thirty feet and nobody knows why."*
⚠ **Not free: atmospheric pressure is modelled NOWHERE** — one constant
has to be introduced. Cheap, honest physics, but not zero.

## ⚠⚠ The bailer is the rung below — so this does NOT block drilling

Recorded because the recommendation was made and withdrawn inside one
exchange, and the reasoning is the useful part.

The argument for pump-before-drilling was *"drilling without a pump
abandons every well early."* ⛔ **False, and it skips a rung.** How did
Sichuan lift brine from 300 m? Not with a pump — with a **bailer**: a long
tube with a leather foot valve, lowered and hauled on a windlass. And
**drilling already needs one**, because bailing is how cuttings come out
of the hole.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The pre-pump lift is the BAILER, and it is already in drilling's
> scope.** The same tool clears the hole and lifts the product. Early oil
> wells were produced by bailing before anybody fitted a pump.

So drilling ships **complete** — bail to clear, bail to produce, slow and
labour-hungry and functional at any depth — and the pump is a
**throughput upgrade**: continuous and unattended instead of a man on a
windlass. ⭐ Which also preserves the decline curve as designed: flowing →
**bailed** → pumped → stripper → abandoned, with the pump *extending* the
economic life rather than enabling the well.

⭐ And the leather survives either way: a bailer's foot valve is leather
too.

## The seven-lens pass

Run 2026-10-08 at the user's request. **Four lenses at ⭐⭐⭐ — a higher
score than drilling itself gets.**

### 1 · Pedagogy — ⭐⭐⭐

The suction limit is **derivable** (10.3 m of water = 1 atm), measurable,
and felt before it is understood. Disciplines exercised: `physics`
(ships), `smithing` for the cylinder, `leatherwork` for the packing. The
world is derivable here in the strongest available sense — the law is not
authored anywhere, it is a consequence of a constant.

### 2 · Creative expression — ⭐⭐

**Ordinary case with no code:** an author places a pump row and it works.
**Bespoke without breaking:** four numbers, so a hand pump, a horse gin, a
water-wheel pump and a steam pump are all **rows**. ⭐ Enormous content
surface for one mechanism — seven named consumers above — which is this
lens's best possible argument, and it says the pump is **substrate**, not
a feature of whoever builds it first.

### 3a · Immersion — ⭐

The limit's existence is what stops pumps being magic. ⭐ An *unexplained*
limit is not a betrayal — the fiction betrays itself when it is
**inconsistent**, not when it is mysterious. Honest mystery is the
intended state before the discovery loop exists.

### 3b · Participation — ⭐⭐⭐

The one that was underrated. A pump is **infrastructure that serves more
than its owner**: your mine's pump keeps the levels *below* yours dry, and
[mining-slate](./mining-slate.md) already frames breaching a sump that
drowns the levels below as murder-by-.

> ⭐⭐ **The pump is the first machine where STOPPING it is an act against
> somebody else.** A weapon, a strike action and a negligence case, with
> nobody authoring a conflict.

*Can the polity do something we did not want?* — yes: levy for a pump,
condition a lease on keeping one running, or shut one off to win an
argument. Who pays for a shared pump is the textbook public-goods problem,
and mining ships an answer already: *he is paid the way the pump is, out
of the hoist toll.*

### 4 · Values — ⭐⭐

Two genuinely undecidable questions: **who pays** for a pump others depend
on, and **may you stop your own pump** when stopping it floods them. Your
property against their survival, with no derivable right answer — which is
exactly lens 4's territory rather than lens 1's. ⭐ And the **hoist toll is
the gauge**: it converts an undecidable choice into a calculable one.

### 5 · Continuity — ⭐⭐⭐

The pump spans **every epoch**: shadoof → screw → hand pump → horse gin →
water wheel → steam → electric. The test passes cleanly — the new object
answers the same command, and only the dynamics (lift, rate, attendance,
cost) change. ⭐ Better than epoch-surviving, it is **epoch-CAUSING**: the
steam engine exists because the pump had a limit. The strongest continuity
case in the backlog.

### 6 · Economy — ⭐⭐⭐

- **Produces:** ⚠ **no good at all** — it produces **access**. Unusual, and
  worth stating: the pump's output is *"the well keeps producing."*
- **Consumes:** power, and ⭐⭐ **seals** — leather packing wears, so
  repacking is **recurring** consumable demand, which this economy is
  short of, and it gives `trade-tanning` a repeat customer rather than a
  one-off sale.
- **Who pays:** the owner, or the commons via a toll.
- **Was the demand there first?** ⭐⭐⭐ **Three waiting customers before
  oil exists** — the mine's water table, the village well, the oil lift.
  Criterion 1 satisfied better than almost anything in the backlog, and
  **non-circularly**, which is the test the rubber slate failed.

### 7 · Governance — ⭐

It judges nobody, but it creates a **duty**: if the levels below depend on
your pump, do you owe them? **Criterion** = dependence; **appeal** = the
courts, which ship; **entrenchment** = **tier C**, the polity's call and
not the code's. Derivative of 3b and 4 rather than its own argument.

### ⭐⭐ Altitude

- **Invariant:** the ~10 m suction limit. It is arithmetic; an author who
  changes it is changing the atmosphere.
- **Grain:** lift, throughput, power source, seal material — per-row
  defaults an author sets freely.
- **Title:** who pays for a shared pump. We ship the hoist toll as **a**
  gauge, never as **the** answer.

## What already ships

- ⚠⚠ **The `pump` verb is TAKEN.** `platform/cmd/device/pump.yaml` is
  **bellows only** — *"Work a furnace's bellows"*, `requires:
  BurnerMixin`. A fluid pump is therefore a **verb collision**, and
  first-come is not one of the seven answers. ⭐ **Lean: the ladder's first
  rung — unify behind an interface.** A bellows and a lift pump are the
  same object (a piston moving a fluid, and **air is a fluid**), so one
  verb over two devices is likely honest rather than a second word.
- **`LiftMixin`** is already named in mining-slate's Left as the hoist's
  mixin — check whether the pump is the same mixin or its sibling.
- **Leather:** `trade-tanning` ships `tanpit`, `tan`, `leatherwork`. The
  seal has a supply chain today.
- **The power ladder's rungs all exist:** `LoadDevice` + the gym (men),
  hitch/unhitch + draft/return (animal), the water pack (water), and
  energy's epoch ladder (steam/electric).
- **Fluid movement:** `bulk.md`'s transfer and drain-through;
  `watershed.md`'s `Conduit` conveyance ladder. ⚠ Three unrelated things
  are already called `Conduit` — name with care.

## Open

1. **Is the pump `LiftMixin`, or its sibling?** A hoist lifts a solid up a
   shaft; a pump lifts a fluid up a pipe. The shared half is *raising
   something against gravity with a power source*; the divergent half is
   everything about containment. ⭐ The usual test: if a guard is needed to
   re-narrow which fields mean anything, they are two mixins.
2. **Does the suction limit need real atmospheric pressure, or a declared
   constant?** One number either way; the question is whether anything
   *else* would consume an atmosphere (weather? altitude? the respiration
   stack?) — if two consumers exist it is a field, if one it is a constant.
3. **Does this build take the governance half, or only the machine?** The
   public-goods problem (3b/4) is the best content here and also the
   largest. ⭐ Lean: ship the machine and the **toll**, which is one
   shipped gauge, and leave the levy and the shut-it-off-to-win-an-argument
   politics to the polity rather than designing them.
4. **Which customer builds it?** The mine fluid pass is the natural host
   (dewatering is the oldest demand and mining already funds it), and
   drilling then inherits a built pump as § *Sequencing* in
   [drilling-slate](./drilling-slate.md) always said it should.
