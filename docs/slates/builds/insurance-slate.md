# Insurance slate — risk pooling as an industry

**Captured 2026-07-31**, named as fire's dependency in
[fire-combustion-slate § The fire service](./fire-combustion-slate.md)
and flagged there as its own industry. Fire is *why* insurance exists;
cargo is where it should start.

Related: [contract.md](../../subsystems/contract.md) (**a policy is a
contract**), [banking.md](../../subsystems/banking.md) (**reserves**),
[freight-slate](./freight-slate.md) (cargo risk),
[farming-slate](./farming-slate.md) (crop risk — the correlated case),
[guild-slate](./guild-slate.md) (**the mutual**),
[sanitation-slate](./sanitation-slate.md) (the lemons family),
[auction-slate](./auction-slate.md) (the winner's-curse sibling),
[vocations.md](../../vocations.md).

## The precondition

Insurance works when losses are **rare, large, and INDEPENDENT.** Rare
and large is why you cannot self-insure; **independent** is what makes
the pool function.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Correlation is the killer — and it is what makes this a business

A conflagration takes the whole block, so an insurer with every policy
on one street is **insolvent by design.**

> **The insurer's book is a map, and concentration is the risk.**

Write everything in Wharfside and one bad night ends you. **Portfolio
construction — not pricing individual policies — is the real skill**,
and in a world with honest geography it is **checkable**: you can look
at where the book is.

## ⭐⭐⭐ Adverse selection is the lemons problem, third instance

The people most likely to claim are the most likely to buy; price for
the average and the good risks leave, degrading the pool.

Which completes a pattern:

| Market | Asymmetry between | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| second-hand | **buyer ↔ seller** | the **appraiser** |
| auction | **bidder ↔ bidder** | the **assayer** |
| **insurance** | **insurer ↔ insured** | the **underwriter** |

> **Information asymmetry has produced three markets and ONE vocation
> family** — somebody with an instrument who can assess.

## ⭐⭐⭐ A policy is the first contract that is NOT fully escrowed

The structural break. Every contract so far escrows what it promises —
a gig locks the fee, an auction locks the bid. **A policy cannot**,
because pooling means **covering more than you hold.** That is the
entire point.

So the replacement for escrow is a **reserve ratio** — and because the
counterparty **cannot verify it themselves**, it must be **regulated**:

> **Insurance is where the contract substrate meets banking**, and the
> reason solvency regulation exists at all.

Which makes it **the most regulated industry in the register** for a
non-moralizing reason: of the four failure modes — **adverse
selection**, **moral hazard**, **fraud**, **insolvency** — only the last
takes everyone else down with it.

## ⭐ Build cargo first, because its risks are independent

Marine insurance preceded fire insurance historically (Lloyd's coffee
house) for exactly this reason: **one wagon robbed does not mean all
wagons are.** The pool works.

| Product | Risk shape | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **cargo** | **independent** | a functioning private market — **start here** |
| **fire** | **block-correlated** | needs geographic diversification |
| **crops** | **region-correlated** | see below |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Crop insurance is the correlated-risk case, and it is why the state does it

**You cannot diversify away a drought** — every harvest fails at once.
So either the polity backs it or **nobody offers it**, which is
precisely why real crop insurance is government-backed almost
everywhere. **That lesson lands directly on the farming build.**

## ⭐⭐ Mutual vs. stock — and the mutual is the guild's economic instrument

- **mutual** — the policyholders **are** the company; surplus returns to
  them;
- **stock** — investors own it and keep the profit.

The mutual came first historically, and **guilds ran exactly this**:
funeral funds, sickness funds, mutual aid. Which gives guilds something
they currently lack —

> **A concrete economic function beyond identity.** A guild that insures
> its members is doing what guilds actually did.

And it is the **cooperative** form arriving in the *economy* rather than
in the constitution.

## ⭐⭐⭐ Insurance prices prevention, so the fire code enforces itself

Your premium depends on your firebreak, your extinguisher, your
construction — and the insurer **surveys before writing** (the
inspector, third instance).

> **The insurer is a private regulator, and a more effective one than
> the state, because it holds the money.**

You need no public inspection if nobody will cover you without it —
historically exact (insurers demanded fire safety long before
regulators did), and it closes the fire loop: **prevention stops being
a chore because it is a discount.**

## The state's ladder

**unregulated** (caveat emptor) → **solvency-regulated** (a reserve
`parameter`) → **state-backed** (catastrophe and crop pools) →
**state-provided** (social insurance). Another **Tiebout** dial, each
rung a real policy position.

## Vocations

**underwriter** (assesses and prices) · **adjuster** (investigates
claims — the fire investigator's sibling) · **broker** (matches risk to
capital) · **surveyor** (inspects before writing).

⚠ **Life insurance is deferred** — it sits awkwardly with a death model
where players do not stay dead. It works for NPCs and dependents; do not
bend the mortality design around it.

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐ What insurance actually is in a simulated economy

**(User, 2026-07-31 — the sharpest question in the thread: *"the actual
cost of restoring it is zero real cost… the actuarials say whatever we
want them to say until there's enough history to disagree with a
lie."*)**

### Where the fiction is NOT arbitrary

The **substrate** cost of restoring a building is zero. The **in-world**
cost is not, if the world is honest: rebuilding consumes **materials**
that were extracted and **labour** that was spent, which trace back to
the one genuinely irreducible resource —

> **Player time.** Every in-world price is ultimately denominated in
> extraction-hours. **The tokens are a fiction; the hours are not.**

And the discipline is load-bearing: **if restoration were free, nothing
would have value — including the things insurance protects.** Free
rebuilds dissolve the endogenous-value spine.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Insurance is a TRANSFER, not a sink — the FIRE is the sink

If claims are paid from premiums collected, **nothing left
circulation**: money moved from many players to one. The wealth
destroyed was **the building**, and re-creating it consumes materials
that must be re-extracted.

> **The sink is the destruction. Insurance only decides who feels it.**

Which **validates the instinct, relocated**: *if you need to drain the
economy, destroy things.* Insurance is **the shock absorber, not the
drain.** Using it as a drain would need an insurer that collects and
does not pay (fraud) or payouts that vanish money (breaking conservation
accounting).

- ⭐⭐ **The real adjacent lever is the insurer's MARGIN.** Premiums
  exceed expected payouts — so **an NPC insurer's margin is a genuine
  sink; a player insurer's is a transfer.** Same shape as NPC shops, and
  a clean dial.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The lossy salvage loop and fire are the same sink at two
  speeds.** Depreciation is slow destruction; fire is fast
  depreciation. **Destruction is the honest sink in any economy** — fees
  and taxes are transfers wearing a sink's clothes.

### ⭐⭐ The genuine weirdness

> **Insurance is the first product whose price is a claim about the
> FUTURE rather than a fact about the PRESENT.**

A sword's price is anchored by materials and labour you can see. A
premium is anchored by a statistical assertion **nobody can check
today** — bounded below by expected loss and above by what risk
aversion will bear, but unobservable from the product.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The actuarials — right, and the record is what disciplines them

*"They say whatever we want until there is enough history to disagree"*
is exactly right — **and we keep append-only ledgers.** Fires, thefts,
wrecks and deaths are all events in the record. So **loss rates are
derivable by anyone**:

- the underwriter's edge is **not secret data** but **better reading of
  public data**;
- **a bad actuary is provably bad, after the fact**;
- and **the press can compute it** — *"the loss rate in Wharfside is X
  and they are charging for Y."*

> **The record makes actuarial science playable.** Most games cannot do
> this because they keep no honest history. **Truth arrives on a
> schedule.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ And therefore: insurance is a GAUGE, not a lever

> **You do not balance insurance. You balance the WORLD, and insurance
> reports it.**

Set the hazard rates — ignition probability, spoilage, banditry
frequency — and **premiums discover them automatically.** The lever
lives at the physics layer where it belongs.

Which is *more* valuable than a lever: **a premium climbing in one
district is the economy telling you something you did not know.**

## Open questions (for requirements)

1. **Reserve ratio shape** — a flat fraction of exposure, or
   risk-weighted? (Flat first; risk-weighting needs the loss history
   that does not exist yet.)
2. **Who audits solvency** — the inspector post, or is it derivable
   from the bank ledger by anyone? (Instinct: **derivable**, per the
   record doctrine — then the inspector merely *enforces*.)
3. **Does a policy transfer with the property?** (Real answer: usually
   not — which is a nice friction on property sales.)
4. **Claim adjudication** — an adjuster's call, appealable to the
   courts? (Yes, and it is a good small civil case.)
5. **Reinsurance** — the honest answer to correlation, but probably a
   later wave. Name it; do not build it.
6. **Premium payment cadence** vs. the game clock — real-time or
   game-time? (Follow the wage precedent.)

# Banking as a vocation, credit, and ratings

**Captured 2026-07-31**, immediately after the insurance pass. Kept in
this slate because credit, insurance and ratings are one information
economy — and because they share the same vocation family. Split into
its own slate if it outgrows this.

Related: [banking.md](../../subsystems/banking.md) (**shipped
infrastructure**), [terminus-banking](../../staging/terminus-banking.md)
(five corpo banks, locked), [livelihood-slate](./livelihood-slate.md)
(the WPA floor), [sanitation-slate](./sanitation-slate.md) (the
pawnshop).

## ⭐⭐ The cut: custody and payments are a MENU; credit is the VOCATION

Banking already ships as **infrastructure** — two-tier money, the
sealed `postTransaction` chokepoint, custodial accounts,
`Account.ISSUANCE` as the only mint source. **The vocation does not
exist.** Same pattern as fire: the physics is there and nobody works in
it.

**You do not want a player standing between you and your own money.**
Holding and settling are *utility* functions and stay automatic.
**Lending is where the judgment lives, and therefore where the job is.**

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The rule the whole register has been circling

Assessing a borrower is **adverse selection** — the borrower knows
their prospects, the lender does not. **Fourth instance:**

| Market | Asymmetry | Vocation |
|---|---|---|
| second-hand | condition | **appraiser** |
| auction | value between bidders | **assayer** |
| insurance | risk | **underwriter** |
| **banking** | **creditworthiness** | **the banker** |
| **credit reporting** | **prospects** | **the credit surveyor** *(below)* |

> **If both sides know the same things, build a MENU. If one side knows
> more, build a VOCATION.**

A general test — and it **explains** the gated-capability criterion
rather than sitting beside it: **the gate is almost always an epistemic
advantage.** Information asymmetry is the most productive generator in
the design.

**⭐⭐ And unsecured credit is where REPUTATION BECOMES MONEY.** Secured
lending exists twice already (the turnpike bond on toll revenue, the
pawn ticket on a chattel); lending on *nothing but the person* gives
renown a **direct financial consequence** instead of a social one —
which is what a credit score is.

## ⭐⭐⭐ Maturity transformation → the bank run

Banks borrow short (deposits, on demand) and lend long. That fragility
produces the subsystem's best mechanic:

> **A run is a coordination failure that becomes true by being
> believed.** The bank is solvent but not liquid; everyone withdraws at
> once; it fails *because* people expected it to.

Same shape as a conviction crossing or an auction ring. Genuinely
social content, and Diamond–Dybvig (Nobel 2022). Plus:

**⭐ A newspaper can break a bank** — truthfully or otherwise, which
makes the press's credibility **economically** load-bearing.

**⭐⭐⭐ And the run is what finally gives the shipped
`central-bank-governor` seat something to do.** One of the five
`OFFICE_APPARATUS` offices currently administers almost nothing;
**lender of last resort is its actual job.** The three remedies are all
legislatable: a **reserve requirement** (`parameter`), **deposit
insurance** (⭐ insurance again — *state-provided*, the top rung of the
ladder above), and the **lender of last resort** (the office).

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐ "May banks create money?" — as a bill

In real fractional-reserve banking **lending creates deposits**, the
most counterintuitive fact in monetary economics. It appears to collide
with the conservation chokepoint. It does not:

> **The chokepoint mints on loan origination and burns on repayment.**
> Creation stays chokepointed and logged; it merely stops being
> CB-exclusive.

So the invariant survives and **full-reserve vs. fractional-reserve
becomes a legislated choice** — the Chicago Plan, positive-money, a live
argument among real economists — with **visible consequences**:
fractional reserve grows the economy *and makes runs possible*; full
reserve is stable *and starves credit*.

Possibly the strongest single educational artifact in the economic
chain, and it is a **`parameter` clause**.

**And usury caps** are the oldest law there is, religiously charged, and
already flagged as what decides whether the pawnbroker is humane or
predatory.

## The bootstrap ladder — how credit stands up from nothing

**Collateral before reputation:**

> **pawn** (needs **no** history — the chattel *is* the assessment) →
> **microloan** (small; its purpose is to **manufacture** history) →
> **unsecured** (reputation) → **large secured** (mortgages, business
> credit)

**⭐⭐ Which makes the pawnshop the credit bootstrap**, and it is already
in the design: works day one, needs no infrastructure, and **generates
the world's first repayment records.** The poor person's bank is the
world's first bank.

### ⚠ Correction: credit does NOT replace welfare

> **Credit substitutes for SAVINGS, not for INCOME.** Welfare covers
> *having no income*; credit covers *income arriving later than the
> need*.

Someone with no income cannot service a loan, and lending to them anyway
**is** the predatory-lending failure:

> **Conflating the two is how you get payday lending** — which is what
> appears when welfare is inadequate and credit is the only thing on
> offer.

So the shape is **welfare as a floor that persists + credit as a ladder
on top**, never welfare *then* credit. And the floor partly exists: the
**onboarding coin is a CB mint** categorised `onboarding` — *that is
welfare* — with the **WPA public-works floor** as its ongoing form.

## ⭐⭐⭐ Ratings agencies own a METHOD, not data

Because the ledger is public and append-only, **credit history is
derivable by anyone.** Which **inverts** the real-world structure:

| | Real world | Here |
|---|---|---|
| the moat | **privileged data** + an opaque model | **none** — the data is public |
| the product | a number nobody can check | **a published judgment under your name** |
| competition | for **issuer fees** | between **methods** |

**The agency's only asset is its reputation, and a bad rating is
falsifiable.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ "Isn't it just software?" — the rule is the record's boundary

> **Automate what the record already knows. Make a vocation of what it
> does not.**

The ledger gives **payment history** free. It does **not** give
**prospects** — is the shop busy, is stock moving, is the owner
competent. **Someone has to go and look.**

That is literally how the trade began: **Dun & Bradstreet started as
correspondents who visited businesses and wrote reports.** The **credit
surveyor** is the fifth instance of the assessor family.

**⭐ Plus the standard lending shape: the score is software, THE
EXCEPTION IS THE JOB.** Automated decisioning handles the routine; the
referral queue — no history, changed circumstances, a fraud pattern — is
the roleplay. Exactly how real lending runs.

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The mortgage-crisis trap, and the defence only this world has

The real failure was **issuer-pays**: the issuer picks *and* pays the
agency, so agencies compete by being generous and issuers shop for the
best grade. **Nobody who relies on the rating pays for it.**

Three defences:

1. **Investor-pays / subscriber-pays** — the reader pays (the original
   model; undermined by free-riding, since ratings leak).
2. ⭐ **No regulatory mandate.** Much of the crisis came from
   *regulations requiring* ratings, which created captive demand and
   made agencies quasi-official. **Never let law require a rating** —
   that is what manufactures the rent.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **The record makes a shilled rating FALSIFIABLE.** *"Agency X
   rated these forty loans top-grade; twelve defaulted; the base rate
   was four percent."* **The press can compute that.** Real agencies
   escaped accountability because verification was expensive; here it
   is a query.

> **You cannot fix the conflict of interest. You can make acting on it
> visibly expensive.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Which is the general anti-corruption mechanism in this economy

The same one as tamper-evidence's *detectable, not impossible*, and the
same as *a bad actuary is provably bad after the fact*:

> **Three subsystems, one mechanism: the record does not prevent
> misconduct — it makes misconduct DISCOVERABLE.**

## Chartering and competition

**⭐⭐ Charter for accountability, never for exclusivity.** Anyone may
publish a rating; a charter means accepting an **auditing obligation**,
not receiving a monopoly. *Exclusive designation is what created the
captive demand.*

> **Competition is healthy when the payer is the READER, and toxic when
> the payer is the RATED.**

And **structural separation** applies again: an agency that also lends
or underwrites has a conflict — the same remedy the vertical-integration
case wanted.

## ⭐⭐ A fifth kind of market power

Rungs: **moneylender** (own capital) → **the bank** (deposits, a
charter) → **the corpo bank** (a network; the terminus design already
locks five).

That top rung is none of the four monopoly shapes catalogued in the
freight chain:

> **Power from being UNFAILABLE.** Too-big-to-fail is not geography, nor
> a network effect, nor vertical integration — it is the implicit
> guarantee that your collapse hurts everyone, so you will be rescued.

**Different remedy too: capital requirements and a resolution
authority**, not rate caps or non-discrimination. The most modern
politics in the economic chain.

## Vocations

**loan officer / credit assessor** · **credit surveyor** (the one who
goes and looks) · **money changer** (coinage ships; with clipping and
debasement, **assaying coin** is a skill) · **accountant** (books, which
the record makes checkable — press material *and* fraud material) ·
**collector** — ⭐ where a **private** claim needs **public**
enforcement, since you cannot simply take their goods; **likely the
courts' second-most-common civil case after nuisance** · **teller** (the
attendant shape — honest entry-level texture rather than depth).

## The ratings agency as a business

**Eighth business in the economic chain**, and categorically different
from all seven.

### ⭐⭐ The first pure information business

The turnpike owns an asset, the depot holds goods, salvage transforms
material, the auction house at least *possesses* lots temporarily, the
bank holds money. **A ratings agency owns nothing, moves nothing,
transforms nothing.**

> **Its entire product is an opinion someone will act on.**

Which makes the **revenue question** the design question.

### ⭐⭐ Timeliness is the product, not the number

Once published, a rating is **public and unownable** — the record is
public, so anyone can recompute it. What you sell is **first**, and
**the detail behind it** (how real financial-information businesses
work: the terminal, the research subscription).

| Product | Economics |
|---|---|
| **the published rating** | a **reputation asset** — monetised by **subscription + timeliness** |
| **the bespoke report** (go and look at *this* counterparty) | **a service**, per job |

> **The published rating builds the name; the bespoke report pays the
> bills.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The fix for issuer-pays is not WHO PAYS — it is WHETHER YOU CAN SUPPRESS

Ratings shopping works because the issuer commissions three agencies and
publishes the best. So:

> **Charter obligation: if you commission a rating, IT PUBLISHES —
> whatever it says.**

That kills shopping **without banning the revenue model**, and it aims
at the actual mechanism rather than at the money. Real regulators
circled this (unsolicited ratings, shopping disclosure) and never quite
landed it.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Low barriers, high accountability — the inverse of the real industry

Because the data is public and the track record computable:

- **entry is trivial** — anyone with an opinion may publish a rating;
- **survival is hard** — your errors are **arithmetic**, and the press
  can print them.

Real ratings had it backwards: **high barriers** (official designation)
and **nil accountability**. This inverts both — **a direct consequence
of the record**, not a rule anyone enforces.

### ⭐⭐⭐ And it is the same business as the press

Both sell **judgments under a name**; both are worth exactly what their
track record says; both move the world **by being believed.**

> **The ratings agency is the press with different subject matter.**

So [press-slate](./press-slate.md)'s machinery — credibility derived
from the record, the publisher, the catalogue-as-publication —
**applies unchanged. No new reputation mechanic needed.**

### Failure modes — one of them genuinely nasty

- ⭐⭐⭐ **A rating can CAUSE the outcome it predicts.** Downgrade a bank
  and trigger the run that justifies the downgrade. **Reflexivity** —
  real (sovereign downgrades) and **mechanically true** in a world that
  has bank runs. It gives *power from being believed* real teeth, and
  it is a legitimately hard thing for a player to sit with.
- ⭐⭐ **Model capture** — if everyone uses the same method, everyone is
  wrong together (the crisis's deeper failure). **Method diversity is
  the systemic defence** — another argument against chartering
  exclusivity: **you WANT disagreeing agencies.**
- ⭐⭐ **False precision** — a letter grade hides uncertainty. Ratings
  should carry **confidence**, and confidence is **computable from
  sample size**: three loans is not three hundred.

  > **A new borrower cannot have a reliable rating, and an honest agency
  > says so rather than guessing.**

  Which protects new players from being mis-rated — and closes the loop:
  **the record's thinness is exactly where the surveyor is needed.**

### ⭐⭐ Method disclosure is a STRATEGIC CHOICE, not a mandate

Publish your method → **reproducible and trusted, and copyable.**
Conceal it → **distinctive, and doubted.** A genuine business decision
worth leaving open rather than legislating.

### Rungs

**the individual with an opinion** (costs nothing — a player who reads
the ledger well can simply publish) → **the agency** (surveyors,
subscribers, a method) → **the incumbent whose word moves markets.**

That top rung is again **not** one of the monopoly shapes from the
freight chain:

> **Power from being believed** — the same as the press's, and it
> **decays the moment the record disagrees with you.**

## The accountant (and the auditor) as a business

**Ninth business in the chain** — and it answers *"isn't this just
software?"* harder than ratings did, because unlike prospects, **the
transactions genuinely are all recorded.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ The ledger is CASH; accounting is ACCRUAL

The ledger knows money moved. It does **not** know:

- **what a transaction was FOR** — the same coin leaving your account is
  a wage, a bribe, a loan repayment, or a gift;
- **when revenue was EARNED** vs. received;
- **when a cost was INCURRED** vs. paid;
- **how to spread an asset's cost across its life.**

**Judgments, not facts** — and the entire intellectual content of the
trade. The rule holds again: **automate the cash, make a vocation of the
accrual.**

It also yields genuinely good business states: **profitable but
insolvent**, and **cash-rich but losing money** — both real, both
visible only on an accrual basis.

**⭐⭐ And depreciation is the accountant's native object, which we
already have PHYSICALLY.** Condition decay **is** depreciation:

> **The books are the story you tell about a physical fact in money
> terms** — and straight-line vs. accelerated are **both defensible
> stories.** That is exactly where the judgment lives.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The categorical difference from ratings

> **A rating is about SOMEBODY ELSE. Accounts are about YOURSELF.**

An agency assesses a third party; an accountant states *your own*
position for others to rely on. **Self-report is a fundamentally
different trust problem** — which is precisely why **audit** exists as a
separate function.

**Two vocations, and they must not be one firm:**

| | Works for | Product |
|---|---|---|
| **accountant** | **you** | prepares the accounts |
| **auditor** | **those relying on you** | attests they are true |

*Enron and Andersen is the case*: the auditor was also the consultant,
so the fees came from the audited.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Audit has the IDENTICAL disease to ratings — which generalizes

The audited party picks and pays the auditor: **issuer-pays with a
different hat.** Same two cures — **mandatory publication** (*if you
commission it, it publishes*) and **structural separation.**

Which is now a **class**, not two coincidences:

> **ANY ASSESSMENT PAID FOR BY THE ASSESSED HAS ONE CONFLICT AND TWO
> REMEDIES.**

Ratings · audits · insurance surveys · building inspections ·
appraisals. **One conflict, one remedy pair** — worth stating once in
[vocations.md](../../vocations.md) rather than rediscovering per
industry.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Demand comes from the BANKER, not the business owner

What makes the vocation pass the register's demand test. **Nobody hires
an accountant because they enjoy tidy numbers.** They hire one because
the **bank** will not lend without statements, the **insurer** will not
write without them, a **buyer** will not purchase on a shoebox of
receipts, and the **revenue** wants a return.

> **The accountant sells access to credit.** Good books are a business
> asset — clean audited accounts borrow cheaply; a shoebox does not.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Which resolves the privacy question properly

All-public transactions = no financial privacy *and* no interesting
business. All-private = the real world, where fraud is undetectable. The
[record-integrity slate](./record-integrity-slate.md) already has the
answer:

> **The ledger's INTEGRITY is public; its CONTENTS are gated.**
> Seal-don't-hide — prove the books balance without revealing every
> line.

**And that is exactly what an audit IS: a trusted party sees the private
detail and publishes a public attestation.** So accounting and audit are
**the business face of the record-integrity design**, not a separate
mechanism.

### Fraud — and how you finally convict the boss

Cooking the books means **the accounts are a CLAIM and the ledger is
GROUND TRUTH**, so a false statement contradicts a record that exists —
the [enforcement slate](./enforcement-slate.md)'s **true /
honest-error / lie** triad applied to financial statements. *Honest
error is genuinely common here*, since accrual judgments differ in good
faith.

> **⭐⭐ And forensic accounting answers the
> [policing slate](./policing-slate.md)'s boss-insulation problem.**
> That slate notes the boss stays clean because the ledger records
> *acts and actors*, and asks for conspiracy law. **Following the money
> is the other answer — they got Capone on tax evasion.**

The **forensic accountant** is the detective's financial sibling, and
the route to the untouchable **that requires nobody to testify.**

### Tax, and the rungs

**Self-assessment plus audit** is the better system *and* the better
content: you file, and you **might** be checked — which makes evasion a
playable choice with real risk, and gives the auditor a public-sector
sibling, **the revenue inspector.**

**Rungs:** **bookkeeper** (records) → **accountant** (prepares) →
**auditor** (attests) → **the firm.**

And the auditor's power is, for the **third** time in this chain,
**being believed** — the same as the press and the ratings agency, and
it evaporates the same way.

## The notary — the register's first honest NO

**Tenth in the chain, and the result is negative.** Kept because a
rejection is worth more than an addition.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Most of the notary is already free

| What a notary sells | What we already have |
|---|---|
| **verify identity** | the kernel **knows who acted** |
| **witness execution** | the record **witnesses everything** |
| **attest it happened** | the ledger **IS** the attestation |
| **non-repudiation** | append-only — **you cannot deny an act** |

So the stamp-and-seal (common-law) notary does work the substrate does
for nothing. By **criterion 2** — *if anyone can do it, it is a chore,
not a job* — **it is not a vocation here.**

> **The register's first honest NO.** A register that only *adds* roles
> is a wishlist; one that can reject a real-world job because the
> substrate already provides it is a **tool**. **The demand test
> worked.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The disposal technique this yields (add to the method)

> **When a real-world vocation does not fit, DECOMPOSE it — the parts
> land in different places.** Never accept or reject wholesale.

The notary decomposes into four; **one is a vocation, one is
constitutional, one is a friction mechanic, one is free.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Drafting is the real vocation — THE SCRIVENER

The **civil-law** notary, not the common-law one, and it survives for a
reason specific to this world:

> **Clauses are machine-enforceable. So a badly drafted clause executes
> exactly as written — which is not what you meant.**

A genuine, discoverable, consequential mistake — the same fork
[legal-code-slate](./legal-code-slate.md) established (clauses execute;
prose is judgment-only). Writing an instrument that **does what you
intended** is a real skill with a real failure mode: **criteria 2 and 5,
both satisfied.**

**⭐⭐ And it pairs with the advocate:**

> **The advocate argues AFTER the dispute. The scrivener writes
> BEFORE it.**

Two halves of legal practice — and **this world makes the second more
important than real life does**, because here the clause actually runs.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Cross-jurisdiction recognition is real — but CONSTITUTIONAL, not notarial

Does a Terminus deed mean anything in Hinkley Hills? A judgment? A
marriage? A charter?

That is **full faith and credit** — an
[amendment-library](./amendment-library-slate.md) entry, **not a job.**
The *feeling* lands at a counter (somebody certifies your foreign
instrument), but the mechanism is a **registrar's office**, already in
the civic list.

⭐ **With a suburb next door it stops being theoretical: the first
cross-border deed is the test case.**

### ⭐⭐ Ceremony survives — as FRICTION, not as a person

The one thing notarization does that verification does not: **it makes
you stop.** The formality is a **speed bump on purpose** — *this is
serious enough to require effort.*

> **Formality as friction is a legitimate design tool for irreversible
> acts** — selling your home, binding yourself to a long contract,
> naming an heir.

A witnessed act, at a place, with a wait, is good design for things you
cannot undo, and it makes a **location** matter.

⚠ **But the ceremonial witness is texture, not depth** — the teller's
tier. **No judgment in it**, so it is a job you can hold, never a craft
you can be good at.

### Where that leaves it

> **Kill the notary. Ship the scrivener.** Route cross-jurisdiction
> recognition to the amendment library as **full faith and credit**, and
> keep the ceremony as a **friction mechanic on irreversible acts**
> rather than as a role.

## The innkeeper — CONDITIONAL, and the condition is NPC schedules

**Eleventh in the chain, and the first entry the register should record
as *conditional* rather than yes or no.**

### ⚠ The objection that killed the first draft

**(User, 2026-07-31: "players don't *need* to sleep and the day/night
cycle is purely decorative. The exception is NPCs I guess.")**

The first pass justified the inn with sleep and recovery. **That is
backwards** — inventing a sleep *need* to justify a vocation is
**manufacturing demand**, which is criterion 1 failing in disguise:

> **⭐⭐ NEVER INVENT A NEED TO CREATE A MARKET.** If a vocation requires
> a new player obligation in order to exist, the demand was fabricated.

Belongs in [vocations.md](../../vocations.md) beside the odometer rule.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The real function: the bed is a LOGOUT LOCATION

Not a sleep mechanic. The question is not *"what does sleeping do"* but
**"what happens to your body while you are gone, and where should it
be"** — which
[presence-hollowing-slate](./presence-hollowing-slate.md) presumably
owns.

| Where you log out | Cost | Safe? |
|---|---|---|
| **your own home** | free | yes |
| **an inn** | **paid** | yes |
| **the roadside** | free | **no** |

**The inn occupies exactly one cell: away from home** — and demand
exists **only if the third row has a cost.**

⚠ **That cost must be INCONVENIENCE, NOT LOSS**: you wake where you left
off, but without the recovery, goods possibly pilfered, the team
wandered. **Never "you died offline"** — the same rail as the fire
pass.

**But be honest: this justifies a BED, not an INNKEEPER.** It is a thin
reason for a vocation.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The strong answer is the NPC exception, inverted

NPCs **are the world's population**, so:

> **The day/night cycle is not decorative if the NPCs obey it. Their
> schedule IS the world's schedule.**

A player logging in at 03:00 plays a **different game**: shops shut,
offices closed, streets empty, the watch on patrol — and **concealment
suddenly mattering**, because night is when you move unseen.

And in that world:

> **The inn is where the world is still awake.**

That is the demand, and it has **nothing to do with beds.** It is what
inns were actually for — **the common room, not the mattress.** It gives
the inn a function no other building has, it serves everyone who plays
at odd hours, and it is **cheap**: the behavior substrate already has
**cadence-triggered brains**, so a work/home/sleep schedule is an
extension, not a new system.

⭐ **And it makes the day/night cycle load-bearing retroactively** —
better than leaving it decorative *or* bolting a sleep meter onto
players.

### The rest of the business (justification, not foundation)

Once the inn exists for the reason above, these become its *other*
products rather than its excuse:

- ⭐⭐⭐ **It sells LIABILITY, not lodging.** **Innkeeper's strict
  liability** is one of the oldest rules in common law — the innkeeper
  answers for guests' goods almost absolutely, because you are helpless
  asleep and inns were historically in league with thieves. **The fifth
  bailee** (warehouse · impound · auction house · bank · inn). *You pay
  for the guarantee, not the mattress.* And the classic con is
  **detectable**: chattel chain-of-title makes *"goods keep going
  missing at the Bell"* **a provable pattern across guests.**
- ⭐⭐ **Weather sets the demand curve for free** — in summer you camp,
  in winter you pay, and **in cold the real product is the fire, not the
  bed.** Thermal already ships.
- ⭐ **Recovery is a rate multiplier, never a gate.** Sleeping rough is
  always allowed; it is just worse.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The posting inn is fast travel that is not teleportation** —
  **swap a tired team for a rested one and pay the difference**, which
  is what actually made pre-rail speed possible. A **player-built**
  middle tier between walking and the TPA that **does not touch the
  capacity line**: goods still move at wagon speed, only the *team*
  changes.
- ⭐⭐ **The inn is the multiplayer surface of the road.** Roads are
  lonely; everyone stopping at the same waypoint means **strangers
  meet** — rare and valuable where players otherwise cluster in cities,
  and where news gathers for the press.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The guest register is surveillance you can legislate.** Inns
  have always been licensed because they **harbour strangers**;
  requiring a register is how states track movement. A real
  civil-liberties object on the **camera-vs-witness** axis: a locality
  requiring registers catches more fugitives and **repels more
  travellers**.

### Rungs, and where the skill is

**taking in a lodger** (a spare room — ⚠ *adjacent to the live
residences build; name it, leave it to them*) → **the inn** → **the
coaching inn** with stabling and posting → **the chain**.

⚠ Honest about the gate: **capital and reputation, not craft.** The
innkeeper is a **proprietor**, not an artisan — so **the staff are where
the vocations are**, and the skilled one is **the ostler**, who keeps
the teams sound. Husbandry-gated, and the job the freight economy
actually depends on.

### ⭐ The verdict: conditional

> **The inn is downstream of whether the world has a rhythm.**

- **NPCs get schedules** → the inn is the place that stays open when
  nothing else does. **A real vocation**, and everything above becomes
  its business.
- **NPCs do not** → **the inn is decorative** and belongs in **content**
  — a nice room in a town — **not in the register.**

**Record it as conditional with the condition named. More useful than
either a yes or a no.**

> ⭐ **CONDITION DESIGNED (2026-07-31) →
> [npc-behavior-slate § NPC schedules](./npc-behavior-slate.md).**
> *Derive the crowd, simulate the cast* — and **the schedule turns out
> to already exist: it is the employment SHIFT ROSTER**, so a shop is
> shut because nobody is rostered, not because of a new system. Which
> makes **opening hours a business strategy** and the inn's *"we are the
> one place open"* a rostering choice. ⚠ And the **12× clock** sharpens
> it: **night is a PULSE, not a demographic** — everyone gets it every
> session, which is *stronger* demand, but it means closures must be
> staggered and **the inn is the always-open fallback**. So the
> innkeeper is justified from the **tuning** side too. **Condition met.**

## The veterinarian — not a medic for animals

**Twelfth in the chain.** Passes the register's test cleanly; the useful
question is **why it is not simply the medic with a different target.**
Four genuine differences.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The patient cannot tell you what is wrong

A player patient can describe symptoms; an animal cannot. Veterinary
diagnosis is **purely observational** — nothing to lean on but what you
can perceive and measure.

> **The vet is the purest instance of *competence buys information*.**

No self-report, no testimony, no history unless the owner remembers.
**The instrumentation thesis with the crutch removed** — which makes the
vet **mechanically** different from the medic, not just
target-different.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The economics ARE the practice

A person is treated because they are a person. **An animal is treated
iff treatment costs less than replacement.**

> **The vet's real work is a cost-benefit calculation — and sometimes
> the answer is to destroy the animal.**

Brutal, honest, playable: **a lame ox is an economic decision**, and it
is *the* defining fact of large-animal practice.

**⭐⭐⭐ Which splits it into two practices, two value systems, one
skill:**

| Practice | The animal is | Decisions are |
|---|---|---|
| **livestock** | **capital** | economic, herd-level |
| **companion** | **a relationship** | emotional, individual |

Precisely the [ranching](./ranching-slate.md) and
[pets](./pets-slate.md) consumers — a clean fit with the husbandry
line that it is **one care model, three outputs.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The product is PREVENTION, not cure

Most livestock practice is **population-scale prevention** — parasite
control, biosecurity, condition management. The disease thread already
says it: **good husbandry is immunity**, and prevention is the
unexplored half.

**Third appearance of the same shape today:**

> **The mature form of every risk profession is PREVENTION. The
> emergency is the failure case.**

**Insurance PRICES prevention; the fire code MANDATES it; the vet
PRACTISES it.** So the vet's routine work is inspection and advice, and
the sick animal is **what happened because nobody called earlier.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The public face — and the biggest lever in the register

Animal disease is a **public** problem: it spreads between herds, and
some of it reaches people. So the vet has a **regulatory** face:

- **The meat inspector is a vet.** Somebody certifies what leaves the
  abattoir, and **condemnation is a `directive`** — it cannot
  self-execute, so it **forces inspection into existence**, exactly like
  the turnpike's repair obligation. That puts a **professional inside
  the stockyard fight** [zoning](./zoning-slate.md) and
  [freight](./freight-slate.md) already set up.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **And the vet is the only vocation that can SHUT THE FREIGHT
  NETWORK.** Quarantine and movement controls are legitimate
  **barricade** uses (already designed), so **an outbreak closes the
  roads** — historically exact (foot-and-mouth closed Britain),
  enormous economic power, and it makes **one professional's judgment
  consequential in a way a medic's never is.**

### ⭐⭐ And the death is real

Euthanasia is part of the job, and it is **mercy, not failure** — the
opposite of the medic, for whom death **is** the failure state. Players
do not stay dead; animals presumably do:

> **The animal's death is real in a way the player's is not.**

That asymmetry is what gives **pets genuine stakes**, and it makes the
vet the person you call when it matters most.

### Rungs, and one distinction worth keeping

**the husbandman treating their own** (self-serve — *not* a vocation) →
**the horse doctor** (a practical trade, no formal training) → **the
veterinarian** (competence-gated) → **the inspector** (a public office).

**⭐⭐ The demand sits in the gap** between routine care anyone can do
and hard cases requiring competence — the same shape as the
accountant's routine bookkeeping vs. accrual judgment.

⚠ **Keep the FARRIER separate: shoeing is a CRAFT, not medicine** —
smithing applied to horses, in a crafting branch. *The farrier makes and
fits; the vet diagnoses and treats.* **Bad shoeing laming a horse is a
crafting failure, not a medical one.**

## The ostler — and the first COMPLEMENTS pair

**Thirteenth in the chain**, and worth testing honestly because the
first reading says it may not qualify.

### ⚠ The honest first reading: staff, not a vocation

A teamster can unhitch, water and feed their own team. The stable, the
fodder and the yard are **the innkeeper's capital, not the ostler's
craft.** On that reading the ostler is the teller — a wage job.

### ⭐⭐⭐ What survives: quality is invisible at the point of sale

A badly-kept team does not fail tonight. It **goes lame two towns on.**
Feed, water, grooming, feet checked before a stone becomes an abscess —
**none of it observable when you hand over the reins.**

> **Sixth instance of the lemons problem.** You cannot tell a good
> stable from a bad one by looking.

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Which makes the STABLE's reputation load-bearing, not the
beds'** — a teamster returns to the yard that kept their team sound, and
that repeat-business loop on unobservable quality is **exactly how
coaching inns competed.**

### ⭐⭐ It passes — but only at the top rung

**Stable-hand** = a chore. **Master ostler** running a posting stable =
a proprietor with real judgment: which horses are fit, which need a day,
what the fleet can turn tomorrow. **Inventory management of living
assets with condition variance** is a genuine business skill.

Which yields a register-level finding:

> **⭐⭐ Most vocations are LADDERS where only the upper rungs qualify.**
> The bottom of every trade is a chore you do for wages.

A useful brake — it stops the register inflating job titles into
professions, and it is why **the teller, the ceremonial witness and the
stable-hand** all belong as **texture under** a real vocation rather
than beside it.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Road capacity is STABLE capacity

You can build the finest turnpike in the realm and it moves nothing
without fresh teams. A posting stable with six horses turns six changes
a day.

> **The stable is the bottleneck. The posting network is a CAPACITY
> network, not a speed network.**

Same congestion shape as the tollgate queue and the depot's two
capacities — and it means **the freight network's real throughput is set
by animals, not roads.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ And the road and the stable are COMPLEMENTS — the first pair in the chain

Every business so far **competed** or **monopolized**. These two **need
each other**: the road is worthless without teams, the stable worthless
without traffic, and **each one's improvement raises the other's
value.**

Which brings in genuinely different economics — **double
marginalization**: two separate owners of complementary goods each take
a margin, and the **combined price ends up HIGHER than a single owner
would charge.** So:

> **⭐ The turnpike buying the posting stables is EFFICIENT, not
> predatory.**

**A real counter-example to the vertical-integration story** from
§ *The salvage yard*, and an important one: it gives the antitrust arc a
case where **the right answer is NOT to break it up.** Without it the
thread moralizes; real competition policy turns on **which** integration
helps.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The rental moral hazard, and the return inspection

The hirer does not own the horse, so **they do not bear the cost of
overworking it** — a tragedy of the commons *inside a rental*, and the
**third** moral hazard in the chain after insurance and banking.

> **The ostler inspects on return** — was this team abused?

The same condition-assessment shape as the second-hand market and
impound intake, and it makes **handing a team back an interaction rather
than a menu.**

⭐ **Legislated hook: how hard may a hired team be worked?** A real
historical concern (post horses died fast), a **`parameter`** clause,
and it connects straight to the **vet**.

## The almanac-maker — and the inverted information business

**Fourteenth in the chain**, out of the substrate audit's *"weather has
no vocation."* It sits on a fact that makes it unusually clean.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Our weather is stateless and procedural — so the future is COMPUTABLE

Real forecasters fight chaos. Ours is a **deterministic function** of
place, time, pins and climate lean. The information **actually exists**
and is **actually knowable**; the only question is whether *you* can
compute it.

> **The cleanest possible "competence buys information" case — there is
> no randomness to hide behind.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Split the almanac in two, and the split is historically real

| Half | Character |
|---|---|
| **celestial** — sun, moon, tides, season, calendar | **genuinely deterministic** (`CelestialApi` ships) |
| **weather** | procedural, complex — **approximable, not certain** |

Which is exactly what real almanacs were: **the astronomical tables were
accurate, and the weather predictions were guesswork dressed in the same
authority.**

> **⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ That is the ratings agency's shilling structure precisely**
> — reputation **earned** on verifiable claims and **spent** on
> unverifiable ones. Except here **the almanac is FALSIFIABLE**, because
> the record accumulates: score last year's predictions against what
> happened.

**Fourth instance of *the record disciplines the claim*** — after the
actuary, the rating and the audit — this time applied to
**forecasting** rather than to a conflict of interest.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The gate is unusual: a TIME SERIES, not skill or capital

You cannot predict a season from a day. The asset is **years of
observation** — so a new maker is **necessarily** worse, an established
one's records are genuinely valuable, and:

> **A weather record is an asset you can steal.**

#### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Which inverts the ratings agency

| Business | Owns | Because |
|---|---|---|
| **ratings agency** | a **METHOD** | the data is **public** |
| **almanac-maker** | the **DATA** | observation is **private in aggregate** |

The weather is **public in the instant** — anyone present sees it. What
is private is that **nobody else wrote it down.** Exactly how real
climatology works: *anyone could have measured; nobody did.*

**⭐⭐⭐ So the labour is DILIGENCE, not brilliance** — showing up every
day and recording it. Boring, valuable, **unshortcuttable**: a rare
vocation shape and a good fit for a patient player, in contrast to the
appraiser's judgment or the assayer's instrument.

### The rest of the product

- **The calendar** — feast days, market days, when the courts sit, when
  the legislature meets. In a world with a **docket**, **the almanac is
  where you find out when things happen.**
- **⭐⭐ It is a publication**, so it rides the publisher machinery —
  **third instance** after the ticker and the auction catalogue.
- **⭐⭐⭐ And planting dates** — the direct economic hookup: **the
  farmer is the customer**, and the value is a **tighter planting
  window.** Passes the demand test cleanly and lands on the build
  running now.

### Failure, and the liability question

**A wrong frost date costs a crop** — consequential *and* traceable,
since the reliance is visible in a way most information businesses can
hide from.

> **⭐ Reputation, not liability.** The market punishes; the courts do
> not.

Same as the rating — though **professional negligence is a legitimate
`parameter`** if a locality wants it, and that is a real argument worth
having, because **liability makes advice cautious and useless.**

### ⚠ One tuning constraint

If a competent player predicts weather **perfectly**, storms stop being
dramatic.

> **Predictability must DECAY WITH HORIZON.** Tomorrow is fairly
> knowable; next month is a **lean**.

Real meteorology, and it preserves drama exactly where drama lives (a
storm arriving) while allowing planning exactly where planning lives (a
season). **The celestial half stays fully knowable — eclipses were never
a surprise.**
