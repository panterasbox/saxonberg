## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The spine is stratigraphy — deeper is older **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

Layers lie in order, and what is in a layer dates it. One idea carries fossils and
archaeology both.

- ⭐ **Index fossils are a surveyor's instrument.** William Smith's 1815 geological
  map of Britain matched strata across the country by their fossils — and he did it
  to **find coal**. A fossil is a **reading**: which layer you are in, and so where
  the seam is. Mining's survey ladder gets a rung that pays.
- **Context is information.** An object's layer and neighbors *are* what it tells
  you; dug out carelessly, the information is gone — why looting destroys knowledge
  (lens 4).

## 2. ⭐⭐ Fossils are nature's moulds and casts **[DECIDED]**

Paleontology speaks of **mold and cast** fossils;
[mould.md](../../subsystems/mould.md)'s rule — *the container decides the shape of
what sets in it* — is the mechanism. An organism leaves a hollow in sediment and
minerals fill it; **permineralization keeps the form and replaces the substance**
(the [moulding slate](./moulding-slate.md)'s impression).

**Found** as a **seeded layer** of the ground (the deposit field, *seeded, never
drawn*), exposed:

- by **digging** — quarries and mines, through the byproduct stream;
- ⭐ by **erosion** — Lyme Regis's cliffs fall in winter storms and show new fossils:
  **weather reveals them**, derived, so beachcombing after a storm is real once
  foraging exists.

**What they are for** — the demand first:

| use | |
|---|---|
| **survey** | the index fossil (§ 1) |
| ⭐ **fertilizer** | England's **coprolite rush** (1850s–1880s): phosphate nodules of fossil dung dug by the ton, dissolved in sulfuric acid, sold as superphosphate — onto [soil.md](../../subsystems/soil.md)'s phosphorus reserve |
| **collectors and science** | the fossil dealer — **Mary Anning** of Lyme Regis sold the specimens that founded paleontology and was barred from the Geological Society as a woman; the museum's first stock |
| **gems** | jet, amber, polished petrified wood — the [lapidary](./gems-and-precious-metals-slate.md) |

## 3. ⭐⭐ Wood's afterlives — burial conditions decide **[DECIDED]**

Today wood is **its species' material row** plus **state**: seasoning (green to
seasoned, by moisture), grain and stock form ([assembly.md](../../subsystems/assembly.md)),
and rot through decomposition (moisture above ~20 %, oxygen, warmth; shipworm in
warm salt water). Decomposition's *burial cuts both ways*, extended over geological
time:

| conditions | wood becomes | in our model |
|---|---|---|
| air and water | **rot** | decomposition |
| anoxic and acidic — a peat bog | ⭐ **bog oak** — preserved for millennia and **blackened** as its tannin meets iron in the water | **oak with a history**: hard, prized for carving and jewelry, **cracks if dried too fast** (the seasoning model derives it). ⭐ The iron-tannin black of iron-gall ink and the dyer's black — its third appearance. Found by peat cutters. |
| pressure and heat over ages | **lignite → jet → coal** | the **coal family**, not wood; jet carves and polishes, coal ships |
| mineral-rich water | **petrified wood** | ⭐ **not wood** — silica that took wood's form: **a stone carrying a wood grain as an impression**. Will not burn or rot; the lapidary works it like agate. |
| floating on water | ⭐ **driftwood** | **wood with a history** — waterlogged then sun-dried, salt-laden, worn smooth, perhaps shipworm-bored: states the models have or decomposition designs |

⭐ **Driftwood's novelty is how it arrives.** Wood floats until waterlogged (density
and moisture), so rivers carry fallen trees to the sea and currents carry them
ashore. On treeless coasts **driftwood was the timber supply** — Iceland and Norse
Greenland built from Siberian driftwood. A shore's driftwood is a **flow resource**
derived from upstream forest and the currents, as the fishery derives from a reach;
collecting it is **foraging** (deferred), and the [river slate](./river-slate.md)'s
log drive is its deliberate human twin.

**The wood-model gap is small** — bog oak and driftwood are rows or states on their
species; petrified wood and jet are mineral rows; driftwood's arrival waits on
foraging.

## 4. Found property — who owns what is found **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

- **Authored ruins** are decomposition's history, read in derived state.
- ⭐ **Emergent finds** — what players lose, drop or bury is still chattel with a
  chain of title ([chattel.md](../../subsystems/chattel.md)), so a find raises a real
  question: **lost, or abandoned?** Lost-property law and **treasure trove** answer
  it (in Britain the Crown takes a find of treasure and rewards finder and
  landowner). **Who owns what is found is a polity's rule**, with the chattel record
  as its evidence.
