## 0. The wheel is several principles — this slate owns one

Physically "the wheel" is: **rolling** (rolling resistance far below sliding friction),
**continuous rotation** (spinning wheel, potter's wheel, lathe, grindstone), **force
multiplied** (wheel and axle — windlass, capstan), **power from flow** (water wheel,
windmill), and **rotation transmitted or stored** (gears, pulleys, flywheel). The
machines slate owns the last three and the rotary working ends. **This slate owns
rolling** — the wheel on a vehicle — and the trade that makes it.

⭐ **On a vehicle, the wheel is the part playing `running gear`** (the machines slate's
roles): the draft **derives** from that part and the surface (the
[roads slate](./roads-slate.md)'s running gear × surface) instead of a constant on the
cart; and the wheel **wears and breaks** by the machines slate's duty wear — rolling
miles over a surface.

## 1. ⭐⭐ Each part its wood, chosen by grain **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

| part | wood | why |
|---|---|---|
| **the hub (nave)** | **elm** | interlocking grain that will not split under a dozen mortises |
| **the spokes** | **oak, riven, not sawn** | riving follows the grain — far stronger; [forestry](../../subsystems/forestry.md)'s `rive` ships |
| **the rim (felloes)** | **ash** | tough and springy, takes the road's shock |
| **the tire, the hub bands** | **iron** | the smith's — the wear surface |

All three woods, riving and **seasoning** ship. A wheelwright's real capital was **years
of timber stacked to season** (George Sturt, *The Wheelwright's Shop*, 1923 — the
classic account, written as the craft died).

## 2. ⭐⭐ The wheel is an assembly, built in order **[DECIDED]**

1. **Turn the hub** on a lathe (a gap the [carpentry slate](./carpentry-slate.md) names).
2. **Mortise it**, staggered so the hub does not split.
3. **Drive the spokes.**
4. **Fit the felloes**, each carrying two spokes, doweled together.
5. **Tire it** (§ 4).

[assembly.md](../../subsystems/assembly.md)'s model exactly: parts with roles, joints
from the shipped vocabulary (mortise, wedged, pegged), wear on the part that answers it.
⭐ **The dish** — a cartwheel is a shallow cone, spokes angled outward: it resists the
side thrust of the horse's gait, and with the axle arms angled down the load-bearing
spoke stands vertical. Subtle, teachable, and why a flat wheel collapses.

## 3. ⭐⭐ Wood moves, and the wheel shows it **[DECIDED]**

- **A dry summer shrinks the wood and loosens the spokes** — a loose wheel rattles, then
  fails. ⭐ Carters **drove their wagons through a pond or ford** to swell the wheels
  tight — real, and derivable from the shipped moisture and seasoning model.
- **The tire wears thin** by miles and surface; **felloes rot** where they stay wet;
  **spokes break**.
- ⭐ **A wagon with a broken wheel is stranded** until it is mended — the wheelwright is
  someone you need, and **repair is most of the income** (upkeep is somebody's income).

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ Tiring — the shop's great communal act **[DECIDED]**

The smith forges an iron hoop **slightly smaller** than the wheel, heats it red-hot so it
**expands**, and the whole shop carries it out, drops it over the wheel on the tiring
platform, and **douses it** — it **shrinks**, pulling every joint into compression with
a crack of steam.

- ⭐ **Cooperative** — smith, wheelwright and the shop's hands at once: the trade's best
  participation moment.
- ⚠ **It needs thermal expansion**, which the [thermal model](../../subsystems/thermal.md)
  does not have. The honest earlier rung, **strakes** — separate iron plates nailed across
  each joint of the rim — needs none, so the trade ships without the kernel gap and
  tiring arrives with it. Expansion is small kernel work with outsized teaching value
  (why bridges have gaps, why a stuck lid loosens under hot water).
- **The traveller** — a small wheel rolled round the rim to measure its circumference for
  the tire: measuring by rolling, a real instrument.

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ Carts and wagons — logistics' supplier **[DECIDED]**

The wheelwright usually built **the whole vehicle** — body, bed, axles, shafts — and this
is the supply [logistics](../../subsystems/logistics.md) and haulage
([conveyance.md](../../subsystems/conveyance.md)) need:

- **Vehicles as assemblies** — a cart or wagon is a bill: wheels (running gear), axles,
  bed, body, shafts or pole; the shipped cart becomes one, so a broken axle or wheel is a
  part failure, mended, not a cart destroyed.
- **The range** — the handcart and **wheelbarrow** (construction's), the two-wheeled cart,
  the four-wheeled wagon (with a turning front axle), the **ox cart**, the dray; gun
  carriages when the garrison arrives.
- **Regional wagon styles** differed county by county — content.
- **Elsewhere** — the coach and its springs (the luxury coachbuilder, a later rung);
  water wheels (the millwright's); spinning wheels (the turner's).

## 6. The epoch ladder — the same `fit` and `mount`, opposite structures **[DECIDED — lens 5]**

Solid disc (Bronze Age) → spoked (the chariot) → dished wood with an iron tire →
⭐ **the bicycle wheel turns the principle upside down**: a wooden wheel **stands on its
bottom spoke in compression**; a wire-spoked wheel's **hub hangs from its top spokes in
tension** (wire from the [cannery](./cannery-and-bottling-slate.md)'s metal-forming) → the
**pneumatic tire** (Dunlop, 1888, on the [polymers](./polymers-slate.md) pack's rubber) →
steel and cast wheels.

## 7. ⭐⭐ The wheel needs a road and an animal **[DECIDED — lens 1, lens 6]**

Between roughly the 3rd and 6th centuries the Middle East largely **abandoned the wheeled
cart for the camel** — without good roads a camel was cheaper than carts and their upkeep
(Richard Bulliet, *The Camel and the Wheel*). Mesoamerica had wheeled toys and **no
carts** — no draft animals to pull them. The wheel is a system with roads and animals,
and **we already derive it**: `wheelPassable`, the roads slate's surfaces and haulage's
animals make a world without roads make wheels worthless by itself.

## 8. The lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — grain and wood choice; the dish; compression vs tension; wood movement;
   thermal expansion; the wheel as a system.
2. **Expression** — a wagon is a bill of parts; the painted wagon ([paint](./paint-slate.md));
   regional styles.
3. **Immersion** — the shop, the seasoning stacks, the tiring platform, the pond.
   **Participation** — ⭐ tiring as a cooperative act; the stranded wagon on a muddy road.
4. **Values** — Sturt's lament: a craft of knowledge in the hands displaced by factory
   wheels — the Luddite thread, gently.
5. **Continuity** — the ladder (§ 6).
6. **Economy** — the demand there first (every cart in haulage, the cannery's freight on
   the valley road, farm carts); repair as the steady income; seasoned timber as capital.
7. ⭐ **Governance — wheel-width law.** Narrow wheels cut ruts, so Britain's turnpike acts
   regulated wheel width (the Broad Wheels Act, 1753) with cheaper tolls for broad wheels —
   directly on the roads slate's wear by tonnage.
