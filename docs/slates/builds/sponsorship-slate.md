## 0. The owner's frame

On how GIPHY got the studios on board: *"what can we do to encourage the same sorta deal
that giphy gets. just be cool and popular? I mean I feel like we can do better because
giphy is just a glorified search engine but we're a game. we can actually create
incentives. that feels like a marketers dream."* And on paying: *"we have a funding
model that theoretically if players care enough about content they might vote for its
funding out of the budget … that's all governance the compact deals with."*

## 1. What GIPHY actually had **[EVIDENCE: informed speculation, 2026-11-08]**

- **The studios published on GIPHY themselves, as marketing.** The "official" channels
  (HBO, Disney, Netflix …) are rights holders uploading their own GIFs, because a
  reaction GIF circulating on every phone is free advertising. GIPHY sold brands
  promoted placement; it did not, as far as anyone has published, pay for content.
- **Everything else was user uploads under DMCA § 512**, as on Discord.
- **Consolidation makes deals few.** About 6–8 companies hold most quotable film and TV
  (Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, Comcast/NBCU, Paramount, Sony, Amazon MGM, Netflix,
  Lionsgate), so the deals are a dozen relationships, not thousands.

## 2. ⭐⭐ We sell recall, not impressions **[DECIDED — the owner: "yeah this will work"]**

GIPHY sells a GIF seen for a second, in no particular context. A realm offers **a word
people learn** (an emote is sent by its verb only, emotes-slate § Layer 2b), and a
word you learn by using it, in a community where it means something, is remembered.
What a game can offer that a search engine cannot:

- **The diegetic premiere.** A trailer screens at a watch party in the city's cinema
  ([saxonberg-city-slate](./saxonberg-city-slate.md): every building a window onto a
  live system), with lobby cards as real paper goods to collect.
- **Earned emotes.** Attend the premiere, unlock `;inigo`, and every later use
  advertises that you were there (emotes-slate Layer 3: *the glyph is the badge*; the
  Twitch subscriber-emote mechanic).
- **The community chose it.** A sponsorship the Compact accepted is a story no search
  engine offers (*the players brought your film here*), and the deal is a public
  record (a release).
- **Honest numbers.** The sponsor receives the public counts every player already
  sees: uses, reactions, reach. Nothing private.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The guardrails **[DECIDED]**

This is where a game becomes the attention company
([measurement.md](../../measurement.md); *neutrality is the attention company's answer,
not ours*):

1. **Accepting a sponsorship is a governance act.** The realm's polity accepts it; never
   the engine, never the company alone (lens 7: name the process).
2. **A sponsored emote says so.** Its provenance is visible, as the meme classification
   shows the film.
3. **Readers filter by source.** "Sponsored" is one more source class on
   `social.emote.render`, beside player and NPC.
4. **No player data is sold, ever.** Sponsors get the public counts and nothing more.
   ⚠ Check against measurement.md's tier-A list (amendable by nobody); if it is not
   already there, it belongs there.

## 4. Licensing the Compact pays for **[DESIGNED]**

The studios' clip-and-still licensing departments exist and quote anyone. The costs:

- **The studio's fee** is negotiated per use; low hundreds per still is a guess, not a
  published rate. Archive clip footage runs ~$50–350 per second with minimums.
- ⚠ **The hidden cost is the actors.** Union rules (SAG-AFTRA) generally require
  performer consent and payment for use outside the work, negotiated per recognizable
  performer. It is usually what kills small deals.
- **The terms** would be one realm, a term, non-transferable, so a license lives in the
  realm's private pack, never the open base pack.

A launch catalog of licensed stills is unaffordable. **One licensed classic the
community wanted is plausible**, and that is the point: spending the realm's money on
*Princess Bride* rather than a library wing has no derivable answer (lens 4, values).
The license is a recorded asset of the realm with an expiry, so renewal is a
[budget](./budget-slate.md) line.

## 5. ⭐ Who to approach first **[DESIGNED]**

Studios go where the audience already is, so "be cool and popular" is part of the
answer. Before then, the people who own their own rights and want reach:

- **Streamers and creators.** A streamer can license their own face, voice and moments
  for free, for exposure, which is Twitch emote culture exactly. ⚠ Only what is theirs:
  the game footage in the same clip is the publisher's (emotes-slate, the source
  findings).
- **Indie distributors, anime licensors, game studios.** Reachable, and hungry for an
  audience that cares.
- **Free catalogs:** Creative Commons (the Blender open movies), the public domain
  (US: published 1930 or earlier, as of 2026), and the US federal government's own
  footage for political quotables.

## 6. ⭐⭐⭐ Built as real business **[DECIDED — the owner, 2026-11-08]**

The owner: *"I feel like this is actually my main route to being able to use the content
I want, maybe not at the platform level but at least in the hosts and outlets that have
an audience significant enough to consume the content at scale … I actually want to
build it as if we're doing real business already. like if a studio comes knocking I want
to be able to show them 'this is what you're losing by sending this takedown notice'
and that requires the engine actually be running."*

- **The first realm doing real business is `mud.panterasbox.com`**: a real desk, a
  real terms page, a real designated agent.
- **Money flows both ways, by tier.** The owner: *"both are on the table."* A sponsor
  may pay the realm (campaigns), and the realm or a federation may pay rights holders
  (the blanket license).

## 7. The offer: four tiers **[DESIGNED]**

1. **Permission (free).** The rights holder lets fans keep using the work, in exchange
   for attribution, an *official* badge, and the report. GIPHY's official-channel deal.
2. **Official presence.** They author their own emotes and their brand exists in the
   world, under a corpo ([corpo.md](../../subsystems/corpo.md); brand placement is § 11).
3. **Campaigns (paid).** The premiere, earned emotes, lobby cards (§ 2).
4. **The blanket license (later, the federation).** § 8.

## 8. ⭐⭐ The blanket license: the music model, not GIPHY's **[DESIGNED]**

The owner: *"if we do enact some sort of federation of saxonberg servers that individual
operators can join maybe we can offer terms collectively so as to normalize across the
brand and also afford our more modest operators the same privileges that larger
creators get."*

The model already exists: the performing-rights organizations (ASCAP, BMI) sell a bar
and a stadium the same **blanket license** to a whole catalog, and pay rights holders
**by measured use**. A federation of Saxonberg servers can do the same: one set of
terms, every member server, payouts by the usage count (§ 10). ⭐ So the report is not
only a sales pitch, it is the **basis for paying rights holders**, and it must be
accurate, auditable and public from the start.

## 9. ⭐⭐ The takedown desk: "here's what you're losing" **[DESIGNED]**

- ⛔ **Comply first, every time.** Prompt removal is what keeps the DMCA § 512 safe
  harbor. Content is never held hostage while we negotiate.
- **The offer arrives with the removal**, never instead of it:

  > *Removed: 14 emotes citing The Princess Bride (1987), realm-wide, 2026-12-03. Over
  > the last 90 days they were used 4,212 times by 611 players and seen by 2,950. Here
  > is the full report, and here are three ways to bring them back officially.*

- **A takedown is per work:** one act disables every illustration citing it, recorded.
  Counter-notices are supported.
- **The desk:** the takedown intake and the designated agent, the per-work removal,
  counter-notices, and the offer letter generated from the report.

## 10. What the engine needs **[DESIGNED — product level; the plan decides hosts]**

1. **A work is an entity.** A film, show, game, speech or broadcast is one record,
   keyed **globally** so federated servers agree (Wikidata IDs: open, and they cover
   every work). A meme emote cites a work; a work names its rights holder (a corpo).
2. **Usage is counted per work.** Uses, unique players, reach (witnesses), reactions
   and sentiment, per day. ⚠ **Today nothing counts emote uses durably**: reactions
   tally in the moment and renown reads valence, but no ledger of performances exists.
3. **The report is public** to anyone, rights holder or player. The owner: *"public is
   better unless people try to game the numbers."* That is the measurement doctrine
   (*the mirror shows you*), and it is the honest pitch: a studio sees what everyone
   sees. A wiki page per work with live numbers is the natural home.
   - **Aggregate only, with a size floor**, so a small realm cannot expose a person.
   - **Anti-gaming:** count **unique players** ahead of raw uses; same-account acts
     count for nothing and script-fired acts are marked
     ([expressive-acts](./expressive-acts-slate.md) already decided both); every
     count is auditable back to the events. A public number worth money will be gamed,
     so gaming is designed against from the first build, not patched later.
4. **The desk** (§ 9).
5. **Published terms, and deals as contracts.** Our terms page; a deal is a contract
   record ([contract.md](../../subsystems/contract.md): clauses over verifiable
   conditions, escrow), e.g. *a premiere with at least N attendees*.

## 11. ⭐⭐⭐ Brand placement across the simulation **[DECIDED — the owner, 2026-11-08]**

The owner: *"if we're talking about breaking the seal on sponsored content, it's not
gonna end at emotes we have a whole simulation to market. and again, a codified
incentives structure answerable to a codified governance framework."* And: *"personally
I'm eager to do business with Coca-Cola but I'll settle for Pepsi."*

### 11.1 A brand joins a corpo, and picks a package

- ⭐ **The usual route: a real brand joins one of our fictional corpos** (Coca-Cola as
  a Veshko brand). It fits [corpo.md](../../subsystems/corpo.md)'s three axes as
  shipped: the brand is a mark with an owner, the corpo is the capital, and the
  product is made by its trade like anything else. The owner, on why the corpo: *"it
  gives the brand an accountability trail in the game through the corpo's own committee
  structure and whatever offices it creates as an org. it also means whoever running
  these corpos has to be respectful of the brands they're representing in the game but
  that's the sort of thing we want to incentivize anyway, real brands just up the
  stakes."* The corpo's board is a real committee (it holds title over `/corpo/<key>`),
  so the trail is the board's record. The brand's guidelines are clauses in its deal,
  and the board answers for keeping them.
- **Packages to choose from**, because brands do not want to write content: *"they
  probably dont want to join a game server and start writing their own shit."*
  Billboards (a building's painted face), screens ([display.md](../../subsystems/display.md)),
  product placement (brand rows on goods in their trade packs), events (the premiere,
  [performance](./performance-slate.md)), sweepstakes. ⚠ A sweepstakes with a real prize
  is a lottery if entry costs anything, so no purchase necessary, and some US states
  require registration above a prize value (Open 3).
- **A brand may also set up shop entirely** (its own corpo, its own content), by
  negotiating with the Compact. The owner: *"more content is always better … the engine
  is open to anyone so none of this stuff is off the table."*
- **The launch corpo catalog grows or shrinks** with who is being courted.
- ⭐ **The opt-out needs no new mechanism.** Each real brand ships as **its own pack**
  depending on its corpo's pack (`brand-coca-cola` on `corpo-veshko`). The Compact's
  existing act of deciding which packs to install *is* the opt-out, and one standing
  amendment (*no real-brand packs*) opts a realm out entirely, for an operator who
  does not want *"that capitalist slop."* The engine never learns "real brand" as a
  category.

### 11.2 ⭐⭐⭐ A brand buys presence, never physics **[DECIDED — law, the owner: "this is a good rule"]**

- Coke's sugar and caffeine run through [metabolism](../../subsystems/metabolism.md)
  as they really do, and its supply chain is simulated like everyone else's. **No brand
  pays for its product to be healthier, cheaper to make or better than the sim says.**
- Platform law, not grain: a realm that sells physics breaks derivability (lens 1) and
  the fiction (lens 3a).
- It is also the pitch: *your product, in a world that simulates how things are
  actually made.* The brands that stay are the ones confident in their product.
- **Disclosure:** a can on a shelf is ordinary product placement. A scripted NPC
  praising it, or a player paid to promote it, is an endorsement, which the FTC
  requires be disclosed; the *sponsored* source class (§ 3) covers speech.

### 11.3 ⭐⭐ A brand earns standing like any author **[DECIDED — the balance is open]**

The owner: *"the brand may want terms where they can also earn standing for their
content, the same as any other content author. this would probably be labor standing,
though an argument could be made about capital standing. they're not offlimits from
acquiring capital standing through the normal means either."*

- **Labor standing** for what the brand authors, through the same authorship ledger
  as every author ([provenance.md](../../subsystems/provenance.md)).
- **Capital standing by the normal means**, never as a term of the deal.
- ⚠ **The standing economy is unbalanced, and that is the next conversation**
  ([standing-mint-slate](./standing-mint-slate.md),
  [company-and-capital.md](../../company-and-capital.md)). The owner's starting
  positions:
  - **Bounded offerings at intervals**, not an open market or one big pile.
  - **Design capital standing as if it were equity** to learn whether running the
    system is worth actual capital risk, **while never implying equity anywhere**
    (illegal: real equity is a security, company-and-capital Part 3).
  - **Value and risk, measured as well as possible.** The revenue lines are this
    slate's sponsorship, subscriptions or cover fees, and a desktop build (Electron) on
    Steam. The owner: *"there's value to be had after the initial capital investment
    here, but there's also risk. I wanna know as best as I can how much of both are on
    offer."*

### 11.4 ⭐⭐ All real money goes to the Compact's budget **[DECIDED — the owner]**

The owner: *"all real moneys go to the compact budget. I dont want anyone running their
own side hustles if I can avoid it. people will create external markets to trade on and
I can't stop that but I don't have to make it easy."*

- A brand pays the realm, never a corpo's board, an office holder or a player. A
  corpo's in-world treasury is game money; any real-money marketing spend on its
  behalf is appropriated by the Compact from the [budget](./budget-slate.md).
- External markets will exist; nothing in the engine makes them easy.
