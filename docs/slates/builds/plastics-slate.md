## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Thermoset and thermoplastic — derived **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

The single most useful thing to know about a plastic, and it falls out of the shipped
crosslinking law:

- **A thermoset** is crosslinked as it cures — chains tied together for good. It sets
  **once**; heat it again and it chars, never melts. Bakelite, the first.
- **A thermoplastic** is long chains *not* tied together — heat softens it, it can be
  **melted and moulded again**. Celluloid, then polyethylene, nylon, PVC.

So **recycling is derivable**: a thermoplastic can be remelted; a thermoset cannot.

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ Bakelite, from the shipped retort **[DECIDED]**

Bakelite (Baekeland, 1907) is **phenol + formaldehyde**, cured under heat and pressure.
Both come from chains that ship:

- **Phenol** — from **coal tar**, the retort's product (destructive distillation's Stage
  A, shipped).
- **Formaldehyde** — from **methanol** ("wood spirit", from the destructive distillation
  of **wood** — the same retort) oxidized over a hot copper or silver catalyst; or from
  the [chemical works](./chemical-works-slate.md).
- **The cure** — heat and pressure in a closed vessel; the window is narrow and reached
  **by measuring** (the rubber precedent), not by a lock.
- **What it was for** — ⭐ **electrical insulation**: switches, sockets, the grid's
  fittings, telephones, radios — *"the material of a thousand uses"*. The grid ships;
  Bakelite is what its fittings are made of.

## 3. Celluloid **[DECIDED]**

Cellulose (cotton or paper's fiber) treated with **nitric acid** (the chemical works)
and plasticized with camphor (Hyatt, 1870). ⭐ It was developed in answer to a prize for
a **substitute for ivory in billiard balls** — the
[gems slate](./gems-and-precious-metals-slate.md)'s ivory question, answered by
industry. Combs, collars, ⭐ **film** (photography, a named gap). And it is **flammable**
— nitrocellulose is a near cousin of guncotton
([gunpowder-slate](./gunpowder-slate.md)): film stores burned, a hazard the fire model
derives.

## 4. The petrochemical rung — from drilling's crude **[LEAN]**

[Drilling](../../subsystems/drilling.md) ships crude and its
[fractionation](../../subsystems/fractionation.md). **Cracking** heavy fractions gives
small molecules (ethylene) that **polymerize**: polyethylene (1933), PVC, polystyrene,
and nylon (1935). The industrial-to-modern epoch's plastics — the same `mould`, the
same thermoplastic remelt, a new feedstock. Its own later rung.

## 5. Moulding **[DECIDED]**

[mould.md](../../subsystems/mould.md) decides the shape at every rung:
- **compression moulding** — the thermoset's way: powder and heat in a closed mould;
- **injection moulding** — the thermoplastic's: melt forced into a mould, cooled, popped
  out, again and again. The mould is capital; the parts are cheap.

## 6. The lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — ⭐ thermoset vs thermoplastic from crosslinking; feedstocks from coal
   tar, wood spirit, crude; why one recycles and one cannot.
2. **Expression** — the mould's form is the author's; a plastic's color is its
   pigment ([paint](./paint-slate.md)).
3. **Immersion** — the Bakelite telephone; the film store fire. **Participation** — the
   moulder, the works chemist.
4. **Values** — ⭐ **plastic does not rot.** [Decomposition](./decomposition-slate.md)'s
   agents (fungi, bacteria) have nothing to eat in it; it persists for centuries and
   breaks only into smaller pieces. The convenience against the permanence is real, and
   derivable.
5. **Continuity** — natural polymers → Bakelite → celluloid → petrochemicals: the same
   cure and mould, new feedstocks. ⭐ **Synthetics collapse a luxury** — imitation ivory,
   tortoiseshell and amber; the gems slate's *a capability survives the epoch, its
   scarcity does not*.
6. **Economy** — insulation, film, buttons, combs, then everything; the mould as capital;
   the feedstocks as other industries' by-products.
7. **Governance** — film-store fire codes; a deposit or ban on plastic waste (the
   [aluminium-can slate](../tails/aluminium-can-slate.md)'s deposit as law).
