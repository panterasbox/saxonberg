## 0. What plastics are for **[DECIDED — lenses 1 and 2]**

The owner asked what plastics should *do* — an epoch marker, a material system, or the
waste story — and left it to the lenses. **Lenses 1 and 2 agree: plastics are the
material of mass production** — the first material whose economics belong to the
mould instead of the hand. The three options are one thing: mass production **is** the
modern epoch's marker; thermoset, thermoplastic, moulds and feedstocks are its
material system; and mass production is **why** the world fills with plastic.

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The mould is capital **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

- An injection mould is expensive to cut and every part out of it costs pennies:
  **fixed against marginal cost**, the core lesson of mass production, and plastics are
  its purest case — no other material in the realm teaches it as cleanly.
- A part's price derives from the feedstock, the minutes on the press, and **the
  mould's cost spread over the run** — so the thousandth comb is cheap and the first is
  not, and a short run never pays.
- ⭐ **The toolmaker** cuts the mould — a precision craft, the
  [machines slate](./machines-slate.md)'s fitter at its peak. **The mould is the
  author's form** (§ 6, lens 2).
- **Moulding by kind** — **compression** for a thermoset (powder and heat in a closed
  mould), **injection** for a thermoplastic (melt forced in, cooled, popped out, again
  and again).

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ Thermoset and thermoplastic — derived **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

From the shipped crosslinking law, taken to its conclusion:

- **A thermoset** is crosslinked as it cures — chains tied together for good. It sets
  **once**; reheated, it chars and never melts. Bakelite.
- **A thermoplastic** is long chains *not* tied together — heat softens it, and it can
  be **melted and moulded again**. Celluloid, later polyethylene, nylon, PVC.
- ⭐ **Recyclability falls out**: a thermoplastic remelts; a thermoset cannot.

## 3. ⭐⭐ Feedstocks follow cost **[DECIDED]**

- **First, by-products.** **Phenol** from **coal tar** — a by-product the gasworks
  dumped — and **formaldehyde** from **methanol** (*wood spirit*, off the same retort's
  wood charge), oxidized over a hot copper or silver catalyst, or from the
  [chemical works](./chemical-works-slate.md). ⭐ Plastics turn waste into feedstock:
  the tar finds a buyer and the retort's margin rises.
- **Later, oil.** **Cracking** crude's heavy fractions gives small molecules (ethylene)
  that polymerize — polyethylene (1933), PVC, polystyrene, nylon (1935). The same
  chemistry fed by whatever is cheapest: a later rung, **[LEAN]**.

## 4. ⭐⭐ The first consumer — Bakelite displaces ebonite **[DECIDED — lens 6]**

The demand was there first: **electrical insulation**. The grid ships, and ⭐
**ebonite** — hard rubber, rubber cured with sulfur at 20 % or more — already ships as
the pre-plastic insulator. **Bakelite** (Baekeland, 1907: phenol + formaldehyde, cured
under heat and pressure) is its historical successor — switches, sockets, the grid's
fittings, then telephones and radios: *"the material of a thousand uses."* Plastics have
a real incumbent to displace on day one.

## 5. Celluloid **[DECIDED]**

Cellulose (cotton or paper's fiber) treated with **nitric acid** (the chemical works)
and plasticized with camphor (Hyatt, 1870) — a thermoplastic. ⭐ It was developed in
answer to a prize for a **substitute for ivory in billiard balls**. Combs, collars,
⭐ **film** (photography, a named gap). And it is **flammable** — nitrocellulose is a
near cousin of guncotton ([gunpowder-slate](./gunpowder-slate.md)): film stores burned,
a hazard the fire model derives.

## 6. The lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — ⭐ structure decides behavior (tied or free chains); ⭐ fixed against
   marginal cost; why it persists (nothing evolved to eat it); feedstocks follow cost.
2. **Expression** — ⭐ the mould is the author's form, and color goes all the way through
   with no paint; industrial design (the Bakelite radio, the Art Deco telephone) is
   creative work whose output is a mould. The tension: every part from one mould is
   identical — the opposite of the [mason's mark](./masonry-and-concrete-slate.md).
3. **Immersion** — the Bakelite telephone, celluloid collars, the plastic everything.
   **Participation** — the moulder, ⭐ the toolmaker, the works chemist; the polity can
   flood a market with cheap goods, ban them, or mandate them.
4. **Values — a double edge, both real.** ⭐ **Plastics saved animals** — celluloid
   replaced ivory, synthetic tortoiseshell spared the hawksbill turtle. ⭐ **And they
   never go away** — [decomposition](./decomposition-slate.md)'s agents have nothing to
   eat in them; they persist and break only into smaller pieces. Convenience against
   permanence, and disposability as a new habit. The world shows both and judges
   neither.
5. **Continuity** — a Bakelite cup is still a cup: the same commands. The material
   changes what an object *does* (it does not shatter like ceramic, does not rust, and
   insulates). ⭐ **It collapses luxuries** — ivory, tortoiseshell, amber, horn: the
   [gems slate](./gems-and-precious-metals-slate.md)'s *a capability survives the epoch;
   its scarcity does not.*
6. **Economy** — ⭐ waste turned feedstock (the retort's tar); the mould as capital and
   economies of scale; ⭐ **it undercuts craftspeople** — the comb maker, the horn
   worker, the button maker: the machines slate's Luddite thread again; the demand there
   first (insulation, § 4).
7. **Governance** — waste law, deposits and bans (the
   [aluminium-can slate](../tails/aluminium-can-slate.md)'s deposit as law); fire codes
   for celluloid; the chemical works' pollution upstream.

## 7. ⭐ No gate **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

Destructive distillation held its plastic **behind the epoch on-ramp** — a knowledge
gate. The [inquiry slate](./inquiry-slate.md) records that **rubber shipped with no
gate** (2026-10-16): *"the cure window is narrow enough that it is reached by measuring
rather than by tradition, which is the industrial break encoded in physics instead of a
lock."* Bakelite's cure is the same kind of narrow window (ratio, heat, pressure).
**Lens 1 picks rubber's way**: reaching the window **by measuring** is itself the
industrial lesson. The knowledge-by-law route stays a better way to *earn* the curve,
never a lock on it.
