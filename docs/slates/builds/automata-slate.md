# Automata slate — an assistant that provably cannot cheat

> **Status: UNBUILT, and the memory half already ships.**
> `player_frames` is Mongo-backed, windowed per owner, owner-only, with
> the exact index a consult needs (`{owner: 1, seq: -1}` — *"the backfill
> read, newest N for one owner"*).
> **Left:** the consult verb + context assembly · the mode model
> (`{trigger, recipe, voice}`) · the trigger set and its rate caps · the
> overnight digest + the decaying pyramid · mode slots on the automaton ·
> the frame filter predicate · ⭐ **the minimum experiment (§ 12)**
> **Size:** ⭐ **the experiment is a tail. The feature is a build**, and
> its economics are [llm-economy-slate](./llm-economy-slate.md)'s.

**Captured 2026-10-04.** The player's own agent: *"an 'automata' that
experiences everything the player experiences… the player can consult
their automata about what to do next."*

---

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Why this is ours to build — the seam, not the model

> **User: "Everything the player sees the LLM sees exactly in its native
> format, prose. The novelty of that is both marketable and makes me
> curious to see it actually working."**

True, and the strongest part is one notch past the prose.

A graphical game wiring an LLM assistant must **translate** — screenshots,
state dumps, a scene graph. Every translation is a programmer's guess
about what mattered, lossy in **both** directions: it leaks what the
player could not see and drops what they could.

> ⭐⭐⭐ `player_frames` is written at `Avatar.handleMessage` — **above the
> multiplex, below `filterMessage`.** Post-sensorium. So it is not merely
> *exactly* what the player saw; it is **perceptually correct by
> construction.**
>
> **The hard problem in agent design — *what may this agent legitimately
> know* — is solved here as a side effect of a seam that already existed
> for a different reason.**

And the claim that follows is about **honesty, not capability**:

> ⭐⭐ **An assistant that provably cannot cheat.** Its only input is the
> owner-only record of what you were told.

That is *verifiable*, and it is the inverse of what people are actually
suspicious of — a cheat engine, a surveillance layer, an oracle. ⭐ The
novelty being sold is **the constraint**, not the model. (Lead with *your
automaton remembers what you saw, and nothing else*; let the mechanism be
the proof rather than the headline.)

⭐ **And the bet is already validated from the other side.** The
live-drive work concluded *"the agent is a PARTICIPANT not a feed — the
world IS the render."* **The automata is that finding pointed at a player
instead of a test harness.**

## 2. What it is, and what it must never be

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **a memory prosthesis** | what did I see, what did I forget |
| ✅ **a loose-end finder** | what did I start and not finish |
| ✅ **a tutor** | how do I do the thing I want |
| ✅ a **companion** | a voice, layered on the above |
| ⛔ **an oracle** | *what should I do to win* — needs world access; **breaks everything** |

⭐ The first three are **one build** — same context, same call, different
question. The fourth is a voice on top. **The fifth must be refused in
writing**, because it is what everyone will ask for.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The arrow, honestly

> *"like one of those little hotkeys that draws the arrow in rpgs"*

⚠ **The RPG arrow points at the objective because the game knows the
objective.** Ours cannot, by the constraint that makes it safe.

> **The RPG arrow points at the goal. Ours points at a loose end you
> created.**
>
> *You've carried that letter three days. You asked Gus about the gate
> twice and never went east. You have 40 iron and no buyer. Three people
> have mentioned the mine; you've never been.*

All of that is in **your** record. ⭐⭐ And it is better than a quest
arrow, because it is **a quest log you generated by playing** — the
honest answer to *"the last generation that knows what it is to be
lost"* that does not simply hand over the map.

⭐ And the abstraction law governs it: *an abstraction is legitimate while
it still costs somebody the activity.* **Imperfect memory is what keeps
it a prosthesis instead of a replacement** (§ 5).

## 3. The context — and the brain/automata split

| | **the brain** ([llm-content](./llm-content-slate.md)) | **the automata** |
|---|---|---|
| whose perception | the NPC's | **yours** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ what it emits | **actions on the bus** | **language to one person** |
| world effect | mutates it | none |
| failure mode | an NPC does something stupid **in public, permanently** | bad advice, privately |
| latency tolerance | high — a beat reads as realism | **low** |

> **The brain's output is actions; the automata's output is words.** Every
> other difference follows.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Tools vs vocabulary — the answer to *"how does it know how the game works"*

> **The brain needs the verb list as TOOLS (enforced — it acts).**
> **The automata needs the verb list as VOCABULARY (so its advice is
> typeable).**

Same data, two roles. An automata saying *"you should investigate the
mine"* is useless; one saying *"try `go west`, then `search rubble`"* is a
tutor. ⭐ And its suggestions double as **onboarding** — a player who did
not know `search` exists learns it from their automaton. Lens 1 with no
tutorial.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The index already exists: `HelpTopic`

[help.md](../../subsystems/help.md) — *"the in-game **rulebook**: a
uniform, searchable, navigable index of how the world works."* Four
properties make it the right thing:

1. ⭐ **Harvested, not registered** (*"`warm()` pulls three projectors"*),
   so it cannot drift from the code.
2. ⭐ **A light slice already exists** — `HelpIndexEntry` =
   `{id, kind, title, summary, keywords}`, built for typeahead and
   exactly prompt-sized.
3. ⭐⭐ **It is small.** **296 command views** × ~30 tokens ≈ **~10K
   tokens for the entire verb surface** — stable, so it sits in the
   cached head. **~$0.001 per consult on Haiku.** Effectively free.
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **It carries a `spoiler` flag.** The reference is *already
   partitioned by what a player may know* — so the hardest part of giving
   an agent a reference is solved by content somebody already authored.

⚠ **No codebase index is needed.** For the brain, the action space is
**enforced by tool schemas** (`llm-content`: *"its verb list **is** its
tool schema"*), and ⭐⭐⭐ **the manual is the refusal stream** — the world
answers, and *the refusal is the progression UI* means our refusals are
already written to inform a human, which makes them ideal environment
feedback.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ Modes — `{trigger, recipe, voice}`, and the trigger comes first

> **User: "I think talking about different modes starts at talking about
> different triggers."**

A mode is **not** a personality. It is **a declared context recipe plus a
trigger plus an authored voice** — and the trigger is first because it
decides both the value and the bill.

**The recipe declares:** depth (how far back) · breadth (which frames) ·
output budget · model floor · thinking on/off · and ⭐⭐ **readers** —
what else it may query.

> ⭐⭐ **A mode declares which readers it gets**, and that is where modes
> stop being flavour. A **tutor** needs the help index. A **steward**
> needs your chronicle. A **navigator** needs *your discovered* portion of
> the location graph. A **narrator** needs the biome, the ground, the
> weather, the hour.

⭐ Because a reader's size is knowable, **the cost class is derived, not
declared** — the author says what the mode reads, the engine says what
tier of instrument can run it. Same declare-then-derive shape as the
parcel's inference declaration. ⭐⭐ And modes are then **authorable with
no engine code**, like a `Reading` row.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Capability GATES modes; it does not dim them

> **A mode either runs correctly or is unavailable.** Not *"narrator mode
> but worse"* — **"narrator mode is beyond this automaton."**

1. ⭐ **The author can guarantee the experience.** A mode that degrades
   silently under a thin entitlement leaves the author with no idea what
   players see.
2. ⭐⭐ It is **the refusal-is-the-progression-UI rule** verbatim — the
   mode must **exist** and refuse, so you can be *told* what you lack. A
   fog of quality names nothing.
3. Capability becomes a **legible ladder of unlocks**.

⚠ And it does not contradict graceful narrowing, because they are
different situations:

| situation | response |
|---|---|
| **out of budget right now** (throttle · breaker · aggregate cap) | ⭐ **narrow** — temporary, everyone at once, legible |
| **this instrument isn't good enough** (capability) | ⭐ **gate** — persistent, specific, nameable |

### ⭐⭐⭐ Modes are SLOTTED — cartridges

[slot.md](../../subsystems/slot.md) already ships **Slotted/Slottable,
`accepts` + `fitsSlot`, capacity.**

> **An automaton is a `Slotted` host. A mode is a `Slottable` Idea.
> Capacity is the grade. `accepts` is the compatibility rule.**

So the market has a concrete object — **mode cartridges**: crafted,
graded, branded, bought, traded, stolen, inherited, swapped, all on
existing machinery (chattel, crafting, corpo marks, the retail counter).
⭐ A **mode-wright** is a trade, and a creative one: a recipe plus a
voice.

⭐⭐ **And the fiction writes itself**, because interchangeable cams and
punched cards are literally how a music box and a Jacquard loom work. A
cam at medieval, a punched card at industrial, a module at modern — same
slot, different costume. **`Epoch` gets a second reader.**

⭐ Slots are also a better instrument tier than memory depth: same
economics, far better surface, and *which modes am I carrying* becomes a
real decision. ⚠ (Tiering by **model** is wrong — caches are
model-scoped, so each tier would be separately cold and an upgrade would
cost a player their warm cache.)

### What does NOT derive

⚠ **The voice.** Two modes with byte-identical recipes can read
completely differently, and that difference is the author's whole
contribution. **Cost derives from the recipe; character does not.**

⚠ **The selection.** Which mode you run is the player's — the author
declares the nature, the player configures the use.

## 5. ⚠⚠ The trigger set — and one correction

> **User: "just be careful with that idle thing. sounds like you're using
> llms to engineer the attention economy which is the thing people would
> cry foul over."**

⛔ **An earlier pass ranked an IDLE trigger first** — *nothing has
happened for N minutes* — on the grounds that it was cheap to detect and
high-value. **That was wrong, and the tell was in the framing:** *cheap
to detect about a person's attention, high lift on engagement* **is the
objective function of an attention economy.** An idle trigger detects
disengagement and injects stimulus to recover it. That is the
notification playbook, in a system whose stated property is *the feed
hides the measurement; the mirror shows you.* **Idle is the feed.**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The test: trigger on what the player DID. Never on what the
> player STOPPED doing.**
>
> An act is consent-adjacent — you did something, the system responds.
> **Absence is not** — you did nothing, and the system acts on your
> nothing.

| trigger | fires on | |
|---|---|---|
| **refusal** | the world said no | ⭐⭐ the best tutor moment there is — the refusal is *already* written to inform, and you already want to know. `controller-rejected` notes carry the reason |
| **repetition** | the same attempt three times | ⭐⭐ **revealed intent**; one counter |
| **arrival** | a new locality | the navigator's moment |
| **engagement ended** | with its `AbortReason` | ⭐ the same moment the solicited rating wants — one trigger, two consumers |
| **threshold** | a derived figure crossed | the steward's set; mostly already derived |
| **schedule** | ⭐ [calendar.md](../../subsystems/calendar.md) ships — the `CalendarApp` on the aether implant | consent, in advance |
| **asked** | you asked | consent, now |
| ⭐⭐ **a player-installed tripwire** | *"tell me if I've been going in circles"* | **same data, opposite institution** — your instrument, watching what you asked it to watch, reporting to you. That is the mirror |
| ⛔ **idle** | **the absence of your input** | ⛔ a fact about your attention |

⭐⭐⭐ **And the honest way to serve the stuck player: make asking nearly
free.** One keystroke, trivial cost, and the stuck player asks — so the
need never has to be inferred. **Reduce the cost of asking instead of
inferring the want.** That is almost always the honest fix when a design
reaches for proactive help, and here it is cheaper *and* needs no
attention measurement.

⚠ **Companion flag for the warehouse:** a report saying *"idle-triggered
consults raised session length 12%"* is exactly the finding that would
**justify** the bad design, and *the warehouse informs the build, never
the runtime* does nothing about **us** optimising for engagement at design
time. So: ⭐⭐ **session length is not a success metric.** Refuse the
metric rather than re-fighting the conclusion.

### ⚠⚠ And one absolute architectural rule

> **Never spend a call to decide whether to spend a call.**

Trigger evaluation is **pure engine logic** — counters, facets, timers,
local state. The moment it involves the model, the gate costs what it was
protecting. (Same reason as `llm-content`'s witness gating: *"most NPCs
should not be listening, which is also realistic."*)

⭐ **And a rate cap makes the automaton selective**: if a mode may speak
six times an hour and nine things happened, it must **choose** — which is
what makes it read as judgment rather than reflex. Cost is
**`firing rate × recipe`**, and ⚠ an event trigger's rate is a property of
*how the player plays*, so **every trigger declares a maximum rate** or
one mode eats a day's quota in a bad afternoon.

## 6. Memory — and the nightly wipe changes the design

`schema/player_frames.yaml`: **Mongo-backed**, *"bounded per player by a
frame COUNT with oldest-first eviction"*, **owner-only reads**, disposition
**`reset: wipe`**, and *"the highest-volume collection in the system."*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **`reset: wipe` means *"you've carried that letter three days"* is
> NOT answerable from the frame store.** So the overnight digest is not a
> cost optimisation — **it is the feature's memory.**

⭐ And per the standing doctrine (*owner-scoped content with a place → a
document*; ⚠⚠ *no new collections*), the digest is a **`StoredDocument`
owned by the automaton** — which lands a fiction rule for free:
**your automaton holds its own memory. Lose the automaton, lose the
memory.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ Most long-term memory already exists, and it isn't prose

`chronicle` (the append-only identity ledger — deed vs claim) · `belief`
(+ the `DISCOVERY` realm) · `renown` · `participation` · `advancement` ·
`accountability_events` · `authoring_events` · `contacts` · `recall`.

> **The digest holds only what no ledger keeps: loose ends and
> intentions.** *You meant to go east. Three people mentioned the mine.*
> Those are **patterns in what you were told** — not events — which is
> exactly what no event ledger can hold.

⚠ **Hard rule: the digest CITES, never COPIES.** A paraphrase of a ledger
is **a second source of truth that drifts.** The automaton queries the
chronicle at consult time (cheap, authoritative, current) and keeps only
what no query could answer. ⭐ That also defuses the feedback loop from
§ 7 — the automaton's own output never becomes "history."

### Compaction: a pyramid, not a rolling summary

| | |
|---|---|
| **rolling summary** (one doc, rewritten nightly) | ⚠ compounding paraphrase drift, and ⚠⚠ **not append-only, so every night invalidates the whole cached head** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **tiered pyramid** (days → weeks → seasons) | **append-only per tier, and old tiers freeze** |

> So the prompt head is `[frozen: older seasons] [stable: this season]
> [volatile: today]` — **the right memory architecture and the right cache
> architecture are the same choice.**

### ⚠⚠ Retention reopens A3 — and decay is the answer

A nightly LLM-written narrative of a player's day, kept indefinitely, **is
a dossier** — which is why `player_frames` wipes. Three things make the
digest legitimate:

1. ⭐ **It is the player's, not the platform's** — owner-only, and
   **nothing else in the system may key on it** (the A15 firewall shape).
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **It decays by tier.** A day survives a week; a week survives a
   season; older tiers **thin.** That is the honest privacy answer *and*
   the honest fiction — a clockwork automaton whose old pages have faded
   is more believable than one holding a perfect transcript of your life.
3. ⭐ **The abstraction law demands it**: an automaton that remembers
   everything perfectly has taken **remembering** from you.

⭐⭐ Which gives the instrument tier a second dial beside slots: **a
better automaton fades slower** — mirroring the cartography answer, where
the map degrades and the survey's quality is the product.

### ⛔ Two API features that look like the answer and aren't

⚠ **Server-side compaction** (`compact-2026-01-12`) summarises as a
*conversation* approaches the window. Our consults are short and
**separate**; the problem is cross-day. Wrong tool.
⚠ **The memory tool** (`memory_20250818`) puts **the model** in charge of
what to remember. Backwards: the game's ledgers are authoritative and the
digest is deliberately narrow.

## 7. Filtering, the unprompted voice, and no streaming

### ⭐ Filtering rides the shipped routing engine

`FeedPredicate` is *"a predicate over the frame's FACETS, not over topic
strings"* — with the rationale that topic-path lists are *"a list of sixty
that drifts every time a topic is added, and the drift is silent."* So
automata inclusion is **a predicate set in an existing vocabulary**,
player-configurable per arrangement by construction.

> ⚠⚠ **Filter for cost, never for relevance.** **The frame you skimmed
> past is precisely the loose end worth surfacing**, so a filter tuned to
> *what I care about* would **defeat the feature.** Relevance is the
> automata's job; the filter's job is only the bill.

⭐ **And filter differently per stage** — the overnight digest wants
**breadth** (batched at 50%, compounding); the live window wants
**signal**.

### The unprompted voice

> **User: "the caching thing means we want to force one call an hour…
> sometimes the automata just talks to you unprompted."**

⭐ Better than forcing it: **`max_tokens: 0` is a documented cache
pre-warm**, so the keepalive and the utterance are **separable.** Warm
hourly; **speak only when there is something new.**

⚠ Because **an unprompted push that is wrong is worse than silence** —
one useless notification teaches a player to ignore the channel, and then
the feature is dead even when it is right. **The automaton must be allowed
to say nothing.** ⚠ And gate the warm on activity, or you pay hourly for
AFK players at zero value.

### No streaming — and the benefit nobody designed for

> **User: "streaming is out… the message is the atomic unit here not the
> characters."**

Correct — there is nowhere for a character stream to land. Three
consequences:

1. ⭐ **Total latency is what is felt, so output length is a latency
   lever.** 300 tokens vs 1,200 is ~1s vs several. **Terseness is the
   latency design, not a style preference** — and it is cheaper. Two
   independent reasons to cap hard.
2. A frame-granular option exists (several short frames), but making the
   answer short enough not to need it is better.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **A non-streamed answer is a single atomic frame, so the
   automaton's advice lands in `player_frames` like anything else you
   were told.** The next consult **sees what it told you last time** — so
   *don't repeat yourself* stops being a prompt instruction and becomes
   **a property of the context.** ⚠ It wants a facet so the filter can
   tell *what the world told me* from *what my automaton told me*.

## 8. Persistence and RAM — no Redis

- **history:** ✅ `player_frames`, on disk, windowed, owner-scoped, with
  the exact `{owner, seq}` index a consult needs. ⭐ Plus
  `{owner: 1, body: "text"}` — a per-owner text index, so a consult can be
  *recent frames + a search for the question's terms* rather than the
  whole window. **Retrieval, already indexed**, which decouples how much
  the automaton remembers from how much fits in a prompt.
- **the digest:** a document on the automaton (§ 6).
- ⭐⭐ **the prompt cache lives at the provider, not in our process** — so
  the thing that would normally want a hot store is held **off-box**, keyed
  by prefix. We store the source and re-assemble.
- ⚠ **Do not cache the assembled context server-side.** That is the one
  thing that would grow per-player, unbounded, in process — and it is
  redundant with the provider's cache.
- ⚠ **Redis is a new dependency on a one-box deployment** for state that
  already has a home. Unjustified until something measured says otherwise.
- ⭐ The automata **objects** ride residency's cold-tail eviction; the
  precedent to copy is [pets.md](../../subsystems/pets.md)'s **residency
  pin** — *what loads a pet* is *what loads an automaton*.

## 9. Configuration and cost

Priced against the Anthropic table (first-party, cached 2026-06-24);
⚠ **token counts are estimates pending `count_tokens` on real frames.**

A consult ≈ 15–25K in / ~300 out → **Haiku 4.5: ~$0.021 cold, ~$0.004
warm.** ⭐⭐ Against `llm-content`'s **$0.50–$1.00 per player-hour** for
three live NPCs, an automata at ten warm consults an hour is **~$0.05 per
player-hour — an order of magnitude cheaper than NPC dialogue.** It
*feels* like the bigger exposure because it is appetite-gated rather than
witness-gated, which is what the entitlement is for.

**Config:** Haiku 4.5 · **thinking off** (its default, and the
lowest-latency configuration available) · **no streaming** · **1-hour
cache TTL** · overnight **Batch** digests at 50%.

⭐⭐⭐ **The frame store is the ideal cache shape** — append-only, so each
consult in a session shares a byte-identical prefix with the last and
reads almost everything at 0.1×.

## 10. The allowance

**Decided:** a **per-player allowance**, derived from **participation**
(real-time decayed, derive-on-read) — **unbuyable**, because a price
against a stock cannot bound a flow. Coin rations *within* it and makes a
consult a real choice; slots and fade-rate are what coin buys. Full
mechanism, caps and breakers:
[llm-economy-slate](./llm-economy-slate.md).

⭐ Two gates, each doing what the other cannot: **the entitlement bounds
the person; the price makes it a choice.**

## 11. ⭐ Open questions

1. ⚠⚠ **Does a model read MML well?** `player_frames.body` is MML markup.
   Whether tagged prose helps, hurts, or needs stripping is **unknowable
   from here and ten minutes to find out** — and it is the first thing a
   prototype answers.
2. **Which modes ship**, and which one is the commons' subsidy. ⭐ The
   tutor is the obvious candidate: teaching people the game is the
   commons' interest, and the help index is cached and nearly free.
3. ⚠ **The cheap-mode incentive.** A mode declaring expensive readers is
   one fewer people can run, so authors are pushed toward thrift. ⭐ The
   counterweight is a real policy lever: **the commons can appropriate for
   a specific mode** — *"this season, every citizen's automaton can run
   Navigator."*
4. **Does the automaton get a persona by default**, or is voice entirely
   a cartridge property? (Voice-in-the-cartridge is cleaner and makes the
   bare automaton deliberately plain.)

## 12. ⭐⭐⭐ The minimum experiment

Everything in [llm-economy-slate](./llm-economy-slate.md) gates the
**feature**. It does not gate the **experiment** — `llm-content` set that
precedent deliberately: *one NPC, operator-funded, no metering, no budget
system.*

> **One verb → newest N frames for one owner (the index exists) → one
> Haiku call, thinking off → one frame back.**
>
> No entitlement, no quota, no caps, no digest, no modes, no committee.

It answers the four things discussion cannot:

- **does it feel good** — the only question that matters;
- **what a consult actually costs** — replacing the estimates with
  `count_tokens` on real frames;
- **whether the context assembles sensibly**;
- ⚠ **and § 11 Q1**, the MML question.
