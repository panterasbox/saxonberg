> ships ([combat.md](../../subsystems/combat.md)): footing (poise) beat to beat, the wound

## 0. The frame **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"In another sense, combat is the game and the rest of the economy is just a metagame around it."* And the
shape: *"there's a beginning, middle and end here. Pre-combat is prep and knowing your enemy,
combat itself is a live decision matrix, and then after combat you need to recover and
regroup before the next fight, which may be from a nearby threat."*

1. **Before** — preparation and knowing your enemy: gear and its upkeep, conditioning, terms,
   the place and its witnesses, what you know of the foe. This is where the economy enters.
2. **During** — a live decision matrix: footing, reach, tempo, the read through the fog, the
   moves.
3. **After** — recover and regroup: wounds, endurance, gear wear, and the next threat close
   by.

## 1. The prototype — the Viper and the Mountain **[LEAN]**

The owner asked for the fight that exercises the most design rather than any matchup. The
duel from *Game of Thrones* does:

| act | the story | what it exercises |
|---|---|---|
| **before** | a long spear and light leather against a giant in plate; ⭐ the spear is coated with venom; trial by combat, to the death, before a crowd | reach against mass; armor weight against tempo; **coatings** (missing); terms; onlookers |
| **during** | he keeps his distance, circles, outpaces, wounds the leg behind the knee, drops him, spears him through a gap | reach control ✅, tempo ✅, endurance ✅, footing ✅, **aiming at gaps** (missing), a leg wound costing movement (partial) |
| **the turn** | he stands over the downed giant demanding a confession — and the giant is not finished | the slow, interruptible coup ✅; ⚠ **a downed fighter cannot act at all** |
| **after** | the Mountain dies days later of the venom; the verdict follows | poison as a slow toxin ✅ (the dose from a coated wound is missing); the trial's verdict as law ✅ |

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ Aiming, and gaps in armor **[DECIDED that aiming is needed — the owner]**

The owner: *"we need targeting, that implies there's good spots and bad spots to hit. Is that
on the model? Do we model places the armor isn't covering in addition to the armor's own
composition? Because both matter."*

What the model has:

- ✅ **what armor is made of** — plate, mail, padded, hide, each answering edge, point and
  blunt differently ([materials-response.md](../../subsystems/materials-response.md));
- ✅ **which body sites a piece covers** — a breastplate claims the torso only, and blows
  resolve per site;
- ❌ **gaps inside a covered site** — armpits, the backs of knees, a visor slit; a covered site
  is fully covered by every layer;
- ❌ **choosing where to hit** — torso by default; the only aimed blow is the head on an open
  window.

**The lean** — gaps as one more fact per armor piece (how much of each site it leaves open,
and where), reachable by **point** with a footing advantage; aiming as a choice between an
easy blow on armor and a hard one into a gap. That is the Viper's fight, and why a spear
finds gaps and a mace does not care about them. ⚠ **No helmet ships**, so today every earned
head blow lands on a bare head.

The same system serves **vital zones** on animals — the hunter's heart-and-lung shot
([bestiary](./bestiary-slate.md)).

## 3. ⭐⭐ The cap is the honest health bar **[LEAN]**

The owner: *"earlier I told you we needed hitpoints or something that we can drill down so
the player knows how far along combat is, and you suggested poise. So our bones are still
showing."* Footing swings beat to beat and cannot say how far along a fight is. **The wound
cap can** — it only falls, every real wound lowers it, and it never recovers mid-fight:

```
  ▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░┊        footing (swings) under the cap (only falls)
            ┊ ← how badly you are hurt: "how far along"
```

Derived from real wounds, nothing to pump up, and it means something afterwards.

## 5. The gaps, ranked by how many fights need them **[LEAN]**

1. **Aiming and armor gaps** (§ 2).
2. ⭐ **Bout rules as an institution's terms** — rounds, scoring, a referee, seconds, the
   ten-count. combat.md already says terms are a legal concept combat is only the first user
   of; a sport is terms plus an institution, and the three measurement layers (the engine
   counts touches, an institution scores, a polity rules).
3. **Who acts first, and composure** — the empty `g(composure)` seam.
4. **Space** — a doorway limiting engagement; improvised weapons from the room.
5. **Preparation** — coatings, bestiary weaknesses, traps.
6. **Grappling** — the pin, wrestling.
7. **Downed but not out** — one desperate move (a grab at someone who comes into close range)
   while the coup is delayed: what makes gloating dangerous.
8. **The play frame for violence** — staged fights.
9. ⭐ **Evading what you cannot block** — a foe far heavier than you erodes footing faster
   than any defend restores; you get out of the way rather than parry. *You can't parry a
   bear.*

## 6. ⭐⭐ Sport on campus — where it is learned safely **[DECIDED as the home — the owner]**

The owner: *"pedagogy — we probably want sparring and staged fights actually on the
university campus as sport."* [campus-grounds](./campus-grounds-slate.md) records **no
sparring mechanic at all**; [tourism-and-sport](../tails/tourism-and-sport-slate.md) § 5 already
argues that sport is the gym's social form and the campus its historical home.

Wasters (an oak waster ships), padded gear, non-lethal terms, a coach; a fencing team and a
boxing club; bout rules (§ 5.2) as the club's terms. Students learn the armor grid and the
footing contest with no one killed.

## 7. What other games teach **[EVIDENCE]**

- **Dark Souls** — stamina, reading the wind-up, learning patterns: already our shape.
- **Mount & Blade, For Honor** — the direction of an attack or a block: aiming in another form.
- ⭐ **Pokémon's type chart is our armor grid** — players learn type charts gladly.
- **Slay the Spire** — the enemy's next move is shown; our fog could show intent, clearer as
  you get sharper.
- **Hitpoints** (D&D, MUDs) — rejected; § 3 is the honest replacement.

## 8. The lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the armor grid as a type chart worth learning; reach, mass and tempo;
   sparring on campus.
2. **Expression** — fights as authored stories; the play frame for staged violence.
3. **Immersion** — the Viper's turn; the crowd at the arena. **Participation** — the coach,
   the cutman, the referee, the second, the bookmaker.
4. **Values** — consent in sport; the play frame; ⚠ fights between characters presented as
   children is a line to decide.
5. **Continuity** — wasters to live steel, the same footing contest.
6. **Economy** — purses, betting (the gap walk's vice item), coaching, the medic, gear and its
   upkeep.
7. **Governance** — dueling law, trial by combat, a sport's sanctioning body.
