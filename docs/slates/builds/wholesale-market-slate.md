## The owner's account (the spec)

*In high school I worked at a small family-run open-air produce stand. We grew
some of our own produce, and a couple of things like corn and strawberries were
delivered, but most of our stock came from vendors at the **South San Francisco
Produce Terminal**. No one ever went there except my boss, Chuck, who drove the
truck — he usually got up at three or four in the morning. After a year I really
wanted to see it, so two friends who also worked there and I went.*

- *Forklifts zipping around that supposedly had governors, but I know were going
  faster than 25 or whatever.*
- *It's entirely a cash business, all out in the open — you can walk around and
  witness everything first hand. If I were ever going to plan a heist, I'd rob the
  produce terminal.*
- *Two rows of stalls with trucks parked in the middle, and a little bar at the end
  of the row. I'm a student of bar culture and I've been to many kinds of bars, but
  the bar at the produce terminal was a single experience.*
- *Everyone was very familiar with each other. The same growers were in the same
  stalls every day. We only went twice a week, Tuesdays and Fridays.*
- *Besides the growers it's basically all the Bay Area small-market produce
  vendors. Imahara Produce across town went the same days we did, but they also did
  Japanese imports, so they went to the airport — right next to the terminal —
  regularly.*
- *It's all very personal: the growers show off what they have that day and convince
  you to buy more, and the vendors look at the quality and figure out how much it's
  worth. There's some haggling, but most of it seemed very rote, like people had
  standing orders.*
- ***The Crane melon** — grown by a single grower, the Crane family. It basically
  tastes like a Crenshaw but it's more durable and just better. Everyone wanted
  them, and the grower would ration them out — it wasn't about price, it was about
  inventory.*
- *If we weren't with Chuck we'd have gotten some different looks, but Chuck and the
  Stowell family have a legacy that goes back to some of these growers.*
- *The grocery stores don't go there — they buy direct from the growers. It's all
  medium and small market stuff. I don't know if anyone can just walk in and buy.*
- *Corn and strawberries we got delivered and didn't buy at the market — mostly
  because corn has such a low shelf life. Berries I'm not sure; maybe the same, or
  maybe because they're just a lead in the produce business.*

The general pattern matches the great markets — Smithfield, Billingsgate, Les
Halles, the Fulton Fish Market: they ran at night, in cash, at a pace outsiders
never saw, and their pubs were licensed to open at dawn because the porters' day
ended when everyone else's began.

## What it teaches — the seed

1. ⭐ **A market of relationships, not prices.** The same growers in the same
   stalls; the same buyers on the same days; most trade **standing orders**; the
   family's history with the growers deciding how you are received. Standing orders
   are **recurring contracts** (the contract subsystem; the standing-instructions
   slate); who you are to the growers is **regard** and the families' **chronicle**
   (households and their history); the looks strangers get are regard at work, not
   a gate. The markets doctrine's *dealing is a bid on a line* is the spot trade —
   the relationship sits under it.
2. ⭐⭐ **Allocation, not auction — the Crane melon.** One family's variety, scarce
   because one family grows it, **rationed by the grower to the buyers they choose,
   not sold to the highest bidder**. A seller may **allocate** instead of auction,
   and choosing who gets it is a relationship act with consequences for regard; the
   **variety** is the family's (a cultivar row only they grow, their acreage the
   ceiling); lens 4 (loyalty against price) and lens 6 (scarcity money cannot buy).
   The markets slate had not considered it.
3. **Grade judged on the spot** — the growers show off, the buyers read quality and
   decide what it's worth: appraisal, the shipped grade reading where competence
   decides the detail seen. **A good buyer's eye is the skill.**
4. ⭐ **Cash and in the open** — every trade a public act anyone in the room can
   witness: the measurement doctrine's transparency made physical — and the obvious
   heist (cash, thin security, a dead hour).
5. ⭐ **The channel is decided by the goods** — corn and berries came **direct**
   because their shelf life is too short to sit in a market (spoilage's clocks
   choose the route); big grocers buy **direct from growers**, so the terminal is the
   **small and middle trade's** place; **imports come through the airport next
   door** — markets cluster at transport hubs (the border slate's break-of-bulk
   hubs).
6. **Its shape and hours** — two rows of stalls, the trucks parked between; alive in
   the small hours and done by mid-morning (the world clock giving a place its own
   hours); probably **trade-only**; ⭐ **the soft rule broken in plain sight** (the
   forklifts' governors — the land-use covenant's soft law in its purest form); ⭐
   **the bar at the end of the row**, keeping the market's hours for the market's
   people.
