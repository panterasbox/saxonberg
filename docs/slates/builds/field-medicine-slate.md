## 1. What medicine is for here **[LEAN]**

The medium test ([law-and-order-slate](./law-and-order-slate.md) § 6): death does not heal (the
owner, 2026-10-28 → [mortal-vessel-slate](./mortal-vessel-slate.md)), so medicine protects the life
inside the window, the gear on the corpse, ⭐ **capability** (a leg wound costs walking, an open wound
festers), and ⭐⭐ **playable time** — wounds mend offline, but convalescence is zero unless safe. What
a medic gives you is *tonight*. The clinic bed is where you log off hurt; nobody spends an evening
sitting in one.

## 3. Public health and response **[LEAN]**

**Prevention is public health**, invisible when it works — water and drains, food safety, hygiene,
quarantine, all on shipped substrate. Its pedagogy gem is **John Snow's Broad Street pump (1854)**,
derivable because contamination is tracked by kind and source. **Response** is EMS and the clinic.
The split is the same budget dial as patrol against stations.

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ Scope of practice — from rules to standards **[LEAN]**

The module is policing's four parts (situation, playbook, policy, duty), with medicine's policy named
by real practice: **EMS runs on protocols under a medical director's standing orders.** That is the
legal-code slate's rules against standards one level down — **a protocol is a rule** (the NPC medic,
the EMT seat), **clinical judgment is a standard** (the player physician, graded by medic-judgment's
*the deed is the decision; the body is the grade*). Scope of practice is the ladder between them:
first-aider · EMT · nurse · physician · surgeon. Medicine runs the usual direction — the player is the
superset at the top. `analyze patient` returns candidates unranked, so an NPC on protocol can be
honestly wrong, and a player answers for it.

⭐ **One firewall, three times** — policing, forensics, medicine: *the engine knows, the person
infers.*

## 6. ⭐⭐⭐ The scenes **[LEAN, except where marked]**

| who | brings |
|---|---|
| a bystander | hands, a rag; `intervene`; the cry |
| a party member with first aid | a dressing, water, warmth |
| the party medic | `medicine` competence, `analyze patient`, `treat … for …`, a splint, an antidote |
| the constable | the duty to aid; securing the scene; the summons |
| the EMT crew | a seat, protocol, the kit bag, transport ⚠ |
| the nurse or physician | `operate`, `transfuse`, `prescribe`, tending |
| a mage | mend; can leave, cannot take anyone |

**1 · The fight is still going.** **Tactical Combat Casualty Care** (1996): care under fire — stop
the threat, get them out of the line, stop massive bleeding; then field care; then evacuation. The
engine holds most of it: the killing stroke is interruptible, so **the first act of anyone present
is `intervene`**; a fight heals nobody (convalescence is zero in combat; the footing cap reads wounds,
not dressings), so no one can be an MMO healer — a medic in a fight only stops the bleed clock. ⭐
**Treating someone in a live fight makes you a participant** — in reach and fair game; protected only
where a polity's law marks medics **[DECIDED — lens 3a chose; the owner did not object]**. ⚠
*"Dragging the victim clear also spares them"* — and no verb drags.

**2 · Bleeding out.** Systolic pressure holds to about 15 % loss while **the pulse pressure narrows
first**; shock at 30 %, the dying window at 36 %. A bystander's rag works and seeds sepsis days later —
still the right call. The medic reads what the bystander cannot and chooses which wound first. Past
30 %, the blood must be replaced: ⭐ **the walking blood bank** — whole blood from a compatible
teammate. Types are seeded on identity, so the party's blood types become field medicine.

**3 · The blade still in it.** It bleeds at 0.35× while embedded, and only surgery extracts it.
Pulling it out is the classic fatal mistake — ⭐ and **it should be possible**: wrong acts are allowed
wherever the body grades the result **[DECIDED — lens 1 chose; the owner did not object]**.

**4 · Fine, until they are not.** Blunt force tears an organ: *"something is wrong inside; you cannot
tell what"* — even to the victim. It wants surgery, and the clock is the bleed rate. **This scene is
why EMS exists** — the golden hour, made literal.

**5 · The broken leg in the den.** Not dying; cannot walk. The rule of four, the trip plan — and ⚠
blocked on carrying a person.

**6 · Rescuers become casualties.** *"Is the scene safe?"* — three shipped hazards punish the brave
untrained:

| hazard | the trap | the right way |
|---|---|---|
| bad air | run in and drop beside them | ventilate; the brigade with breathing apparatus |
| live current | touch them and join the circuit | cut the power — the grid's sever ships |
| drowning | swim out and drown together | ⭐ **reach, throw, row, go** |

**7 · Cold, poison, heat.** The wounded lying still get cold. *Flushed, sweating, disoriented* fits
heat, a toxin and an infection; the antidote names one toxin, and a wrong call spends the vial.

**8 · Three days later.** The rag festers; the nurse tends, and `undress` waits for the clot.

**9 · Eight casualties.** **START triage** — walking, immediate, delayed, ⭐ **expectant**. The engine
lets you make the hardest call and never makes it for you.

⭐ **Dying anyway** — the body you return in carries the wounds you died of
([mortal-vessel-slate](./mortal-vessel-slate.md)), so the smart play is to walk the shade to the
clinic before taking passage: the broken body is made at the door that can fix it. **Nothing heals
until you re-embody** — the shade is a degraded experience you want out of, and exploring as one is
allowed **[DECIDED — the owner]**: *"Being a shade is a degraded experience; you should want to get
out of it as fast as possible unless you're explicitly opting out, like using it to explore … we want
players to explore."* Its faint perception is the
[site-conditions-slate](./site-conditions-slate.md)'s.

## 7. The book **[LEAN]**

**Ship:** ⭐ **the Good Samaritan act** — aid to someone who cannot consent is lawful, so the
bystander helps; **the duty to report a death** to the coroner; ⭐ **a vestigial plague ordinance** —
*"no vessel from an infected port shall land within forty days"* (*quaranta giorni*) — dead law until
an outbreak makes it live.

**Leave to the legislature:** licensing (the College of Physic's charter) · the duty to rescue (none
in the US; a crime in France) · reporting violent wounds · treating those who cannot pay (the EMTALA
question) · live quarantine.
