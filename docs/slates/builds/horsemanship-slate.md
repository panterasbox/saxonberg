## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Riding is two minds, not a vehicle **[DECIDED — lenses 1 and 3a; the owner]**

**A horse is an agent with its own will, and riding is a negotiation.**

- **The rider asks; the horse decides** — pets' offer (*"a moment the animal decides"*)
  applied to every stride. The rider's **aids** (reins, legs, voice) ask; a horse that
  knows and trusts its rider (the bond ships) answers more readily.
- ⭐ **The spook** — a horse reacts to what it perceives: a sudden noise (hearing ships),
  something flapping, a smell. **How hard** it reacts is its **temperament** (seeded)
  against the stimulus; **whether the rider stays on** is the rider's **seat**
  (competence) against the spook's force. Deterministic — never rolled
  ([uncertainty.md](../../uncertainty.md)).
- **Falls are real harm** ([harm.md](../../subsystems/harm.md)) — riding has always been
  among the most dangerous ordinary things people do.
- **An unbroken horse cannot be ridden.** **Schooling** builds the horse's training over
  time with a handler; a green horse and a schoolmaster are different animals to ride.
- ⚠ **What it changes** — the horse stops being driven like a vehicle. `Mountable` stays
  (the rider occupies the mount slot); the horse's movement becomes **its own act,
  answering the rider's asks** through its brain ([behavior.md](../../subsystems/behavior.md)),
  not a `Drivable` host. (`drive` is deliberately unafforded today anyway.)

## 2. ⭐⭐ The horse is an athlete with its own body **[DECIDED]**

- **Gaits** — walk, trot, canter, gallop: speed paid for by **the horse's** wind
  ([exertion](../../subsystems/exertion.md)'s stocks, on the horse). A horse blows,
  lathers and needs rest, so **a long ride is managed at the trot**; galloping the whole
  way kills the horse.
- **Carrying capacity** — rider and tack around a fifth of the horse's weight, through
  [encumbrance](../../subsystems/encumbrance.md) on the horse.
- **Feed and water** — a working horse needs **grain**, not only grass (ranching's feed,
  [metabolism](../../subsystems/metabolism.md)).
- ⭐ **Hooves and shoes** — shoes wear by miles and surface (the
  [roads slate](./roads-slate.md)); **a thrown shoe** on the setts means lameness. Enter
  **the farrier** (a vocations GAP).
- **Tack** — saddle, bridle, bit, stirrups, in leather (tanning). ⭐ **Saddle fit** is
  textiles' fit: a badly fitted saddle makes **sores**. Enter **the saddler** (a GAP).
- **Grooming** — cleanliness's mud and sweat. **Stabling** — a horse's home; horses are
  herd animals, and one alone frets.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The horse trade — the dealer's honesty and the buyer's skill **[DECIDED]**

- ⭐ **Age is read from the teeth** — a derivable reading where competence resolves
  detail. The dealer's classic fraud was **"bishoping"** — filing an old horse's teeth to
  fake youth; hence *"don't look a gift horse in the mouth."* The horse fair is the
  [festival slate](./festival-slate.md)'s hook.
- **The studbook** — a breed registry and pedigree on ranching's herdbook; the
  Thoroughbred's *General Stud Book* (1791) is a governance institution for a breed.
- **Horse theft** was a hanging crime; **the brand** is chattel traceability on a living
  animal.

## 4. ⭐⭐ What horses are for — the demand was there first **[DECIDED — lens 6]**

| use | where it lands |
|---|---|
| personal travel — faster than walking, limited by wind and feed | locomotion, logistics |
| draft — carts and wagons | haulage ships; the [wheelwright](./wheelwright-slate.md) |
| ⭐ the courier and the relay — change horses to keep the speed (the Pony Express) | the post |
| herding from horseback | ranching's stockmanship |
| the race — a relative, measured contest; betting as a contract | sport, the festival |
| ploughing | farming |
| cavalry | the garrison, when it comes |
| the pit pony | mining ships |

## 5. Values and governance **[DECIDED]**

- ⭐ **Animal welfare law began with horses** — Britain's **Martin's Act (1822)**, the
  first animal-cruelty law, protected horses and cattle; *Black Beauty* (1877) was written
  against the overworked cab horse. The working horse's life is the polity's case.
- **The knacker** — the horse's end, already designed in the
  [rendering slate](../tails/rendering-slate.md) (knacker → tanner → chandler).
- **Liability** — the livery stable's, and the rider's on the road; road rules for riders.

## 6. Continuity **[DECIDED — lens 5]**

Horse → bicycle → car. ⭐ **"Horsepower" is literally the horse** — Watt's unit, the
[machines slate](./machines-slate.md)'s measure. The ride and draft acts survive; the
horse's role shrinks to sport and leisure, and its keep becomes a luxury.

## 7. The trades

**The farrier** (shoes, hoof care) · **the saddler** (tack and its fit) · **the livery
stable** (boarding and hiring horses — the "hack") · **the horse dealer** · **the vet**
(the animals cluster's GAP) · **the schooler** (breaking and training).

## 8. The lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the horse's physiology (gaits and wind, feed, hooves); reading age by
   teeth; animal behavior (the spook, herd animals); a riding Discipline at last.
2. **Expression** — a horse's name and temperament; a stable's character; regional tack.
3. **Immersion** — the ride managed at the trot; the spook at a flapping awning; the
   forge's hiss of a hot shoe. **Participation** — ⭐ the horse as a partner whose trust
   is earned; the dealer and the buyer; the farrier, the saddler, the livery.
4. **Values** — the working horse's welfare; the knacker; the race's whip.
5. **Continuity** — horsepower; horse to engine.
6. **Economy** — the horse as capital; feed, shoes and tack as steady costs; the livery and
   the hack; the relay post.
7. **Governance** — cruelty law, the studbook, horse theft and the brand, liability.

**The drive, in one line:** at the horse fair you check a mare's teeth and catch the
bishoping; you buy an honest one, tack up, and trot to the Delight to save her wind; she
spooks at a flapping awning and your seat holds; she throws a shoe on the setts, and you
lead her lame to the farrier.

## 9. Open

3. **Schooling** — a training stock on the horse, and what each level permits.
4. **The relay** — how the post changes horses, and the stations.
5. **Mounted combat** — the cavalry, when the garrison has a front.
