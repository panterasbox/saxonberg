# Diplomacy slate — the embassy, between realms and between servers

> **Status: UNBUILT** — designed at high level 2026-10-19 with the owner,
> the first item of the gap walk (*"nothing at all on embassies or
> ambassadors"*). Jurisdiction is a field on the Locality and jurisdictions
> STACK (a dorm room resolves university, city, realm); no jurisdiction cuts
> the chain today. Federation between servers is designed as records only
> ([antecedents-slate](./antecedents-slate.md) Phase B) with no place, person
> or ceremony.
> **Left:** the embassy as a jurisdiction that cuts the HOST'S SEATS, never
> its laws (inviolability) · the envoy's immunity and persona non grata as the
> host's one remedy · the diplomatic bag and the tax exemption · asylum (it
> falls out) · recognition = a lot granted · a Government row that stands
> for another server · the embassy as the place foreign records are presented
> · the four phases toward a body politic of servers
> **Size:** a build for phase 1; the later phases are their own

## 0. The frame

The owner: *"the embassy that actually matters to me is an embassy for someone
from another game server entirely. like the beginning of a federated model for
distributed game servers all coordinating as a single body politic … I'd think
it starts with embassies in the saxonberg locale as new servers are recognized
diplomatically. that said, you can take that same embassy design and apply it
to the fictional realms and it still holds up. specifically the idea that an
embassy is sovereign that's real lens 7 shit."*

**The convergence.** [civics.md](../../subsystems/civics.md): *"declaring
jurisdiction is authoring the Locality … Federation is pointing at an existing
key; secession is repointing."* An embassy is ground whose jurisdiction field
names a foreign government. The antecedents slate already holds the federal
principle (*"a unilateral, revocable acceptance decision by each sovereign
instance"*, the acceptance policy *"a published artifact"*); the embassy is the
place it was missing.

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Inviolable, not sovereign **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

Under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) an embassy is **not**
the sending state's territory — that is a popular misconception. Its premises
are **inviolable**: the host's law still applies, but the host's agents may not
enter without the head of mission's consent. The Ecuadorian embassy in London
is the clean case: British law applied to Assange for seven years, and the
police waited outside.

Lens 1 chose the real thing over the myth, and it is the stronger lens-7 answer:
**the law applies and enforcement stops at the door** — the gap where every
interesting case lives.

So the embassy is the **first jurisdiction that cuts the chain**, and what it
cuts is the host's **seats**, never its laws:

- **The place — inviolability.** No host office acts inside: no arrest, no
  search, no inspection, no exclusion declared over it. In the terms of the
  settlement model's exclusion sanction (*"a declaration, by an authority,
  over an extent"*), the embassy is **the extent the host's authority does not
  reach**. The barrier belongs to the GROUND, not the staff — it holds with
  nobody home.
- **The person — immunity.** The envoy cannot be tried by the host. The host's
  one remedy is **persona non grata**: an exclusion over the realm's whole
  extent, declared by its government — [settlement-model](../../settlement-model.md)
  § 9's sanction applied to a diplomat. The sending state recalls or the envoy
  leaves.
- **The exemptions.** No tax on the premises; ⭐ **the diplomatic bag** passes
  customs unopened — an explicit exemption in the customs design
  ([ships-and-harbors-slate](./ships-and-harbors-slate.md) § 19,
  [land-use-covenant-slate](./land-use-covenant-slate.md)). Abuse of the bag is
  a real temptation and a real scandal, which is the point.
- ⭐⭐ **Asylum falls out.** A person sanctioned by the host who reaches the
  door is beyond the host's reach for as long as the embassy will keep them.
  Nothing is built for it; it is the first three together. Lens 4 lives here:
  the embassy decides whom to shelter, and the host decides whether a standoff
  is worth a rupture.
- **The rupture.** Breaking relations closes the embassy; the premises revert,
  and whoever was sheltered is exposed. The host may also violate the premises
  — possible, recorded, and an act the other polity reads.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Where embassies go **[DECIDED — the owner]**

- **Embassies of other servers are in the City of Saxonberg** — the seat of
  government, our Washington, D.C. ([saxonberg-city-slate](./saxonberg-city-slate.md)):
  the government's parcel, run by a committee answering to the executive and
  through them to the legislature, and *"the one place where the government can
  tell you what to build."* Embassy Row is the legislature's content to lay
  out.
- ⭐ **Recognition and the land grant are one act.** The government recognizes
  a server by granting it a lot in Saxonberg. Recognition is physical and
  visible — a building on a street — never a flag in a table.
- **Fictional realms' embassies are in their own capitals** (the Terminus
  realm's in Terminus). The two planes never share a street.
- ⚠ **The Saxonberg venue rule holds: a venue is never a workplace.** No
  diplomatic act requires presence. An embassy provides ceremony, an address,
  and **legibility** — the other polity's charter, its treaties and its
  published acceptance policy are readable with nobody home. Presenting your
  records works while the ambassador is offline.

## 3. One design on two planes **[DECIDED]**

| | between fictional realms | between servers |
|---|---|---|
| the sending state | a `Government` in the catalogue | ⭐ a `Government` row that stands for **another server** — its authority lives elsewhere |
| recognition | that realm's government, by its own seats | ⭐ **the Compact's government**, by granting a Saxonberg lot |
| the envoy | a seat, held by a cast character or a player | a seat held by **a player of the other server**, accredited here |
| credentials | letters of credence ([credential.md](../../subsystems/credential.md) already has `presentsKey`) | the other server's signed identity |
| consular work | helping your nationals abroad | ⭐ **the place foreign records are presented**: a visitor from server B brings their transcripts to B's embassy, reads our acceptance policy there, and has them accepted or refused — the federation protocol made physical |
| treaties | documents between governments ([legal-code-slate](./legal-code-slate.md)) | bilateral acceptance terms, readable by anyone |

## 4. ⭐⭐ Listing is not recognition **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

Two acts that look like one:

- **Listing** a server in the client's realm directory is the **operator's**
  act — the operator carries the Steam liability
  ([distribution-and-hosting-slate](./distribution-and-hosting-slate.md) § 4).
- **Recognizing** it is the **polity's** act — entrenchment tier C, with a named
  criterion and an appeal ([measurement.md](../../measurement.md) layer 3).

A server can be listed and unrecognized (you can visit; there is no embassy and
your records count for nothing), or recognized by treaty before any shared
directory exists. Keeping them apart stops the operator quietly becoming the
foreign ministry.

## 5. The phases **[DECIDED in order]**

1. **Embassies between fictional realms** — the cut chain, the envoy's seat,
   immunity and persona non grata, the bag, asylum. Entirely inside one server;
   buildable now.
2. **Recognition of another server** — begins as a **human act with no
   protocol**: the government recognizes, a Saxonberg lot is granted, and the
   other server's delegation staffs it through accounts here. The embassy holds
   their charter and acceptance policy.
3. **The protocol** — signed record exports presented at the embassy
   (antecedents Phase B: what signs, push or pull, revocation).
4. **The body politic** — a multilateral treaty, a league of servers, in time a
   congress. The embassy is its first organ, and nothing has to federate before
   it exists.

## 6. Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — inviolability vs sovereignty, immunity, recognition, the
   consular function, treaties: international law as lived procedure. Decided
   § 1.
2. **Expression** — an embassy's building, ceremony and reading room are the
   sending polity's content on ground the host granted.
3. **Immersion** — the door you cannot be followed through. **Participation** —
   *can the polity do something we did not want?* Shelter a fugitive; abuse the
   bag; violate the premises; break relations.
4. **Values** — whom to shelter; whether a standoff is worth a rupture;
   recognition of a server whose values differ from ours.
5. **Continuity** — the same embassy across epochs; between servers, it survives
   either side's upgrades because what crosses is records and documents.
6. **Economy** — a granted lot; the bag's temptation; consular fees if a polity
   levies them.
7. **Governance** — ⭐ the whole slate: a jurisdiction that cuts enforcement,
   immunity, persona non grata (criterion, appeal), recognition as tier C,
   listing kept with the operator.

## 7. Open

1. **What the barrier cuts, exactly** — which seats and acts count as
   enforcement (arrest, search, exclusion, inspection; a tax assessment?).
2. **The envoy's seat** — an office of the SENDING government held on our
   server; how a foreign-server player holds a seat here (an account here, or a
   presented identity).
3. **Who may enter** — the head of mission's consent as a door policy.
4. **What a recognized server's lot costs** and who builds on it.
5. **Phase 3's protocol questions** — antecedents Phase B's open list.
