## 1. What exists

- **Policing is designed in depth and none of it is built** — the department, law against
  policy, the six policy dials, the whistle (*"you are fighting the response time of a
  department"*), the kit of denial, *wanted* as belief, the Gray. Enforcement, intervention,
  courts and prison hang off it.
- **Emergency care has the bedside and not the emergency** — nothing gets anyone *to* the patient
  or the patient to care.
- [settlement-model.md](../../settlement-model.md) § 9 already holds the four threat axes, the
  enforcement mode per settlement and the outward progression from Terminus to the wilds.

## 2. ⭐⭐ One spine — the call for help **[LEAN]**

Someone summons (a whistle, a horn, a phone, the aether), a dispatcher routes it, responders cross
real ground. **Response time is geography for both services** — the criminal races it, and so
does the patient. It is the call slate's unbuilt push column (dispatch, the turnout, the hue and
cry), which already decided that *radio changed the reach, not the mechanism*.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The dying windows decide what EMS is **[LEAN]**

The windows run 90 seconds (asphyxiation, electrocution) to 10 minutes (starvation, toxin). A crew
crossing a district rarely makes 90 seconds, so:

> **The bystander saves the life; the service saves the hour after.**

First aid is a competence for everyone (*stop the bleed*, bystander CPR), and the service's work
is transport, triage, handoff, and the wound not killing you *yet*. The windows stay where they
are.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ Response time = density × (epoch or magic) × budget **[LEAN]**

- **Density decides *who* responds.** Informal control works where everyone is known; police are
  an invention of anonymity — the hue and cry failed in 1820s London because nobody knew whose
  shout it was. That is the settlement model's own rule, *a city is where the general store
  fragments*, applied to law and care: in a village everybody is the watch and the herb-wife is
  the clinic; in a city those split into constable, detective, market inspector, coroner,
  ambulance and surgeon.
- **Epoch decides *how far and how fast*** — the kit: the summons' reach, the responders' speed,
  the patient's.
- **Magic substitutes for epoch** (§ 5) — the term a medieval author can open without breaking
  the aesthetic.
- **The polity decides *how much*** — budget, policy, who pays.
- ⚠ **Epoch and density are independent.** Medieval London had a watch, constables and a coroner;
  an industrial mining camp runs on the hue and cry.

### By epoch — the kit

| | the summons | police | care | moving the hurt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **prehistory** | shout; a signal fire on the hill (a *place*) | kin, feud, exile | kin, the healer | carried by your people |
| **medieval** | church bell, horn, the hue and cry (a legal duty, 1285) | watch and ward, the parish constable by rotation, sheriff, outlawry, sanctuary; ⭐ **the coroner (1194)** | infirmary, barber-surgeon, midwife | litter, cart |
| **industrial** | ⭐ **the call box** (a place again), telegraph, whistle | Peelers, detectives, fingerprints | ⭐ **lay first aid** (St John, 1877); **mine rescue brigades**; ⭐ **the Humane Society** (1774) — drowning, rescue stations on the river | horse ambulance; triage (Larrey) |
| **modern** | phone, emergency number, radio | radio cars, forensics, CCTV | paramedics, CPR, defibrillators | motor ambulance, helicopter |
| **future** | ⭐ **the implant calls for you** | predictive policing, drones | remote monitoring, drone-carried kit | — |

- **The summons keeps returning to a place** — fire hill, bell tower, call box — the same thing
  as the prehistoric comms node.
- ⭐ **The implant can call the ambulance while you are unconscious** — *the inability to be
  disconnected* as a reason to choose it, and a privacy cost with it (it knows when you are dying).
- **The industrial column is waiting content** — accidents, mine damps and drowning all ship.

### By density — who comes, and who pays

| | police | care | who pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the wilds** | nobody | ⭐ whoever is in your party | you |
| **camp** | the hue and cry | the mine rescue brigade, the company first-aider | the company, or mutual aid |
| **village** | the parish constable, custom | the herb-wife, the bell, neighbors carry you | standing — volunteers paid in regard |
| **suburb** | ⚠ nobody (settlement-model § 9) | a volunteer squad | subscription |
| **city** | a constabulary | ambulance, infirmary, specialists | tax or fee-for-service — a vote |

The pay column is the settlement model's *care* need — *voluntary, a service bought, never an
obligation levied* — shifting with density from regard to subscription to tax.

⚠ **The suburb** — total impunity and the thinnest care, where most players live. Is that
right, or is it the first place a volunteer squad forms, as player content? **[OPEN]**

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ Magic is its own permission **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"Nothing stops something mana-powered from interacting with the aether field, and magic is its
own axis — you can take magic into medieval if you want and the parcel allows magic. … Both
future and magic function the same, but they have different permissions structures."*

- **Function is shared** — design-lenses § 5: an object from any epoch answers the same commands.
- **Permission is separate, because the shapes differ.** Technology is a **ladder** (*nothing
  later than industrial*); magic is a **presence** (allowed, not, or which kinds).
- ⚠ [Epoch.ts](../../../packages/server/src/mud/lib/craft/Epoch.ts) says *"`future` is the last
  word and not a separate list"* — true of function, wrong of permission. Until this lands, a
  medieval parcel that allows magic cannot be expressed.

### The grid **[DECIDED — lenses 2 and 1 chose]**

- **The magic term filters by verb and noun** — the vocabulary `suppressesMagic` already ships.
  The covenant term is the **soft, voted** twin of that **hard**, physical field.
- Lens 2 chose: a parish that allows healing and bans fire. Lens 1 agreed: the grid's axes *are*
  the Disciplines, so the posted law names what it permits.

### Two stamps **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

Every object carries the **epoch it was built in** and **whether it is magical**, and both must
pass. A mana-powered needle-grade charm passes a medieval ceiling that allows magic; a
mana-powered cell phone fails the ceiling whatever powers it. The power source is the shipped
wall socket (`ManaPowered`) and changes nothing about what the device is.

## 6. ⭐⭐⭐ What the postulate decides **[DERIVED — arcane-science]**

The owner: *"our lenses should help some here even though magic isn't necessarily 'real'."*

- **No evacuation by spell.** *"You can only teleport yourself. A caster cannot relocate a third
  party."* A dying patient is unconscious and cannot cast; the medic cannot take them. A mage can
  **leave, alone** — abandoning the party is a real choice with nothing forcing it.
- **The sending is station to station, one way.** Information is energetically free and limited by
  *specification* — the reason the teleport network is a graph of surveyed terminals. So a caster
  in the wilds can send **to** a surveyed station, and nothing can answer, because the wilds are
  unsurveyed. And a probe is a signal: a call for help can also give you away.
- **Wards follow density.** An alarm ward is held, so it is a **binding**, and *magic is poor at
  what must be held* — it needs a supply, the piped aether line. Surveillance by magic — the
  enforcement slate's *camera* mode — concentrates where the line runs.

## 7. ⭐⭐⭐ The party **[LEAN, except where marked]**

### No healer class

The medic is whoever has the competence and the kit — anyone may `treat` with a dressing, graded by
`medicine`. ⭐ **Everyone carries their own kit, because your kit is what someone else uses on
you.**

### What threatens a party

1. **Dying** — only someone present can act.
2. ⭐ **Wounded, not dying** — a leg wound costs walking. The problem is getting out a person who
   cannot walk.
3. **Exposure** — cold in 300 seconds; shock and cold together are how backcountry patients die.
   Warmth is medical kit.
4. **No mending while hunted** — harm.md D3a: convalescence is zero in combat or soon after harm.
   The party must make a safe camp, and the **camp watch** is a duty rota.
5. **The slowest member sets everyone's escape** — the bestiary's *escape* quotient. One leg
   wound collapses it for the whole party.

### ⭐⭐ The rule of four

*Never fewer than four: if one is hurt, one stays and two go for help* — someone must stay, one
runner alone is the next casualty, and a 70 kg body takes two bearers. Party size is a safety
decision a player can calculate. A mage's one-way sending can replace the two runners.

### Calling for help off grid

| method | reaches | needs |
|---|---|---|
| runners | the nearest station, on foot | two people and the walk |
| the sending | a surveyed station, one way | a caster |
| horn, signal fire | whoever is out there | someone out there |
| ⭐ **the trip plan** | the lodge, *before you left* | nothing in the field |

⭐⭐ **The trip plan** — you sign out with where you are going and when you will be back; overdue,
they come looking. It needs no comms at all, and it converges with the `route` verb, whose plan
*"is a hypothesis that states its assumptions"*: **a trip plan is a route plan left with
someone**, and the overdue party is the push column's first wilderness trigger.

### ⭐⭐ The lodge — a generic archetype **[DECIDED — the owner]**

One `archetype` row says what any lodge has: the posted edge of coverage · the sign-out book · a
surveyed stone that receives sendings · the kit counter · the rescue roster (volunteers paid in
standing, bounties for retrieval). A second lodge needs no pack code.

### Dying anyway — the corpse run

Gear goes to the corpse (*"lost by design — this is the stake"*), and `passage` gives a body where
the shade stands. Die in the den and take passage there, and you stand in the den without gear.
So the shade walks home, re-equips and goes back — **the cost of death grows with remoteness on
its own**, with no knob. Retrieval is a job the lodge's roster can take, paid by the dead.

### Custody **[DECIDED — the owner]**

Carrying a dead friend's gear is **custody, held for the owner** — chattel already records custody
without title and leaves *what is theft* to the courts, so this needs no mechanism. Added: the
owner can see who holds their things (the person-targeting slate's records). ⭐ chattel.md also
says *"custody of a body is a different concept, and it belongs to the deferred coroner economy"*
— the coroner of § 9.

### Kit against loot

Encumbrance is real: every kilo of kit is a kilo of loot not brought home. A party that skimps is
betting, and the bestiary's readout lets them price the bet.

| | stops bleeding | splints | keeps warm | evacuates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| medieval | linen, pressure | sticks and cord | wool, fire | litter, carrying |
| industrial | gauze, tourniquet | splint | blanket | litter, horse |
| modern | hemostatic dressing | vacuum splint | foil blanket | helicopter — coverage and a landing |
| magic | the mend spell | — | a warming binding, draining while held | ⛔ none |

## 8. Gaps found

- ⚠ **Nothing carries a person** — no litter or stretcher in content; hitching hauls a cart with
  one hauler. The wounded-not-dying case needs a two-bearer litter or a fireman's carry first.
- ⚠ **No trip plan or overdue trigger** — `route` makes the plan; nothing leaves it with anyone.
- **The NPC field medic for hire** needs the medic brain medic-judgment lists as unbuilt.

## 9. ⭐⭐⭐ Players and NPCs in the seats

### The NPC specifies the seat **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"I basically want to completely design the NPC, and then the player is going to be performing some
kind of variant of that experience. It's a little weird, because the NPC experience is usually a
subset of the player's, because a player has more agency — but in this regard I think we want the
opposite to be true, because we're dealing with governance. At least the law enforcement stuff.
The medical services one I don't feel as strongly about."*

The policing slate makes policy **executable** — the brain reads the commissioner's dials at the
act — so the NPC officer is the policy embodied, and the seat has a complete definition:

- **A player's authority is never more than the NPC's** — the seat grants only what policy grants
  the brain.
- **A player's conduct may differ** — judgment where the brain applies a dial mechanically,
  measured against the same dial. That is the player's agency.
- ⚠ **A seat confers authority, never verbs** (verb conferral is retired). Anyone may `restrain`;
  a citizen's arrest is real. The seat adds **standing** — the ledger reads the same act as
  authorized force or as assault by who did it, under what policy, on duty or not (the
  intervention slate's *terms that authorize force to stop force*). A citizen who tries what only
  an officer may do is told who may.

### Witness for conduct, wall for kit **[DECIDED — the owner; lens 1 chose]**

- **A player officer who goes past policy is not refused; the act is recorded.** The policing
  slate's lesson is *legal but against policy* — fired, not charged — and its loop runs on *the
  gap between written policy and actual practice.* NPCs cannot produce that gap; only players can.
- **The kit is a wall.** The armory issues the department's sidearm only on duty, and it comes
  back at the end of the shift — an object refused at a boundary. *No firearms, except a
  warrant-holder on duty* is a license in the law, applied uniformly, not an exception.

### ⭐⭐ The policy check at the act **[LEAN]**

Every dial is a predicate on the record, for any officer: **was the stop above the threshold** (the
threshold is about **belief**, and the engine knows what the officer's *character* believed) · was
force within the ceiling · was there authority to search · was aid rendered.

⭐ Belief is in-fiction knowledge, so **an officer who stops someone on what they read on Discord
has no belief to justify it**, and the record shows an off-policy stop — an honest answer to *one
line is every line* ([off-grid-slate](./off-grid-slate.md) § 3), at least for officers. The dials
are the counterfactual; nothing simulates what an NPC would have done.

### NPC corruption is authored, never emergent **[DECIDED — the owner]**

An NPC follows policy exactly. The policing slate's *constable on the take* is a cast NPC with a
second master, written and named. An NPC never deviates by accident.

### ⭐⭐⭐ A player answers for every NPC **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"There's always a player accountable for everything that happens in the game. If an NPC uses
excessive force, the committee that maintains the parcel the NPC comes from is the defendant. They
may have acted within the poetic license given to all authors by the Compact, or they may have been
violating baseline player rights also guaranteed by the Compact. That would need to be adjudicated
in Compact courts, and if the NPC was out of bounds it may compel code changes, as well as maybe
even something recompensatory by the defendant."*

- **The defendant is found by where the NPC comes from, not where it acted** — title is who
  maintains the code, not who owns the land in the fiction; chattel's parcel rung keys on the
  template path the same way.
- **Two outcomes:** within **poetic license** (a harsh town, a brutal watch — consented to on entry
  under posted law), or over the **rights floor**. ⚠ The line between them is the rights list, and
  Art. IX is open ([off-grid-slate](./off-grid-slate.md) § 8) — one more reason it cannot wait for
  the convention.
- **Both faces of Art. VI judge it** — **verification** (*"that an implementation meets the
  requirement it claims; code review"*: did the code do what the posted policy says?) and
  **spirit** (was it fair?).
- **The remedy** — code changes are Art. VI § 7's **cure order** with a remediation window, as
  drafted. ⚠ **Compensation is new**: Art. VI has no damages. It would be paid from the defendant
  committee's account through the ledger's one payment path.

#### Sometimes there are two defendants **[LEAN]**

Policy is read where the NPC stands, so a brain can follow its code while following *another*
committee's policy:

| what went wrong | defendant |
|---|---|
| the brain broke the policy (a bug, a bad brain) | the committee holding title over the brain's code |
| the brain followed a policy that breaks the rights floor | the committee whose extent the policy covers |
| a kernel brain misbehaved | ⭐ the executive — the platform has maintainers too |

#### The record an NPC act needs **[LEAN]**

The **policy version** in force (already designed) · ⭐ **the code version** the brain ran at —
verification needs to know which code acted · **the NPC's belief** at the act, against the dials.

#### Two tracks, kept apart **[LEAN]**

- **In the fiction** — the department, the policy, discipline, the local courts.
- **Outside the fiction** — the Compact court, the authors, cure orders, compensation. The prison
  slate's guardrail the other way round: real responsibility never gets a costume, and never gets
  lost inside one.

### The practical differences **[LEAN]**

| | NPC officer | player officer |
|---|---|---|
| availability | always on shift | clocks on and off; an NPC covers a beat no player holds (the call slate's `rota`) |
| logging off mid-arrest | — | custody passes to an NPC, never dropped |
| judgment | the dials, mechanically | their own, measured against the dials |
| a breach | the policy's, and the code's maintainers' | theirs, and on the department's record |
| the kit | from the armory | from the armory, only on duty |
| the seat | appointed | appointed by the commissioner; the Discipline as a requirement is a committee dial |

### Medicine, more lightly **[LEAN]**

The usual direction fits: medic-judgment grades the *decision*, so the player is the superset and
an NPC medic is the floor. Where medicine acts on a person without asking — **implied consent** for
the unconscious, **restraining** the delirious, **quarantine**, ⭐ **mandatory reporting** (a stab
wound reported to the watch, a second bridge between the services) — it is **scope of practice**,
the police pattern with a few dials.

## 9a. ⭐⭐⭐ The coroner

The bridge between the two services: every death care does not prevent becomes the question *was
it a crime?* [end-of-life-slate](./end-of-life-slate.md) owns custody of the body, remains and *"the
coroner economy"*; this is the office. What ships: `analyze postmortem` (cause inferred from the
wounds, never read off the stamp; readability falls with decay; the `forensics` Discipline),
per-corpse identity, `order burial`.

### The decisions **[DECIDED — the owner]**

*"Morgue as durable host, NPCs don't come back, polity pays."* And then: *"Cast NPCs come back too —
anything staged gets restaged. If we have to deal with some diegetic disagreement, we'll deal with
it."*

- ⭐ **The morgue is the durable host.** A corpse is not persisted — mortality.md: it *"does not
  survive a restart"* — so a deploy erases the evidence, the body and the gear on it. Custody moves
  the body into a host that persists (*capture the host*), and the body, the evidence and the
  effects survive together. ⚠ Everything outside a morgue still vanishes at a restart — what the
  party carries out is safe on a persisted body; what is left in the den is not.
- **NPCs do not come back the way players do** — no shade, no `passage`. **Anything staged is
  restaged**, cast and extras alike. The corpse stays behind as evidence while the restaged NPC
  walks the world; the diegetic disagreement is dealt with when it arises.
- **The polity pays.** The coroner is a department with a budget line, like the watch; an
  unclaimed body goes to potter's field at public cost.

### The office's bundle **[LEAN]**

1. **Custody** of the body — end-of-life's stage 1.
2. **Identity** — who is this.
3. **Cause and manner** of death.
4. **The effects** — the gear inventoried and held for the owner: § 7's custody, run by an office.
5. **Release** — to whoever claims the body, or to potter's field.
6. **Referral** — an unlawful death goes to the watch, as medicine's mandatory reporting does.

### ⭐⭐ Cause is a fact; manner is a finding **[LEAN]**

- **Cause** is the mechanism — the engine knows it, the coroner infers it from the body.
- **Manner** — natural, accident, homicide, suicide, undetermined — is a **legal finding**, the
  intrinsic/social split again.
- **The evidence firewall holds.** The harm ledger already derives who killed whom; the coroner
  never reads it. The finding is a **claim on the record**, from the body and the witnesses, and
  can be wrong — *honest error* is how a murder is ruled an accident. A finding is appealable to
  the local court.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The dead testify at their own inquest **[LEAN]**

A player comes back, so the victim can stand before the coroner and say who did it:

- **belief makes it honestly uncertain** — ambushed from behind, in the dark, by someone hooded;
- **it is testimony, so it can be a lie** — framing a rival, a faked death for a burial club's
  payout; the enforcement slate's lie discriminator covers it;
- **no speak-with-dead magic is needed** — a corpse holds no mind, and the person is already
  walking around.

### By epoch and density **[LEAN]**

| | who | how |
|---|---|---|
| village | the priest and neighbors | the township keeps the body unmoved until authority comes |
| medieval | ⭐ the crowner (1194) | an inquest *on view of the body*, with a jury of local men; the township fined for burying first |
| industrial | the coroner and the police surgeon | inquest and autopsy |
| modern | ⭐ the medical examiner | a physician, autopsy, toxicology |
| future | — | the implant's record of how you died — instrument evidence, contestable |
| the wilds | nobody | no inquest — the necropolis's impunity hole, by the same mechanism |

Two committee dials, not defaults:

- **Coroner or medical examiner** — many US counties still *elect* a coroner with no medical
  training, others appoint a physician. Legitimacy against expertise, the same axis as *the
  credential required to serve*.
- **The inquest jury is sortition** — the constitution's one process at local scale.

### ⭐ The deodand — law a polity may write **[LEAN]**

The medieval coroner was a revenue officer: whatever caused a death was forfeited to the crown, the
ancestor of **civil asset forfeiture**. Never the funding — the polity pays — but law content a
polity may adopt, with the controversy that comes with it.

### The seat **[LEAN]**

Seat-first, as § 9 decided: the NPC coroner is the procedure (custody, examine, inventory, find,
release, refer); a player coroner's judgment is the examination and the finding, graded by the
shipped competence ladder.
