# Construction slate — building something that is not there yet

> **Status: UNBUILT** — parked 2026-10-18 from the underwater session; not
> yet designed. Structures exist only finished → the rubber build's `construction.md`
> (in review: what a place is made of), [assembly.md](../../subsystems/assembly.md)
> (a made thing that keeps its parts), the repair build (upkeep of
> envelope layers).
> **Left:** the whole conversation — a structure that exists half-built,
> and the labour that raises it
> **Size:** a build

The founder: *"we don't really have a lot of actual construction mechanics;
all of our structures clone fully built or not at all. We do have a few
things like carpentry but right now there's no demand for something like a
welder or the rivet guy or anyone else that does shit underwater."*

## What is already decided around it

- **The underwater trade's demand is REPAIR, not construction**
  ([underwater-slate](./underwater-slate.md) § 11): water wears the works
  that already exist — piles, intakes, outfalls, weirs, hulls — so a diver
  is needed on a schedule physics sets. That does not wait for this slate.
- Building **in** water — bridge piers, harbour walls, the cofferdam, the
  caisson (underwater § 7, the bell as a Location) — is this slate's
  hardest case and the hard-hat diver's actual historical job.

## Questions to open with

1. What is a half-built structure — a structure with a stage, a set of
   rooms that appear, or an assembly whose parts arrive?
2. Who pays for it, and who is it for — public works (the markets
   faucet), a player's own build, a commission?
3. How the labour divides (mason, carpenter, smith, the diver below the
   waterline), and what each brings.
4. How it meets the parcel and the content author — does a player's
   construction become authored content (succession, not class change)?
