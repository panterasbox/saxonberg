## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ A gem's beauty is derived **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

[glass.md](../../subsystems/glass.md) decided *"color is a grade, not a choice"* —
iron tints glass by Beer–Lambert. Gems are the same physics:

- **Color from trace elements** — chromium makes corundum a ruby and beryl an
  emerald; iron and titanium make corundum a sapphire. One row, an impurity deciding
  the color: the mineral is the identity, the color a grade.
- ⭐ **Brilliance from refractive index and cut** — light entering a well-cut stone
  reflects off the inside of the back facets and returns to the eye (total internal
  reflection past the critical angle the index sets); a badly cut diamond looks
  dead. **Fire** is dispersion: the index differs by color.
- **Hardness** (Mohs) and **cleavage** — properties
  [materials-response](../../subsystems/materials-response.md) already reads.

A gem's look is computed from its material, impurity and cut — never authored as
"sparkly".

## 2. ⭐⭐ The identification ladder, and paste **[DECIDED]**

[instrumentation.md](../../subsystems/instrumentation.md): competence resolves
detail, never access.

- **Streak** (ships) — a mineral's powder color beats its surface color.
- **Scratch** — the harder scratches the softer.
- ⭐ **`feel`** — diamond conducts heat so well it feels cold on the lip: the
  principle of real electronic diamond testers. The touch channel ships.
- **Specific gravity** — weigh in air and in water; density is on every row.
- **The loupe and the refractometer** — instruments with a ceiling.
- ⭐ **Paste** — lead-glass imitation gems, a real eighteenth-century craft, made
  by the glass trade. It fools most eyes and fails the tests; the connoisseur is
  somebody who knows the tests.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The lapidary — the hardness ladder decides what cuts what **[DECIDED]**

- **Cleave** along the crystal's planes (extraction's shipped `split` protocol,
  [ground.md](../../subsystems/ground.md)); **saw** where it will not cleave; then
  **grind, facet, polish**.
- ⭐ **An abrasive must be harder than the stone** — emery (impure corundum) cuts
  almost everything; only diamond dust cuts diamond. Derivable from the ladder.
- **Cabochon** is the old, forgiving cut; **faceting** the precision craft, the
  cutter's grade setting the brilliance.
- **Weight lost to the cut** is the lapidary's economy: a bigger stone or a better
  one.

## 4. ⭐⭐ Precious metals — assay, refining, the hallmark **[DECIDED]**

- **Gold, silver, platinum** as rows; **silver from the sulfides**, already designed.
- **Cupellation** — silver separated from lead in a bone-ash cup: a role for the
  orphaned **lead**, and rendering's bone ash. **Parting** gold from silver with
  acid waits on the acid works.
- ⭐ **The touchstone** — streak gold on a black stone and compare it with streaks
  from needles of known purity: the classic purity test, a `Reading` row.
- ⭐⭐ **The hallmark — lens 7.** English law from 1300 required silver to be tested
  before sale; the Goldsmiths' Company did the testing, and *hallmark* is the mark
  struck at **Goldsmiths' Hall**. An institution certifying what an instrument
  measures — the three-layer measurement pattern of
  [horology](./horology-slate.md) and [performance](./performance-slate.md). Here it
  is a **guild act** — the [guild slate](./guild-slate.md)'s second real feature
  after the map room. A forged hallmark is a crime with a long history.
- **The smith's crafts** — raising, soldering, filigree, gilding, enamel (glass on
  metal — the glass trade), engraving.

## 7. Where they are found — geology decides rarity **[DECIDED]**

Rarity comes from the **seeded deposit**, never a value field:

- **Pegmatites** grow giant crystals (beryl, tourmaline, topaz) in **granite** — the
  Ferrow's granite yet again: tin, pitchblende ([radioactivity-slate](./radioactivity-slate.md))
  and gems.
- **Placers** — sapphires, gold and diamonds concentrated in river gravel; the
  Weeping Moor's stream-tin district is a placer already.
- **Geodes** — amethyst and agate in volcanic rock: Waponi Woo's ground.
- **Kimberlite pipes** — diamonds, rare and deep.

## 9. ⭐⭐⭐ Where luxury's scarcity comes from — the lens discussion

The [markets slate](./markets-slate.md)'s luxury section settled the frame: *value is
only what someone pays; restocking is the author's dial; the world supplies tools.*
Gems are the first luxury where **all five sources of scarcity are honest at once**:

1. **Geology** — the seeded, finite, depleting deposit: the world's own seed, not
   content and not a value field.
2. **Hours × wage** — faceting takes a skilled cutter many hours, and skilled
   cutters are few if the labor pool is finite (markets 5b, open).
3. **Competence, and standing by your own hand** — only a high-band cutter makes top
   brilliance; silk's *"NPCs can make silk; only you can make YOUR silk"* — a master's
   stone carries their mark.
4. **Provenance** — a named stone with a history is unique: the relic pattern of the
   [organized-religion slate](./organized-religion-slate.md), value as chain of title
   plus a claim.
5. **Authored heirlooms** — content's rarity, for gems an author places.

**The demand side:**

- **Status needs visibility and an eye** — worn and seen; the connoisseur's
  competence decides what an observer can tell.
- ⭐ **Industrial demand sets a floor** — most real diamonds are abrasive grit, so a
  stone that fails as jewelry still sells: the honest split between gem grade and
  industrial grade.
- ⭐ **Value density makes gems the ultimate portable wealth** — the most value per
  gram of anything: carried by refugees, settling debts across borders, and
  **smuggled** — customs' hardest case, and the diplomatic bag's temptation
  ([diplomacy-slate](./diplomacy-slate.md)).
- **Collateral** — the pawnbroker's pledge.
- ⚠ **NPC purses** — who among the cast buys a ring — remains markets 5b.

### ⭐ How value is made — the cartel and the synthetic

Diamonds are not rare; their price was **made**. A cartel controlled supply for most
of the twentieth century, and the engagement-ring tradition was largely an
advertising campaign (*"A Diamond Is Forever"*, 1947) — value from **supply control
plus marketing**, and players can try it: a gem cartel is the polity doing something
we did not want. Then **synthetics** — Verneuil's flame-grown rubies (1902), lab
diamonds — are chemically the same stone, answer the same commands, and collapse
the price. ⭐ Lens 5's sharpest lesson: **a capability survives the epoch; its
scarcity does not.**

### The seven lenses

1. **Pedagogy** — optics, the hardness ladder, identification, cupellation, how value
   is made.
2. **Expression** — a setting's design and an engraved seal are the player's; color
   and brilliance are derived.
3. **Immersion** — the jeweler's loupe; paste found out. **Participation** — the
   cartel, the smuggler, the forged hallmark.
4. **Values** — ivory, tortoiseshell, mine labor, conflict stones: each a choice a
   polity may ban, never one the engine makes.
5. **Continuity** — cabochon to brilliant; natural to synthetic.
6. **Economy** — the five sources and the demand side. ⚠ The weak spot: a backstop
   import of cut gems caps their price — the author's dial; **the commons ships no gem
   import line**, and an author may add one.
7. **Governance** — the hallmark, the ivory ban, the mining concession, customs on
   stones, the cartel.
