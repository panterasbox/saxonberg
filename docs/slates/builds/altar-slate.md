## The altar — object *and* class **[DECIDED]**

Both, and the split is the design:

- A **class of altars** (`AltarMixin` — a *kind* of thing, like Container /
  LightSource) supplies the **function** (below). Many exist: wayside shrines,
  household altars, craft-shrines, wild-altars, the prophet's.
- A specific altar is a **bespoke instance** — Named, historied — but the key:
  **function comes from the class; *power* comes from accreted history.** An altar
  grows *heavier* the more true rites are witnessed on it — significance accretes
  on the object via belief / chronicle / **renown-of-the-object** (the deep-law's
  "each rule paid in a death," applied to oaths).

So **an oath on an ancient, much-sworn altar binds *truer* than one on a stone
you set this morning** — not a magic stat, but centuries of witnessed rites making
the community *and* the belief-substrate hold it heavier. **Sacred weight is
earned through use.** A player *can* consecrate a new altar (instance the class),
but it starts **cold** and earns its gravity (friction = growth). *Class =
mechanism; instance-history = meaning.*

## What altars do — the rite-instrument **[DECIDED]**

The physical **anchor** for the "witnessed-and-held" engine — the seam wiring a
*place you stand* into the *social/spiritual systems.* Its rite does four things:

1. **Witnesses** — the act becomes public and *recorded* (chronicle; the honest
   count). Weight a casual act can't carry.
2. **Binds** — an intention becomes a *held obligation* (an oath stakes regard /
   reputation / conviction; breaking it has consequence — contracts, the
   deep-law's oaths).
3. **Consecrates** — confers a durable *status/mark* on a person or thing (a
   naming, a dedication — belief-store, Status).
4. **Costs** — the **sacrifice**: you give something up (a valued thing destroyed
   = an economic *sink*; or a commitment made). The cost is what makes it *real.*

Verb-surface (`swear` / `offer` / `consecrate` / `dedicate`) fired *at the altar*
into chronicle / belief / regard / conviction / contract. **The "magic" is
grounded** — power from belief (the gods live on it) + real witnessing
(accountability) + accreted weight, never a supernatural stat.

## Sacrifice — the mechanic **[DECIDED structure; tuning OPEN]**

The concrete rite, and it makes a real choice. A **hunted kill** becomes a fork:

- **Butcher it** → leather (gear) + meat (sustenance) — the material use.
- **Offer it** → the rite — the spiritual use.

…and you can't do both. **That opportunity cost *is* the worship** — sacrifice
*costs real value*, which is what separates it from free prayer. "The god you
feed," made literal: your kills can feed a god.

**The load-bearing guardrail [DECIDED]:** **a sacrifice costs real value and
returns *only recognition* — standing, alignment, renown-with-a-patron, the world
seeing your devotion — *never* a material boon.** ("The world may *react* to your
alignment; it may never *pay* you for it.") The instant a sacrifice drops loot,
you've rebuilt the transactional blood-altar and switched which god the game
serves. Recognition, never reward.

Generalizes past kills (offer coin, a crafted thing, labor) — but the **kill is
the primal, bloody, most-charged exemplar.** The sacrificed value is an economic
**sink** (conservation-clean; see [mining-slate](./mining-slate.md) *§ Economics*).

## Altar taxonomy — patron & alignment **[DECIDED]**

- **The mechanism is patron-neutral**; a specific altar is **consecrated to a
  patron**, and the *rite fits the patron*:
  - **Pan / Cernunnos — the wild-altar:** the hunter's altar and the **natural
    home of kills-as-sacrifice** (offer the kill back to the wild that gave it).
  - **Goibniu — the craft-altar** (the miners' Goibniu-shrine): consecrate honest
    work, a masterpiece, the maker's mark.
  - **Vesta — the hearth-altar:** home, welcome, the oath/covenant binding (the
    household troth-stone).
  - **Eir — the mend-altar:** healing and tending rites.
  - **Aletheia — the reformed "altar" is the honest count itself** (being truly
    seen; *no stone, no blood* — which is why old stone altars feel archaic).
  - **Mammon / Moloch — the dark altars:** debt-bonds, sealed hoards, and the
    forbidden sentient-sacrifice — the **tainted** ones.
- **Alignment accretes and can flip.** An altar's lean is a property that grows
  with use (its renown-as-object, with a valence): true covenants make it warm and
  strong; dark rites **taint** it. Altars can be **consecrated** (dedicated,
  purified) or **desecrated** (turned); a **tainted altar** is a real
  hazard-place (the corruption-seam / dead-zone parallel).

## Sentient sacrifice — the forbidden **[DECIDED]**

Offering a **person** (any experience-bearer — personhood, not species, is the
line) *is* the evil-definition (treat a person as a thing to consume = the
hollowing; Moloch's signature rite). **Never a player affordance** (the evil line;
players locked Good) — only the **antagonist's rite** and the **horror you
uncover.** The **consent hinge:** *self*-sacrifice (martyrdom, your own life freely
given) = the highest good; sacrificing *another* = the defining evil. (Full
treatment in the bible *§ Sacrifice*.)

## The Ordinance mirror — the Feed as the anti-altar **[DECIDED]**

The altar binds by your **consent** (sworn, witnessed, held because you *chose*).
The Ordinance needs no altar: it **binds you without your oath** — obligations you
never swore — and its Feed is **sentient sacrifice industrialized**, where *you*
are the offering and don't know it. *The altar is the consent the Ordinance
deletes*, at the level of the given word.

## The exemplar — the prophet's wilderness altar **[DECIDED; one fork OPEN]**

The concrete anchor: a **deep-time altar out in the wilds**, older than the city
(and older than the prophet — he *found* it and made it his pulpit, so it carries
two histories: the undatable one and his fresh, iconic use). A **John-the-Baptist
forerunner** who was never baptizing — he **sacrifices on the altar** to *seal*
his prophecies of the coming founding (the sacrifice as the *stake/witness*, not a
purchase). Its **alignment is the live question** — a clean covenant-stone, or has
the bloody form *tainted* it (has he been feeding Moloch)? Reading the altar's
accreted lean is the quest.

**Today (present-day state):** still out past the edge — the reformed city had no
use for it, so the wild kept it; a thin **pilgrim-track** wears in to a scorched
stone in the open air (no shrine, fence, or sign). **Divided reverence:** a thin
stream of the **devout** still come (oaths, tokens); the **reformed mainstream
passes it by as archaic** (the honest count left blood behind — the altar is
*invisible* to them); **some avoid it** as uncanny (it still carries a trace). The
split maps the founding fault-lines (Lawful-herald / Chaotic-fanaticism /
clergy-embarrassment). **Not institutionally maintained** (the clergy reformed
away from it) — tended commons-style by pilgrims, or by **one last keeper** (the
fading-faith-in-one-figure, the clergy-lineage-in-miniature). **Condition:**
deep-time **whole** (it endures — *not* a ruin; it outlives the city), *heavy with
legible use* (worn hollows, smoothed horns, oaths older than record = accreted
weight made visible), the prophet's recent scorch on top. The residue is
**ambiguous by design** — old blood or worse? warm or wrong? — you cannot read it
blessed or tainted, and neither can anyone (his unresolved alignment written into
the stone). The freshest scorch **keeps his fate open** (recent-and-continuing, or
recent-and-stopped?).

- **[OPEN] the prophet's valence:** the **martyr who redeems the blood-form** (he
  stops offering kills and offers *himself* — the self-gift) **vs.** the
  **warning who's slid into feeding Moloch** (the god-you-name-vs-feed drift).
