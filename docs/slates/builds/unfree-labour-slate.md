## 0. The thesis

⭐⭐ **Slavery is one of history's most consequential economic and political
systems, and sugar is the trade most bound up with it.** Films and books
depict it so people understand it; a game can too. *"We're adults; we can be
sensitive about this."* What a game adds is **agency**, and agency is both
the value and the danger.

⛔ **The failure to design against is *Slave Tetris*** (an educational game,
2015, pulled): not that it depicted slavery, but that it made the system a
clean optimization. **The rule: the mechanic teaches what slavery was** —
the enslaved have wants and ways out, cruelty has a cost, and every act is
on the record. A system without those is an asset ledger.

## 1. The forms of unfree labour

| form | what binds | how it ends | history |
|---|---|---|---|
| **chattel slavery** | the person is claimed as property (and, by law, their children) | flight · self-purchase · manumission · revolt · abolition | the Atlantic sugar islands |
| **indenture** | a contract for a term, often signed under pressure | the term ends; flight (illegal) | the Caribbean after 1834 |
| **debt bondage** | a debt work never quite repays (the company store) | paid, forgiven, or flight | peonage, the US South after 1865 |
| **serfdom** | bound to the land, not to a person | emancipation; flight to a town (*"town air makes free"*) | medieval Europe; Russia to 1861 |
| **convict labour** | a sentence, leased out | the sentence ends | convict leasing; penal colonies |

**This slate designs chattel slavery.** Indenture and debt bondage are later
forms on the same register model (§ 13); serfdom and convict labour are
noted, not designed (the [prison slate](./prison-slate.md) owns sentences).

## 2. ⭐⭐ The doctrine this changes **[DECIDED]**

The design said, in four places, that this cannot exist:

- [land-compute-and-license](./land-compute-and-license.md) § the owner:
  *"there is no slavery in this game, so an NPC cannot be owned at all."*
- [chattel.md](../../subsystems/chattel.md): *"a person is nobody's
  property"*, pinned by a test that no person class composes the chattel
  mixin.
- the **13th module** ([amendment-library](./amendment-library-slate.md),
  credit): *"exit is bedrock; nobody can be bound to stay"* and *"a contract
  must never alienate exit."*
- the sugar slate's lens 4: *"the engine never models a coerced worker as a
  mechanic."*

None of them is in Tier A of [measurement.md](../../measurement.md) — they
are doctrine, liftable — and the founder lifted them for NPCs:

- ⭐ **A person is still nobody's chattel.** The enslaving claim is **not**
  chattel title (§ 3): it is a record a jurisdiction keeps. The class test
  stays; the chattel mixin never composes on a person.
- ⭐ **Exit stays bedrock for real people.** For an NPC it is denied only
  **inside a jurisdiction that honours a register**, and only as long as it
  does. Geography is always a way out (§ 6).
- ⛔⛔ **A player is never entered in a register** (§ 10).

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ The register — a claim a jurisdiction honours **[DECIDED]**

Slavery was a **legal status that stopped at a border**: *Somerset v
Stewart* (1772) held it unenforceable on English soil because English law
did not recognise it — the **free-soil** principle; serfs had *town air*;
the Underground Railroad was a geography of jurisdictions; and a **fugitive
slave law** was one polity choosing to honour another's claims.

1. **Enslavement is an entry in a REGISTER kept by a jurisdiction's
   office** (the British colonies kept slave registers from 1817). The
   ranching herdbook's shape: a **filed record — the claimant does not hold
   the pen**; the office does.
2. **The claim has force only where that register is honoured.** Inside, the
   claimant's claim is enforceable; outside, the person is free — unless the
   ground they reach has chosen to honour that register.
3. **Abolition is the office CLOSING the register**: its entries stop
   resolving. No statute engine — civics' *"no legal machinery"* holds; the
   law is data and abolition changes the data.
4. **The acts are recorded** (§ 9) and **whether they are crimes is the
   jurisdiction's law read at read time** — the ledger already derives blame
   on read, so re-legislating re-scores history without rewriting it. After
   abolition the past reads differently.

**Defaults:**

- ⭐ **No register exists anywhere by default.** Slavery is absent from the
  world unless authored content creates a register (§ 8). *"Illegal unless
  established"* is structural, not a rule.
- ⭐ **Free soil by default.** A jurisdiction honours no other's register
  unless it chooses to; a fugitive-slave law is a deliberate act (§ 8 says
  by whom).

## 4. What the claim gives an NPC owner **[DECIDED]**

The **pets precedent**: title over a kept animal commands nothing — it
complies or refuses by bond.

- **Labour without a wage.** The person holds a seat on the owner's roster
  (a cutter, a boiler) and works its shifts; **no wage is paid**, and they
  cannot quit. Leaving is not quitting — it is **flight**, a separate and
  dangerous act.
- **Transfer.** The claim can be sold: a **deed filed with the register's
  office**, so the record moves. Nobody carries a person like a good.
- **No remote control.** The owner cannot make them do arbitrary things; the
  work is the roster, and everything else is the person's own.

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ The person's side — what they want **[DECIDED]**

An enslaved NPC has a **derived pressure toward getting out** — computed
when read, never stored (A3; combat morale is the precedent). It reads:

- **treatment** — food, rest, harm done to them, regard for the claimant;
- **possibility** — the distance to free soil, a route they know, someone
  who will help or hide them;
- **cost** — the risk of being caught.

From it, **four resistances**, in order of scale:

1. **Everyday resistance** — slow or careless work: output and quality fall,
   and nobody did anything you can point at.
2. **Flight** — walking toward free soil when the pressure beats the risk
   (§ 6).
3. **Self-purchase** — freedom bought with money of their own (historically
   from provision grounds and Sunday markets; Spanish *coartación* made it a
   right). Only where the jurisdiction's law allows it — a lever.
4. **Revolt** — collective; **deferred** (it needs NPCs acting together,
   which nothing supports).

⭐⭐ **Violence works both ways** (lens 1 with lens 4). Harm raises **fear**,
which suppresses flight now, and **grievance**, which lowers output and
raises flight and revolt later. Terror deterred — that is part of how the
system held, and leaving it out lies in the comforting direction — but it is
**never a free win**: cruelty has a real, rising cost, so an owner who reads
the model learns why it was used and why it was self-defeating. *Never make
coercive control mechanically rewarding* (the household design pack).

## 6. The ways out **[DECIDED]**

- **Flight is geography.** Escape is reaching ground where the claim has no
  force. Maroons in the hills, a hiding place, a guide, the route itself —
  real content, built from places, borders and jurisdictions.
- **Self-purchase** — § 5.
- **Manumission** — the claimant closes their own entry (the deed filed),
  freely or for payment from anyone.
- **Abolition** — the office closes the register (§ 3). ⭐ **Who is
  compensated** is the polity's question with no calculable answer: Britain
  paid the enslavers in 1833, not the enslaved. Lens 7 with nothing to
  compute — exactly what the lenses want.
- **Revolt** — deferred (§ 5).

## 7. Inheritance — a jurisdiction's law, off by default **[DECIDED]**

*Partus sequitur ventrem* — a child of an enslaved mother is enslaved — was
the system's engine: it is why US slavery kept growing after the slave trade
was banned in 1808, and leaving it out would misteach why the system lasted
(lens 1). A polity must be able to choose what we dislike (lens 3b). So it is
**a law a jurisdiction can set on its register**, no register has it unless
set, and it **waits on NPC households and children**
([household-lifecycle](./household-lifecycle-slate.md)).

## 8. ⛔⛔⛔ Players — never a coercive role **[DECIDED, values]**

The founder's call, and the reason is **roleplay**: *"I just don't know that
we want to give people a platform to roleplay slaveowners. It's like
roleplaying Nazis — it's just obscene, and I'm not sure we can code or
design our way around that. It's not the game that sucks, it's the
players."* A grand-strategy game abstracts the enslaved into numbers; a MUD
makes them characters in a room **other players are in**. The persistent-
world precedent (Second Life's Gor communities) is what such a world gets
when it hosts ownership roleplay. **The only real control is what is
offered.**

- ⛔ **Players never hold a coercive seat** — not claimant, not overseer, not
  slave-catcher. Those seats exist and the system runs, held by NPCs only.
- **The player's relationship to slavery is witness, trading partner, or
  opponent:** see the estate; buy its sugar (the ledger records that you
  did); buy someone's freedom, pay toward a self-purchase, hide a fugitive,
  guide them to free soil, push for abolition.
- ⛔ **Registers are AUTHORED only.** Players cannot establish one, as people
  or as a polity — option (a). A realm's history may include slavery (a
  place where it is legal, or was until abolition); **players can only end
  it.** A troll faction voting slavery in for shock value is unreachable.
- ⚠ **What this costs, said plainly.** Lens 1 loses the owner's first-hand
  view (the economics stay visible from outside: the NPC estate still runs
  on fear, grievance and output). Lens 3b's *any NPC position could be held
  by a player* breaks for these seats — **positions the platform declines
  to offer people**, recorded as a values decision, not hidden. And lens
  3b's *can the polity do something we did not want* is answered no for
  establishing slavery — deliberately.

## 9. The ledger

New accountability kinds, each naming its actor and parties: **enslave**
(an entry made — authored content and NPC claimants only), **transfer** (a
deed), **harbour**, **guide**, **free** (manumission or a paid
self-purchase), **catch** (an NPC catcher). Whether each is a crime is the
jurisdiction's law at read time (§ 3.4). ⭐ **The economic and coercive
kinds are the ledger's first that are not physical harm** — looting was kept
off it because a theft kind *"would force a what-is-theft ruling the courts
should make"*; this slate answers that the same way: **record the act, let
the law read it.**

## 10. ⛔ Entrenchment — a player is never registered

**Proposed for Tier A of [measurement.md](../../measurement.md)** (amendable
by nobody): *a real person can never be entered in a register, bound by a
contract that alienates exit, or held in a coercive seat.* It is the 13th
module aimed at people, and it is what makes the rest of this slate
permissible. (The measurement doc's own edit lands with the build.)

## 11. ⭐⭐ Prerequisites — what does not exist

The honest version sits on systems that are not built:

1. **NPC motive** — wanting to quit, flee, resist ([mind](./mind-slate.md)).
   Nothing today gives an NPC a reason to leave anything.
2. **NPC self-movement** — an NPC deciding to go somewhere of its own
   accord, across localities ([npc-behavior](./npc-behavior-slate.md)).
   NPCs move only by authored brains today.
3. **The register as an office** — a jurisdiction-kept record with legal
   force by place ([legal-code](./legal-code-slate.md)); civics today has no
   legal machinery.
4. **Harbouring** — hiding another person (concealment covers yourself,
   objects and exits only).
5. **Economic ledger kinds** (§ 9).
6. **Collective action** — revolt (deferred).

⚠ **And one fix that is NOT this slate's, found here:** an NPC whose wage
goes unpaid **keeps working forever** — arrears are recorded with the worker
as creditor, and nothing makes them stop. That is the engine modelling forced
labour by accident, the one outcome nobody chose. **An unpaid hand walks
off** belongs to employment and can ship before this slate (the sugar
build's estate is the first place it bites).

## 12. Lens pass (2026-10-18)

1. **Pedagogy** — why sugar became slavery's trade (inversion, mill capital,
   crop-time labour); why terror was used and why it failed; free soil as
   geography; why inheritance made it last; abolition's compensation
   question.
2. **Expression** — a register, an estate and its people are rows; a second
   slave society needs no code; the ordinary case is an authored history.
3. **Immersion** — the estate is a place you can walk into and see.
   **Participation** — witness, trader, opponent; ⛔ never the coercive seat
   (§ 8).
4. **Values** — the heaviest in the design: cruelty never free (§ 5), every
   act on the record (§ 9), and the founder's roleplay line (§ 8). The
   choice it forces on a player: **trade with the estate or not** — sugar
   from slave labour is cheaper — with no calculable right answer.
5. **Continuity** — chattel slavery → indenture → debt bondage → the free
   labour market: the same seat, bound by different things.
6. **Economy** — unpaid labour lowers an estate's costs and raises its risk
   (flight, slow work, revolt); free-labour sugar competes on price; the
   abolitionist pays to free people.
7. **Governance** — the register is the jurisdiction's; abolition and
   compensation are polity acts with no calculable answer; free soil and
   fugitive laws are cross-jurisdiction choices.

**Altitude** — *invariant*: a player is never registered, never in a
coercive seat (§ 10); a person is never chattel. *Grain*: the pressure's
weights, the cost of violence. *Title*: which places have a register, and
when it closed.

## 13. Later forms

- **Indenture** — a register entry with a term, signed or pressed; ends when
  the term does. NPC parties only (§ 10).
- **Debt bondage** — an entry backed by a debt the work services; needs
  recourse credit, which credit deliberately does not have today.

## 14. Open

1. Who keeps the register — a seat in the jurisdiction's government (an
   office, NPC-held), or the government row itself.
2. Whether a jurisdiction may honour another's register (a fugitive-slave
   law) as an authored setting, given registers are authored-only.
3. Whether any authored place in the shipped world should have a register,
   past or present, and where (the sugar island is the obvious candidate,
   not decided).
