## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Every body produces waste **[DECIDED — option b, the owner]**

The owner reopened the call that players do not (NPCs could): *"most games don't make you
poo but some do. ONI does and that's actually been where a lot of our design comes from."*
Without human waste, cholera, the cesspit, the night-soil men, the Great Stink and Snow's
pump have no source; in *Oxygen Not Included* colonists foul the floor without an
outhouse, and germs and polluted ground follow — much of why its base-building matters.

Three options were weighed: **a.** NPCs only; **b.** every body produces it, the
meaningful decisions are infrastructure and place; **c.** a full need on a timer. **b is
decided** — lens 1 needs the source, lens 3a wants bodies to be bodies, and stewardship's
engagement rule rejects c (*never a chore on a timer*).

- **Metabolism produces waste** for every body.
- **Relieving is a simple, felt act** — urgency through
  [wellbeing](./wellbeing-slate.md)'s discomfort channel; never punishing.
- ⚠ **Tone** — at the limit the body will not wait and you go where you are: honest, and
  plain, never humiliating. Its handling belongs to the **privacy** conversation.
- ⭐ **What matters is where it goes** — each sink's consequence derived:

| sink | consequence |
|---|---|
| **the privy pit** | fills, and **seeps into groundwater** — ⭐ **a privy near a well fouls the well**, the classic rural epidemic, through the [irrigation slate](./irrigation-slate.md)'s groundwater law |
| **the cesspit** | emptied by the **night-soil man** and sold to farms (§ 3) |
| **the water closet** | to the sewer, to the outfall — the reversed conduit, shipping |
| **the open ground** | fouls water and spreads disease |

The meaningful decisions are a **settlement's** — build privies, pay the night-soil man,
lay the sewer — the decisions ONI makes interesting.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Air pollution **[DECIDED]**

Water carries contamination; the air carries nothing yet. This is the
[zoning slate](./zoning-slate.md)'s unbuilt **emission model over `signalAt`**:

- **Every burner emits** — smoke and soot from the flue (ships) spread and settle: ⭐ **soot
  on the washing on the line** (cleanliness deposits from the air); the city's smoke and
  lamps washing out the stars.
- **Sulfur dioxide** from coal — ⭐ **acid rain eats limestone and marble**
  ([masonry](./masonry-and-concrete-slate.md)).
- ⭐ **The inversion** — cold air trapped under warm holds smoke at ground level; the London
  smog of December 1952 killed about four thousand people in a week (later estimates run
  far higher) and produced the **Clean Air Act (1956)**. Derivable if
  [weather](../../subsystems/weather.md)'s temperatures can describe an inversion.
- **Air carries no kind of its own** at first — smoke, soot and sulfur, each read by the
  senses that can (smell ships) and by instruments.

## 3. ⭐⭐ The night-soil loop **[DECIDED]**

- **Night soil returned nitrogen and phosphorus to farms** — the cesspit's contents,
  carted by night and sold: **someone's income**, closing human waste back to
  [soil](../../subsystems/soil.md)'s reserves.
- ⭐ **The sewer broke the loop** — flushing it to the river ended the return, which is part
  of why farming turned to guano and then synthetic fertilizer. A real, derivable story
  about where nutrients go.

## 4. The nutrient's destination — eutrophication **[DECIDED]**

Soil's open D18: nitrate leached from fields and sewage in still water feed algae, starve
the water of oxygen, and kill fish — landing on [fishing](../../subsystems/fishing.md)'s
habitat fit. It closes a seam two shipped systems left open.

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ The economics of an externality **[DECIDED — lens 6, lens 7]**

The settlement model: waste is honest *"only as an externality — a spill that already costs
somebody."* Three real answers, each a polity's choice and each a lesson:

- **Nuisance law** — the neighbor sues; the measurable reading is the evidence (zoning).
- ⭐ **Coase** — with clear rights and cheap bargaining, the polluter and the neighbor reach
  the efficient result **whoever holds the right**: a counterintuitive economics lesson,
  playable between players.
- ⭐ **Pigou** — the polity **taxes the emission**, priced from measurement; or a **cap with
  tradeable permits** — the zoning slate's *"cap at the boundary"*.

## 6. The sanitary institution **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

Chadwick's *Report on the Sanitary Condition of the Labouring Population* (1842) led to the
**Public Health Act (1848)** — the **medical officer of health** and the **sanitary
inspector**: the three-layer measurement pattern (the engine measures, an institution
certifies, a polity imposes) applied to public health. Beside it, the Alkali Act (1863),
the Clean Air Act (1956), and *Rylands v Fletcher*'s strict liability
([flooding-slate](./flooding-slate.md)).

## 7. The lens pass (2026-10-20)

1. **Pedagogy** — ⭐ the **fecal-oral route**, and **hands as a vector** (Semmelweis, 1847:
   doctors washing their hands cut maternity deaths sharply — the wash ships; spoilage's
   contamination is event-seeded, so dirty hands carrying pathogens is a contact event);
   dilution and its limit (organic decays, persistent does not — shipped); the nutrient
   loop and the sewer that broke it; eutrophication; smoke, sulfur, acid rain, the
   inversion; externality economics.
2. **Expression** — a town's sanitation is its character (Rejection's open drains,
   Terminus's sewers); the privy's design (pit, earth closet — dry earth sprinkled over —
   water closet); ordinances as a polity's authored law.
3. **Immersion** — ⭐ **a town smells of its sanitation**, derived from what is there (the
   cesspits, the tannery, the river) — the Great Stink becomes a reading; the night-soil
   cart at night; soot-blackened stone. **Participation** — the night-soil man, the dustman,
   the scavenger, the sewer worker (the Victorian **tosher**, searching sewers for lost
   coins), the sanitary inspector, the medical officer; the nuisance suit; Coase bargaining
   between players; ⭐ the polity fouling upstream on purpose (Rejection already does), or
   cutting the sewer budget.
4. **Values** — dignity and privacy; ⭐ **who lives downwind and downstream** — the LULU
   usually sits beside the poor (environmental justice); the sewer's cost against its lives
   saved; the warm coal fire against clean air.
5. **Continuity** — privy and cesspit → earth closet → water closet and sewer → treatment
   works; smoke → ⭐ **smokeless fuel** (London's smokeless zones burned **coke**, which the
   retort ships) → gas → electric. The same acts; **the consequence moves** — from your
   yard, to the river, to the plant.
6. **Economy** — night soil as a sold fertilizer; the cesspit-emptying fee; the sewer as
   public works (Bazalgette — the [markets](./markets-slate.md) faucet); the dump's fee and
   scavengers; treatment as capital; pollution taxes as revenue; permits as tradeable
   assets.
7. **Governance** — nuisance suits with measurable evidence; the Public Health Act; the
   Clean Air Act; the Alkali Act; *Rylands*; Coase against Pigou as the polity's choice;
   who pays for the sewer (rates); where the LULU is sited.
