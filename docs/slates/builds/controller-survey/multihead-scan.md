# Multi-headed controllers — scan

**How the scan scored:** `mh/scan.py` (in this dir) walks every controller `pack-roots.controllerFiles()` would (527 files: kernel `platform/idea/cmd/**`, `lib/**/*Controller.ts`, world `idea/cmd`, every pack's `src/**/idea/cmd/**` + pack `lib/*Controller*`). Per file: score = 2 × distinct kind tests (`MixinApi.isX` + `instanceof` names) + 1.5 × distinct refusal-reason prefixes (`reason: '…'`) + 0.5 × quoted `case` labels + 2 × `switch` + 0.8 × methods + 0.3 × imports + lines/60. Ranking in `mh/ranked.txt`. **The score was a weak signal.** It rewards size and exhaustive wording switches (Cut, Dip and Shape rank high on `case` counts that are pure presentation). Two signals the scan didn't weight found the real heads, so I grepped for them separately:
- **two engines in one file**: a protocol-plan arm (`*PlanFor` / `cutPlan` / `engagePlan`) next to a `CraftingApi.craft` or bespoke `engageStep` arm. Hits: Carve, Stir, Fell, Saw (plus Mill and Heat by reading).
- **bare form is a readout**: no arg reads the instrument or board, and an arg acts (Teleport, Course, Journey, plus the `switch (model.subcommand)` family).

I read ~30 controllers: the scan's top 25, plus Heat, Stir, Pour, Dry, Fit, Wallet and the fight family.

**Summary:** 6 SEVERAL ACTS, 9 ONE ACT SEVERAL HOSTS, 13 FINE, 2 UNCLEAR (of 30 read), plus one family verdict (≈20 subcommand-switch controllers, SEVERAL ACTS by construction).

## Last build's unifications

- **`heat` ⊃ `boil`: SEVERAL ACTS (the worst of the seven).** Boil was folded into heat's placing arm (`setOnTheFire`, 162–182), which is an instant `ContainmentApi.move(…,'on')` with no engagement, no heat read and no latch. The other two arms are engaged holds at the fire. The player who types `heat pot` to boil a bandage did a `put`, and the world did the boiling. Split it: an engaged "hold it in the fire" (`Heated` protocol for build/instrument/tempered) and a placement shape for setting a vessel on the fire.
- **`wash --hard` (ex-`scrub`): FINE.** One path, one factor (`effort()` 74–77, 1 → 1.5/3 with a brush) and a word (`act()` 80–84). This is how a manner should fold in. The real fork in Wash is hands vs things (ONE ACT, SEVERAL HOSTS).
- **`pour` (bulk + crafting): FINE.** One `PourTarget.receivePour` path. Bulk transfer, banking an ingredient into a build, a lost-wax casting and a melted mould are host outcomes (`PourDone.kind`). One small residue: `kind:'ingredient'` "adds X to Y" is a solid put into a build, so watch it.
- **`stir` ⊃ `shake`/`churn`: ONE ACT, SEVERAL HOSTS, half-done.** The technique read (`workingOf`, 112–121) is right: the vessel names the word. But the controller still carries three arms and two duration constants (`STIR_MS`, `CHURN_MS`). Only the compounding arm asks the vessel for a plan. Finish it with one `agitatePlanFor`.
- **`dip` ⊃ `melt`/`gob`: ONE ACT, SEVERAL HOSTS, with one doubtful host.** Already a protocol (`DipBath.dipPlanFor`, 83–87); the controller is dumb. Coat, candle and gather all "draw it out with the bath on it". The paper **sheet** (`not-a-mould`, `mould-full`, `no-pulp`) is a vat lift that leaves nothing on the mould's own face, and the `face` arg means nothing to it. `melt` survives *inside* the candle plan (`f.melted`, 179–183): two acts in one plan, owned by the bath, so the controller can't see it. See hard case 2.
- **`cut` (seven targets): FINE at the controller, multi-headed one level down.** The controller is one path (63–109). But its options `--for/--tight/--generous` mean something only to cloth, and its exhaustive `CutBegin`/`CutDone` switches mean a new cuttable host edits the kernel controller. Worse, the **`Cuttable` protocol itself is keyed by verb**: `CutAnswers` (Cuttable.ts 171–183) has 11 entries (`cut`, `groove`, `scrape`, `saw`, `rive`, `crosscut`, `butcher`, `scribe`, `snap`, `groze`), each with disjoint begin/done/refusal types. One method, `cutPlan(req)`, routes eleven acts by a `verb` string. That's the multi-headed beast moved from controller to protocol (hard case 1).
- **`fight` gambits: FINE, and the model.** `FightGambitController` (61 lines) is one act: `queueGambit(word)`, with the combatant refusing. Every other subcommand has its own controller over `FightControllerBase`. This is the shape the subcommand-switch family should copy.

## Hard cases for the owner

1. **A protocol keyed by a verb string is a multi-headed protocol.** `Cuttable.cutPlan(req)` answers 11 verbs whose answers share no fields (`CutAnswers`). It reads as "one interface" and is a dispatch table with a switch at each implementer (Rope, Corpse, Bole, Billet, Sheet, IceSheet, SugarLoaf). Is that unification, or should each act get its own small protocol (`Crosscuttable`, `Sawable`, `Butcherable`, `GlassScored`) that a class may implement several of? The test I'd apply: do two verbs' answers share a type? Only `cut` itself is shared across hosts.
2. **An act hidden inside a plan.** Dip's candle plan melts the cake first (`f.melted`). Is "melt then dip" one act (the chandler's motion) or a fold that hides `melt` where no view can name it? The controller is clean either way, so the controller test can't see this; it needs a plan-level test.
3. **"One goal, several means" (Teleport).** The player believes one thing ("I go to X") while cost, refusal and time differ by means (free / fare / mana). By the owner's test that's one act. But the means order and the fall-through (`route-not-found` → spell) are game rules in the controller. A `TravelMeans` roster moves them out. Is a means a host?
4. **The placement wearing a verb.** Heat's set-on-fire arm and Dry's hang arm are both `ContainmentApi.move` plus words, and their consequence is the world's clock. Should every "put it where the world will do X" be a `put` shape with its own word (fine as a view alias), never an arm of a timed act?
5. **The same act under two verbs** (the inverse beast). Fire's own header says a crucible furnace is "one mechanism on different dials" with a kiln, yet `smelt` is a separate 1035-line controller with its own chemistry. Water/Feed are twins. Fell's crosscut duplicates `cut`. If the rule is "one act, one head", it cuts both ways.

## Candidate rules this suggests (principle + the test, with evidence)

1. **One controller, one engine.** *Test: does the file reach the world through two different mechanisms (a host's plan here, `CraftingApi.craft`/`engageStep` with its own constants there)?* If yes, it's one act with an unfinished protocol, and the arms will drift in cost and time. Evidence: Mill's recipe arm grinds instantly without water (271–330 vs 160–240); Carve's recipe arm is instant while the Carvable arm is engaged (135–216 vs 117–132); Stir's two constants (30–33).
2. **The bare form that reads is its own controller.** *Test: does the no-arg path move or change nothing?* Then it gets its own shape and controller, the way `fight` → `FightStatusController` does. Evidence: Teleport's board (112–135), Course's `readout` (86), Journey's status, Wallet's `show`.
3. **A subcommand switch is a split already made in the view and undone in the controller.** *Test: `switch (model.subcommand)` into disjoint methods.* The fix is one controller per subcommand over a base, with no judgment needed. Evidence: Wallet 38–55, House 43–50, Reserve 48–56, Style 92–103; 15 nouns already did it (`*ControllerBase.ts`).
4. **A branch with its own refusal vocabulary, its own engagement and its own outcome is its own act, whatever word it rides.** *Test: name the branch's refusals, duration and outcome; if no other branch shares any of the three, split it.* Evidence: Heat (c) (no engagement, `no-fire-to-set-on`, moved), Dry (a) (instant, `cloth-sodden`, wetness), Fit paste (glue refusals, a face coat), Fell crosscut (`CrosscutRefusal`, a length off a log).
5. **Options that only one host reads mark a head.** *Test: an option or arg ignored by most hosts.* `cut --for/--tight/--generous` (cloth only), `dip inside|outside` (coat only), `fit … with <pot>` (paste only). Either the host owns its options (a shape per host) or the act isn't one.
