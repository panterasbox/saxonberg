## 1. What this is

The game's **first combat area**, and the first place the **Law↔Chaos
alignment axis** is made explicit. Everything before it (campus
onboarding, *An Honest Count* —
[eternal-university-narrative-slate.md](./eternal-university-narrative-slate.md))
is passive and implicit: observe, investigate, notice people. This area
is the payoff — *where's all the violence?* Here.

- **After** campus onboarding and (ideally) *An Honest Count*, so the
  player already understands the world's who-counts stakes before first
  raising a hand. Onboarding is deliberately combat-free
  ([onboarding-slate.md](./onboarding-slate.md)).
- **Not** on campus, and **not** the census murder-quest — the
  sanctioned "small wilderness zone" the lore permits, out past the
  city/campus edge.
- **Area name:** working title; candidates in §10.

## 2. The premise

> **A frontier settlement at the last counted mile, where the counted
> world quits and the wild takes over — and where a new player finally
> gets to apply violence to a problem, and learns, with their hands, that
> there are two ways for a good person to do it.**

## 3. The reframe from the old newbieland

The EotL newbieland was a killing gallery with an alignment sign nailed
to a fork: **Rainbow Village** (good monsters — butterflies, Bambi, a
child) to the west, **Bewitched Forest → Hell** (evil monsters — a
terrorist with a bomb, Jack the Ripper, a witch) to the east. Its whole
engine was *choose your victims to choose your soul on the Good↔Evil
axis.* We **keep the feeling and transplant the axis.**

**Keep:**

- **Choice-first** — a fork almost immediately.
- **Iconic-fear-cartoon casting** — real fears, juvenile and cartoon-safe.
  The tell is the original terrorist's bomb, which **only hurts
  monsters** (*"Amazingly, you didn't get hurt."*). That Looney-Tunes
  safety is the register we protect.
- **Alignment as the spine, expressed through what you do**, not a stat
  screen · a level-capped safe haven · generous XP · freebies.

**Drop:**

- **The Good↔Evil axis.** Players are **floored Good** (alignment-slate's
  B2 — [alignment-slate.md § Two asymmetric axes](./alignment-slate.md#two-asymmetric-axes)).
  "Kill the child to fall" is off the table morally *and* mechanically:
  murdering an innocent is not an alignment choice, it is a witnessed
  crime with blame and collapsing regard, and it accomplishes nothing.
- **The killing-gallery-with-no-story.** We want lore, a quest,
  personality, interactivity — a real small area, not a respawn arcade.

**Transplant onto:** the **Law↔Chaos axis** — the political axis,
"legitimate disagreement among good people." **Both paths are Good.** The
question is never *are you good?* — it is *what kind of good person are
you?*

## 4. The theme — a deliberate genre collision

The theme *is* the collision — a world that refuses to commit to one
genre. This area specifically (not every later one) is a deliberate
**genre salad**, all on one board, no one blinking. The old newbieland
had the instinct (terrorist + Jack the Ripper + witch + incubus); we make
it deliberate.

**What licenses it:** the area is **the margin — the last place on the
census before the roll goes quiet.** Past the settlement the woods are
*uncounted*, and the uncounted is where things that fit nowhere else wash
up (the TPA leaks, the ruins bleed, the multiverse is a permission slip).
The un-genred finish is the **signature of having stepped off the map the
census keeps.** Deadpan, never winking: out here the Big Bad Wolf is just
another thing the warden has a form for. Ties to the eternal spine (the
census, the ruins) with nothing ratification-fragile.

## 5. The combat curriculum — three lessons, in order

The area is a **teaching experience** whose shape is a single argument.
**The combat ceremony is precious** — the consent/terms/blame machinery
exists to distinguish killing a monster (trivial) from killing a person
(weighty) — so it is spent deliberately.

1. **Monsters — "violence is free."** Pure-evil fodder: a genre salad of
   hostile margin-creatures (storybook, gothic, terminal, plain frontier,
   the witch's constructs). No ceremony, no consent, no blame, no dilemma
   — you swing, a lot. Guilt-free: the moral axis is locked Good. **This
   is the volume of violence**, and where the genre mix gets its volume.
   The first fight (in the draft, *the Wolf*) is the pure poise-loop
   tutorial, **audible from the crossroads**, drawing you in.
2. **People — "but not on people."** A human crosses your path while you
   are still in kill-mode, primed — and the game **catches your hand.**
   Your aggression settings interpose. A designed lesson, not a hazard:
   the friction *is* the feature. Four things learned in one beat: your
   settings exist; they default to safe; murder is a deliberate, costly
   *crossing* (never hard-blocked — expensive, not impossible); and
   **people are a different category of thing.** Reinforced inside the
   boss fight: some of the wolfshead's human crew *yield* mid-fight, and
   a henchman who throws down his weapon becomes a person you cannot
   finish.
3. **The wolfshead — "unless the law hands you a licensed exception,
   and then *how* is your soul."** Lesson 3 answers the door lesson 2
   closes: *you can't just murder people* → *but here is the one man the
   community has lawfully declared huntable, dead or alive.* That is the
   literal meaning of **wolfshead** (medieval *caput lupinum* — a person
   you may hunt like a wolf). Outlaw status unlocks legitimate violence
   against a person; *how* you use it is the moral payoff.

**Monsters free → people forbidden → the outlaw licensed.** The
curriculum is the whole moral engine, delivered as level design instead
of a lecture.

## 6. How the Law↔Chaos axis is expressed

The axis model itself (sources, banding, the mirror, never a number) is
[alignment-slate.md](./alignment-slate.md)'s; this section is only how
*this area* exercises it.

### 6.1 The definition, said out loud

Both paths are violent and both are Good, so the difference is
**authorization and the finish**:

- **LAWFUL — violence by the order, and bounded.** You are **sworn in**
  (a real conferred deputization), fight under a warrant with the militia
  at your back, and **stop at defeat** — honor the down-but-not-dead
  window, take the wolfshead **alive**, walk him to a reckoning on the
  record. *"I will use force, and I will answer for exactly how much."*
- **CHAOTIC — violence by your own hand, and direct.** No swearing-in, no
  warrant, no terms offered. **You finish what you start** — run him into
  the woods and put him down yourself. *"I will use force, and I will own
  it myself."*

### 6.2 On the fodder — identity + prose, not a per-kill gate

Making every kill a dilemma would be decision fatigue and would gum up
the violence. For tier-1 fodder the axis is **texture, not a gate**:

- **Identity (strongest).** One identity through all the fodder — the
  **sworn deputy** under warrant or the **unsworn hunter**. *Who you are
  while you swing* accretes standing toward a pole.
- **Prose (the finish).** The identical kill, path-tinted through the
  scene composer ([messaging.md](../../subsystems/messaging.md)). Lawful
  *accounts for* its violence (the body dragged off the road, a note
  made); chaotic leaves it where it falls and moves on.
- **Lethality (weakest — fine).** Nobody takes a swarm of rats alive, so
  lethality is the **wolfshead** signal, not the fodder one.

### 6.3 On the wolfshead — the real fork, two payers

"Dead or alive" on a poster does **not** by itself cleave Law from Chaos
— a real frontier court pays either way. The true fork is **who
authorizes your violence and who pays you.** The fix is **two payers**:

- **The court pays for ALIVE.** The settlement's rule of law is brand
  new (the founding era). A newborn court **cannot legitimize itself on
  corpses** — it needs *trials*. A convicted outlaw is a cornerstone; a
  dead one is a hole in the ground.
- **The folk pay for DEAD.** They need him *gone*, and someone with a
  purse and a grudge will pay for a body, off the books.

Alive **strengthens the young court**; dead is folk justice done the old
way, outside it — rule of law vs. the vigilance committee, mapped onto
the governance axis, founding-flavored, ratification-safe.

⚠ **Reconcile at requirements:** [combat-slate.md § Contract kinds that
touch combat](./combat-slate.md#contract-kinds-that-touch-combat) sets a
bounty's default terms to **alive**, with dead-or-alive "a gated,
costlier variant." The two-payer design is consistent with that if the
folk purse *is* the gate — but the requirements pass must say so.

### 6.4 The counted/uncounted geography — the old map trick, reborn

Combat already encodes the lever: **defeat and the coup are two separate
acts, and the coup only bites where there are witnesses**
([combat.md § Two-stage death — the coup](../../subsystems/combat.md#two-stage-death--the-coup)).
So map it to ground, like Rainbow Village vs. Bewitched Forest — now
meaning Law↔Chaos and wired to real mechanics:

- **The counted settlement = witnessed ground.** The witnesses hold you
  to defeat-and-haul-him-in. Lawful violence happens where you can be
  *seen answering for it.*
- **The uncounted woods = no witnesses.** Down = dead, finished at
  leisure. Chaotic violence happens where it is just you and the deed.

*Where you corner him* becomes *what kind of good person you are.*

### 6.5 The mirror, out loud

Alignment is implicit everywhere else; here it gets **named.** The
warden's ledger records a sworn deputy who brought a man to judgment; the
folk-songs remember the one who walked into the woods alone. **Lawful
Good. Chaotic Good.** Stated through witnessed reputation + the private
mirror, no divine gate — the Law↔Chaos axis is godless and witnessed by
design ([alignment-slate.md § The mirror](./alignment-slate.md#the-mirror--reflection-not-reward-or-punishment)).

### 6.6 The no-murder floor

The one thing **identical on both paths**: you cannot murder the
innocent. The deputy and the folk hunter disagree bitterly about
*institutions* (the free political axis) but agree without argument
about the innocent (the locked moral axis, showing up as a
**guardrail**). The player feels the locked axis as a **wall** and the
free axis as a **fork**, in the same afternoon, with their hands.

## 7. The cast — roles the design needs

The draft authored a full cast; per the owner it is re-authored from the
EotL source. What each role must do:

- **The pair — the two paths made flesh.** Two Good NPCs who agree on
  everything but the one question splitting the settlement, the quarrel
  running **through a friendship** — the player picks a side in an
  argument between people who still set a place for each other. They
  built the settlement's safety together and split when the charter came
  down: one bet on the institution, one quit the badge after one burial
  too many.
  - **The Lawful warden** (draft: *Warden Alma*) — a court one person
    deep, mostly aspirational, filing every bounty with full procedural
    gravity. Wants the wolfshead **alive**: the first real trial makes
    the court real. (Kept idea: her seal is a ruin-relic older than the
    charter.)
  - **The Chaotic paymaster** (draft: *Barlow the Undertaker*) — not a
    hothead; disillusioned love, keeper of the **ledger of the dead**
    opposite her ledger of the living. Swears nobody, just pays. Wants
    him **dead**.
- **The wolfshead — the marquee bounty** (draft: *"the Gallant,"*
  warrant name *Cole Mercer*). A beloved outlaw who robs the Line (the
  TPA) with a smile, and a **killer** who launders his bodies through
  his story — capture wearing a friendly face, bite-sized Moloch you can
  put in a box. **He is the fork made flesh**: convicting a man people
  love is the bravest first trial and one the court might lose. His
  **crew** is fodder (a couple of humans who can *yield*); *he* is the
  poise duel; the fork lives in **how it ends.**
- **The human who catches your hand** (lesson 2) and **the veteran
  tutor** (master-apprentice, ideally **path-doubled** so the tutor
  reinforces the identity you picked) — both open (§10).

## 8. The crossroads hub and the board

### 8.1 The hub — two doors on one street

The hinge the area swings on. A geographic thesis teaches the axis
before a word of dialogue:

> **In the old land, Good and Evil were two different countries** — you
> walked west to one and east to the other. **Here, Lawful and Chaotic
> are two doors on the same street, ten feet apart** — and the thing that
> actually wants to kill you is out in the woods past *both* of them.

Law and Chaos are **neighbors who disagree**, not opposite territories.
The old crossroads forked the *road*; ours does not fork the road at all
— both paths lead into the same woods. It forks between two **people**.
Behind the player: the way in from campus, a threshold that keeps the
big folk out (our version of the old level-cap wall, sized for
newcomers). Ahead: the treeline, and the first monster audible from
safety. This is the **last safe ground**.

### 8.2 The board — the argument, in writing

The board is **contested**: the warden nails up the official notice;
the paymaster amends it in charcoal. The posting itself shows both paths
fighting over the same man. The draft ran four bounties — crime / gothic
/ fairy tale / sci-fi — **every one forking the same way**: the mix
teaches *un-genred world*, the fork teaches *the axis*. The wolfshead is
the marquee (the quest climax); the others are variety, so agency is
real. Mechanically each poster is a **bounty Contract** (issuer · hunter
· target; the poster is the authorization) with two payers —
[contract.md](../../subsystems/contract.md)'s open-bounty kind is the
nearest shipped shape. **Violence-for-contract, not loot-the-corpse** —
the economic thesis made concrete in a newbie's first fight.

### 8.3 The choice, and its honest asymmetry

- **Knock on the warden's door and get sworn.** The lawful move is the
  *deliberate* one — oath, badge (real conferred deputization), warrant.
  An extra step; the inconvenience is the point. Now you are **backed,
  bound, on the record, owing a live reckoning.**
- **Take the poster off the post and go.** The chaotic move is
  *frictionless* — nobody's permission. Going without the oath is
  already the chaotic choice.
- **See the paymaster.** The chaotic path's purse and mentor.

**Neither is lesser.** Law is more work and backs you; chaos is free and
alone. Same paycheck, different standing, different soul.
