## The sketch that was set aside — a starting point, not a decision

- The river's level at a distance derives from flow; each stretch has a
  bankfull flow above which water leaves the channel.
- A place citing the river (`river: { course, at, side }`) has its medium
  derived from the stage against its elevation — dry · wading · swimming ·
  underwater — under underwater-slate's water-as-a-state rule, so places
  that cite nothing never flood.
- What floats lifts and the current carries it; what sinks stays soaked;
  fire goes out; silt is left and feeds soil's reserves (the Nile).
- The runoff window derived from catchment size and steepness, so flash
  floods exist (the shipped 30-day mean gives only seasonal ones).
- Levees as works that raise a bank — and send the water to somebody
  downstream (lens 4, no right answer).
