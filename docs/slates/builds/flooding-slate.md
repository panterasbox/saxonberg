## The sketch that was set aside — a starting point, not a decision

- The river's level at a distance derives from flow; each stretch has a
  bankfull flow above which water leaves the channel.
- A place citing the river (`river: { course, at, side }`) has its medium
  derived from the stage against its elevation — dry · wading · swimming ·
  underwater — under underwater-slate's water-as-a-state rule, so places
  that cite nothing never flood.
- What floats lifts and the current carries it; what sinks stays soaked;
  fire goes out; silt is left and feeds soil's reserves (the Nile).
- The runoff window derived from catchment size and steepness, so flash
  floods exist (the shipped 30-day mean gives only seasonal ones).
- Levees as works that raise a bank — and send the water to somebody
  downstream (lens 4, no right answer).

## ⭐⭐ Dam failure — the structural flood **[DECIDED 2026-10-20]**

From the [masonry slate](./masonry-and-concrete-slate.md) § 3a (the dam as a body
resisting thrust). How a dam fails, each with a **warning first** — the
[machines slate](./machines-slate.md)'s sense signature before failure, at landscape scale:

- **Overtopping** — a flood exceeds the spillway; the river's stage is this slate's.
- **Piping** — seepage through an earth dam carries soil away and the channel grows.
  ⭐ **Its warning is a "boil"** — muddy water welling up downstream.
- **Sliding or overturning** (a gravity dam); **the abutment giving way** (an arch).
- ⭐ **Failure releases the stored volume** — the `StorageNode`'s level — so the
  catastrophe scales with what was held, derived.

**Governance — lens 7, at full weight:**

- ⭐⭐⭐ **Strict liability was born from a reservoir.** *Rylands v Fletcher* (1868): a mill
  owner's new reservoir burst through old mine workings into a neighbor's mine, and the
  court held that whoever keeps something dangerous on their land is **strictly liable**
  if it escapes, negligence or not — a founding case of tort law, literally about a
  reservoir. The [accountability](../../subsystems/accountability.md) ledger can carry it.
- **The Johnstown Flood** (1889) — a wealthy club's poorly kept dam (lowered, its
  discharge pipes removed) failed and killed over 2,200 people; the club was never held
  liable. Lens 4 and lens 7 at full weight.
- **Dam-safety inspection** — the three-layer measurement pattern; the
  [river slate](./river-slate.md)'s *"the dam that drowns a valley"* as the values case on
  the other side.
