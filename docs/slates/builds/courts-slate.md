## What the 2026-07 design settled, and still holds

- ⭐⭐ **Courts need a second governance primitive, the POOL, opposite the
  office SEAT.** A seat is single-holder, persistent, appointed or
  elected, the locus of authority *to act*, and it concentrates power. A
  pool is many-eligible-few-seated, drawn **at random** from the
  enfranchised, ephemeral (one case, then it dissolves), the locus of
  impartial *judgment*, and it distributes power. **The jury is the
  anti-office. A chosen judge is a captured judge.** Seats for doing,
  pools for judging.
- **The founding pool of one** — every jury is the founder — and it is
  self-undermining by randomness: as the venire grows the founder's share
  of any panel falls toward zero. Cleaner than an office handoff; the
  founder is diluted out, not asked to cede.
- **Eligibility is the enfranchised human population** — the concrete
  consumer of per-human enfranchisement. A jury of sockpuppets is a
  captured jury.
- **Courts are the legitimacy substrate.** Layer 1 facts are the
  engine's; layer 2 legitimacy is the court's; layer 3 significance is
  human. A case = matter + parties + engine-facts + verdict + a
  **conserved remedy**.
- ⭐⭐ **The court writes verdicts into the ledger; it does not swing the
  bailiff.** Enforcement is a separate institutional act.

# Part 1 — What is left for a court, after the bootstrap

The economic bootstrap and the institutions slate were shaped so a rule
reads the ledger and nobody judges. Default is revealed (inflows stopped
for the horizon). Repossession is a lien-holder taking what was pledged.
Charter breach is a ledger-checkable duty whose remedy is renewal.
Escheat is a treasury act after notice. The Note is non-recourse. The
ladder's gates are counts. **None of that wants a court, on purpose.**

What the engine cannot settle is **intent, authority, legitimacy and
consent** — and those sessions produced five dockets of exactly that
kind, plus the three constitutional ones already known:

| docket | the question only a panel can answer |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **embezzlement** by a player officer | the legs are on the ledger; *did the position's authority cover them?* |
| contested repossession | was the lien validly created; were the goods taken the goods pledged? |
| contested escheat | *I was not dormant; the notice never reached me; that dog was mine* — the property floor's due process needs somewhere to be said |
| charter breach before renewal | a rival with standing who says *Veshko is in breach now* wants a forum before five game-years pass |
| the intake floor's "clean" | presupposes a docket exists to be clean on |
| condemnation | the 2026-07 case #1 — takings; stays the constitutional showpiece, comes second |
| disbarment | wizard-duty's *the successor disbars them* |
| Art. VI attestation | verification before a law's build goes live |

⭐ **Case #1 is embezzlement** — more precisely *abuse of position*, of
which embezzlement is the money-shaped instance. It is the case the
current build produces first; the ledger gives the panel everything
except the one thing a panel is for; and its remedy is the conserved
shape the design already wants — restitution is a ledger leg, removal is
an order to an appointing authority.

# Part 2 — Constraints the surface inherits

- **No verb moves money or property.** A verdict *enters* a judgment;
  collecting it is enforcement.
- ⭐ **No clocks.** A case has states that advance when parties act — the
  contract system's observe-first shape. No trial date, no
  default-after-N-days. A respondent who never answers has said something
  the panel sees; the complainant may still move for the panel.
- **Sortition at the moment of need.** The panel does not exist until a
  party moves for it.
- **Facts are the engine's; testimony is claims.** The record has a
  **fact side** the clerk assembles and a **claim side** people add; the
  panel weighs. A claim is never a query.
- **Judgment seats human, mechanical seats NPC.** There is **no judge**.
  There is a **clerk** — an NPC in a position at the courthouse,
  executing rules: files, serves, assembles the record, draws the panel,
  records the verdict, enters the judgment. Everything a judge does that
  is not judging.
- **The docket is public.** The intake floor and the credit ladder's
  "clean" read it. A pending case is therefore a fact about a person,
  which is why filing has a cost (Part 6).
- **Jurisdiction is situs.** A matter has a place — the account, the
  parcel, the room — and the longest-prefix walk names the locality
  whose court hears it. The Compact's Art. VI court is the same substrate
  with `/compact` as its jurisdiction. One verb, one substrate; where you
  file falls out of what you file about.

# Part 3 — Roles

| role | who | how |
|---|---|---|
| complainant | anyone with **standing** — a relation the ledger shows to the matter (the account's owner, its committee, its appointing authority) | files |
| respondent | a principal — player or NPC | answers, or does not |
| the clerk | an NPC seat at the courthouse | mechanical |
| the panel | drawn from the venire; conflicts excluded | decides |
| witnesses | anyone | testify — claims on the record |

**The venire** is the enfranchised human population of the jurisdiction,
minus mechanical exclusions run *before* the draw: the parties; anyone
holding a position in the respondent's organization; anyone on the
respondent's or complainant's committee. ⚠ At dozens of players that can
empty a locality's pool for a Goodkin case, so the draw needs a
**widening rule**: exhaust the locality, widen to the parent
jurisdiction, and so on up to the realm — the same walk situs takes.

# Part 4 — The case, without a calendar

```
filed → served → (answered) → empanelled → decided → entered
```

Every arrow is an act. *Served* is the clerk's. *Answered* is optional.
*Empanelled* happens when a party moves for it. *Decided* when the
panel's rule is met. *Entered* is the clerk writing the judgment into the
ledger. Nothing advances by time; nothing expires.

**The record** has two sides and both are append-only:

- **facts** — engine state the clerk pulled or a party exhibited: ledger
  legs by transaction id, a position and its tenure, a chattel and its
  chain of title, a chronicle row, a contract's breach row;
- **claims** — pleas and testimony, attributed, never verified by the
  engine.

# Part 5 — The `court` verb

One verb, subcommands as the lifecycle axis — the `job` shape. Category
**civics**: it is the fiction's court, and the Compact's is the same
verb at `/compact`.

| form | who | does |
|---|---|---|
| `court` | anyone | the docket of this jurisdiction — open cases and their state. Public. |
| `court file <respondent> for <matter> [of <subject>]` | complainant | opens a case. **`<matter>` is a closed vocabulary** — `embezzlement` first; `breach`, `condemnation`, `nuisance` later — exactly as `job post` admits only checkable condition templates. The clerk checks standing mechanically, **assembles the fact record**, and takes the filing fee. A matter the clerk cannot assemble a record for cannot be filed. |
| `court answer [id] <plea>` | respondent | a claim, on the record |
| `court testify [id] <statement>` | anyone | a claim, on the record, attributed |
| `court exhibit [id] <thing>` | any party | attaches an engine fact the clerk did not pull |
| `court empanel [id]` | either party | moves for the panel; the clerk draws from the venire |
| `court recuse [id]` | a drawn juror | steps out; the clerk redraws |
| `court serve` | a juror | the cases you have been drawn to; `court serve [id]` opens the record |
| `court decide [id] <verdict> [<remedy>…]` | a juror | a vote. The case decides when the panel's rule is met — unanimity for small panels, a Schedule row |
| `court appeal [id]` | the losing party | to the parent jurisdiction, a larger panel. Named now, built later |

**Deliberately absent:** no judge; no bailiff verb; no fine outside the
closed remedy menu; no calendar; no jury-instruction engine (the fact
record *is* the instruction); no free-form matter.

## What the clerk assembles for embezzlement

- the respondent's **position** on the account's organization, its
  scope, and its tenure;
- every **ledger leg** the respondent authored against that account
  during tenure;
- the position's **rule** — what the charter or the ladder permitted that
  seat to do;
- **who appointed** them.

The record is *these movements, under this authority, during this
tenure*, and the question to the panel is exactly one: **were these
within the position's authority?** Verdict: `liable` / `not liable`.

## The remedy menu

Closed, per matter. For embezzlement:

| remedy | what it is | who executes |
|---|---|---|
| **restitution** | a **judgment debt** entered on the ledger, respondent → the account, capped at the legs proven | enforcement collects; from the estate, non-recourse beyond it, never labor |
| **removal** | an **order** to the appointing authority | the showrunner fires; if they do not, that is a fact about the corpo — charter-breach material |
| **a docket mark** | the outcome stays on the respondent's record | what "clean" reads |
| **a fine** | to the jurisdiction's treasury | a legitimate sink; the clerk enters it as a debt like restitution |

The court never moves the money. The clerk enters debts; the estate pays
them; enforcement is what happens when it does not.

# Part 6 — The cost of filing

A public docket makes filing a weapon: file against a rival and their
standing is questioned while the case pends. Two rules, both mechanical:

- ⭐ **The filing fee is forfeit if the panel finds the filing baseless**,
  and a baseless filing is itself a docket mark on the *complainant*. The
  fee is a Schedule row — small enough that a real grievance files, large
  enough that harassment costs. It goes to the jurisdiction's treasury.
- **The intake floor reads *decided* marks, never pending ones.** Pending
  is visible on the docket; it does not disqualify anyone.

# Part 7 — Where it lives on screen

- **The case is a card** — an Idea, laid out by the subject card:
  parties, state, the fact record, the claims, the panel.
- **The docket is a list card**, the roster shape.
- ⭐ **The panel deliberates in a bound channel** — the committee-channel
  mechanism with the panel as its audience, ephemeral, dissolving with
  the case. *The record is public; deliberation is private; the verdict
  is public.* Jury secrecy for free.
- **Filing happens at the courthouse.** The clerk affords the verb; the
  act has weight; Terminus gets a building. **Serving happens anywhere**
  — a juror who has to travel will not, and the venire is thin; the
  channel and the card are the jury room.
- **Being drawn is a notification**, through the notify policy — not a
  summons you can miss.

# Open questions

1. **Panel size after one.** One at founding is the design; the question
   is when it becomes three and whether that is a population threshold
   or a polity act. *Leans a Schedule row the polity raises*, so the
   founder's dilution is a decision somebody made rather than a surprise.
2. **Is the drawn panel named?** Athens said no; modern practice says yes.
   Hidden protects against pressure; named makes recusal checkable.
   *Leans named to the parties, hidden from the docket* until the verdict.
3. **The widening rule's stopping point.** Up to the realm, or does a
   Compact-jurisdiction case draw realm-wide from the start? *Leans the
   latter.*
4. **Does an NPC respondent answer?** A brain can be given a plea (the
   dialogue substrate), or NPC respondents simply do not answer and the
   panel decides on the record. *Leans the latter for v1.*
5. **Appeal.** One appeal, to the parent, larger panel — or none until a
   consumer wants it? *Leans named-not-built.*

# The drive

A scene, because the whole thing is one:

1. A player holds the loan-officer position at Goodkin. They move money
   from the branch's operating account to their own — a leg the position's
   rule does not cover.
2. Goodkin's showrunner (a committee member holding no position) walks
   into the Terminus courthouse and `court file <officer> for embezzlement
   of <the branch account>`. The clerk takes the fee, checks standing,
   and the case card shows the record already assembled: the position,
   the tenure, the legs, the rule.
3. `court` from anywhere shows the docket with the case pending. The
   officer's "clean" flag is unchanged.
4. The officer `court answer`s with a plea. A teller `court testify`s.
5. The showrunner `court empanel`s. With a founding pool of one the
   founder is drawn, notified, and `court serve` opens the record; the
   panel channel exists with one member.
6. The founder `court decide liable restitution removal`. The case
   decides; the verdict is public; the clerk enters a judgment debt on
   the officer and an order of removal in the showrunner's queue.
7. The showrunner fires the officer. The debt sits on the officer's
   estate; `court` shows the case entered; the officer's record carries
   the mark, and the intake floor now reads it.
8. A second, baseless filing by the officer against the showrunner is
   decided `not liable`; the officer's fee is forfeit and the mark lands
   on them.
