## 0. Astronomy — no build; its pieces routed **[DECIDED]**

The sky ships as real sun and moon geometry (`time.md` § Celestial — altitude, day
length, seasons, illuminance driving light and temperature, latitude per place); stars,
planets, eclipses and comets do not exist. Run through the lenses, astronomy beyond
navigation proved a curiosity rather than a build (the owner: *"I dunno what the actual
applications are here beyond the navigation stuff, it seems like more of a
curiosity"*). Its useful pieces have homes:

- **the pole star at night** → navigation ([expanse.md](../../subsystems/expanse.md)) —
  latitude without the sun;
- **star transits for true time** → [horology](./horology-slate.md)'s time service;
- **the telescope** → the [optics slate](./optics-slate.md)'s open question 4, answered:
  *feed a new astronomy read*, when there is a sky for it;
- **constellations and their myths** → lore, through the museum
  ([stratigraphy-and-finds](./stratigraphy-and-finds-slate.md) § 0);
- ⚠ **a cost recorded, not reversed** — the 360-day year matches the calendar exactly, so
  the calendar never drifts, which deletes the leap-year lesson and calendar reform
  (Britain dropped eleven days in 1752). Deliberate, for 12× and 90-day seasons.

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The station is a ship **[DECIDED]**

The [ships slate](./ships-and-harbors-slate.md)'s model — *"a Ship is a Structure whose
position is a variable"* — fits a station exactly: a structure with rooms (its floorplan)
whose position changes.

- **Docking is "alongside"** — a ship is always alongside a room, exits wired on arrival
  and unwired on departure; a docking port is a berth.
- **Launch is the ship launch** — from a pad, not a slip: a change of position, paid in
  fuel.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Orbit is an expanse **[DECIDED]**

The expanse is already a spherical graph (*"nodes are foci plus a radius"*). Orbits are
**altitude bands** around the planet, and a station's position **derives from its period
and the clock** — nothing ticks; an unobserved station reconciles on load, like
everything here.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ What it teaches **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

- ⭐⭐ **Weightlessness is not the absence of gravity** — a station is *falling* around the
  planet all the time: Newton's cannonball, thrown fast enough to fall around the world
  instead of into it. The most common misconception in physics, fixed where it lives.
- **Higher orbits are slower** — Kepler's third law: low orbit about ninety minutes; a
  geostationary satellite once a day, so it hangs over one spot.
- ⭐ **The rocket equation** — fuel grows **exponentially** with the speed wanted: why a
  rocket is mostly fuel, and why it drops stages.
- ⭐ **Docking is counterintuitive** — to catch something ahead of you in orbit you **slow
  down**, dropping to a lower, faster orbit: a puzzle a player can solve.
- **Fuel from shipped chains** — kerosene from the refinery; liquid oxygen from separating
  air (an industrial gas not yet made).

## 5. ⭐⭐ Satellites — continuity with what is designed **[DECIDED — lens 5]**

- **Communication** — the modern-epoch version of the aether's reach.
- ⭐ **Positioning ends celestial navigation** — the expanse's *"nothing fixes you by being
  seen"* gives way to a receiver that fixes you by signals: the navigator's skill replaced
  by an instrument, lens 5's sharpest case.
- **Weather satellites** retire the almanac-maker's guesswork.
- ⚠⚠ **Imaging meets the map firewall** — the world's index must never reach a client
  ([location-graph.md](../../subsystems/location-graph.md)). A satellite photograph must
  be a **claim a player buys** — a dated *seen* channel — never a view of the true map.
