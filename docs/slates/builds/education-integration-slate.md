# Education vertical — world & economy integration slate

> **Status: design conversation, captured. Not requirements.** Written
> 2026-08-06. Synthesises a conversation on the one gap the education
> slates leave open: **how the vertical funds itself and plugs into the
> greater game.** Not the academic apparatus (college-slate), not the
> campus as a place (eternal-university-slate) — the **seams** between
> education and the rest of the world: money, leaving campus, and the
> party.

> Scope, in the three questions the conversation actually asked:
> 1. **Who pays** — for a student to engage, for a teacher to earn?
> 2. **Leaving campus** — how does education integrate *outward* into the
>    shared economy and the adventure, rather than walling itself in?
> 3. **The party** — what does an education major *contribute* out there?

## Part 0 — Three "who pays" layers, kept apart

The conversation's first move was to stop conflating three different
questions hiding inside *"education costs money":*

| Layer | Denominated in | Who pays | Settled? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | **real dollars** | the platform licenses study.com's catalogue (transfer credit, test prep) | ✅ theirs to price; a licensing expense |
| **In-fiction cost** | **zorkmids** | what enrolling, examining, credentialing costs *in the game* | ⭐ this slate |
| **In-fiction viability** | zorkmids | whether a student / teacher can *live and engage* while doing it | ⭐ this slate |

> ⭐⭐ **The one seam where the layers touch — and its one-way rule.**
> (User, 2026-08-06: *"managing that expense is the legislature's to do
> as part of the budget."*) Elegant — the in-fiction polity diegetically
> **curates** which licensed content the world activates. It holds **only
> if the direction stays one-way**: the legislature's *zorkmid budget*
> decides *which* catalogue is switched on (the polity chooses what is
> taught); the *real* licence is paid out-of-band by the platform.
> **"The polity prioritises the catalogue," never "zorkmids buy the
> content."** Blur it and you have breached the college-slate firewall
> (*"real money never buys in-game advantage"*).

Everything below is the **zorkmid** layers.

## Part 1 — The survival version of the problem is already designed out

Two shipped-doctrine decisions dissolve the framing that actually sinks
educational games — *students can't afford to exist, so they grind
instead of learn*:

- ⭐ **"Income is aspirational, not survival"** (economy-slate, Law 2).
  No rent, no upkeep, no food-or-die. A broke student does not starve;
  being broke costs **nice things and advancement**, never existence.
  *"The real churn risk is rolelessness, not unemployment."*
- ⭐ **"The course gates nothing"** (college-slate). Competence gates
  capability; a course is the **fast, credentialed** path onto a ladder
  you can also climb by apprenticeship, self-study, or deed. A broke
  student still advances.

> **So the poverty trap is defanged. What remains are two sharper knots —
> and they are different problems.**

---

## Part 2 — Knot 1: the credentialed path costs coin the broke student lacks

Advancing is free-ish. The **credential** — enroll → assessment →
transcript — is the premium product, and it is exactly what the vertical
sells to educators. Gate it behind a grind and the pitch is behind a
grind.

### ⭐⭐ The answer is latent across two slates but never connected: earn-by-learning

- economy-slate: **transformation is where learning cashes out
  diegetically** — *"a recipe is knowledge."*
- college-slate: treats the lab purely as **assessment.**

> ⭐⭐⭐ **Fuse them. The vocational classroom IS a crafting venue** (the
> Dave's-Bar four-tuple: inputs, tools, recipes, supervised labour).
> Supervised practice produces real — lower-grade, provenance-stamped —
> goods. **The student earns *while* learning; the lab section is a
> workshop with an exam attached.**

This funds the vocational student without a side-job that competes with
class for their time — the earning *is* the coursework. It stacks with
the ladder already in college-slate + vocations:

> **student → TA → instructor.** Master a course, get hired to teach it.
> The ladder is *simultaneously* the teacher-supply answer and a
> senior-student income source — junior tuition / subsidy pays senior
> wages, so the money **circulates** rather than being minted.

⚠ Not every subject is vocational-productive (Poli Sci 200 forges
nothing). Earn-by-learning funds the **trades**; theory courses lean on
Part 4's external payer.

---

## Part 3 — Knot 2: teacher pay bottoms out in "who funds the school"

The user's phrase — *"earn or some kinda subsidisation"* — maps exactly
onto vocations.md's four payment shapes:

| Lane | Who pays | Needs | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade** | students (tuition) | students to already hold coin | circular at bootstrap |
| **Wage** | the University-as-business | school revenue (tuition / sponsor) | ⭐ **works now — no fiscal cycle** |
| **Treasury** | tax → appropriation | **the fiscal cycle** | ⚠ **blocked** (vocations' keystone gap) |
| **Stipend** | the reserve (minted) | nothing | launch hack — it inflates |

- **Wage runs today**: teaching is employment (a Position on the
  University business, shifts + roster) — *if the school has revenue.* A
  player-master selling sections lives here too.
- **Treasury is the honest "subsidisation"** — free public education —
  and it is blocked on the **fiscal cycle** (tax → budget → appropriate →
  disburse), which vocations.md already flags as *"not one gap among four
  — the gap that unlocks an entire class of work,"* the public teacher
  included.
- The **enroll stipend** (credit-slate: one of only three mint sites) is
  the launch faucet — fine to bootstrap a new student, but minted, so not
  the model.

## Part 4 — ⭐⭐⭐ The bottom line: the vertical needs an external payer, and sponsorship is the keystone

> **The education economy cannot be internally self-funding at bootstrap
> without inflating.** Student money comes from stipends / jobs; teacher
> money comes from tuition; a closed loop either mints or needs an
> injection.

Two honest external payers are already designed:

1. ⭐⭐ **The corpo sponsor — the keystone.** college-slate's
   enrollment-as-contract sleeper: *"employer sponsorship, deferred
   tuition, the Working Scholars shape — a third party funds a cohort
   against outcomes — all come for free on a substrate built for gig
   work."* A sponsor with a **real budget** (economy-slate's bounded
   participant, **paid not minted**) funds **student tuition AND teacher
   wages in one move**, and respects conservation. Available now; rides
   contract.md escrow + verifiable conditions.
2. **The treasury** — public education, the tax-funded lane —
   **fiscal-cycle-blocked** (Part 3).

⚠ **Sponsorship's two costs, both already fenced by credit-slate:**

- It attaches a **purpose** to education (train labour for the sponsor),
  which can distort learning-for-its-own-sake. A feature at the vertical
  scale (education majors *want* a placement) but watch the mission.
- The income-share / indenture shape brushes the **13th module** — so it
  stays inside the fence: *no debt bondage, discharge exists, the
  property floor is kernel, authorship is inalienable.* A sponsored
  student who walks keeps their capacity and everything they learned
  making the first thing.

## Part 5 — ⭐⭐⭐ Leaving campus is the DELIVERY, not the leak

The user's worry: *"most people are gonna wanna leave campus and fight
monsters… what about leaving campus?"* — the campus cannot be a walled
garden when the greater world is the draw (*"a greater economy that your
chosen vocation slots into as part of a shared experience"*).

> ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The reframe: leaving campus is not a leak in the education
> product — it is the half of the product a content library literally
> cannot sell.** college-slate's own principle — *the classroom's job is
> to create demand for knowing, not to deliver knowing* — means the
> knowing is **spent** elsewhere. That elsewhere is the world. A campus
> that keeps you in is the "worse study.com inside a game" failure the
> slate already names.

And you do not build an earning system that works *despite* students
leaving. **The leaving is the earning.** The mechanisms all exist:

- ⭐⭐ **Deed-mode assessment is off-campus by construction.**
  college-slate's fourth mode — *"the engine watched you do it, in the
  course of living"* — cannot happen in an exam hall. **The field IS the
  exam.** Whoever leaves to fight and craft is banking deed evidence
  whether or not they are thinking about a transcript; the credential is
  waiting when they want it.
- ⭐ **The vocation is the thread.** Campus teaches it; the world employs
  it. **Leaving campus is entering your vocation.** The smith learns on
  campus and smiths in the economy: one loop, not two systems.
- ⭐ **The physics you own is consumed out there.** Heat, materials,
  ballistics, metabolism — learned in Magic 101, **spent** in combat,
  delving, hazards, crafting. Monster-fighting is the curriculum's
  consumption sink, not a distraction from it. **The game and the school
  are the same activity measured two ways** — the applied-hours thesis
  arriving through the front door.

### The loop and its heartbeat

> **learn on campus → leave to apply & earn (vocation, loot→crafting,
> deeds) → return to credential & advance → leave again at a higher
> tier.**

The economy is the flywheel that turns campus and world into one cycle.
college-slate's **scheduled sections / self-paced content** gives it a
rhythm — *adventure between sections, return for the ones with stakes* —
which is also a reason to come back.

### The education major, off campus, is a *better* teaching practicum

Teaching is a **Position**, and a Position sits at **any venue** — so the
education major does not only TA a lecture hall; they apprentice a newbie
at a forge, run a guild workshop, tutor in a shop. Two income streams —
**doing** the vocation and **teaching it where the work is** — both
require the world, and both are richer than a sim classroom: teaching
embedded in a working economy where the subject is real and the students
have real stakes. The **NPC-handoff** (college-slate: a player's section
running beside an NPC's, live handoff; economy-slate's graceful
degradation) is what lets them leave campus *and* keep a class —
**teaching and adventuring stop being mutually exclusive.**

### The inbound door is the complement, not the integration

Bringing experts onto campus to testify (the guest lecturer, the
practitioner viva) is nice texture — but it keeps the student on campus.
It is **not** what makes the campus part of the world; the outbound loop
is.

### ⚠ Open: the boundary itself

The campus must be a **permeable district in the shared world**, not a
sealed instance — walk out the gate into the same economy everyone else
is standing in. That is **eternal-university-slate** territory (topology,
the gate, the parking-lot seam). The unresolved dial: **how tightly
scheduled sections tether you, and whether the gate is a hard zone edge
or just another street.**

## Part 6 — ⭐⭐⭐ The education major in a party: the loremaster

*"What position does an education major have in your standard party? What
do they contribute?"* — the trap is the word **position.** Saxonberg has
no party classes; it has people who understand things and can do things.
Two honest reads, and you want both.

### Read 1 — "education major" is an OVERLAY, not a slot

In the fiction they *have* a vocation (medic, mage, smith, scholar) and
contribute **that**. What makes them an education major is that they are
learning to **teach** it — a second-order layer on any base role: the
medic-who-teaches, the mage-who-teaches. **They never trade a combat slot
for "the teacher."** The party is not picking teacher *instead of* healer
— it is getting a healer who also makes everyone else better. Additive,
so "who'd want one" never bites.

### Read 2 — ⭐⭐ teaching crystallises into a native archetype that is unusually strong *here*

Because of a decision already made: **saxonberg does not surface stats.**
No *"fire resistance: 40%,"* no quality numbers, no damage readouts — the
world is honestly simulated but **opaque.** So **the scarcest resource in
any party is understanding**, and the educated character supplies it.
Three contributions, all diegetic, **none a stat buff**:

| Contribution | What it is | Rides |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Legibility (the diagnostic)** | reads the real physics the world will not show as numbers — *"layered armour, a thrust slides off — it needs a crushing blow"; "not fire, a phase threshold — hit it with cold."* The epistemic-advantage thesis **as a party role**: in a game with no weakness-tooltip, the loremaster **is** the tooltip, earned. **Resists wiki-automation** for the same reason the exams do — the live situation is world-sampled and unique, so it cannot be pre-answered, only *read.* | vocations' epistemic gate; college-slate world-sampling |
| ⭐ **Conferral (the force multiplier)** | does not buff the party — **teaches** it. The mid-fight *"brace against the charge — like this"* leaves each teammate permanently, actually better: a real **deed / competence event, not an aura.** The applied-hours loop running *inside the party.* | advancement **conferrals** |
| ⭐ **Command (the coordinator)** | pedagogical leadership in field-tactics clothing — the Warlord who positions everyone | the **command Discipline** + formations |

### ⭐⭐⭐ The payoff — the whole pitch in one line

> **The education student's real-world skill — understanding a subject
> well enough to teach it — is literally their in-game superpower.**

You cannot fake being the party's loremaster; you have to actually grasp
the simulated physics, and *"understand it well enough to teach it"* is
the deepest grasp there is — **to teach is to learn twice.** So the
pedagogy track and the loremaster role are the same competence seen
twice, and the education major who leaves campus to adventure gets what
study.com can never give them: **the party is their classroom, the
dungeon is their lesson plan, and their students are their friends. Their
teaching practicum is the raid.**

No other game offers that role, and it falls straight out of two
decisions already made: **no stat readouts, and competence you can
confer.**

### ⚠⚠ The one seam that is not clean yet: conferral in the field without becoming a buff

Legibility is information; command is coordination — both are honestly
non-numeric. **Conferral is the risk.** If *"brace like this"* resolves to
a mechanical advantage, it is a stat buff wearing a lesson's clothes —
exactly what economy-slate bars (*effects are diegetic events, not stat
math*). The design constraint, unresolved:

> **A field conferral must leave a real, durable competence gain in the
> *taught* character — a Transcript deed they keep — not a timed modifier
> on the teacher's presence.** The teacher's contribution is that the
> party is *permanently better educated*, realised through the taught
> member's own subsequent honest actions — never a number the teacher
> radiates.

Getting this resolution right is what keeps the loremaster honest. It is
the highest-value open mechanic in this slate.

## Open questions / forks

1. ⭐⭐ **The governing fork — public good or market good?** Is the
   **credentialed path** meant to be *cheap / free at point of use*
   (public-education model → needs the **treasury** lane, i.e. the fiscal
   cycle, or a **sponsor**) — or a *market good* (tuition → needs the
   **earn-side** standing first)? Everything above sequences differently
   by the answer. *Leans: hybrid — trades self-fund via earn-by-learning;
   theory + the credential lean on a sponsor now, treasury later.*
2. ⚠⚠ **Conferral-in-the-field without a buff** (Part 6). The one
   unresolved mechanic; resolve it before the loremaster ships.
3. ⚠ **The campus / world boundary rhythm** (Part 5). Section-tether
   strength; hard zone edge vs. permeable street. Owned by
   eternal-university-slate.
4. **Does sponsorship distort the mission?** A purpose attached to
   learning. A feature at vertical scale, a watch-item for the free
   player.
5. **The legislature / real-dollar seam** (Part 0). Confirm the one-way
   rule holds wherever the catalogue budget is implemented.

## What this slate does NOT cover

- The **academic apparatus** — course / assessment / the item generator —
  college-slate.
- The **campus as a place** — eternal-university-slate (owns the
  boundary).
- The **fiscal cycle / lending** — credit-slate (this slate consumes its
  outputs; the treasury lane waits on it).
- **Combat mechanics** — the loremaster's three contributions are named
  here; how command and conferral resolve inside a fight is combat's
  build.
