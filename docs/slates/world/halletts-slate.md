## 0. The owner's frame

The show's studio is **Pantera's Box's** — the owner's studio and brand, thirty years older than the game —
not Saxonberg's or the Compact's, and the game's architecture never borrows the studio's brand as a
metaphor. The owner lives in **Terminus** (moving into the Prime Minister's house in Saxonberg before being
elected would be premature), so the show works in Terminus. *"The vibe I'd wanna go for more than anything
else is like whatever our version of Beale Street is, broadcasting right from there"* — the broadcast desk
**visible from the street through a window, like the old radio DJs.** And: *"this place should have a
history; I shouldn't be its first tenant."*

The precedent: Memphis radio in the late 1940s and '50s — **WDIA** (1948, the first station programmed for
Black audiences; B.B. King and Rufus Thomas among its DJs) and **Dewey Phillips** doing *Red, Hot and Blue*
for WHBQ from the Hotel Chisca's mezzanine, fans crowding in to watch. The DJ was a performer in plain
view, on the same streets as the clubs.

## 1. The Dial **[DECIDED — the owner]**

Terminus's music and nightlife strip. When it was alive **every storefront was a station**, and walking it
end to end was like turning a radio dial — music from one door, a voice from the next, then a piano — so
everyone called it **the Dial**. It has an official name on the city's plat that nobody uses.

- **Its history** (light, future-proof): the live years came with the aether becoming a network (the
  story bible's Widening); attention moved indoors and the street went quiet (the bible's Feed — the harm
  the project was built against); now its storefronts are dark **and each remembers what it was**.
- **It fills back in with history already in its walls**: when later tenants come, **Mayfield & Co. sells
  old storefronts that each had a life** — the music hall, the record shop, the club — not empty lots.
  Each opens when there is a player to run it.
- **The show is its anchor tenant** — the one tenant that draws the traffic the rest live on.
- **It answers the politics problem without a lecture**: the show lives among storefronts and music, not
  government buildings; people who would bristle at a game about governments meet it on a music street.
- **Where it runs:** open — the lean is between the arrival plaza and the waterfront, so newcomers walk it.

## 2. Hallett's **[DECIDED — the owner]**

**The last live studio on the Dial**, the one that held out longest. Stations of that era were known by
their personalities: this one by **June Hallett**, who ran it and **played the piano in the front window**.
The sign over the door still reads **HALLETT'S**; under it now, **home of Pantera's Box** (the Lounge is
already *the Pantera's Box Lounge*, so the building keeps its own name and the business sits inside it).

- **The owner leases it from the Halletts** — the family still holds the building and kept it shut,
  waiting for someone to put it back on the air. Leasing keeps the history alive in the world: the landlord
  is the station's past. (Title stays theirs; the lease is a tenure with its protections — § 7s; holding.md
  tenure terms.)
- **What is still inside:** the booth in the front window, dusty, the glass still lettered; a wall of
  photographs from the crowded years; ⭐ **the station's record library** — the recordings June played,
  **content inherited** for the jukebox down the street (recording-slate's sound recordings).

### The piano in the window **[DECIDED — the owner: "I do play piano on stream"]**

Early stations commonly kept a studio piano for live music between records; Hallett's sat in the glass.
**It went quiet when the station did, and the family kept it** — under a dust sheet when the owner walks in.
The performance slate's own frame is this exact case (*"I can sit down and play a piano performance in my
own office at home, but the game experience can model something completely different"*), and its first
decision is that **the performance happens off the page and the game supplies the venue around it**:

- **The front window is both the broadcast desk and a stage** — and *"a stage is a source the room hears"*:
  the room is **the sidewalk**. Passers-by hear the owner's real piano through the stream, **audio by
  default** (the real office would break the scene); video the performer's choice.
- **The owner's character sits at June's piano** while they play — the on-page side shows who plays where;
  the sound is the real performance, off the page.
- **The fiction around it is text**: people stopping, a crowd building outside the glass, applause batched
  into one line, someone asking for one more. Stream nights become piano nights on the Dial.

### The window is the on-air sign

Lit and occupied when the show is live, dark when it is not; a screen in the glass shows the stream for the
street (venues own their screens — this one is the show's own).

## 3. The show — the first broadcaster **[DECIDED]**

The broadcasting slate already names it: *"the first broadcasting institution is the owner's show — a
committee with seats, the owner and the stream moderators whose grants reach into Twitch and YouTube."*

- **A broadcasting business** — the trade's top rung (*the stream*, carried by the aether). Its content is
  partly off the page (the owner's face, voice, piano — marked), mostly the game rendered outward (redaction
  guards that crossing). **Not outside the fiction** — the earlier proposal of a free-floating organization
  belonging to no model was wrong.
- **Its records live with its place** — the business, its positions, its house rules and its program
  channel under Hallett's path, authored under the lease's grant (§ 7s; class 4).
- **The owner appoints**, as Bobalu Smallberries, proprietor of the show.
- **Its crew** (moderation § 25): **constables** over the program's chat — in the game and relayed from
  Twitch, YouTube and Kick, each carrier's tools granted to the seats — and optional moderators. Its reach
  stops at its own boundary; bigger matters go to the Constabulary. A private venue: its house rules may
  curate. Most of its constables will also sit elsewhere; each act from one seat.
- **Not special to the owner**: every broadcaster gets the same — a station, its staff, its house rules, its
  program on whatever screens choose to carry it.
- **Court day** (moderation § 14a) is held here: the judiciary on air.

## 4. The founder's home

Not the dorm; in town while the founder is the only one here (an *outside Terminus* need not exist at

## 4a. Under the ownership model (separation-of-powers § 7t)

The Halletts are a **disclosed fictional owner** (the founder's pen behind them, their sale proceeds to the
public treasury). Hallett's **outside** (facade, sign) is theirs; the **inside** is the show's while it holds
the space — one voice; **preservation covenants run with the land** (the lettering, June's piano); the
show's staff, equipment and records are its own and leave with it.
