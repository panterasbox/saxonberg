## 0. ⭐⭐⭐ The foundation — what a museum is in an information space **[DECIDED — the owner]**

The owner: *"Museums as keepers of artifacts have mostly no meaning in an information space, because
nothing here is essential. The only essential anything is the silicon storing the long-term bits that keep
state between reboots, and everything else is transient by construction. So that idea of a museum and any
of the different mechanics you suggested attaching to it are just kind of absurd, or require some serious
commitment to the bit. However, as an information space, museums fill an essential function: they organize
basically every idea in the game into some navigable, comprehensible physical space, at least in the
fiction. It coalesces the abstract into material and shared experience. So I think we need to lean into
what museums are good at and try to avoid shoehorning them into something they're not."*

### What drops

Everything that depended on irreplaceable objects: **provenance and forgery as a museum's core** (forgery
stays in the trades — a maker's mark can be faked on goods — but it is not what museums are about),
**heists** (nothing unique to steal), **conservation**, **acquiring rarities**. ⚠ § 2's *common shape*
below is superseded where it leans on these.

### ⭐⭐⭐ A museum is the knowledge of the world given a floor plan

The game holds enormous structured knowledge — the tech tree's laws, the Disciplines, the species, the
materials, the lore and its history, the realm's places, the Compact's law — living in graphs, documents and
the out-of-fiction wiki. **A museum lays one region of it out as rooms you walk through with other people.**

The model is not the Louvre but **the Exploratorium** (San Francisco, Frank Oppenheimer): built around
**phenomena, not artifacts**, its exhibits demonstrate something, and it published *cookbooks* so anyone
could build the same exhibits elsewhere.

- **An exhibit is a recipe** — content that shows an idea working, installable anywhere (a player may put
  one in their home); the cookbook is content packs.
- ⭐ **An exhibit runs on the real engine; its label is a claim** **[DECIDED — lens 1, invariant]** — the
  law-and-code split again ([tech-tree-slate](../society/tech-tree-slate.md) § 6a): the cooling exhibit
  runs the actual cooling law, so it cannot be wrong; the label is a person's description, signed,
  possibly wrong, contestable on the argument map. **A label contradicting what its own exhibit visibly
  does is a finding anyone can make. Demonstrations are never faked.** Every exhibit explains itself
  through the physics receipt (tech-tree § 6d).
- **What makes a museum is the arrangement** — which exhibits, in what order, with what labels: the
  curator's argument.
- ⭐ **A museum is the wiki inside the fiction**, in the world's own names — laws named by its peoples,
  places by theirs ([place-names-slate](./place-names-slate.md)).

### What is scarce

Not objects: **curatorial judgment** (selection and order — the engine could generate a hall of every
species, but that is an index, not a museum; a museum says *these, in this order, because*), **attention**
(a visitor's time is what a museum competes for), and **space** (rooms on parcels, which cost rent).

### ⭐⭐ Generated halls and curated museums **[DECIDED — lens 2]**

- **The ordinary case is generated** — a hall laid out automatically from the knowledge graph, every species
  or every node of a field: complete, always current, costing nothing to author.
- **The bespoke case is curated** — a person's selection and argument over the same exhibits. That is where
  the value and the vocation are.
- ⭐ **Generated halls draw only on public knowledge** **[DECIDED — lens 3a]** — no undiscovered places, no
  corpo's hidden hand, nothing behind the spoiler line; a curated museum may choose to show more, as its
  author's call.
- ⭐ **Curated exhibits refer to knowledge by stable id** **[DECIDED — lens 5]** — a law's id, a Discipline
  node, a species — never by content path, so rewriting the world's content orphans no curator's work (why
  credentials key to nodes, not course rows).

### The trade

- **What it produces:** understanding and shared experience — **a venue where people meet over ideas**:
  field trips, openings, a docent's tour, a date.
- **The disciplines**, anchored in real fields: **exhibition design** (arranging space so an idea reads in
  order) · **heritage interpretation** (Freeman Tilden's *Interpreting Our Heritage*, 1957 — the classic
  principles for making a place meaningful to a visitor) · **knowledge organization** (classification and
  taxonomy) · **label writing** (short, true, signed) · **docent work** (the guided tour — talk, which a text
  world does well).
- **The pedagogy:** spatial memory — **the method of loci**, the memory palace, built for everyone;
  discovery by wandering, finding the game's knowledge without knowing what to search for.
- **The economy:** who pays — admission, patrons (corpos endowing halls for prestige), the polity for public
  museums, the university for teaching collections; ⭐ **what it commissions** — exhibits must be *built*
  (a working model engine, a reconstructed mill, a diorama), so **museums pay crafters for work valued for
  showing, not using**, as art is: real demand for the trades from an institution that makes nothing
  physical; what it employs — curators, docents, exhibit builders. Curation passes the vocation test wherever
  someone pays.

### Whose speech **[DECIDED — lens 7]**

- ⭐ **A public museum is government speech, not a public forum** — *Pleasant Grove City v. Summum* (US
  Supreme Court, 2009): a monument a government displays is its own speech, so it may choose its message,
  unlike a forum, which must stay viewpoint-neutral. So **the Compact's channels are forums**, kept
  content-neutral ([moderation-slate](../society/moderation-slate.md) § 24), while **its museums speak for
  it** — choosing their story is the polity's legislative business, on the record (Saxonberg's doctrine).
- **Private museums are private speech; corporate museums disclose their sponsor.**
- **Labels are claims on the argument map** — anyone may object; an unanswered objection stays visible.
  Interpretation stays contested with no artifacts at all: rival museums making rival arguments over the
  same exhibits.

### Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — the method of loci, phenomena-first exhibits, Tilden, exhibition design, knowledge
   organization; **exhibits run the real engine, labels are claims**.
2. **Expression** — **generated halls the ordinary case, curated museums the craft**; exhibits installable
   anywhere.
3. **3a Immersion** — the wiki inside the fiction, in the world's names; **generated halls public-only**.
   **3b Participation** — curators, docents, builders; museums as venues; rival arguments.
4. **Values** — what deserves a museum, and a public museum's story: the curator's, and the polity's.
5. **Continuity** — generated halls stay current; **curated exhibits key to stable ids**.
6. **Economy** — attention, judgment and space are scarce; admission, patrons, polity, university pay;
   commissions are demand for the trades.
7. **Governance** — **public museums are government speech**; private speech and disclosed sponsorship;
   labels contestable on the argument map.

## 2. The common shape — ⚠ superseded where it leans on irreplaceable objects (§ 0)

What the gathered museums already share:

- **Exhibits are things in the world**, with provenance — a chain of custody the record can verify.
- ⭐ **A label is a claim**, signed by whoever wrote it, contestable — the finds slate's rule, and the
  argument map's. A museum is an interpretation, never the world's own voice.
- **Forgery is play** — the record never certifies in-fiction documents or objects
  ([record-integrity-slate](../platform/record-integrity-slate.md) § *The interface*), so a forged exhibit
  is caught by people reading provenance, never by the engine.
- **A curator is a seat** — a person's, by the curator-seat idea; an NPC curator is the floor.
- **Teaching, never a shortcut** — the Rejection warning: a museum that hands you what the world makes you
  earn fails lens 1.
- **Whose museum** — the polity's to author (Saxonberg's doctrine), an institution's (the TPA's), or a
  player's (the curator).

## 3. Two museums from the campus design

### The technology museum — the ladder, walked through

The ladder from Muscle to Future as **one building, a room per rung**, each with its prime mover working.
The reference is Disney's *Carousel of Progress*, a rotating theater through the eras of household
technology. It is a museum, not the campus's structure — the campus itself is zones by rung
([eternal-university-slate](./eternal-university-slate.md) § 2026-11-18). **[OPEN — where it stands: on
campus, in Saxonberg, or both.]**

### The realm museum — a preview of the starting realm

A museum of the realm's **published places**, each with its rung, its trades and its landmarks, **read
from live data** — so it fills as the content builds add places and never needs the realm designed first.
⚠ **Only what is public** (paths are spoilers): undiscovered places are fogged, one click to reveal, never
prevented.
