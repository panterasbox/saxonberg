## 0. The thesis, kept from the draft

⭐⭐⭐ **Sugar is the last demand root nothing feeds.** Simple syrup, the
mojito and the old-fashioned ask for `sugar`; the cooking pack's sack has no
maker. And the session found a **second, hidden root** (§ 6): the realm
already pours rum that no cane was ever crushed for.

## 1. ⭐⭐ Where cane grows — nowhere yet **[DECIDED]**

Every authored site is at **42° N** (the Kestrel basin), plus one at 66° S
and one at 71°. Cane needs frost-free ground, and at 42° the climate field
correctly refuses it. History offered three answers — a warm place across
the sea (Madeira, the Canaries, the Caribbean), a marginal mild coast at home
(medieval Sicily, Valencia, Andalusia, irrigated), or **refining imports in
town** (Venice and Antwerp imported raw sugar and made loaves in city sugar
houses). The founder chose:

1. ⭐ **The Terminus sugar house first** — the medieval European city's actual
   trade: it buys raw sugar and does the boil, the cone and the claying.
2. ⭐ **The sugar island after** — a new locality at a southern node of the
   Greywater with a low-latitude climate site, where cane is grown, crushed
   and boiled to raw sugar and molasses, then shipped home.

⚠ **Until the island exists, raw sugar is a STOCKED LINE** — a producer stock,
the pattern Veshko's rum uses today. The markets slate's import backstop is
designed, not shipped; when it is, raw sugar arrives at the import price.
**The island replaces the stocked line with produced sugar.**

⭐⭐ **And the island gives the sea its first cargo.** Sugar was *the*
maritime commodity; the Greywater's ships get their first reason to sail
somewhere, as potash gives the Kestrel its first downstream freight.

**Beet** — *"I don't really care about sugar beets"*. Beet sugar is
19th-century (Marggraf 1747, Achard's factory 1801); a cold realm's medieval
sweeteners are honey and maple, which ship. Beet is a **later-epoch row** on
the same chain (slice and diffuse in hot water instead of crushing), not
built.

## 2. The crush — stage 2, the island **[DECIDED]**

- **`mill` with an edge-runner row** — an upright stone rolled round a
  basin, the olive mill's shape, then the pulp pressed. The three-roller
  vertical mill (Brazil, c. 1600) is a later mill row. *The stones do not
  care what is between them* — no new verb.
- **Juice recovery is a fraction by mill and passes** (edge-runner and press
  half to 60 %, rollers more, a second pass more again); the rest stays in
  the **bagasse**.
- ⚠⚠ **The draft's fuel trade-off was backwards; the truth is better.**
  Pressing harder gets **more juice** (more water to boil off — latent heat,
  ~2.26 MJ/kg) **and drier bagasse** (which burns better); bagasse's fuel is
  set mostly by its fibre. **The shortfall comes from the boil's EFFICIENCY**:
  open kettles over a poor arch need more heat than the bagasse gives, and
  the difference is **firewood**. ⭐ History's lesson — **Madeira**
  (Portuguese for *wood*) and **Barbados** were stripped of forest to boil
  sugar; the fix was a better furnace, the **Jamaica train** (§ 3). The
  trade's lever is the boiling house, and its failure mode is the forest.
- **Bagasse is dried before it burns** — the shipped drying physics, spread in
  the sun or stacked in a bagasse house. ⭐ **Its ash is little ash** for the
  ashery (potash) — cane-mill ash went back on the fields.
- ⭐ **Cut cane inverts.** Sucrose inverts within about a day of cutting — a
  `chemical` maturation profile on cut cane; **inverted sugar will not
  crystallize**, so a crop that sits makes **less sugar and more molasses**
  by derivation, not a fail state. The mill and the boil run together because
  the chemistry makes them.

## 3. The boil, the strike and the grades **[DECIDED]**

1. **Temper and skim** — lime into the warm juice; impurities rise as scum,
   skimmed. Tempering is a skill (too little and it will not grain, too much
   and it comes out dark); lime ships from the quarry (its third buyer, after
   potash's causticizing and building). ⭐ **The skimmings go to the
   distiller** — fermented skimmings were part of rum's wash.
2. **Concentrate** — **cane juice is a fourth `evaporative` profile** (about
   15 % sugar; brine, sap and lye are the others — *before designing a
   mechanism, look for the shipped one with a different feedstock*),
   fire-heated like sap. ⭐ **The Jamaica train** — a row of kettles over one
   flue, ladled from the largest and coolest to the smallest and hottest — is
   a **built** efficiency (an assembly), the lever of § 2.
3. ⭐⭐ **The strike — the cut is the skill.** **Boiling point rises as water
   leaves** (boiling-point elevation), which is **why a thermometer reads
   concentration** — the confectioner's soft-ball, hard-ball and crack stages
   are just temperatures. Before thermometers the boiler used the **thread
   test** (a drop drawn between finger and thumb) — a **`Reading` row**, the
   potash egg test's sibling, the thermometer its later rung. Stop early:
   **cane syrup** (treacle); at the strike it **crystallizes** as it cools;
   push on and the rising temperature crosses into **caramel, then burnt** —
   the calibration the sap profile already had to get right (what ruins it is
   boiling too far, not the arch being too hot).
4. **Grade by sequence** — the first strike cures in the moulds and its
   drainings are molasses; **re-boiled, the molasses gives second and third
   sugar**, darker and cheaper, until what is left will not crystallize at all:
   **blackstrap**. *"The cheap sugar and the expensive sugar come out of the
   same pan at different times."* Inversion shortens the sequence.

**Grade end to end:** crop · freshness (inversion) · temper · skimming ·
strike · sequence.

## 4. The loaf — stage 1, the sugar house **[DECIDED]**

- ⭐ **The cone is a draining mould.** The strike is ladled into conical
  moulds point-down, the tip plugged; it crystallizes in the cone's shape;
  unplugged, **molasses drains** into a pot below over days to weeks in the
  **curing house** — the mould subsystem's draining shape (the cheese hoop),
  so the loaf sets and separates its molasses with no new mechanism.
  ⚠ **An assumption, not a settled base**: *"moulds shipped without a real
  design conversation"* — the [moulding slate](../builds/moulding-slate.md) is that
  conversation, and this design should be checked against what it decides.
- ⭐ **Claying is a leach.** Wet clay on the loaf's wide end lets water seep
  down through the crystals, washing the remaining molasses out of the tip —
  the top whitens, the base stays darker. **The same act as leaching lye from
  ash** (potash) and, later, bucking (cleanliness's laundry): water
  percolating through a porous solid carrying out what dissolves — **three
  consumers of one percolation mechanism**, shared substrate owned by
  whichever builds first.
- ⭐ **One loaf holds several grades** — the white head, the middle, the brown
  foot. A grocer cut the loaf by grade; pricing within one loaf is honest and
  derived from how far the washing reached.
- **Unclayed is muscovado** — raw sugar drained in a barrel, brown, moist,
  cheap; the same chain with claying skipped.
- **The loaf dries** (a stove house) and is sold whole; people cut it with
  **sugar nips**, and loaves were wrapped in **blue paper** to look whiter. ⭐
  The cocktails' sack comes from **nipping and pounding a loaf** — the shipped
  `mill`, or a mortar.
- ⚠ **The cones are earthenware** and pottery waits on the moulding slate
  (brick first), so for this build the cone is a **stocked row**. ⭐ Sugar
  becomes **pottery's first customer** when pottery ships.

## 5. Sugar is tags **[DECIDED]**

No new mechanism. A recipe slot's `category:` matches **a tag on the input's
material** (crafting.md, *match input slots*), and the **sweetener vocabulary
already shipped** with the taps build (crafting.md): `sugar`, `honey` and
`syrup` are slottable; the family tag **`sweetener`** is carried by all and,
by design, **gates nothing** (*a cocktail accepting granulated sugar and a
dough accepting honey is not a distinction any cook would recognise*).

- Produced sugar's material carries **`sugar` + `sweetener`**; grades (first
  white, later brown, muscovado) live within `sugar`, so a recipe's
  `minGrade` already handles a cocktail that wants the good stuff.
- **Honey is already refused** — its material has no `sugar` tag. ⚠ The
  draft's criterion that the refusal **names why** (*"this wants sugar; honey
  is a different sweetener"*) is the one thing to verify in crafting's
  `insufficient-input` sentence.
- ⭐ The session's lesson, from the founder: *"I generally like to unify
  things because the agent so often diverges code to solve problems instead
  of finding where things converge."* A `parent:` category mechanism was
  proposed and withdrawn — **a material carrying several tags already IS
  role membership.** (The potash slate's `flux` role is the same: tags.)

## 6. ⭐⭐ Molasses — and the rum nobody made **[DECIDED]**

**Rum already exists, made from nothing.** The distilling pack sells dark and
light rum (Hollis Cane and Volk are Veshko's liquid), and Veshko's yard
**stock "makes it up to par"** — a producer stock line with no feedstock. The
mojito asks for rum and the realm pours it, but no cane was ever crushed for
it. **Molasses is the missing input to a product the realm already sells.**

- The draft's *"no rum"* becomes **"no new rum mechanism"**: molasses (with
  the skimmings) ferments on the shipped fermentation mechanism (a molasses
  wash profile), and the shipped still makes rum from it (fractionation, the
  cut as the skill).
- ⭐ **Veshko's rum line buys molasses** instead of conjuring rum — a small
  edit to a shipped producer stock that closes a demand root nobody noticed
  was unfed.
- **The grade carries** — first-boiling molasses lighter and sweeter,
  blackstrap dark and bitter; light rum from the one, dark from the other.
- **Treacle as food** (gingerbread, treacle tart — the baking trade ships) and
  **stock feed** (ranching) are rows; **dunder** (spent wash recycled into
  the next ferment) is a hook for distilling.

## 7. The lens pass (2026-10-18)

1. **Pedagogy** — inversion; boiling-point elevation (the thermometer reads
   concentration); the energy balance (efficiency against firewood); lime
   tempering; crystallization and the strike; why each boiling is darker.
2. **Expression** — the ordinary case is rows (a crop, a sugar house, cone
   moulds, a second island); personalization from supply-chain depth (your
   loaf's grade is your temper and your strike); jaggery, palm sugar and date
   syrup are tag rows for other realms.
3. **Immersion** — the curing house dripping, the boiling house's heat, the
   blue paper. **Participation** — the cane cutter, the miller, the boiler
   (the skilled seat), the curing-house hand, the grocer cutting by grade, the
   rum maker buying molasses, later the sea captain. ⭐ *Can the polity do
   something we didn't want?* — **strip an island's forest to boil sugar**,
   Madeira's story, derived from the energy balance.
4. **Values** — ⚠⚠ **the heaviest of any trade designed so far.** Sugar is
   the history of the Atlantic slave trade. The draft's non-goal (*no
   plantation, no plantation labour*) is a legitimate scope line, and lens 4
   says a trade with this history cannot pretend not to have it, so it is
   recorded plainly here: ⛔ **the engine never models a coerced worker as a
   mechanic**; if a polity ever builds coercion, the **accountability
   ledger** records it (the indentured-diver and Morecambe Bay precedents in
   [underwater-slate](../builds/underwater-slate.md)); and ⚠ **the island must not
   become a plantation by default content** — its authored people are
   smallholders, millers and boilers.
5. **Continuity** — edge-runner → rollers → steam mills; open kettles → the
   Jamaica train → vacuum pans; the thread test → the thermometer; claying →
   bone char → the centrifuge. The same acts throughout.
6. **Economy** — demand first: three cocktails and ⭐ **Veshko's rum** (the
   hidden root). Consumes cane, lime, firewood, cone moulds (pottery's first
   customer); produces loaves by grade, muscovado, molasses, skimmings and
   bagasse ash (for the ashery). ⭐ The island gives the sea its first cargo.
7. **Governance** — the island's land and who holds it; ⭐ **sugar duties** —
   among the most taxed goods in history, so a customs duty at Terminus's
   wharf is a real lever with a computable price effect; coercion, if it ever
   appears, on the ledger.

**Altitude** — *invariant*: sugar is a tag, not a mechanism; honey never
satisfies sugar. *Grain*: recovery fractions, the temper window, the strike
temperatures. *Title*: the Terminus sugar house, the island.

### Findings that are not sugar's

- **The kernel's light table calls ≥ 200 lux `blinding`** (an ordinary
  bright room), while `analyze light` calls it good working light; blinding
  withholds detail (worn clothes read "something"). The island's train floor,
  spill-lit from a tropical yard, reads glare all day.
- **A new player's 20-zorkmid Arrival Note cannot be spent:** after `bank
  open` the account is empty and `buy` says "You can't cover…", a refusal that
  never says why.
- **A shop counter lists its lines by template key** (`raw-sugar-block (14)`).
- **No NPC has a bank account** — the smallholders' `sell cane` refuses
  `no-account`; NPC wages go to arrears.
- **The idle economy** — a fresh house's float is less than a day's wages;
  W0 turns the arrears into walk-offs.
- **Simple syrup cannot be made by hand:** its slots want 0.25 L and `add`
  pours a fixed 0.06 L.
- **The mojito is unmakeable at the Lounge by design** (agent-coordination's
  standing vacancy), so the drive's step 10 asserts the supply under it.
