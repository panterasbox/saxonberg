## What shipped (cleanliness, !380)

Little ash only. A wood fire accrues `ashKg += kg burnt × ashFraction` in its
grate (`Burner`; oak 0.01, pine 0.005; coal and charcoal leave none).
`sweep <hearth>` without a flue brush rakes it out as
`/trade/fuel/thing/wood-ash`, **made from the hearth** — so it is the
hearth owner's, and raking a stranger's grate is the ordinary taking case. A
deep ash bed **chokes the draught** (`fire.ash.choke*`, as a share of the
burner's own capacity), so a grate nobody rakes burns worse and says so. Two
sinks compete for it by tag `potash`: the chandlery's lye hopper
(0.3 kg/L) and the glass batch's flux — the glass demand was there first.

## ⭐ The requirement (user, 2026-10-18)

**The potash design must accommodate BIG ash and LITTLE ash both.**

- **Little ash** — a hearth's grate: a by-product of a paid input, raked as
  a chore, owned by whoever owns the hearth. Shipped.
- **Big ash** — the ground after a wildfire or a burn: a deposit on the land
  that you **gather like excavation**, not raise like forestry. It lies on a
  place, is finite, and is spent by taking — the extraction/mining family
  (a `Deposit`-shaped read on ground that burnt, `dig`/`gather` the ground
  protocol), with the fire as its one source. Rain leaches it: big ash left
  out loses its potash, which is a clock the ground already runs.

One material, one tag, one set of sinks — the two differ only in WHERE the
ash is and HOW you get it (a grate you rake vs ground you work).

## Open, from the lens pass

1. **Quality by what burnt** (lens 1). Hardwood ash leaches far stronger lye
   than softwood; leached or rained-on ash is spent. Ash should carry its
   potash strength from the fuel, and the lye's strength follow from it —
   *why the soapmaker wants beech ash and not pine* is the lesson.
2. **The potash works** — boiling leach down to cake for sale or export; the
   ash-man who collects door to door (the participation hole little ash
   opens).
3. **Ash glazes** for a potter; **bucking** — lye run through linen packed in
   a tub, the old laundry.
4. **Continuity** (lens 5) — washing soda (Leblanc, then Solvay) replaced ash
   in the same sinks: soda should answer the hopper and the glass `flux` by
   tag, so the later epoch answers the same commands. Unverified.
