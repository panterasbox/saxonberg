## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Why silk is a third RGO shape

The taps doctrine sorts animal yields by **whether the act of taking
feeds back on the rate** ([taps.md](../../subsystems/taps.md) § the
feedback law): milk directly, eggs through a state the act prevents,
wool and honey not at all. **Silk is a fourth row: the take ends the
animal.** The filament is reelable only while the pupa is inside; when
the moth emerges it breaks the shell and the thread is cut into short
lengths. So the cocoon must be **stifled** — the pupa killed by heat —
within about ten days of spinning, or kept on purpose for seed.

That puts it nearer the carcass chain than the taps, but with a
population for a body, like the hive:

| | the land half | the animal half |
|---|---|---|
| what it is | **mulberry** — a tree cut for its leaf, on the shipped polycarp cycle (the hazel stool is the precedent: the "fruit" is what you cut) | **a rearing** — a tray of worms is ONE organism; the individual worm is below the resolution anything acts at (the bee precedent) |
| what it eats | ground, water, light — the husbandry model unchanged | ⭐ **leaf you carry to it.** Unlike the hive it does not forage: the reservoir is yours, and the labour is the feeding |
| the clock | a game year | ⭐ **warmth.** Five ages and four moults over roughly a month at ~25 °C, slower in a cold spring — degree-days off the realm's temperature, derived, never stored |

⭐ The realm's default site is **42°** (`DEFAULT_CLIMATE_SITE`), which is
about where sericulture historically thrived — Valencia, the Cévennes,
Lombardy. A cold spring slows the worms, so a serious rearing house
**burns fuel** (braziers), which is demand the fuel trade already wants
and a job the `stokes` brain already does.

## 2. The chain, and its verbs

```
mulberry (cut leaf) ─▶ REARING (feed · 5 ages · climb to spin) ─▶ cocoons
  └ fruit, wood          │ frass ─▶ soil                       │
                          └── keep for seed: moths ─▶ eggs ─▶ next year's lot
cocoons ─▶ stifle (heat: sun / oven) ─▶ REEL ─▶ raw silk ─▶ spin (twist) ─▶ weave ─▶ dye
                                          │ pupae ─▶ hen / fish feed
                                          └ waste, pierced, floss ─▶ spin ─▶ spun silk
raw silk ─▶ degum (a timed preparation: sericin off, ~20–30 % of the weight)
```

**One new verb: `reel`.** Its decision is **how many cocoons go to the
thread** — and keeping it even, because each filament thins from the
outside of the cocoon to the inside, so a good reeler adds a cocoon as
the thread falls. That count is silk's `yarnCount`; its real unit is the
**denier** (grams per 9,000 m), and a real unit is a falsifiable
precision claim, so the reading is banded by competence (#66).

**Everything else is borrowed** — a verb exists where a decision exists:

| step | rides | why no new verb |
|---|---|---|
| cut leaf | `harvest` (the coppice cut) | the stool precedent |
| feed | the employment/brain shape for hands; a short act for the hobbyist | the decision is the *supply*, not the motion |
| stifle | `heat` (an oven) or the sun | heat is the platform's |
| degum | a `MaturationProfile` | retting's shape — judge the moment |
| twist (throwing) | `spin` on a reeled skein (R3) | |
| spun silk | `spin` — the `fibre` tag already admits it | |
| weave, dye | shipped | dyeing reads `protein` already: silk takes alum directly, saturated colour with **no code change** |

⚠ **The textiles doc called silk "a second cellulose fibre: more
content, no new lesson."** Both halves were wrong — silk is a protein
fibre and the richest lesson in the vertical. Fixed with this slate in
[textiles.md](../../subsystems/textiles.md) and
[textiles-slate.md](./textiles-slate.md).

## 3. The rearing — what a player sees and decides

- **Ages are words.** *"In their fourth age, asleep for the moult."* The
  card shows the derived state (#28); no counter, no bar.
- **Hunger is soft.** Underfeeding is a limiting factor — the worms grow
  slower and spin lighter cocoons. ⛔ Never instant death: every limit
  soft, as with work and bodies.
- ⭐ **Show the dirt (#63).** A hungry tray is *"restless, heads raised"*;
  a fed fifth-age tray sounds like rain on leaves — the real, famous sound,
  and the trade's juice (#64).
- **Mounting.** In the fifth age they stop eating and wander; given
  brush or straw they climb and spin over about three days.
- **The deadline.** From the spin, about ten days to emergence. Stifle,
  or keep for seed — the decision the whole trade turns on.
- **Heft, not a number.** A cocoon's shell is judged by weight in the
  hand, the hive's precedent.
- ⚠ **Light the house.** A rearing house is an interior, and an unlit
  interior is pitch black — every tray would read "something." The build
  authors it lit.

**The hatch is a decision with no verb**: the eggs wait out the winter
(one-brood strains need the cold) and hatch when brought into warmth.
Hatch too early and there is no leaf; too late and the season is short.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ Disease — germ theory was discovered here

- **Muscardine** — Agostino Bassi showed in **1835** that it was caused
  by a fungus: the first microbial disease proven in an animal, decades
  before Pasteur. It spreads in the damp; the dead worms mummify white.
- **Pébrine** — the epidemic that ruined European sericulture in the
  1850s–60s. **Pasteur (1865–70)** found it passes through the EGG and
  saved the trade by putting the mother moths under a microscope and
  keeping only clean lines (cellular grainage).

**The model** (the uncertainty doctrine's: roll what the world IS, never
what your act DID): the egg lot carries a **seeded pébrine load from its
origin** — an epistemic fact, on the silent-second-population pattern
([spoilage.md](../../subsystems/spoilage.md) § `ContaminableMixin`). No
sense reports it. A heavily infected lot collapses late, in the fourth
or fifth age, after the leaf and the labour are spent.

**Detection has two epochs, and the first is the one that ships first:**

1. **Before optics** — by outcome and provenance. You buy eggs from a
   grainer whose lots hatched clean, or from far away where the disease
   has not reached. ⭐ This is Baricco's *Silk* exactly (§ 9).
2. **After optics** — the spores are ~3 µm; the shipped hand lens (10×)
   cannot see them, and that refusal is the lesson. A compound
   microscope can — [optics-slate](./optics-slate.md) Half A, made by the
   glass trade. That turns the grainer's reputation into a **certificate
   anyone can check** (§ 7).

So silk does not wait on optics; optics adds a rung to it. The
`ContagionSpec` machinery is [disease-slate](./disease-slate.md)'s; silk
needs only the per-lot seeded load and its read at the rearing, which
spoilage's seam already carries — if the build finds otherwise, that is
the disease slate's work, not silk's.

## 5. Selection — one line of genetics

Keep the heaviest cocoons for seed, and next year's lot moves toward
them: **R = h²S** — the response is the heritability times the selection
differential. It is the breeder's equation, it is derivable, and
Darwin opens *On the Origin of Species* with domestic breeding.

- **Strains are rows** an author makes: one brood a year or two
  (univoltine/bivoltine), cocoon colour (white, gold, green), shell
  weight, heritability.
- **A player's line drifts by what they keep.** That is the expression
  payoff — a line with a colour nobody else has — and it falls out of the
  chain rather than a customizer.
- ⚠ Kept small on purpose: one trait moves, by one formula. Expression is
  inelastic; a full genetics model is [lineage-slate](./lineage-slate.md)'s
  territory, not this one's.

## 6. ⭐⭐⭐ Labour, and what a luxury IS

The owner's observation, which this slate exists partly to record:

> Labour in this game is *running commands regardless of vocation*, so a
> demanding product is only somewhat more demanding — not like life,
> where the demand is really impacted and the market responds. Silk's
> time element is the honest reason it is scarce: the commitment makes
> players willing to do it rare. An NPC that does it all undermines that.

### The finding: a FREE hand breaks scarcity; a PAID one does not

**Money is the only scarce thing** (markets-slate's doctrine), and a
shift wage is real money settled out of the business's own account. So
a hired feeder converts the **time** cost into a **money** cost, at that
seat's wage, for exactly the hours the biology demands. That is also the
history: silk was not dear because rearing was rare — whole villages did
it — but because a kilo of raw silk carried a great many household hours
and somebody spent or paid for every one.

What WOULD break it: a hand paid from nowhere; hands without limit; or a
hand that makes the employer's decisions for them.

### The model — scale, not a mode switch (R5)

- **One tray is a hobby you can keep alone.** The schoolroom silkworm:
  feeding is a short act, a missed feed costs growth not life, and the
  little silk it gives is entirely yours. ⭐ Many players reared
  silkworms as children — one of the few subjects here that is an
  *experience someone had*, not a curriculum (#13, #17).
- **A rearing house is a business that needs hands.** Its labour splits
  three ways:

| | who | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **motion** — feeding, cutting and carrying leaf, the braziers | hired hands with a brain (the `stokes` shape) | many hours at a low wage |
| **judgment** — the hatch, inspection, stifle or keep, what to breed from, the reel | you, **or** a skilled seat | a skilled wage, and the result is THEIR competence |
| **capital** — trees (years to establish), eggs, the house, fuel | the proprietor | waiting and risk |

- ⭐⭐⭐ **Standing only by your own hand.** Seat doctrine lets an NPC
  hold any seat, so you may hire a master reeler — but the silk carries
  *their* mark, because money may buy goods and never standing. **NPCs
  can make silk; only you can make YOUR silk.** The rare commitment is
  what earns the name.
- **Being the proprietor is play in itself**, and it has a real shape:
  the **putting-out system** — the merchant supplies the eggs and buys
  the cocoons from rearing households.

### How luxury falls out, with no "luxury" flag

**Price ≈ the hours a good embodies × the wage of whoever holds them,
plus land and risk.** A luxury is a good whose hours are many and whose
skill is rare. The market responds through mechanisms that ship or are
designed: imports at a per-town price (the ceiling local silk competes
under — as Europe's did), seat wages a business sets (a scarce reeler
can be bid up), and dealers bidding from their own books.

⭐⭐ **The build ships a labour bench**, on the textiles
mill-throughput bench's precedent: it reads the shipped rows and prints
**hours per kilo for linen, wool and silk**, so the luxury gap is
measured rather than asserted and the price ladder is derived from it.
If the rows do not produce a large gap, the bench says so before any
player does. *Rough real-world magnitudes for calibration, to be
checked, not trusted:* on the order of 100–200 kg of leaf per kg of raw
silk; fresh cocoons to raw silk historically ~8–10 : 1; degumming loses
20–30 % more.

### ⛔ What is NOT silk's to solve

Two questions cut across every trade, and a trade build does not solve a
cross-cutting capability:

- **who wants luxury** — status demand (R9; and #90 Status, unwritten);
- **whether NPC hands are a finite labour pool** — if they are infinite
  and cheap, every hour-heavy good is cheap.

Both → [markets-slate](./markets-slate.md). Silk is the first trade whose
price *should* depend on them, and the bench is the instrument that will
show whether it does.

## 7. The institutions

- **The grainer** sells egg lots and stands behind them. Before optics
  their standing is their record; after, a certificate anyone can re-read.
- **The leaf market.** Cut leaf wilts within a day, so leaf is local and
  its May price swings hard — historically leaf was speculated on in
  Italy and France. A mulberry grower with no worms sells leaf; a
  rearer with no trees buys it. Perishability forces locality.
- ⭐⭐ **The conditioning house** (Lyon, *Condition des soies*, 1805).
  Silk takes up ~11 % of its weight in water, so sellers damped it; the
  public conditioning house weighed it dry and certified the trading
  weight. It is exactly lens 7's civic-instrument pattern — a measurement
  made common — and it carries the warning that **whoever runs the oven
  holds the standard**, which is political work in a neutral costume.
  The material row's `waterAbsorptionCapacity` is the field it reads.
- **The reeler, the throwster, the mercer** — each its own seat (#86:
  a tailor who also sells silk thread is a vacancy deleted).

## 8. Kermes — the second instance

The vertical's underlying shape is **insect husbandry on a host plant**,
and the RGO programme was designed without it. **Kermes** — a scale
insect farmed on kermes oak — is the source of the word *crimson* and
was the luxury red of silk and wool before cochineal. It is the
second-instance test: the same population-on-a-host shape with a **dye
row** out the end, needing **zero new code**. If kermes needs code, the
silk substrate was cut to one consumer.

Later rows of the same shape, not this build: cochineal, lac (shellac and
lac dye), wild silks (tussah, gathered off oak in a `Wood`). Hooks only:
spider silk (the mining slate's deep ecology), sea silk (byssus, pen
shells — fishing).

## 10. Lens pass

1. **Pedagogy** — Disciplines: husbandry, the pathology of the worms,
   the reel, selection. ⭐ **Dominant: observation** — the hatch, the
   moult, the deadline, the disease. What derives without a lookup: ages
   from warmth, the deadline from emergence, dye from protein, the line
   from R = h²S. Disease seeded, never rolled, or the dominant skill
   becomes luck.
2. **Expression** — strains as rows, a player's own line, the house, the
   mark, the cloth; the named works above pass on the substrate named.
   Kermes is the second-instance test.
3a. **Immersion** — ages as words, heft not a number, the rain sound; ⚠
   the house must be lit.
3b. **Participation** — grainer, leaf seller, rearing household, reeler,
   throwster, mercer, the conditioning house. The polity can corner the
   leaf, ban imported silk (Britain did, in the 1760s), strike the
   filature.
4. **Values** — kill the pupa or not (grain: the trade stifles); the
   reeling girls (the wage table, unflattened). Standing to the grainer
   whose lots hatch clean — the cocoons are the evidence.
5. **Continuity** — `reel` at a hand basin or a steam filature, `weave` on
   a drawloom or a Jacquard: same commands. Who held the obsolete half:
   the **drawboy** (Jacquard, 1804), **all of Europe's sericulture** (a
   disease, not a machine), **Japan's silk households** (nylon, 1939 —
   entering at the fibre-as-material seam). A trade worth building with
   its obsolescence dated, like ice.
6. **Economy** — produces raw and spun silk, pupae, frass, mulberry fruit
   and wood; consumes land, a month of labour, fuel, water, lye. ⚠ **The
   demand was mostly NOT there first** — silk's real demand is status
   (R9). The real but small demand: silkworm-gut fishing leaders (the
   leger rod's "heavy gut line"), sutures, silk bolting cloth for the
   mills, dyeing's premium cloth. Value per weight makes it the ideal
   long-haul cargo for logistics and maritime.
7. **Governance** — egg certification judges a business: criterion a
   reading anyone can repeat, appeal another moth under the glass.
   Conditioning: the standard-holder warning. ⚠ **Sumptuary law** (who
   may wear silk) should be possible as a polity law (Tier C), never as
   engine policy — whether civics can express a law about clothing is
   **unchecked**, a named-work gap.

## 12. Opens and risks

- **Calibration.** Every number above is a magnitude to check; the
  labour bench is the authority, and the cadence at the 12× clock (a game
  day is two real hours) must be felt in a drive, not computed.
- **The population host.** `ColonyMixin` is apiculture's pack `lib/`. A
  third pack wanting it without depending on its owner is the signal to
  promote it to the kernel — the plan decides, not this slate.
- **The deadline vs absence.** Ten game days is about twenty real hours.
  A player away for a day must not find a whole crop silently ruined —
  the deadline is visible coming (`look`), and a hired hand can stifle.
  Lens #27: the world agrees not to charge you for your absence.
- **Sumptuary law's expressibility** in civics — unchecked (§ 10.7).
