## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ Twist three times, alternating **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

- **Fiber is spun into yarn** (`spin` ships), **yarns twisted into a strand the
  opposite way**, **strands laid into rope**, twisting again.
- ⭐ **The alternating twist is why rope holds itself together** — each layer's twist
  balances the torque of the one inside it, so well-laid rope does not unravel.
- ⭐ **The ropewalk** — laying needs the rope's full length laid out, so **a rope cannot
  be longer than its walk**. Ropewalks were buildings a quarter-mile long (Chatham's
  Royal Navy ropery is about 346 m); the spinner walked backward down it, paying fiber
  out from around the waist. A place whose **length** is its capability — the
  [size slate](../builds/size-slate.md)'s real measures at their most literal.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Strength, derived **[DECIDED — lens 1]**

- **Breaking strength** = the fiber's material × the construction × the cross-section —
  so it grows with **the square of the diameter**.
- **What weakens it** — ⭐ **a knot** costs roughly **half**; **chafe** wears it at edges
  and over rock; **rot** attacks wet natural fiber
  ([decomposition](../builds/decomposition-slate.md)), which is why ships' rope was **tarred**
  (tar ships).
- ⭐ **A splice keeps about 90 %** — why sailors splice instead of knotting.
- **It breaks when load exceeds strength** — the [machines slate](../builds/machines-slate.md)'s
  overload. ⭐ **A shock load** — a fall caught on the rope — far exceeds the hanging
  weight; derived from the fall's energy and the rope's stretch, which is why climbing
  rope stretches (nylon's later advantage).
- ⭐ **The capstan effect** — rope wrapped round a post holds a load that grows
  **exponentially with each turn** against your pull (the capstan equation): why one
  belayer holds a falling climber and one deckhand holds a ship.

## 3. Rope is a length; knots are joints **[DECIDED]**

- **Sold and cut by length** (the fathom), coiled; a length is cut from the coil — the
  bole's cross-cut precedent ([forestry.md](../../subsystems/forestry.md)).
- ⭐ **A knot is a joint** — assembly's joint rows, loaded on first use, each with a
  strength factor, a reversibility and a failure mode. A closed vocabulary: the
  **bowline** (a loop that does not slip), the **clove hitch** (to a post), the **sheet
  bend** (joining two ropes), ⭐ the **reef knot, which spills under uneven load** — the
  classic mistake, an honest failure.
- **The acts** — `tie <rope> to <thing> [with bowline]`, `untie`, `splice`, `coil`,
  `cut`.
- **Competence, deterministically** — a well-dressed knot holds its rated share and a
  sloppy one slips sooner; no roll. Knot-craft is where the missing **seamanship**
  Discipline begins.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ The rope at an exit, and the belay **[DECIDED — lenses 1, 2 and 3b]**

**Rope changes how an exit can be traversed.** A rope anchored at the top of a drop
makes a climb passable for someone who could not otherwise make it — a rope down a
shaft, a handline across a ford, a rope ladder.

- **Whether it holds is derived** — its strength against the climber's weight and load
  ([encumbrance](../../subsystems/encumbrance.md)), the anchor's knot, and **chafe at
  the lip**, which wears it with every use and frays visibly *before* it fails.
- ⭐ **The belay** — a partner holding the rope, with turns round a post or their own
  body (the capstan effect), catches a fall. Cooperative, non-combat play — your life in
  your partner's hands — the [sport slate](../tails/tourism-and-sport-slate.md)'s named
  gap, filled by something practical.
- **Why the lenses chose it** — lens 1: loads, shock, the capstan and the belay are the
  richest physics rope teaches, and all happen at an exit. Lens 2: an author draws a
  drop and the rope does the rest, with no code. Lens 3b: the belay as cooperation,
  which nothing else in the game has.
- It rides the shipped climb ([exertion](../../subsystems/exertion.md),
  [locomotion](../../subsystems/locomotion.md)) and exits' fixtures
  ([boundary.md](../../subsystems/boundary.md)) — a rope is an exit fixture you carry.

## 6. The material ladder **[DECIDED — lens 5]**

Plant fiber (flax ✅, hemp, jute, manila and sisal — tropical gaps, coir) → animal
(horsehair, rawhide ✅) → **wire rope** (the
[cannery](../builds/cannery-and-bottling-slate.md)'s metal-forming; invented in the Harz mines in
the 1830s for winding shafts) → **nylon** (the [plastics slate](../builds/plastics-slate.md)'s oil
rung). `tie`, `splice`, `coil` and `cut` at every rung. ⭐ The limits move: wire rope
makes deep shafts and suspension bridges possible; nylon's stretch tames the shock load.

## 7. End of life, values, governance **[DECIDED]**

- ⭐ **Oakum** — old rope picked apart into fiber, tarred, driven into ships' seams as
  caulking; **"picking oakum" was workhouse and prison labor** — the rope's own loop
  closed, and real work for the prison slate.
- ⭐ **The rogue's yarn** — the Royal Navy laid a colored yarn into every rope it owned
  so a stolen length could be identified: provenance woven in, beside the hallmark and
  the mason's mark.
- **Values** — binding a person (consent); the hangman's rope (a polity's choice);
  ⭐ **hemp and cannabis are one plant** (fiber and drug varieties — the 1937 US Marihuana
  Tax Act swept up industrial hemp; the vice cluster inherits it); children on the walk;
  oakum as forced labor.
- **Governance** — the rogue's yarn as evidence of theft; hemp licensing or prohibition;
  **rated strength for mine winding ropes** with a certified test (the three-layer
  measurement pattern); who answers for a failed rope — the maker, or the rigger who
  chafed it (the [accountability](../../subsystems/accountability.md) ledger); the cut
  rope as sabotage.

## 8. Placement **[DECIDED]**

**`trade-cordage`** — the ropewalk, its own industry: it spins its own yarn on the walk,
then lays strands and rope. **The venue: a ropewalk at Wharfside** — real ropewalks lined
