## 1. Diegetic government

*Source: the diegetic-government capture (2026-07-30), shipped as
[civics.md](../../subsystems/civics.md). Its jargon standard (markedness,
"the Compact", committee, member vs resident) lives in
[governance/glossary.md](../../compact/governance/glossary.md). Kept here: the
rationale civics.md cites but does not hold, and the two sections other
slates build on.*

### 1.1 Two layers, joined only by property

- **The Compact** governs the *platform* (players, title, conservation,
  code trust, conduct) and is singular. **Diegetic governments** are
  content, plural at every tier including the realm.
- ⛔ **Rejected premise, so nobody re-derives it:** *"the city government is
  the Compact's diegetic face."* Governments are mintable, so no
  singleton-projection model survives. A realm shares the Compact's thesis
  (accountable office), never its identity.
- **Property is the only bridge.** A city committee's real authority is a
  **landowner's** (title plus authorship,
  [parcel.md](../../subsystems/parcel.md)). ⭐ **The Disneyland model**: Main
  Street reads as public and is private all the way down. "Public" is a
  claim made inside the fiction; the property system recognizes only an
  owner, who says what the land *is* in the story.
- **Two codes of law.** Real law binds *players*; city law binds
  *characters* in its jurisdiction. "No killing" in both is coincidence of
  content, not shared authority. **No supremacy clause is needed**: city law
  is enforceable only through the powers in §1.3, all governed by real law.
  A statute beyond them can be declared but not enforced, and that theater
  is content too. Proceedings are independent.
- Plurality is a feature: travel texture, jurisdiction shopping, refuge for
  outlaws.

### 1.2 Share the organs, not a superclass; keep two staffs

Governments, guilds and corpos are **three sibling Ideas over common
substrates** (Businesses, accounts, a charter Document, positions). Only
the claim differs: a Locality subtree, a practice, a mark. ⛔ No
"Institution" super-Idea; that is template inheritance by another name.
**The meta staff** (parcel owners, code-trusted wizards) and **the diegetic
staff** (seat-holders, players or NPCs) ride different axes and never merge.

### 1.3 The six enforcement powers

A diegetic government can, exhaustively: (1) **move NPCs** (constables,
bounded by the consent substrate) · (2) **exercise property rights**
(banishment is revoked access; denial of service on its land) · (3) **move
money it owns** through the banking chokepoints, never minting or seizing
outside consent or escrow · (4) **keep records** (convictions, licenses,
marriages as Documents and chronicle *claims*) · (5) **touch reputation**
(renown is per-scope) · (6) **petition upward** to the Compact about a
*player*, the one channel between layers.

⭐ **The integrity rule**: diegetic justice moves money, chattel and title
only through the real chokepoints. The fiction narrates freely and never
mints, burns or flips title. There is no statute engine and no court
substrate.

### 1.4 The shelter: domicile and the floor under eviction

*Cited by [tenancy-design-pack](tenancy-design-pack.md).*

Residency follows real municipal **domicile**: where you habitually live,
with intent to remain. Nothing confers it. Two real-law details carry it:

1. **Domicile persists until replaced.** Leaving the dorm without new
   property is not statelessness; it is one tombstone field on the
   residence record, not a system.
2. **Homeless people are still residents.** Courts have upheld voter
   registration from a shelter address or a habitual park bench.
   **Homelessness is no *dwelling*, not no *civic identity*.**

⭐ **The shelter is content, not mechanism**: a city institution (a welfare
department Business or, better, a charity with its own charter) that gives
a homeless character a bed and *an address their records can point at*.
**It evidences residency; it never grants anything.** Scope: a room, a
Business, an NPC. Because it exists, eviction has a destination: a setback,
not a deletion.

### 1.5 Marriage, and the two prisons

*Cited by [household-design-pack](household-design-pack.md) Part 6.*

- **Marriage is a city institution, full stop.** The Compact has no
  marriage law; it is not its layer. Ceremony is content. The record is a
  city-registry entry (a Document plus a chronicle claim). The effects are
  **whatever the couple opts into through real substrates** (joint account,
  shared residence, joint title), **each on its own consent terms**. Another
  city may not recognize it; that is story material, not a bug.
- **The prisons are two institutions.** A city gaol is content for
  characters: short, with exits designed for fun. If the real system
  sentences a player to a creator's prison island, that is *the Compact
  contracting with content as a venue*, not the city's gaol
  ([prison-slate](../builds/prison-slate.md)).

### 1.6 Still open

- **Primary home** across several cities (real doctrine: many residences,
  one domicile). Player-designated?
- **Magistrate vs real court**: one judges characters, the other players;
  outlawry is the boundary case.
- **The Circulation Reserve lever** sits on a meta office (the Central-Bank
  Governor) though it steers a fiction economy. Long term it moves to a city
  seat (a Treasurer); the meta keeps the mint.
- **The Compact's capital district** is now designed as
  [Saxonberg City](../builds/saxonberg-city-slate.md): a venue for the real
  thing, never a fiction claiming to be it; no Government key.

## 2. Banking rationale that did not graduate

*Sources: the attendant and Terminus-banking designs (2026-07-15, shipped
07-16). The attendant design graduated **whole** into
[attendant.md](../../subsystems/attendant.md); nothing of it is kept. The
banking mechanics (till, quota, Terms, royalty, coinage and mass) are in
[banking.md](../../subsystems/banking.md). Kept: the reasoning.*

### 2.1 The business-landscape model

*Cited by [retail-slate](../builds/retail-slate.md).*

- **Everything is a business.** The only distinction is a property of the
  *money*: **Atmosphere** (notional neighborhood flow that can never reach
  the ledger) vs **Ledger** (real, conserved, player-touched).
- **The tier is fixed at birth; no graduation.** An Atmosphere business is
  complete in notional money, **a destination and not a stage**.
- **What is real is derived**: enumerate the player money-loops (**earn ·
  spend · bank · rent**); **their touchdowns are the whole real-business
  landscape.** Hand-author those few; the rest cost a few numbers per
  neighborhood.
- **Minimize the set; complete every member.** A bank with no lending is
  complete because lending exists nowhere yet; it lands everywhere at once,
  never as a per-venue patch (why the Dave's Bar tab was removed).

### 2.2 One archetype, many parameters

A repeated institution is one system with each operator as parameters on
**Terms** (*price*) and **Relationship** (*trust*). Each prices in a
**different currency** (the bind, usage, belonging, data, risk) and sets
*the door* (open, status-gated, value-gated) and *the knowing* (loyalty,
none, who you are, data, computed worth). **Layout is characterization**:
a real venue is a *walk* (doors, hall, hush, a glimpse of the vault), never
a kiosk designed up from its fixture; a room becomes enterable only when a
mechanic needs it.

### 2.3 Refusal and success are different kinds of object

⭐ **Success writes records onto you that outlive the visit; refusal is a
closed door that leaves no trace.** Taken in, you walk out with an
account, the institution's memory of you and an affiliation, true
everywhere. Turned away, nothing followed you out. A refusal must be **in
character, never a system message** (the courtesy stings) · **legible**
(you learn its currency) · **a goal, not a dead end** · **meant to sting**.

### 2.4 Fees, and what waits

- **The custodial loop is free everywhere**; fees live only on movement and
  convenience. Only one bank leans on the per-transaction fee, as a
  learnable "free checking that isn't" trap.
- **Maintenance fees** would shrink idle balances and break Law 2; build one
  complete, as a **reconcile-on-read accrual**, with the first bank that
  charges it. **Graded regard** arrives with the first bank that grades
  trust. Standing rides **regard, not renown** (private view, not fame).
- **The till is the third anti-grief guard** beside the lease and the quota:
  vault coin moves only by the banking verbs, or "loot the drawer" is free
  money.
- **Open:** a refusal *frontage* (a closed door and an in-character line at
  an Atmosphere rival) as complete set-dressing, never a stub of a bank.

## 3. Town patterns

*Sources: the Rejection, Heart's Delight and Hinkley Hills bibles
(2026-09-02/03). The towns are gone with the clean slate; these patterns
are reusable.*

### 3.1 Ground denial: a hazard that needs no stats

*Example: Rejection, Act I (Tremors).*

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The rule is: you cannot be on soft ground.** The creature needs no
> stats. Everything else falls out of that one line.

`HazardMixin` is most of a burrower already (triggered by traverse,
delivered through the weapon grammar, spotted through concealment); what
is missing is that it moves and learns. What falls out: **the map acquires
texture** (rock safe, spoil not) · ⭐ **haulage becomes the game** (the work
goes on, but the yard between pit and smelter is lethal: freight as a
survival puzzle; the climax is *get to ground it cannot follow you into*,
never a boss fight) · **the safe place inverts** (the feared mine is where
you run) · **the collective decision** about ground nobody owns. ⭐ A
habitat the industry made, on the one ground with no owner, states the
institutional problem as geography. A *fought* big creature needs a
**scale layer** the game lacks (multi-cell occupancy, submergence).

- **A creed that hides the threat**: a culture with a category for ordinary
  death files the first victims correctly, honestly and wrongly. Author
  them as accidents, correctly diagnosed, in a readable case book.
- **Standing vs instruments**: those with standing lack instruments; the
  outsider with instruments (shipped `measure`/`analyze`) lacks standing.
  The deduction closes only with both.

### 3.2 Recognition: a charter worth what someone else says

*Example: Rejection, Act II (Deadwood). Cited by towns-slate D14.*

- **The pressure to charter is external**: the need to be *legible* to a
  larger power whose books the town's titles and money already sit in.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **A charter is a petition, not a victory.** A town builds **de
  facto** authority; **de jure** is somebody else's to grant. *An
  unrecognized sheriff can arrest a man in town and cannot follow him out.*
  Nothing there is cognizable anywhere that matters, which is why people
  get *sent* there.
- **Recognition is an act by a player-holdable office.** A player
  petitions, a player decides, a corpo lobbies the seat. ⚠ Design against
  both degenerate ends: always-decline is a treadmill, always-grant has no
  tension.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The knife**: recognition is granted because a buyer needs clean
  title. You cannot purchase what nobody owns. The town wins recognition,
  and that is how it loses itself.
- ⭐ **Never said in-world**: a polity whose legitimacy is at another's
  discretion is the game showing *why the Compact exists*.

### 3.3 Private force, leverage, and who the charter covers

*Example: Rejection, Act III (Matewan).*

1. **Private force has capacity and no authority**; authority comes from an
   office.
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **The frightening one is the deputized one**: private force borrowing
   a public office, which the alignment model calls **capture**.
3. ⭐⭐ **No office to capture is worse**: hired men break no law where there
   is none, and nobody has standing to refuse. *Private force is why you
   need a sheriff*, and a buyer prefers the town unchartered.

⭐ **A charter's first real test is whether it covers people with no
employer.** An unrecognized polity's only weapon is withholding work;
casual laborers have nothing to withhold, cannot afford a wage check-off,
and fall outside the mutual-aid fund. Historically charters did not cover
them.

Smaller patterns from the same town:
- **Whoever holds the debt governs until there is a government**: three
  books (store, lodging, fund) never compared, and a publican holding every
  tab who settles things in his own room.
- **The press detects ledger crime**: a buyer's move is legal on its face
  ([press-slate](../builds/press-slate.md),
  [policing-slate](../builds/policing-slate.md)).
- **A miners' institute** on a wage check-off is school, insurance and
  assembly in one room, and shows **who mutual aid excludes**.
- **Shift cores with one exception**: a job that cannot be scheduled (a
  clamp that burns for days) proves the roster model. Give it no core.
- **Hue and cry vs policing by consent**: two theories of law a road apart.
- **A bed by the week** is the tenancy rung below a lease.
- **A mixed population is characterization, never remarked on**; refuse the
  dwarf-mining-town trope (the species doctrine's essentialism).

### 3.4 How towns form: the founding sequence

*Example: Heart's Delight.*

Seven steps, each a place you can walk to: **the water** → **the land**
(held first, on a grant) → ⭐ **the rail** (a landholder gives the
right-of-way so the stop lands on *his* ground: a location that is a gift
with a motive) → **the shipping point** → **the store** → ⭐⭐⭐ **the name**
(they apply for a post office and are told the name is taken) → **the
map**. Walking depot → store → packing house walks the formation in order.

- ⭐⭐⭐ **The engine already enforces the post office's rule**:
  `AddressRegistry` allows one Locality per prefix. The founding joke is a
  hard invariant.
- **History recovered by a player.** A forgotten founding (the founder went
  back over a pass in winter for strangers, *with no mechanism at all*: the
  collective-action problem solved once by nobody in particular) surfaces
  as documents, then is **published to the wiki by whoever found it**, with
  provenance. After that the town knows. ⭐ The descendant who *is* the
  evidence works the seasonal line, unaware.
- **The site, not the building.** An industry the economy cannot yet justify
  (an ironworks waiting for a pump) is authored as an empty yard with rail
  frontage; the works arrives **in response to an event**, not as furniture.
- **A landmark from two shipped systems**: a `StorageNode` water tower
  wearing a buyer's `BrandedMixin` mark.
- ⚠ **Pick sources that need no villain.** Chinatown is the obvious
  water-rights reference and the wrong one; the true story is worse because
  nobody does anything wrong.

### 3.5 The seasonal economy

*Example: Heart's Delight. Cited by
[irrigation-slate](../builds/irrigation-slate.md).*

⭐ **The calendar is the town**: an agricultural place is four places a
year. **Bloom** (~3 weeks: a postcard, visitors, a non-agricultural demand
event) · **Thinning & the water** (*the anxious season. Irrigation begins;
the head gate matters; a dry year is decided now*) · **Drying** (rain is a
disaster: the first place weather costs real money) · **The pack** (~6
weeks: a factory, two hundred hands) · **Off-season** (~5 months, empty).

- ⭐ **Seasonal employment**: a roster that hires 140 for six weeks and
  nobody for forty-six generates glut, migrant labor, off-season debt and
  the case for a co-operative, with no mechanic beyond a seasonal roster.
  The **timekeeper's window** is where seasonal hours become money.
- **One annual settlement**: money arrives in a lump after the pack and
  everything else is credit against it, so a bad season is a **solvency
  event**.
- **Two class-marked entry points**: capital buys a grower's long loop;
  nothing buys the pack's short one. They rarely become each other. Farm
  labor is migrant and was historically written out of labor law, a
  different problem from mining's.
- **Perishable, high-value, short window means preserve or be close**; the
  cannery sits in the valley.

### 3.6 Water doctrine, and the institution for the wrong commons

*Example: Heart's Delight.*

- **Prior appropriation**: in a dry year the senior right takes all and the
  junior *nothing*. ⚠ Never villainy: a decent person irrigates while a
  decent person watches his trees die.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The doctrine on the river pushes late arrivals to the aquifer, and
  the aquifer has no doctrine at all.** Each junior drills rationally and the
  ground goes down. The readout is a **technician with a dataset and no
  authority** (years of monthly well readings), evidence rather than
  narration. Subsidence is slow, visible, **player-caused**, never a timer.
- ⭐⭐ **Nobody takes the valley; the valley sells**, one owner at a time,
  each correct, the aggregate paving paradise: the subsidence's shape in
  land.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **A marketing co-op is private ordering that works, in the wrong
  domain.** It solves market power and has no instrument for the physical
  commons. **Institutions are shaped by their founders' problem and do not
  adapt.** The pool price must be genuinely arguable (average vs variance).
- **A place downstream of a polity that does not govern it and upstream of
  one that does not answer to it** is where a river authority is felt. (The
  fouling inconsistency [logistics-slate](../builds/logistics-slate.md)
  flags dies with the content; the standing-to-sue question does not.)
- **Who arbitrates a water call?** Nobody, until the first dry year.

### 3.7 The civic triptych

*All three towns. Cited by towns-slate.*

| pattern | failure |
|---|---|
| no institution (Rejection) | needs public authority and cannot get it |
| a special district (Hinkley Hills) | has public authority and will not use it |
| a co-operative (Heart's Delight) | private ordering that works, in the wrong domain |

⭐⭐ **Each holds the institution the next one needs**, and none of them
talk: institutions are local and problems are not. A founding that solved
collective action once, with no institution, keeps the set from being three
flavors of failure.

### 3.8 A place whose content is other people

*Example: Hinkley Hills (The 'Burbs, Blue Velvet, Scissorhands,
Pleasantville).*

- ⭐⭐ **Idle attention**: not enough to do, too much to look at. A commuter
  suburb visibly empties at 06:00 and refills at 18:00. ⭐⭐⭐ **Its play
  surface is the social graph.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Facades**: owned ground where *the street shows nothing* is not a
  suburb. Minimum: an owner-authored exterior and sign per lot, rendered on
  the lane at its gate. ⚠ **People judge; the system never does**: no
  penalty, gauge or score, only talk.
- ⭐⭐ **The street sees condition and infers the person.** Stewardship is a
  Discipline (a *person*); condition is a fact about a *property*. A
  neglected front over a tended back is **a true measurement carrying a
  false conclusion**, how prejudice works
  ([measurement.md](../../measurement.md)).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Permanent ambiguity in an open-source repo**: hide a body in YAML and
  it is found in an afternoon. So *you can resolve the ground, never the
  man.* Nothing is hidden, so nothing spoils, and he never explains himself.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **The record of your pursuit is the content**: belief, regard and
  `accountability_events` already keep the file on what you were willing to
  do. *The mirror town.*
- **The escalation ladder is shipped subsystems**: notice → theorize
  (belief) → share (social graph) → judge (renown; in a nine-person scope
  every opinion is a measurable fraction) → surveil (concealment) →
  trespass (title, no lock involved) → dig. No quest chain.
- ⭐⭐ **The decent path is free and socially expensive**: just knock. Doing
  it publicly marks you. The crowd that adores and the one that hunts are the
  same crowd.
- ⭐ **The weird house is a condition a player can enter.** For every one
  after the first: **it always has something, and it is never a crime**
  (grief, an unzoned business, hidden poverty), and finding it is always an
  intrusion. ⚠ Never teased as dangerous; rewarding paranoia teaches the
  opposite.
- **Gaps it exposed**: identical lots (one variation axis gives land value
  something to be a function of); nothing above a quarter-acre; whether a
  subdivision fills should be a player outcome.

### 3.9 The District razor

*Example: Hinkley Hills. Cited by towns-slate.*

> **Zoning governs use, never self-expression.** Governance is reserved for
> the commons and shared rules, **never your couch.**

That forbids the HOA. ⭐⭐ **The razor: a district can act on what you DO,
never on what you look like.** A grievance with no legitimate channel goes
looking for a pretext: *you cannot get a man for being strange, so you get
him for the thing his fence is four inches over* (selling cuttings from a
residential lot). Regulatory capture at nine-house scale, the same theme as
§3.3.

### 3.10 The open-door policy

*Example: Hinkley Hills. Cited by towns-slate D20–D22.*

- ⭐⭐⭐ **No Bethesda ambiguity**: `ownerOf` is total (`stamp ??
  parcel-extent ?? authorOf`), so everything in a home has an owner.
  **Taking is easy; disposal is the hard problem** (the fence launders
  provenance). *The anti-exploit is the ledger, not the lock.*
- **The door is not the mechanism; witness is.** A lock is **a delay and a
  signal**. ⭐ **An unlocked door is not permission**: locks and title are
  separate systems, which is most of trespass law.
- **`knock`** (unbuilt). ⭐ **Regard is the INPUT to the door, never the
  output**: knocking spends standing, never earns it.
- ⭐ **Locks are locality characterization**: you learn a town's theory of
  trust by trying a handle.
- **NPC homes hold almost nothing**; **burglary on a knowable schedule is
  fine, model it.**
- ⚠⚠ **UNSOLVED: an NPC house as free storage.** Needs a rule before the
  open door ships.

## 4. The wishbook

*Source: the wishbook (study.com audience; cited by
[demo-slate](../builds/demo-slate.md) and, vignette 2,
[power-utility-slate](../builds/power-utility-slate.md)). Every scene is
composed from named systems, almost all shipped. Places are illustrative.*

> **The one wish:** *be who you're becoming, now, visibly, among others, in
> a world where your competence is real power.* The vocation transfigured,
> not escaped.

### 4.1 The texture of year three

The ledgers make age visible: chains of title a dozen owners deep,
chronicles that read like sagas, offices on their third elected holder,
maker's marks that are reputations, scripts handed down cohorts, argument
maps cited like case law, renown that is local, a skyline of player-titled
parcels.

### 4.2 The ten vignettes

1. **The night the gallery flooded** (nursing). Six come up hurt from a
   flooded mine; she triages by physiology (blood volume, SpO₂, the quiet
   one on CO), loses one, and the chronicle records it. **The calm one in
   the crisis, before any license says she may.**
2. **The storm contract** (electrician). Re-energize a flooded substation
   cell before the next front, under an honestly hedged forecast: `analyze
   electrical`, drain and dry against latent heat, rubber over the quick
   metal splice, re-energize from outside the circuit. A mistake is a
   sustained shock, so it pays renown and the transcript credits a deed.
   **Reading the circuit right under pressure.**
3. **The Tuesday seminar** (teacher). Awareness taught by supervised doing
   with a scripted lesson plan; students' deeds land on their transcripts,
   and command credits her only for what she did for others. **Students who
   visibly grow.**
4. **The audit** (accounting). Append-only ledgers walked against roster and
   terms find a mis-keyed fee split, not theft; *suspicion is answerable*.
   **Reading the books and finding the truth.**
5. **The block on Foundry Row** (real estate). A bargain lot's chain of title
   reveals a lease in a deed's clothes; she brokers an honest sale and posts
   the reading. **Diligence is the deal.**
6. **Holding the ford** (ASVAB). Rations planned as logistics, a formation,
   one call at the right moment; command measures the leadership. **The
   captain whose line holds.**
7. **The compiler of Duncan Hall** (CS). A shop brain three storefronts run,
   a composable item-effect bundle; labor standing pays like royalties.
   *Needs the three extensibility bridges.* **Code that runs somewhere real.**
8. **The cartographers of the Third Node** (science). Controlled trials on
   an unmapped magic node, argued on the forum until the map converges,
   minted category-first, *their names first*. *Needs inquiry.*
   **Discovery with priority.**
9. **The regulars' table** (TOEFL). Counter shifts as service English under
   mild pressure; months in, he banters unrehearsed. **Fluency as ease.**
10. **The long transcript** (every student). Guest to dorm key to year
    three; her real degree and the in-world conferral land the same week and
    agree. **The becoming, made durable.**

### 4.3 Daily loops, and the magic grid

**Every loop is practice wearing a livelihood**: clinical rounds, office
hours, a trades maintenance round (vignette 2 is it at ten times the
stakes), an escort rotation, a listings hour, the counter shift. The grind
is not beside the game; it is the students' studying, situated.

The magic grid is where advancement is ours to invent: **schools rhyme with
vocations**, **edges feel like curriculum**, **frontier nodes are the
research endgame**, magic is **the spectacle budget**, and ⭐ **magic is the
author's fine-grained reward instrument**. Real disciplines cannot grant
advancement, so an author rewards a player with an attunement, a rune-keyed
door, an enchantment on a material's response profile or a geas:
engine-legitimate and author-placeable. *Disciplines answer "what am I
becoming"; magic answers "what did tonight give me".*

### 4.4 Gaps it exposes, ranked by wish

1. The three extensibility bridges (item-effect envelope, vetted brain
   catalog, scripted-behavior brain).
2. The inquiry substrate ([inquiry-slate](../builds/inquiry-slate.md)).
3. Contracts and the gig board (shipped since,
   [contract.md](../../subsystems/contract.md)).
4. Courts: vignettes 4 and 5's unhappy versions need adjudication.
5. Threshold ceremonies with Katie-grade landings.

Each vignette is video-shaped (one moment, one wish, nameable systems).

## 6. Guilds: the raw brainstorm

*Source: the guild brainstorm (2026-07-28), since absorbed by
[guild-slate](../builds/guild-slate.md) (charter, ranks, exams, halls,
lifecycle). Kept: the principles and the organ list.*

### 6.1 The principle and the thesis

> **Owner (2026-07-28): guilds are designed as pure game design, explicitly
> NOT for study.com or gamification priorities.** Where they pull apart,
> game design wins, every time.

> A guild turns a *measured* competence into a *recognized* vocation. It
> cannot make you good, so it sells everything else: rank, legitimacy,
> knowledge, tools, work, fellowship and a name.

⭐ **Band vs rank**: a band is derive-on-read and nobody's to give; rank is a
social act, gated on bands and deeds but not reducible to them. The license
is not the skill. If rank could be bought it would mean nothing.

### 6.2 The organs (all shipped)

`Business` (legal person, payroll) · a Group provider (membership) · parcel
title plus a warren (the hall) · the guild's own positions, not the Office
substrate (rank and seats) · the credential substrate (**guilds are
issuers**) · recipes, scripts, the demonstration ladder (knowledge) · the
command discipline and labor standing (teaching) · employment and the
contract board (the guild as labor broker) · the maker's mark plus a
**hallmark** countersign · a durable-goods lending library (tools) · banking
(dues) · a guild board and the argument organizer (standards debates) ·
accountability rows (arbitration before courts) · a presence affix and the
chronicle portfolio (identity).

### 6.3 Ideas and tensions

- **Promotion by portfolio, not exam**: the masterpiece generalized; the
  world already mints every receipt.
- ⭐ **Guilds gate authorization, never ability.** The unlicensed-competent
  outsider is a legitimate role; that path stays playable (fines and
  reputation, not walls).
- **Rank has teeth socially, never mechanically**, and **consumes bands and
  deeds, never produces them**: a guild that grants transcript evidence
  breaches the honesty firewall.
- **Labor vs capital is built in**: a corpo trying to buy a hallmark it
  cannot earn.
- **The guild as newbie funnel**: a porter per hall is the first person who
  takes your ambition seriously; a guild is *who assigns* the daily loops.
- **The discovery interval**: canon is never proprietary; only a new finding
  has an honest window between discovery and publication. A hermetic order
  stretches it, an academy collapses it for renown.
- **Dues are chosen** (consent and exit; standing fades honestly). **Assume
  hostile founders eventually.**
