# Rope {#rope}

There is one rope. What it is for is where its two ends are tied: in a
tender's hands with a diver on the other end it is a lifeline; thrown to a
swimmer it is a heaving line; on a boat's bow it is a painter; tied round a
post at the edge of a pit it is the way down. A diver's air hose is a line
too, but it is not a rope.

## What a rope holds {#what-a-rope-holds}

A rope tells you in words what it would hold — a child, a man, a man and
his load, a horse, an ox and more. A rope twice as thick holds four times
as much, and hemp holds more than flax, and flax more than jute. Every knot
keeps only part of the rope's strength: a bowline more than a reef knot,
and a splice almost all of it. How well a knot is drawn up depends on the
hands that tied it. Chafe at an edge and rot from lying wet take away what
is left. `analyze lay rope` reads a rope closely.

## Tying it {#tying-it}

`tie rope to post` ties it round something fixed, and it stays there. Tied
off beside a drop, the rest goes over the edge: the rope is the way down,
and its end at the bottom is the way back up. `tie rope to <someone>` puts
them on the end of it, and `belay <someone>` holds it for them while they
climb — a fall they take is caught, if you can hold the jerk. Three turns
round a post hold far more than your hands alone. Name a knot with `with`:
`tie rope to post with clove hitch`. A reef knot spills when one end is
loaded harder than the other.

## Making rope {#making-rope}

Rope is laid at a ropewalk, a walk as long as the longest rope it can make.
At the jack, `twist yarn` walks yarns out into one strand; `twist strands`
lays three or more strands up the other way into a rope. More yarns make it
thicker. It is real work — a long rope takes hours — and it goes twice as
fast with someone at the jack's wheel (`turn jack`). How evenly the strands
harden is the craft: a green hand's rope has hockles and holds less, every
time. A rope is as good as its worst yarn.

`twist yarn --mark red` lays a colored yarn in the heart of a strand. It
does not show on the outside — only where the rope is cut, or to a close
look — and it says whose rope it is wherever it turns up.

## Keeping it {#keeping-it}

`coil` a rope before you throw it. `cut rope at 10 m` cuts a length off
with a knife. `splice rope to second rope` lays two into one. A rope dipped
in a tub of pitch is tarred, and a tarred rope does not rot.

See `help tie`, `help twist`, `help belay` for the commands.
