# Paper {#paper}

Everything you read on a screen is current, and it is read the moment it
reaches you. Paper is different in four ways, and they are the reasons to
bother with it:

- **It is a thing.** A letter lies where it was left, belongs to somebody,
  and can be carried off, lost, burnt or torn. Whoever holds it can read it.
- **It is frozen.** A printed sheet says what the document said on the day
  it was printed. Change the document afterwards and the sheet still says
  the old thing.
- **It shows how it was made.** Handwriting is in somebody's hand. A
  printed page carries its printer's mistakes.
- **It goes where the network does not.** It travels as fast as whoever is
  carrying it.

## Writing {#writing}

Writing on paper takes ink: a pot of ink or a pen in your hands. It also
takes sized paper. Unsized paper drinks the ink, and the line blots. Shop
paper is sized already.
`write meet me at noon on sheet` writes on a sheet you are holding.

Whatever you write stays in your hand, and a reader sees it as they know
you: their own hand, a hand they know by name, or a hand they do not
know. Two sheets with the same words in different hands are two different
papers.

`fold` a sheet to make it smaller. It folds three times, to octavo. Put a
letter in an envelope, close it and `seal` it. Once a seal is broken it
stays broken.

## Books {#books}

A book is its pages. `read notebook page 3` opens it at page 3, and
`write … on notebook page 3` writes on that page. Anyone holding a book
can write in it, and every passage keeps the hand that wrote it.

A printed book, like the city directory on the Gazette's counter, reads
its printed pages to whoever opens it.

`tear notebook page 3` tears that page out whole and leaves a stub at the
spine. The torn page goes into your hand and carries its words, or its
printed page, away with it. It still belongs to whoever owned the book.

## When paper fails {#when-paper-fails}

Soaked through, paper turns to pulp. Fire leaves only ash. Ink that gets
wet runs, and drying the sheet does not bring the words back. Tear a loose
sheet and each half keeps its share of the writing.

## Making paper {#making-paper}

Paper is made from rags at a mill. The mill buys clean rags at its
counter, and a worn garment tears into rags.

1. **Beat.** The stamper, driven by the millrace, beats the rags into
   pulp in the vat.
2. **Form.** `dip mould in vat` lifts a wet sheet on the mould's wire. How
   even it comes out depends on the vatman's hand. Its color comes from the
   water in the vat.
3. **Couch and press.** `couch mould` turns the sheet out onto the post,
   and turning the press squeezes the water out. A sheet left unpressed
   cockles as it dries and will never lie flat.
4. **Dry.** Hang it in the loft until it is dry. Until then it is too wet
   to take ink.
5. **Size.** Dip it in the size tub so it will take ink without blotting.

See `help read`, `help write`, `help tear`, `help seal` and `help couch`
for the commands.
