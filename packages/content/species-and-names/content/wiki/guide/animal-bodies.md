# Animal bodies {#animal-bodies}

Every animal is built of parts — a head, a torso, legs or fins or claws,
and the organs inside — and almost everything about it follows from those
parts. What it can do, how it senses the world, what keeps it warm and
what comes off it when it is butchered are all read off the body, not
written down beside it. A new kind of animal works the same way as the
old ones.

## Growing up and changing {#growing-up}

An animal is born young and grows along its kind's curve: a calf becomes a
cow, a lamb a ewe, and each is called what it is at its age. Some animals
change their whole body as they grow. A tadpole lives in water and breathes
it; it becomes a froglet and then a frog that breathes air, and through all
of it it is the same creature. A silkworm moves from larva to pupa to moth
on the warmth its tray has had.

## The seasons in an animal {#seasons}

Animals follow the length of the day. A ewe comes into season as the days
shorten, a hen lays through the long days, a male ruts in his season, and a
frog dens down in the mud when the days are short and the cold sets in.
`look` at an animal to see what season it is in. A cow gives milk only
while she is in milk; ask too soon and she will tell you so.

## Castration {#castration}

A young male can be cut for castration with a knife, by someone who knows
some medicine: `operate <animal> for castration with <knife>`. A bull calf
cut young grows into a steer and then an ox — heavier than a bull, and
steadier in hand, which is why oxen pull the plough. A boar cut young is a
barrow; a cockerel, a capon. A male cut young does not serve.

## Coats and the cold {#coats}

A coat — fur, fleece or feathers — keeps an animal warm, and how warm
depends on how much of it there is. A shorn sheep left out on a cold, wet
night is in danger that a sheep in its fleece is not. Cold-blooded animals
take the temperature of their surroundings: chilled, they slow; colder,
they go torpid.

## Wounds and lost parts {#wounds}

A wound costs what the wounded part did. An animal hurt in an eye sees
less; one that loses a leg moves worse; a body with two kidneys can lose
one and live. A part that comes off lies on the ground as a thing. A crab
grows a lost claw back at its next moult — unless the stump was burned.

## Butchering {#butchering}

What comes off a carcass is what its body is made of: the pelt or the
feathers first, then the cuts, the fat, the organs and the bones. A pelt
carries every hole the animal was killed through, and the leather it is
tanned into keeps them. A crab's shell is chitin, not bone. A whale is
flensed in great blanket pieces of blubber. Each cut taken leaves the
carcass lighter, and it can be worked a cut at a time — the loin today,
the rest tomorrow. `help butcher` has the details.
