# Your phone {#your-phone}

Anything you say, wave or shrug reaches everyone in the room with you —
the room carries it, and you need nothing for that. Reaching **past** the
room — a `dm` to a friend across the city, a line in a chat channel, an
emote at somebody elsewhere — needs a **tuner** at both ends. For most
people that tuner is a phone. Everyone arrives with one in a pocket, a
Hollis Pal bound to them.

`look phone` tells you everything about it: whether it is on, what it runs,
how much charge it has, and whose it is. The **AETHER** row on your status
shelf says the same thing in a word.

## When it cannot send {#when-it-cannot-send}

A phone tunes you only when it is **within reach** — in your hand, or
ready in a pocket or on your belt — switched **on**, **charged**, and
**yours**. A phone in a pack on your back does not, and neither does one
you set down.

When something cannot go, nothing is lost. The line is **held**, exactly as
you typed it, and you are told why and what to do: *ready it*, *switch it
on*, *charge it*. The moment the phone can carry it again — you take the
pack off, switch it on, pick it up off the charger — what you held is sent,
and you see it go. What others sent you while you could not read waits for
you too, and arrives under *While you were away…*.

A phone is read with your eyes: if you cannot see, what arrives waits until
you can.

## Apps {#apps}

What a phone does is what it runs. `apps` lists them; `apps install` and
`apps uninstall` change them. **Messages** is the one that carries `dm` —
uninstall it and your messages are held until you put it back.

Some apps bring **commands** with them. While you carry the phone they are
yours to type; leave it in a pack, switch it off or let it die, and the
command tells you why it will not go — the same as a message would.
**Broadcast** (`apps install broadcast on phone`) is one: it gives you
`program`, to file a show of your own, and `rodney`, to run it on the air.
A broadcast desk gives you the same two while you sit at it.

## Charging {#charging}

A phone draws a little all the time it is on and in somebody's hands, and a
little more for each message. When it is dead it carries nothing. Set it
**on** a charger that has power — `put phone on charger` — and it fills,
whether you stay or not; you can leave it overnight and log off. Every dorm
room in Duncan Hall has one, and the general store sells them. A charger
only works plugged in somewhere: carried, it is just a pad.

## Whose it is {#whose-it-is}

A phone runs only for the person who holds its title. A stolen phone is a
brick. The phone you arrived with is **bound** to you — it cannot be sold or
given away. One you buy is yours to pass on: `title transfer <phone> to
<person>`, and they are asked first.
