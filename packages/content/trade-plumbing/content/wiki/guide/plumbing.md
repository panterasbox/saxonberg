# Plumbing {#plumbing}

A house has water because a pipe brings it. The city's main runs under the
streets, but a newly bought lot is not on it: its yard has a standpipe on
the district tank, and nothing indoors runs until somebody lays the pipe.

## The pipe {#the-pipe}

Lead pipe is cast at the plumber's yard in the goods yards. Melt a pig of
lead on the hearth or the firepot, pour it on the sand bed for sheet or
into the pipe mould for a length of pipe. Casting is also where a plumber's
hand starts: the joints come once that hand is a novice's.

## Laying it {#laying-it}

A lot's **service run** goes from the lane to the house wall: `fit lead
pipe to service run` until it is laid, and the house is on the main. Each
room that wants water then needs its own run — the kitchen's is waiting,
empty, for its lengths. A wiped joint is a plumber's reputation: the better
the hand, the less it weeps and the harder a frost it holds through.

## What goes wrong {#what-goes-wrong}

Lead in soft, acid water gives some of itself up, and the water that stood
in the pipe overnight carries the most of it — run the tap first. A lead
pipe in a room that freezes bulges and splits, and at the thaw it runs out
across the floor; an iron one cracks outright. A split lead pipe can be
mended (`repair`) with enough heat for lead, which a firepot gives; iron
wants the forge.
