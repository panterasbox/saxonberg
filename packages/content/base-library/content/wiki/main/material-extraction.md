# Giving to what it holds {#giving-to-what-it-holds}

Some substances **give something to a liquid held against them**. Oak gives
a cask's spirit oak and a little vanilla; a charred inside gives vanilla and
char. A lead glaze, a pewter jug or a lead pipe gives lead, and gives it
faster the more acid the contents are: vinegar draws more out than water,
and soft water more than hard.

What a wall gives, it gives up. A new oak cask flavors its first fill most
and every fill after less, until it has nothing left to give. A cooper can
burn a fresh char inside it, and the char gives again. A sealed coat inside
(pitch, a glaze that holds water) keeps the wall's own taste from the
contents, though a lead glaze still gives its lead through itself. See
[[guide:vessels|What a vessel holds]].

This is a property of a substance, named for its physics; a kind of
material has it when every member does.
