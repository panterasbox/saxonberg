# Doors and ways {#doors-and-ways}

Every place has ways out of it: `north`, `up`, `pumphouse`, `out`. A way can
stand empty, or something can stand in it: a door, a gate, a curtain, a
window. `look` lists both in one line, and each screen that shows the way
says the same thing.

```
── Obvious exits: south (the front doors, open), west (the west window).
```

## One door, two sides {#one-door}

A door between two places is a single door. Shut it from one side and it is
shut from the other. Lock it from inside and anyone at the front finds it
locked. `look door` and `look south` both describe it from where you are
standing.

- `open door` / `close door` work from either side.
- `go south` walks through an open door. A shut door stops you with *the
  front doors are closed*.
- A door that **swings shut behind you** lets you through and is shut again
  after you. The doors of a house or a dorm room work this way: walk in with
  the key and the door latches behind you.

## Locks and keys {#locks}

A locked door turns away anyone without a key that fits. A key that fits lets
you through without unlocking. You `unlock` a door to let in people who have
no key, and `lock` it again after.

Many doors have a **free side**. From inside you can open and lock them with
no key at all, the way a thumb-turn works. Your own front door is free from
inside, and so is the pump house's. A guest you let in is never trapped.

## Windows {#windows}

A window into another place shows what is beyond it, and lets light, sound
and smell through. It does not let you through: `go west` at the glazier's
hut says *the west window is not a way through*. A dirty window passes less
light. Wash it and the light comes back.

## Curtains, gates and gratings {#others}

- **A curtain**, drawn across, lets you walk through but not see through.
- **A gate** lets light and sound through its bars even when it is shut.
- **A fixed grating** is always open and never shuts. It lets everything
  through except a person.

## Long walks over open country {#tracks}

In town, every way joins two places that sit side by side. Out in the
country, some ways are **tracks**: the long walk to a remote claim or up the
valley. A track's direction is a bearing, the way the place lies from here,
not a promise of a straight road. A track takes longer to walk, and nothing
can hang in it.
