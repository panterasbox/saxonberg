# Smell and taste {#smell-and-taste}

A smell is something coming off a thing and reaching you. A glass under your
nose and a room are the same event at different distances, and a smell carries
from room to room, fainter at every doorway. That is how you find a fire or
something gone off: follow it and notice it get stronger. Nobody tells you where
it is coming from.

## Smelling {#smelling}

`smell` reads the air where you stand, and `smell <thing>` puts your nose to one
thing. Everyone can tell how strong a smell is. What practice in a trade gives
you is the name: an untrained nose smells *smoke* where a maltster smells *peat
smoke*, and a trained nose can pull more smells apart at once. Whatever is too
faint for your nose stays faint however skilled you are.

A smell is named on a thing only when that thing is in the room with you, you can
see it, and it is the only thing there carrying the smell. In the dark you get the
smell and nothing else.

You stop noticing a smell you stand in. Stay a while somewhere that reeks and it
fades; go away and come back and it is there again. Your own smell starts out
faded, which is why you can't tell how much you need a bath, and somebody else can.

`analyze aroma` lists everything you can separate. `measure aroma with` a gas
analyzer gives each smell as a figure.

## Tasting {#tasting}

`taste <thing>` takes a real mouthful: a small bite of food, a sip of whatever a
vessel holds, or a lick of something that is neither. It reaches you the way
eating does, so tasting something harmful harms you a little. You can't taste
what is sealed, and you can't taste a floor or a knife. You read each of the
tastes and what reaches your nose from the back of your mouth.

How much you like something is yours, not the food's. It comes partly from how
you were born, grows with what you get used to, sours on whatever has made you
ill, and rises with hunger. Two people can honestly disagree about the same soup,
though everyone agrees about a rotten one. `eat` tells you how a meal went down.
